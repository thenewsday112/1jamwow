Elvis Presley’s rights holders sue Anthropic, saying it trained AI on his music

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The owners of Elvis Presley’s music have sued Anthropic, alleging it used his copyrighted lyrics without permission to train its AI models and generating infringing versions of his songs.

In a lawsuit filed Friday, Authentic Brands Group, the licensing platform that controls Presley’s intellectual property — along with other companies, trusts and individuals who own rights to his music — accused Anthropic of infringing copyrights to 274 songs without any license or payment.

These span some of Presley’s biggest hits, including “All Shook Up,” “Can’t Help Falling in Love,” “Don’t Be Cruel,” “A Little Less Conversation” and “Return to Sender.”

“The Elvis Plaintiffs’ iconic collective catalog contains some of the most groundbreaking, revolutionary, influential, and enduring musical compositions in human history,” the lawsuit stated.

The plaintiffs allege that the infringement has occurred throughout “the entire development” of Anthropic’s flagship Claude models and continues today as users generate outputs that reproduce copyrighted lyrics or create unauthorized adaptations of the songs.

“As we say in the lawsuit, the Elvis Plaintiffs are guardians of, and highly protective of, Elvis’s song which are some of the most iconic of all time,” attorney Richard Busch wrote in a statement.

A spokesperson for Anthropic wrote: “We disagree with the claims and intend to defend this case fully.”

The complaint further describes Anthropic as having “built its entire business model on infringing copyrights at an unfathomable scale,” pointing to the alleged mass scraping of internet content and pirating of books to develop its Claude language models.

Last year, Anthropic agreed to a landmark $1.5 billion settlement in a copyright infringement lawsuit filed by authors who alleged the company had illegally used pirated copies of their books to train AI models. It was the largest settlement in the history of American copyright cases.

The lawsuit by Presley’s rights holders comes amid a larger wave of lawsuits against Anthropic and other AI companies accused of using copyrighted musical works without consent or compensation.

In January, Universal Music, Concord Music Group and ABKCO Music expanded their lawsuit against Anthropic over the alleged infringement of copyrighted song lyrics. BMG Rights Management, Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell Music filed similar lawsuits against Anthropic this year.

Anthropic and the AI music generation company Suno were also recently hit with lawsuits from the independent music publisher Round Hill — which is represented by Busch, the lead attorney for Presley’s rights holders — and the independent music company Reservoir Media.

“Even after the Defendant supposedly put up ‘guardrails’ after being sued, in one of its latest iterations, Claude continues to infringe,” the Presley rights holders state in their lawsuit.

The complaint notes that when asked recently to re-create specific songs “for a modern audience,” Claude did so.

“These renders keep landing very close to the originals: same structure, same hooks reused with light rewording, and full sets of verses/choruses/bridges,” the complaint said. “That’s edging past ‘inspired by’ into reproducing the copyrighted song, even when the words are swapped out.”

Presley’s rights holders are seeking $41.1 million in statutory copyright damages — up to $150,000 for each of the 274 works they alleged were willfully infringed — according to the complaint.

They also demand a permanent injunction to prevent Anthropic from “further infringing” on Presley’s works and an order for the company to destroy all unauthorized copies of the copyrighted works.

Angela Yang is a culture and trends reporter for NBC News.

Alaska Senate candidate Mary Peltola delays campaign events after car crash

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Former Rep. Mary Peltola is rescheduling several upcoming campaign events after she and her family sustained “minor” injuries in a car crash, her campaign announced Sunday.

“Mary and her family sustained minor injuries but are in good spirits. Unfortunately, per the advice of medical professionals, Mary’s upcoming events in Fairbanks and Bethel will be rescheduled for later in the week as she takes time to recover,” Peltola campaign manager Cheryl Bruce said in a statement.

“Mary is disappointed that the accident is delaying the start of her Alaska Always Tour, and she is eager to get back on the campaign trail to speak directly with Alaskans about her plan to take on the rigged system and lower costs for working families,” Bruce added.

Peltola was en route to the Alaska Federation of Natives’ annual banquet Saturday when a vehicle transporting the candidate and her family was struck by another driver, according to the campaign. A spokesperson for the campaign said they had no reason to believe the accident had been politically motivated.

The police department in Anchorage confirmed Sunday that a collision involving three vehicles occurred on evening and those involved sustained “non-life threatening injuries.”

Police added that the incident is under investigation.

Peltola, a Democrat, is locked in a competitive Senate race against GOP Sen. Dan Sullivan. She was elected to the House in 2022 but narrowly lost her re-election bid in 2024, the same year that President Donald Trump won the state by 13 percentage points.

Sullivan’s re-election race, in which he’s seeking a third term, is tighter than expected, with Alaska voters tying Trump to high gas and grocery prices resulting from the ongoing war with Iran.

Trump and Vice President JD Vance rallied voters in Alaska separately earlier this month.

Republicans have accused Democrats of recruiting another candidate named Dan Sullivan to run for the Senate, saying that voters may be confused by the presence of two Dan Sullivans on the ballot if they plan to vote for the incumbent senator.

Democrats have not taken credit for recruiting the second Dan Sullivan into the race. He’ll appear on the ballot as Dan J. Sullivan, while the incumbent senator will be identified by his name, political party and a clear notation that he’s the incumbent.

Last week, the Justice Department sued Alaska’s election officials, seeking to block certification of the election if Sen. Dan Sullivan loses. It cited Dan J. Sullivan’s presence on the ballot as a point of “voter confusion.”

On Saturday, Democrats filed a motion urging a federal judge to dismiss the case.

Brazil's President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, the Workers' Party (PT) candidate for re-election, reacts after voting during the presidential runoff election, in Sao Bernardo do Campo, Brazil, on October 25, 2026. | Photo Credit: Reuters

Brazilians began voting in Sunday's (October 25, 2026) polarising presidential election between incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Flavio Bolsonaro, a contest that will determine whether the U.S. will significantly expand its growing influence in Latin America.

Polls before Sunday's (October 25) vote showed the two candidates, who need more than 50% of the vote to become Brazil's next leader in January, in a neck-and-neck race. The election pits the left-of-centre veteran politician Mr. Lula against Mr. Bolsonaro, an ally of U.S. President Donald Trump and the son of imprisoned former President Jair Bolsonaro.

Flávio Bolsonaro bested Mr. Lula by more than 2.2 million votes in the first round of the election earlier this month. More than 33 million voters out of nearly 160 million did not turn out.

The difference between the two candidates is stark. Mr. Bolsonaro and Mr. Lula have opposing ideological views on issues ranging from U.S.-Brazil relations and climate change to social welfare.

A win for Mr. Bolsonaro would signify a pivot to the right by Latin America's most populous country and its largest economy in a year when Trump allies have notched victories in Chile, Peru and Colombia.

A victory for Mr. Lula would leave Brazil and Mexico, which is the region's second-most populous country and led by President Claudia Sheinbaum, with left-of-centre leaders.

In many ways, Brazil's runoff vote resembles a rematch between the country's two polarized political forces that trigger passion as much as loathing. Four years ago, Mr. Lula beat Mr. Bolsonaro's father by the narrowest margin since the country's return to democracy in 1985. Polls suggest that this election will be even closer.

Mr. Lula, who first governed the country between 2003 and 2010, has faced a tough reelection battle because of concerns about the cost of living and security. Flávio Bolsonaro made a tough-on-crime approach the cornerstone of his campaign and pledged to bring down food costs.

Both have been hit by corruption suspicions involving family members and allies, with Mr. Bolsonaro implicated in a sprawling scandal rocking Brazil. Mr. Lula spent more than a year in prison on corruption convictions that were eventually annulled.

Mr. Bolsonaro's admission that he accepted about $12 million from disgraced former banker Daniel Vorcaro to produce a film on his father's life has cast a shadow over his campaign. He is under investigation for corruption, money laundering and tax evasion in connection with the case.

In the lead-up to the vote, Mr. Lula frequently portrayed Mr. Bolsonaro, whose father is under house arrest for attempting a coup, as a threat to democracy. Mr. Bolsonaro has promised to seek an amnesty for his father if elected.

The next President will name at least three Supreme Court justices out of 11 in his next term due to upcoming retirements. Mr. Bolsonaro has long taken aim at the country's top court — which has been rocked by a corruption scandal — and promised to push for judiciary reform.

Mr. Lula, trying to lure undecided voters, has invested in costly incentives since he lost the first round of voting. His government's most recent moves have been endorsing a debt relief program for small businesses and promising free public transportation on weekends. A win for the incumbent came with the Senate's approval of the six-day working week — a popular proposal backed by the government.

Mr. Lula has criticised the U.S. President before Brazil's election, speaking about the risks of U.S. intervention in Brazilian elections after the Trump administration imposed high tariffs against Brazilian exports, sanctioned a Supreme Court justice who chaired the investigation that ended up resulting in the sentencing of Jair Bolsonaro and classified drug-trafficking groups as foreign terrorist organizations, which experts say may have been intended to make Lula look soft on crime.

Flávio Bolsonaro has touted his proximity to the White House and insisted that Brazil needs to strike better deals with the Trump administration, including the rare minerals economy. Brazil estimates that its deposits hold up to 21 million tons, second only to China. He denies he's being helped by the U.S. government.

Voting in Brazil is conducted electronically and the result is usually available within hours after polling stations close at 5 p.m. Brasilia time (2000 GMT).

In einer Dresdner Wohnung bricht ein Feuer aus. Zwei sechsjährige Kinder und zwei Erwachsene erleiden schwere Brandwunden. Fünf weitere Menschen kommen mit leichteren Verletzungen davon. Die Ursache des Unglücks beschäftigt nun die Ermittler.

Bei einem Wohnungsbrand in Dresden wird eine Familie schwer verletzt: Zwei sechsjährige Kinder erleiden schwere Brandwunden, teilte die Feuerwehr mit. Zudem werden zwei Erwachsene schwer verletzt, auch sie hätten schwere Brandwunden. Bei allen handele es sich um Mitglieder einer Familie. Fünf weitere Menschen wurden den Angaben zufolge leicht verletzt.

Die Feuerwehr sprach von einem Brand mit Verpuffung. Als die ersten Einsatzkräfte am Ort des Geschehens vor einem Mehrfamilienhaus im Dresdner Stadtteil Löbtau eingetroffen seien, hätten sich bereits Bewohner des Hauses vor dem Gebäude befunden. Die Wohnung sei nicht mehr bewohnbar. Fünf Bewohner des Hauses - drei Kinder im Alter von einem, vier und sieben Jahren, eine 37-jährige Frau und ein 40-jähriger Mann hätten Symptome einer Rauchgasvergiftung gezeigt und seien vor Ort medizinisch untersucht und behandelt worden.

US south-west braces for more storms after enduring days of rain and flooding

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

More inclement weather forecast in rain-soaked Four Corners region after evacuations and rescues last week

Another round of heavy showers and thunderstorms were expected to hit the south-west US on Sunday after the region endured days of rain that forced evacuations, water rescues and multi-million-dollar damage.

The National Weather Service issued a flood watch for much of the Four Corners – the meeting point of Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico and Utah – even as local officials forecast further receding of swollen rivers that surged since rains picked up on Wednesday.

About 4ft (1 meter) of water flooded the home of Shane Ferrari, the sheriff of San Juan county, New Mexico. He said the area’s situation was stable on Sunday, but he and other residents were trying to clean up and deal with damage.

The New Mexico governor, Michelle Lujan Grisham, said in a statement on Saturday that law enforcement had reported hundreds of water rescues between the cities of Farmington and Bloomfield.

“Families in Bloomfield and Farmington spent the night on rooftops watching the river flood their homes, and our national guard and state police crews have been bringing them to safety,” said Lujan Grisham. “Right now, our focus is saving lives. If you’re told to evacuate, go. Once the water recedes, the state will do everything in its power to help San Juan county families rebuild.”

“No deaths have been reported,” she added.

The San Juan River in Farmington on Friday ran 2 ft above its previous record set in 1909.

San Juan county spokesperson Devin Neeley said the county did not have official numbers on damage to homes or the number of people evacuated, but he was not aware of any loss of life.

Neeley said the flood waters were receding but left mud and silt, much of which has been contaminated with sewage because of failures at waste plants. There were still power outages affecting multiple areas, and it would probably be a few days until residents got their power back on.

“It’s a pretty active disaster zone,” he said. “We’re not on the downswing by any means.”

The flooding is just the latest from two months of on-and-off heavy rain caused by tropical moisture coming ashore from the Pacific. After months of drought, other recent deadly flooding struck the Grand Canyon and areas of New Mexico.

New Mexico has been hit with back-to-back weather events this weekend. A tornado struck Santa Fe on Saturday, damaging at least eight houses, including to roof collapses, according to a statement by the city of Santa Fe. No injuries were reported.

Far-right presidential candidate Marine Le Pen vowed Sunday to allow France to prospect for oil again on its territory if she is elected in May, a proposal reminiscent of U.S. President Donald Trump's “drill, baby, drill " policy to speed up fossil fuel development.

In a speech to her National Rally party, at a meeting that it billed as the launch of her presidential campaign, Le Pen said she would abolish a 2017 law that banned the issuing of new exploration permits for hydrocarbons, in favor of renewable energies. It also banned exploration and extraction of shale gas. The law was named after Nicolas Hulot, a former environment minister.

Le Pen's nearly hour-long address to supporters gathered in the city of Orleans went back over her familiar nationalist, anti-immigration themes, centered around her view that France has been ruined by its leaders but that she can give it brighter future.

“The fires are going out of the old great parties, my friends, and they're stirring on a pile of ashes,” she said.

“We're living not the end of the world but the end of a world, of their world. It's up to us to reinvent the one that is coming," she added.

The 58-year-old Le Pen is making fourth campaign for the French presidency. She is seen as the current favorite to succeed President Emmanuel Macron, who is constitutionally barred from seeking a third consecutive term.

Her supportive crowd shouted “We’re going to win” and “Marine, president" as she spoke.
