Obama unloads on Trump over the Iran war and mocks the president’s diplomatic strategy

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Please refresh the page or navigate to another page on the site to be automatically logged inPlease refresh your browser to be logged in

Obama bashed Trump for causing fuel prices to surge and what he saw as the unnecessary deaths of service members in a war that has raged on for nearly eight months

Former Democratic President Barack Obama has unloaded on Donald Trump for the Iran war, mocking the Republican president’s diplomatic strategy.

“These are not serious people. They’re not serious people. And the problem is you end up having to pay the price for their lack of seriousness,” Obama said Saturday while stumping for Democrats in Ohio.

The U.S., alongside Israel, started launching strikes against Iran in late February, with Trump claiming that Americans were in imminent danger over the Middle Eastern country’s nuclear ambitions.

But Obama argued that the issue had already been dealt with under his administration — until Trump unraveled it.

“Let’s think about this Iran war. The original rationale was, we’re going to go in there and get them to give up their nuclear program, their nuclear weapons,” Obama said. “Now, the thing was, Iran didn’t have nuclear weapons, because I put a deal in place that everybody acknowledged — kept them from getting nukes. And I didn’t have to fire a missile to do it; I just had to engage in diplomacy.”

Obama was referring to the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action, commonly called the Iran Nuclear Deal, which the Middle Eastern country signed in 2015 and Trump withdrew from during his first term.

The deal restricted Iran’s nuclear program to ensure it was only using those materials for peaceful purposes in exchange for sanctions relief.

But Trump argued when withdrawing from the deal in 2018 that it “allowed Iran to continue enriching uranium and — over time — reach the brink of a nuclear breakout.”

Trump has insisted throughout the war that it would be a quick operation, but it has now dragged on for nearly eight months. Soon after the strikes began, Iran effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz, blocking oil tankers from passing through the vital passageway and causing fuel prices to skyrocket.

A total of 21 American service members have been killed and 865 wounded in operations related to the war, according to the Pentagon.

“So seven months later, how’s that war going?” Obama asked.

“Our allies and our bases have been attacked; Americans have been killed; hundreds of Americans have been wounded; we have, we’ve run down our stock of missiles,” the former president said, though Trump has denied reports of munitions shortages. “And Iran now has veto power over the Strait of Hormuz, where oil goes through to the world market.

“This is the reason why the price of oil is up. Nobody disputes that. This is reason the price diesel is up. This is why the reason the just about everything is up.”

Trump is hosting daily rallies across the country to boost Republicans’ chances of keeping control of Congress in November’s midterm elections — all while dealing with his deeply unpopular war.

Forty-seven percent of Democrat voters and 56 percent of Republican voters think ending the war in Iran should be one of the highest priorities for the next Congress, according to a new AP/NORC Center for Public Affairs Research poll.

Obama was in Cleveland Saturday to support Amy Acton, who is running for Ohio governor against Trump ally Vivek Ramaswamy, and former Senator Sherrod Brown, who is running against Republican Senator Jon Husted.

Ramaswamy saw his betting odds plummet over a controversial ad using his opponent’s mother, whom Acton has accused of abuse, to call her a “liar” and urge Ohians not to vote for her.

Acton has an 83 percent chance of winning the race compared to Ramaswamy’s 18 percent on the prediction market Polymarket as of Saturday evening.

Ramaswamy was also trailing Acton by seven points in a traditional poll. Acton had 50 percent of support among likely Ohio voters compared to Ramaswamy’s 43 percent, according to a recent survey conducted by Marist Poll before the campaign ad was released.

In that same poll, Brown was up four points with 48 percent of voter support compared to Husted’s 44 percent.

Two men killed by falling trees as Storm Boelo leaves 90,000 properties without power

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Storm Boelo floods town centre of Largs, Scotland

Two men have been killed by falling trees on Sunday as Storm Boelo continues to bring severe weather to the UK.

A man in his 20s was pronounced dead in north London after the car he was driving was hit by a falling tree. It came hours after a 60-year-old man died in Dumfries and Galloway.

The harsh weather battering the UK had left more than 90,000 properties without power as of 19:00 GMT on Sunday, the Energy Networks Association (ENA) said.

Those still without electricity have faced delays as the dangerous conditions were "limiting network engineers' opportunities to safely start the work", the ENA added.

Roads across Scotland have been flooded, including part of the A82 at Dumbuck Quarry in Dumbarton

The Metropolitan Police were called to Dollis Road in Finchley at around 12:40 GMT to reports a tree had fallen onto a moving car, and the man was pronounced dead at the scene.

Chief Inspector Guy Ellwood, from the North West Command Unit, said: "This is a deeply tragic incident and our thoughts are with the loved ones of the young man who lost his life."

A London Ambulance Service spokesperson said it sent incident response officers, paramedics from its hazardous area response team, and a trauma team from London's Air Ambulance to the scene.

Earlier, Police Scotland said a man died after being hit by a falling tree at at Mid Kelton near Castle Douglas. Meanwhile, two women were also taken to hospital after being hit by a another falling tree in Aberfeldy.

During Storm Boelo and the earlier Storm Austen, which hit the UK on Friday, 838,700 properties across the country faced power cuts, according to the ENA.

Wind gusts peaked on Sunday morning at 85mph in Machrihanish, in Argyll and Bute, and 84mph in both Stornoway in the Outer Hebrides, and Capel Curig in north Wales.

An amber wind warning issued by the Met Office was in place until 21:00 on Sunday across Scotland and parts of northern England.

A yellow warning that also covered other areas of the north of England, including Liverpool, Manchester, Leeds, Sheffield and Newcastle-upon-Tyne, as well as parts of the East and West Midlands and North Wales, also expired at 21:00.

The Met Office urged people to "stay indoors as much as possible", adding: "If you do go out, try not to walk, or shelter, close to buildings and trees."

The strongest gusts have continued to spread eastwards across the UK on Sunday.

The sea wall at New Brighton promenade in Merseyside took a battering over the weekend

Four flood warnings have been issued for parts of England and Wales on Sunday, while there are 17 flood warnings in place in Scotland.

High rainfall totals have been recorded in the Scottish Highlands, peaking in Cluanie Inn which received 130mm.

Scottish government ministers and officials gathered on Sunday afternoon to discuss the impact of Storm Boelo.

In a statement, the Scottish government said while conditions may ease later on Sunday, there is potential for adverse weather to return in the coming days.

In Aberfeldy, Perth and Kinross, two women were taken to hospital after being struck by a fallen tree at around 12:50 GMT on Sunday, police said.

A person is covered in spray as waves crash in Largs, near North Ayrshire, Scotland

The towns of the Clyde coast in Scotland have been hit particularly hard by the severe weather. The main Skelmorlie to Largs road has been badly damaged and a number of businesses in Largs were flooded overnight on Saturday.

In Gourock, a boat ended up on the side of the road and there have been reports of power being out in Girvan all throughout Sunday.

In Dumbarton, on the north bank of the River Clyde, Muhammad Zubair Ashraf's mobile phone shop was flooded overnight.

Travel has been affected across the country, with police in Scotland warning against unnecessary road journeys.

Ferry services have been impacted, some flights to and from Belfast, Glasgow, Aberdeen and Dublin airports have been cancelled, and train services across the UK have faced disruption.
