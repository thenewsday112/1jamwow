Financial Times refuses to correct Putin call ‘disinformation’ – media

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Financial Times has refused to correct an anonymously sourced article about a recent phone call between Russian President Vladimir Putin and the leaders of Norway and Finland, despite objections from both Nordic governments.

Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store and Finnish President Alexander Stubb spoke with Putin earlier this week, drawing criticism from some of Europe’s more Russia-hawkish politicians. Store has since demanded the FT retracts its reporting on the conversation.

The British newspaper claimed that Putin spent significant time “lecturing” the Nordic leaders on “medieval Slavic history” and its relevance to the Ukraine conflict. Citing three anonymous sources described only as “familiar with the conversation,” the FT portrayed the Russian president as “fixated” on history.

Store’s government told Norwegian daily Verdens Gang (VG) that the characterization was inaccurate and that Finland had contacted the FT on behalf of both countries to challenge its account. Finnish media also covered the joint refutation, with the newspaper Helsingin Sanomat citing its own sources to dispute the British paper’s description of the call.

Putin’s foreign policy adviser Yury Ushakov said later in the day that the FT article is “some invented information, or rather disinformation” and that the Nordic leaders were correct to call out the publication.

The FT, however, rejected the request, telling VG that it stood by its reporting.

Its article, published on Tuesday, was written by three journalists.

One of them, American national Christopher Miller, previously worked for Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty, a US government-funded international broadcaster originally established with covert CIA funding to conduct anti-communist propaganda operations during the Cold War. Miller went to Ukraine in 2010 as a volunteer with the US Peace Corps.

His co-authors, Max Seddon and Henry Foy, head the FT’s bureaus in Moscow and Brussels, respectively.

Last week, the newspaper published a lengthy article by Seddon titled “The Putin whisperers,” recounting a dramatic but difficult-to-verify story of American and German nationals attempting to avert a “nuclear Armageddon” through backchannel diplomacy with Moscow.

Foy, notoriously broke a story in 2025 alleging that a plane carrying European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen had experienced suspected Russian GPS jamming. The FT journalist was himself aboard the aircraft when it landed in Bulgaria.

Key elements of the story were challenged almost immediately by flight-tracking service Flightradar24. Bulgarian authorities subsequently debunked the core claims relayed by FT.

Despite the discrepancies, the FT insisted it stood by its reporting. Foy did not respond to requests for comment from fellow journalists who questioned the account.

Ottawa quietly abandoned promise to end thermal coal exports after industry lobbying, A-G says

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Piles of coal at the Westshore coal export terminal are seen at Deltaport in Tsawwassen, B.C., in 2024.DARRYL DYCK/The Canadian Press

Environment and Climate Change Canada abandoned its work on ending thermal coal exports without ever notifying the public and without any record of being directed to do so, according to a new audit.

A report from the office of the auditor-general, tabled Thursday, criticized the move and pointed out that Canada is co-chair of an international alliance meant to help countries transition away from coal.

Burning coal is one of the most polluting forms of electricity generation. In 2021, Canada committed to banning thermal coal exports by 2030.

The audit found that while Environment Canada worked for three years to find a way to enforce the ban, that work stopped altogether in November, 2024 after lobbying from the coal industry.

“The department did not produce evidence of an official direction to discontinue work on the ban in 2024,” the audit said.

Even the work to enforce the ban was scaled down before Ottawa ultimately gave up on it.

The audit found that between October, 2021 and February, 2024, Environment Canada officials were trying to use existing laws to enforce the export ban – including by regulating it as a toxic substance, similarly to what Ottawa is doing now with single-use plastics.

The audit said that by February, 2024, those efforts shifted to considering non-regulatory approaches, including “voluntary measures,” though it doesn’t say exactly what those measures were.

The decision to stop the work wasn’t formalized until August, 2025, when the federal government was looking for cost-saving measures, said the audit. Still, Ottawa didn’t tell the public.

“However, we also found no clear records to show that the department had provided the minister with an assessment of the implications of stopping all work on the export ban,” the audit report said.

“While the commitment had been announced internationally and noted in multiple federal strategies, the work on the ban was stopped with no public announcement.”

Thermal coal exports were higher in 2025 than they were in 2021, the audit report says.

The report criticized the government’s secrecy on the file.

“The lack of public information and updates on the government’s current approach is concerning,” it said.

“Transparency in decision making provides certainty and enables interested parties, such as provincial and territorial governments, coal mining companies, transportation authorities, shipping terminals, and the labour force to contribute to a just transition.”

The report said 61 per cent of thermal coal shipped from Canadian ports in 2025 came up from the United States, largely from Washington, Oregon and California.

The American mines send it by train north to shipping terminals in British Columbia, mainly because regulations in the U.S. make it challenging to build export infrastructure along the Pacific coast to ship to Asian markets.

Former B.C. premier David Eby suggested that Ottawa could use Canada’s role in facilitating exports of U.S. thermal coal as leverage in trade negotiations.

In August, Eby – apparently unaware Ottawa already had abandoned the work – told reporters the federal government should move faster to phase out exports of thermal coal.

Thursday’s reports from the auditor-general’s office also looked into Global Affairs Canada’s role in overseeing $16-billion in commitments to international coal transition programs.

Canada committed $15-billion to the Women-Led Coal Transition Mechanism, and another $1-billion in low-interest loans to the Climate Investment Funds’ Accelerating Coal Transition investment program, which is jointly funded by Canada, the United Kingdom, Germany and Denmark. That $1-billion investment made up 42 per cent of the program’s funding, the audit found.

“For Global Affairs Canada, this contribution was also one of the department’s single largest project investments ever made,” the report said.

“The program finances country‑led investment plans with projects designed to accelerate coal retirement, scale up clean energy alternatives, and mitigate socio‑economic impacts on affected workers and communities. Projects are expected to continue up to 2047.”

The audit reported Environment Canada never informed Global Affairs Canada of the decision to stop work in November, 2024.

“We found that Global Affairs Canada was involved in the work on the thermal coal export ban only at the request of Environment and Climate Change Canada,” the report said.

“In accordance with its responsibilities for foreign affairs and trade policy, Global Affairs Canada provided information on the trade risks associated with potential implementation options for a thermal coal export ban.”

Pentagon fires top military planner

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Pentagon has fired its top military strategist as Washington draws up contingency plans for a possible escalation of hostilities with Iran.

Army Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia has been removed from his position as director for strategy, plans, and policy (J5) on the Joint Staff, a spokesman confirmed late Wednesday, following media reports of his dismissal.

His predecessor, Lt. Gen. Joseph McGee, was forced into retirement a year ago amid reported friction with Secretary of War Pete Hegseth and Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Gen. Dan Caine. Sylvia previously served as vice director of the J5.

The Pentagon did not explain Sylvia’s departure. His responsibilities included developing military strategies and plans to advise the chairman and the US president. He joins nearly two dozen senior military and civilian officials removed during Hegseth’s tenure.

The secretary has portrayed the dismissals as part of an effort to reduce the military’s bloated ranks of generals and make the armed forces more efficient and lethal. Critics, however, have characterized the shake-up as an ideological “purge.”

President Donald Trump has repeatedly raised the possibility of renewing large-scale hostilities with Iran, including resuming the intensive bombing campaign conducted during the first 40 days of the conflict, which the US and Israel launched in late February. A potential escalation could be timed around the November midterm elections.

Military experts have widely expressed concern that another bombing campaign would fail to “finish the job,” as Trump has put it, while further increasing the conflict’s economic toll worldwide. Tehran has repeatedly warned that it is prepared to retaliate against US allies in the Gulf region if it faces renewed attacks.

Conversely, speculation has emerged that Trump could dismiss Hegseth after the midterms, when the opposition Democratic Party is expected to make gains in both chambers of Congress. Critics of the Iran war, including Republican Rep. Thomas Massie, have argued that firing the secretary could help Trump avoid a potential impeachment battle if Democrats take control of the House.

Trump’s public rhetoric remains supportive of Hegseth and his policies.

Astronaut Joshua Kutryk spent more than seven hours hovering in the vacuum of space on Thursday, making him the fifth Canadian to perform a spacewalk.

The International Space Station was some 420 kilometres above the Earth when Kutryk and NASA astronaut Anil Menon stepped out.

The duo placed their suits on battery power at 7:57 a.m. EDT, marking the official beginning of the spacewalk. They were back in the station at 3:08 p.m., after a total of seven hours and 11 minutes.

The astronauts successfully installed seven of eight new solar panel systems needed to upgrade the station’s power capacity.

Following the walk, fellow Crew 13 members Luke Delaney and Jessica Watkins helped Kutryk and Menon out of their equipment and checked their biomedical data.

After taking off his helmet, Kutryk wiped his face and Menon smiled. Kutryk is scheduled to speak to media Friday morning.

NASA mission control in Houston said the spacewalk was “essentially flawless.”

Before the spacewalkers left the airlock, their crewmates wished them luck.

“From Luke and I on the other side of the hatch, good luck. We’re proud of you and we’ll see you on the other side,” Watkins, a NASA flight engineer, told them.

Kutryk is on his first mission in space and his trip outside the space station is expected to be his first of two spacewalks.

Joshua Kutryk is on his first mission in space and his trip outside the space station was expected to be his first of two spacewalks.Supplied

Before Kutryk, the last Canadian to perform a spacewalk was David Saint-Jacques in 2019.

Saint-Jacques answered questions from schoolchildren at the Montreal Science Centre as a livestream showing the spacewalk was projected.

He described the feat as “one of the most exciting and one of most dangerous things we do” and said Kutryk was in “very good spirits” when they last spoke ahead of the spacewalk.

“It’s kind of the peak of the career for an astronaut to get a chance to put on your own miniature spacecraft that we call a space suit and go outside to do some maintenance work,” he said.

Saint-Jacques said spacewalkers train for thousands of hours in a giant pool and a full-size replica of the space station in Houston. They learn to operate the space suit and study the procedures and equipment needed for their mission.

They also receive step-by-step instructions in real time.

Canadian astronaut Joshua Kutryk shares first impressions from space

The crew’s mission is crucial, Saint-Jacques said, as the station is nearly 30 years old and its original solar panels have lost efficiency. According to the Canadian Space Agency, the new panels, paired with previously installed arrays, will help increase power generation capacity on the station by up to 30 per cent. The space station is fully electric, said Saint-Jacques.

While astronauts can face technical challenges, such as a stuck bolt or trouble plugging in a connector, the veteran astronaut said he didn’t doubt Kutryk’s abilities.

The advice he gave the spacewalker? To really take in the view.

“Give yourself the chance to look around, let it all sink in, and come back with some emotions as well, because that really is what you bring back for yourself,” he said. “It’s very easy to get focused in the work, to want to do a good job.”

När Julia Dang upptäckte en knöl i bröstet sökte hon vård. Men remissen från vårdcentralen till sjukhuset skickades aldrig, och hon fick vänta flera månader på en ny undersökning. Nu riktar hon kritik mot vårdcentralen – samtidigt som allt fler patienter anmäler liknande brister i vården.

Det började med en knöl i bröstet i början av maj. Julia Dang, 30, kontaktade Kry vårdcentral i Sickla för att få den undersökt. Efter ett kort besök fick hon beskedet att en remiss skulle skickas vidare.

När Julia själv började ringa runt upptäckte hon att remissen aldrig hade skickats. Hon fick kontakta både sjukhus och vårdcentralen flera gånger för att försöka komma vidare i vården. Först tre månader efter det första besöket på fick hon till slut genomgå en mammografiundersökning.

Under tiden växte knölen från omkring två centimeter till drygt sex centimeter. I Julias journal konstaterar en läkare att ”den fördröjda handläggningen innebär en avvikelse från avsedd prioritering och kan ha medfört fördröjd diagnostik”.

Julia är inte ensam om att ha fått vänta på en cancerdiagnos.

När SVT går igenom klagomålen, som regionens patientnämnd tagit emot, hittar vi ytterligare ett fall på samma vårdcentral. En annan kvinna sökte då vård upprepade gånger under sju månader innan hon fick diagnosen ändtarmscancer.

Kry vill inte intervjuas och svarar skriftligt:

”Varje bedömning är en professionell avvägning som görs av människor, och det innebär att det ibland kan bli fel, även när alla gör sitt yttersta”, skriver Kry.

Även på andra håll i Region Stockholm klagar patienter på problem med cancerdiagnoser.

Inom primärvården, dit patienter ofta vänder sig först och där bland annat vårdcentraler ingår, har antalet klagomål om diagnosställning gått från 15 stycken 2024 till 35 under 2025.

Martin Andersson, chefläkare och chef för avdelningen patientsäkerhet och beredskap i Region Stockholm, svarar:”Det är väldigt ledsamt att höra om patienter som har fått en fördröjd allvarlig diagnos (...). Jag vill betona att vårdgivaren har en skyldighet att utreda avvikelser (...). Sedan är det alltid bra att patienter som upplever brister i vården anmäler det. Att antalet anmälningar till patientnämnden ökar är det dock för tidigt att dra slutsatser om, det behöver utredas och analyseras”.

En patient hade hosta under lång tid och bad vid flera tillfällen om att få bli undersökt. Först senare gjordes en röntgen som visade lungcancer i stadium 2. Enligt klagomålet kan tumörerna ha funnits i flera år.

En patient med långvarig svullnad och smärta i knät hänvisades till fysioterapi. Patienten betalade senare själv för en MR-undersökning, som ledde vidare till diagnosen skelettcancer.

Kvinna hade en tumör som syntes på röntgen flera år tidigare

En kvinna sökte vård under flera år för smärta i magen och ryggen samt tarmproblem. En röntgen visade en förändring, men enligt klagomålet bedömde läkaren att den var ofarlig och inte orsakade besvären. När symtomen senare förvärrades gjordes nya undersökningar. Då upptäcktes en elakartad tumör på samma ställe som förändringen hade synts på tidigare.

Kvinna sökte för knöl i armhålan – fick ingen remiss för mammografi

En ung kvinna sökte vård för en knöl i armhålan. Fyra månader senare återkom hon efter att ha upptäckt en knöl i bröstet. Enligt klagomålet ville läkaren först inte skicka en remiss för mammografi, men ändrade sig efter att kvinnan insisterat. Hon fick en avbokningstid redan nästa morgon och undersökningen visade bröstcancer. Vårdgivaren beklagade att kvinnans oro inte hade tagits på allvar.

Läkaren trodde det var magsjuka – visade sig vara cancer

Ett barn hade kräkts under en vecka och sökte vård på en vårdcentral. Läkaren bedömde att det handlade om magsjuka och tog inga blodprover. Någon vecka senare sökte familjen vård på ett barnsjukhus, där barnet fick en cancerdiagnos.
