Врач объяснил причину сонливости после кофе

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Фото: New Africa / Shutterstock / Fotodom

Врач-сомнолог Максим Новиков из АО «Медицина» (клиника академика Ройтберга) в беседе с «Газетой.Ru» объяснил, почему при хроническом недосыпе или нарушении сна кофе может не бодрить, а, наоборот, вызывать сонливость.

По словам специалиста, кофеин не создает энергию из ничего. Его действие связано с механизмом накопления в организме вещества, участвующего в формировании чувства сонливости. Кофеин временно препятствует реализации действия аденозина на определенных рецепторах мозга, из-за чего человек может ощущать прилив бодрости. Однако это не означает, что потребность организма во сне исчезла.

Именно поэтому кофе способен маскировать усталость. Если человек несколько ночей подряд спит мало, одна чашка не вернет ему потерянный сон — она лишь на время сделает ощущение усталости менее заметным.

При этом не у всех этот механизм проявляется одинаково: скорость переработки кофеина зависит от индивидуальных особенностей, возраста, генетики, беременности, приема некоторых лекарств и других факторов.

Shares were mostly lower Monday in Asia and oil prices fell after rising last week to their highest level in more than four years.

Analysts said investors were bracing for earnings reports of big companies and central bank decisions this week that could shake markets.

Tokyo's Nikkei 225 slipped 0.1% to 62,602.86 and the Kospi in Seoul dropped 1.1% to 5,494.29.

In Hong Kong, the Hang Seng edged 0.2% higher to 23,137.93, while the Shanghai Composite index lost 0.4% to 3,673.83.

Australia's S&P/ASX 200 shed 0.2% to 8,782.30.

The price of Brent crude, the international standard, fell 1.3% to $110.23 a barrel. U.S. benchmark crude sank 1.7% to $101.52 a barrel.

On Friday, the price for a barrel of Brent crude rose 3.1% to settle at $116.72, its highest level since June 2022. It has surged from less than $97 since early October.

Also Friday, the S&P 500 fell 0.5%, adding to its decline from a day before and logging its second-straight weekly loss. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.2%, and the Nasdaq composite slipped 0.2% after giving up a modest gain.

Several AI-related companies rallied, helping ease some of the impact from the broader market slide. Micron Technology rose 2.5%, Intel gained 1.7%, Microsoft added 0.7%, and Advanced Micro Devices closed 1.7% higher.

Stocks that have benefited from the frenzy over AI have been buffeted by waves of selling as investors fret over whether their high prices are justified. Many of those companies have been borrowing to fund investments in AI technology and high interest rates are making those loans more expensive.

Market players are closely watching corporate earnings, looking for a better sense of whether those investments are likely to pay off. Several influential AI-related companies report their latest financial results this week, including Google's parent, Alphabet, along with Microsoft and Amazon.

“This week is less about broad conviction and more about proof. Big Tech now has to show that the spending binge still deserves the market’s faith," Stephen Innes of SPI Asset Management said in a commentary.

“Investors want proof now. They want to know whether the billions going into the compute buildout are building a durable earnings machine or just a shinier version of the same old capex furnace,” he said.

Industrial companies had some of the biggest losses. Caterpillar fell 2.4%.

American Express slumped 3.7%, one of several financial stocks that lost ground, despite reporting profits for its most recent quarter that beat analysts' forecasts. The company did announce its first major job cuts in roughly a decade.

Treasury yields in the U.S. edged higher following sharp drops on Thursday.

Yields have been rising globally, partly over worries about governments taking on too much debt. Inflation also remains a concern and is stubbornly well above 3% in the U.S. That prompted the Federal Reserve to raise its benchmark interest rate at its last meeting in an effort to cool inflation.

Wall Street expects the central bank to hold rates steady at its meeting this week, but is leaning heavily toward another rate hike in December.

This week also will bring several big economic updates, including the Fed's preferred measure of inflation. The personal consumption expenditures index, or PCE, is expected to show that inflation rose at a 3.7% rate in September, sharply above the Fed's 2% target.

The U.S. war with Iran has severely cut the world's oil supply, driving energy prices higher and fueling a sharp jump in the cost of everything from gasoline to food and clothes.

In other dealings early Monday, the dollar slipped to 156.79 Japanese yen from 156.99 yen. The euro was unchanged at $1.1223.

A simple liquid can stop cavities without drilling, major study confirms

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A cavity doesn't always have to mean drilling, injections, or an uncomfortable visit to the dentist. A major clinical trial has confirmed that a simple, inexpensive liquid can stop tooth decay in young children, potentially preventing painful infections and the need for dental surgery.

The treatment, called silver diamine fluoride (SDF), takes only seconds to apply to each affected tooth. Rather than removing damaged tooth material and placing a filling, a dentist uses a tiny sponge tipped applicator to paint the liquid directly onto the cavity. The process requires no drilling, injections, or sedation.

The findings could have enormous implications for childhood dental care. Every year, thousands of young children in the United States visit emergency departments because of untreated cavities. Emergency physicians generally cannot provide the dental procedures needed to address the underlying problem, leaving some children with ongoing pain and infections. Others ultimately need dental surgery under general anesthesia, which makes them completely unconscious during the procedure.

Now, researchers led by the University of Michigan have produced compelling evidence that a much simpler approach can stop many cavities from getting worse.

Major Clinical Trial Confirms Silver Diamine Fluoride Works

Published in JAMA Pediatrics, the Phase III clinical trial included 830 children younger than 6. Participants were recruited through dental clinics, pediatric medical practices, and Head Start and Early Head Start programs in Michigan, New York, and Iowa.

Head Start and Early Head Start are federally supported programs that provide early childhood education and related services to eligible families.

The results showed that a solution containing 38% silver diamine fluoride successfully stopped decay in more than half of the affected baby teeth when treatment was provided at six month intervals.

Dentists refer to this as arresting tooth decay, meaning the damaged area stops deteriorating. Unlike a traditional filling, the treatment does not replace lost tooth structure. Instead, it helps prevent existing decay from progressing.

SDF works through the combined effects of silver and fluoride. Silver helps combat bacteria associated with tooth decay, while fluoride helps strengthen the mineral structure of teeth and resist further damage.

"This is a very effective and safe treatment -- even in children as young as 1," said Margherita Fontana, professor of dentistry at the University of Michigan School of Dentistry and the study's lead investigator.

A Simple Treatment Could Help Millions Avoid Dental Pain

Tooth decay is the most common chronic disease among children, affecting more than 40% of American children. Although cavities may initially cause few noticeable symptoms, untreated decay can eventually lead to severe toothaches, infections, problems eating and sleeping, missed school days, and repeated medical appointments.

For young children, conventional dental treatment can also be particularly challenging. Some are too young to remain still throughout a procedure, while others experience significant fear or anxiety.

SDF offers an alternative for many of these patients because it can be applied quickly without the equipment and procedures required for a traditional filling.

Fontana said the benefits could extend well beyond young children. Older adults, people with developmental or physical disabilities, individuals with severe dental anxiety, and patients who have difficulty obtaining conventional dental care could also benefit.

There is, however, one important tradeoff. SDF permanently turns the decayed portion of a treated tooth dark, which can be especially noticeable on front teeth. The discoloration is primarily cosmetic, but it may influence whether patients and families choose the treatment.

For many people, avoiding pain, drilling, or more invasive procedures may outweigh that drawback.

Why This Decades-Old Treatment Could Soon Reach More Patients

Silver diamine fluoride is not a newly invented substance. Dentists have successfully used it to manage cavities in numerous countries for decades.

In the United States, the FDA approved SDF as a medical device for treating tooth sensitivity in 2014. Dentists have also used it off-label to stop cavities, meaning they apply an authorized product for a purpose beyond its original approved indication.

What has been missing is large-scale clinical evidence from U.S. populations establishing its safety and effectiveness for treating tooth decay, a key step toward obtaining FDA approval for that specific use.

The University of Michigan trial was designed to help fill that gap.

"If we want more children and families to benefit from this treatment, we need rigorous evidence showing both that it works and that it's safe. From a public health perspective, if we want broader implementation across the United States, including in medical settings, we need carefully collected data in U.S. populations, and we now have that," Fontana said.

The investigation began in 2018 and continued despite the major disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic.

The researchers believe stronger clinical evidence could make SDF more widely available, including in pediatric medical settings where children often receive care long before their first dental appointment.

"In medicine, clinicians want high-quality evidence before changing practice," Fontana said. "It is important to have data they can refer to because young children often see pediatricians years before they ever visit a dentist, broader acceptance could allow many more cavities to be treated while a referral to a dental home is successful, and before they become painful, infected or require surgery."

A dental home refers to an ongoing relationship with a dental care provider who can deliver preventive care, treatment, and follow-up services.

Expanding access to SDF could allow more children to receive early intervention before relatively manageable cavities develop into serious dental problems.

FDA Approval Could Expand Access to Cavity Treatment

The trial brought together researchers from the University of Michigan, New York University, the University of Iowa, and Indiana University. The National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research, part of the National Institutes of Health, provided more than $12 million in funding.

The product tested was Advantage Arrest 38% SDF, supplied by Elevate Oral Care.

The findings provide clinical trial evidence that the manufacturer can use in an application seeking FDA approval of SDF as a drug for arresting dental caries, the medical term for tooth decay.

Amr Moursi, a professor of pediatric dentistry at New York University College of Dentistry and a co-principal investigator, emphasized the potential importance of that regulatory change.

"Our results support FDA approval of SDF for managing arrest of tooth decay in young children. Removing SDF from off-label status would be an important innovation which could lead to increased utilization by providers, enhanced payments by insurers and more consistent product quality," said Moursi, a co-principal investigator on the study.

For some children, receiving another application of SDF every few months could be sufficient to keep a cavity under control until the affected baby tooth naturally falls out.

Adults could also benefit, either by using SDF to manage decay over a longer period or as a temporary solution until a filling or another restorative procedure becomes affordable or practical.

The treatment is not suitable for every cavity, and stopping decay does not necessarily eliminate the need for future dental work. Nevertheless, the findings suggest that a procedure requiring only seconds to perform could help many patients avoid more complicated treatment.

"For almost anyone, this can arrest the decay and stop the infection and the pain it causes," Fontana said. "This could benefit many people."

Американская оборонная компания L3Harris и IT-компания Palantir захотели внедрить искусственный интеллект (ИИ) в поставляемые Вооруженным силам Украины (ВСУ) системы борьбы с беспилотными летательными аппаратами (БПЛА) Vampire, вопрос рассматривается. Об этом сообщило РИА Новости.

В сентябре портал TWZ рассказал, что армия США выделила американским компаниям Palantir Technologies и Anduril Industries средства в размере 192 миллиона долларов на перевод ракеты программы Tactical Intelligence Targeting Access Node (TITAN), оснащенной системой искусственного интеллекта, из стадии прототипирования в серийное производство.

‘Someone has been following me’: safety fears after bullet inscribed with ‘RAT’ left on Victorian minister’s car

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Paul Edbrooke says he fears for family’s safety amid heightened concerns for MPs after alleged attack on Helen Haines

Get our breaking news email, free app or daily news podcast

Victoria’s emergency services minister believes he is being followed and fears for his family’s safety after a bullet, etched with the word “RAT” and his former firefighter number, was left on his car.

The Herald Sun first reported on Monday that Paul Edbrooke had returned to his car after a community event in his Frankston electorate on Saturday and found the bullet on the bonnet. He later told reporters he had only been away from the car for about an hour and made the “chilling” discovery on his return.

“I was pretty shocked to find that it was a bullet – not just a bullet, but a bullet with the word ‘RAT’ inscribed on it and my firefighter ID on it,” Edbrooke said.

“The very fact of the matter that this has been put on my car means someone has been following me. It means someone knows what my car is, and that’s really quite scary.

“But I’m not going to be intimidated by this, and I refuse to be bullied.”

He said the incident was also “traumatising” for his children, who are young adults and also use the car. It comes just days after the alleged attack on the federal MP Helen Haines last week. There was no suggestion the two incidents were linked.

“It’s a car [my children] drive. They’ve been in this car. It could have been them that found the bullet, and that terrifies me. It’s totally unacceptable,” Edbrooke said.

He said the five-digit ID number etched into one side of the bullet was from when he was a firefighter and would be a personal detail “only firefighters would be aware of” and was not commonly known by the wider community. He said the only people from whom he was receiving “aggressive threats” and abuse on social media were members of the United Firefighters Union.

The union is taking action against Fire Rescue Victoria – an agency Edbrooke oversees – in the Fair Work Commission over the firefighters’ enterprise bargaining agreement. The parties have been unable to resolve the EBA since 2021.

The UFU has been campaigning against the state’s Labor government, vowing to run an expensive advertising campaign, staff polling booths with firefighters, and distribute authorised material urging voters to put the party last in next month’s election.

It is also planning to hold a members’ meeting on the question of disaffiliating from the Victorian Labor party altogether.

But the union leader, Peter Marshall, condemned the threat against Edbrooke and said whoever had left the bullet “does not speak for or act for firefighters or this union”.

“Firefighters spend every shift protecting life and property. Threatening anyone, including a minister we strongly disagree with, is the opposite of everything this union stands for,” Marshall said in a statement.

He also defended a video posted to the UFU social media account on Friday, in which he warned Edbrooke and the Victorian premier, Ben Carroll, that they were “in the gun”.

Marshall said his gun comment was in reference to the election: “On Friday I said the Premier and the minister were ‘in the gun’. I meant at the ballot box and anyone there, or seeing the video, would know that.”

Edbrooke urged other parties to reconsider their relationship with the union.

Members from the Liberals, One Nation and the Greens have met with the union in recent months – though the Greens appear to have the closest relationship. The party’s candidate for Pascoe Vale, Angelica Panopoulos, is set to host a joint event with the UFU on Saturday, described on social media as a “free day of family fun with the Greens and United Firefighters Union”.

In a joint statement, Greens MPs said the incident was “distressing” and there was “absolutely no place for threats and intimidation towards MPs, public figures or anyone else in our society”.

“Violence or threats of violence are never the answer, full stop,” they said.

“We’ve sent our support to Paul and his family this morning and we hope whoever is responsible is found and held to account for this completely unacceptable and dangerous behaviour.”

The Victorian opposition leader, Jess Wilson, said her thoughts were with Edbrooke and his family.

“There’s just no place for these sort of threats and intimidation in Victoria or this country,” she told ABC Radio Melbourne. “Off the back of the horrific [alleged] attack with Helen Haines last week, it’s incumbent on all of us to make sure we’re trying to take the temperature down.”

Carroll said he was “disgusted” by the incident.

“One thing I know about Paul, he’s tough. He won’t be intimidated by this. He’ll just get up and work doubly hard for our firefighters, for our police officers, and everyone he represents in Frankston,” he said.

Victoria police has confirmed detectives from its security investigation unit were “investigating a threat against a member of Victorian parliament which is alleged to have occurred in the greater Frankston area”. They said it was reported to police on Sunday.

Нелегальная доставка дронов на Украину вынудила Норвегию изменить законы

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Нелегальная доставка дронов на Украину из Норвегии вынудила страну изменить законы. Цепочку событий запустил депутат норвежского парламента Петер Фрелих, сообщает агентство NTB.

Парламентарий от Консервативной партии признал, что совместно со своей благотворительной организацией отправил партию беспилотников с тепловизорами для Вооруженных сил Украины (ВСУ), хотя на тот момент в Норвегии действовал запрет на подобные поставки.

Сначала партию дронов заблокировали на границе сотрудники таможни Норвегии. Вывоз такого рода помощи без специальных разрешений квалифицировался как незаконный в соответствии с нормами законодательства 1950-х годов.

В итоге партию БПЛА все же доставили на Украину, и правительство Норвегии пообещало в ускоренном порядке рассмотреть изменение законов, чтобы норвежцы могли беспрепятственно отправлять Киеву гражданские дроны.

Ранее вице-премьер Италии Маттео Сальвини вновь выступил против помощи Украине. По словам политика, переизбрание дает ему мандат на противодействие поддержке Киева внутри итальянского правительства.

Ex-Premier League player Jermaine Pennant is left needing 50 stitches after being mauled by dog while walking his own pet

Published: 02:36 GMT, 26 October 2026 | Updated: 03:02 GMT, 26 October 2026

Ex-Premier League star Jermaine Pennant was left needing 50 stitches on a leg wound after he was viciously mauled by a dog.

The former Liverpool midfielder, 43, had been walking his own dog - an American Akita named King - when he was pounced on by a German Shepherd.

Pennant said it came after he 'stepped in to protect' King when the other dog 'had a go at him', only for the footballer to eventually be attacked himself.

He told The Sun: 'It was really scary. The other dog had a go at King, so I stepped in to protect him.

'The next thing I know, it was attacking me. I was on the floor and it was on my leg for at least five seconds.

'I felt this huge pressure. Imagine the force of a dog bite. King jumped in to protect me.'

Gory images showed the extent of Pennant's injuries, with his right shin left bloodied and scarred in the aftermath of the incident.

The former Stoke City star was treated for four bite wounds on his leg and told he may need surgery for one larger one.

Jermaine Pennant, 43, shared a picture from a hospital bed after having more than 50 stitches

The ex-Liverpool midfielder was attacked by a German Shepherd while walking his American Akita, named King

Pictures shared by the TalkSport host showed large wounds on his left shin

Pennant later thanked his dog for 'saving his life' in a heartfelt Instagram post

But Pennant ultimately had more than 50 stitches inserted at the hospital, before crediting King for standing up to the German Shepherd and 'saving his life'.

In an emotional Instagram caption accompanying a picture of him embracing his dog, Pennant wrote: 'So I was attacked by a German Shepherd, I tried to protect king but the dog got my leg, I was on the floor with the dog still attached with no sign of letting go, until king intervened as he didn’t know what was going on.

'But the minute he did he fought the dog off and saved what could’ve been an horrendous situation for me.

'King was unhurt with a little nip on his leg, but had blood covered all over him which wasn’t mine, at this point I forgot the agony I was in, and my heart sank, but it wasn’t kings.

'My hero still protecting me and trying to look after me, KING you’re a legend love you, man’s best friend, thanks for taking care of us both.'

Pennant said he has no intention of reporting the matter to the police.

There were some 29,400 cases of out-of-control dog attacks last year, marking a rise of 83 per cent on the 16,040 recorded in 2020.

Pennant retired from football at the age of 35 in 2018 following a 17-year career.

The 43-year-old made 231 Premier League appearances during stints with Stoke, Liverpool, Birmingham City, Leeds United, Portsmouth and Arsenal.
