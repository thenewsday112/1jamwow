Kushner Lawyer Demands VANITY FAIR Pull Book Excerpt Over 'Defamatory Allegations'...

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Chad Mizelle, an attorney representing Jared Kushner, demanded that Vanity Fair take down a recently published book excerpt alleging that President Donald Trump's son-in-law had access to secret foreign bank accounts during his first term.

"We formally put Vanity Fair and Condé Nast on notice regarding the false and defamatory allegations published about our client, Jared Kushner," Mizelle wrote in a statement on X Saturday morning. "Serious questions are now being raised about the credibility of this reporting, including by independent journalists."

He continued: "Despite repeated requests, the author has failed to produce a single bank statement, wire transfer record, account number or other verifiable evidence supporting these extraordinary accusations."

As Mizelle went on, he claimed that they "have seen no indication that Vanity Fair conducted any independent verification of these claims before publishing them."

"We call on Vanity Fair to clarify publicly whether it independently verified these allegations and, if so, what evidence supports them. Or did it simply republish the author's claims without conducting its own fact-checking?" he concluded. "We have demanded that Vanity Fair remove the article and preserve all records relating to its reporting, fact-checking, editorial review and publication."

Mizelle also attached the letter he sent to Vanity Fair over the article in-question, which claimed the outlet has exposed itself to "serious legal risk" by sharing the excerpt from investigative journalist Matthew Cole's forthcoming book, "Princes and Thieves."

In the excerpt, which was published Thursday by Vanity Fair, Cole alleged that Dutch intelligence officials gained access to information from Kushner's compromised phone and discovered evidence of secret financial arrangements involving leaders in the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia. Investigators allegedly found that Kushner could access a UAE bank account that received hundreds of millions of dollars through Azerbaijan.

Cole further alleged that Saudi and Emirati officials arranged for at least $2 billion to be placed into accounts that Kushner could access, including one holding roughly $750 million where he was allegedly a signatory. The allegations have not been independently verified by TheWrap.

Kushner publicly denounced the allegations on X Thursday, writing, "[Vanity Fair] posted an excerpt from a book containing outrageous and categorically false allegations. The allegations made by this author are completely insane and never happened. We denied every one of these claims on the record, yet the author failed to provide a single shred of evidence to substantiate any of them."

"No bank statements, wire transfer records, account numbers, transaction dates, or other verifiable details were provided to support these extraordinary accusations," he continued. "During the period in question, I was one of the most scrutinized people in the world, subjected to intense media coverage, multiple congressional investigations, and a special counsel investigation. I voluntarily cooperated with these investigations, providing over 30 hours of testimony. None of these allegations ever surfaced, because none of these events ever happened."

Before concluding his statement, Kushner blasted Cole's claims as "outrageous fabrications being presented as investigative journalism."

"This is not journalism," he said. "It is a mixture of fantasy, fiction, and advocacy, written by someone who is either deeply disturbed or acting with sinister intent."

Mizelle followed suit with his own statement, adding on X, "Before publication, we sent Simon & Schuster a detailed 13-page letter responding to and categorically denying the extraordinary allegations made against my client, Jared Kushner, and challenging the total absence of supporting evidence."

"Vanity Fair has now published an excerpt amplifying these false and defamatory allegations," he continued on Thursday. "We have seen no credible evidence substantiating any of these claims, and we question what independent verification Vanity Fair conducted before publishing them. We intend to pursue legal action against those responsible and hold them accountable."

Representatives for Vanity Fair, Condé Nast and Simon & Schuster did not immediately respond to TheWrap's request for comment.

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

IDF officials fear Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu may seek to strike Iran before the Knesset election scheduled for Tuesday, leading to tensions with the military's leadership.

The IDF's brass disagree with the prime minister over the urgent necessity of an offensive and fear it may delay the election.

U.S. President Donald Trump was forced to issue a statement on Sunday night saying there would be no attack on Iran overnight, and the administration expressed surprise at Netanyahu's recent remarks.

On Sunday night, Israel's Channel 12 News reported on another spat between Netanyahu and IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir, which occurred during Netanyahu's visit to Palmachim Air Force base Sunday. Netanyahu visited the base and was photographed against the backdrop of an Arrow interception missile battery, threatening Israel's enemies: "You will suffer disproportionate blows, stronger than you can imagine."

Polling station committee members and observers representing parties and candidate lists may not relay information about the number of voters who have actually cast ballots at a particular polling station to their parties. The ban applies to all means of transmitting such information, the Supreme Court clarified in a supplemental ruling on the matter.

The clarification establishes an absolute prohibition on transferring polling station turnout data to political entities.

In response, the Israeli Democracy Guard, a nonprofit organization, welcomed the ruling and called on the Central Elections Committee to immediately update the guidelines for all polling station workers across the country to prevent disruptions on Election Day and violations of the law.

Amcha Yisrael chairman Ofer Winter reiterated Sunday that he has no intention of dropping out of the election race. At the party's closing rally, Winter sharply criticized a campaign spearheaded by Netanyahu to keep Amcha Yisrael out of the Knesset.

"Tens of millions of shekels have been invested to crush Amcha Yisrael. Over the past two months, people have come to people's homes, synagogues and children's bus stops, all for one reason: They are afraid," Winter said.

"Established forces in the political system don't want new people on the scene. We have not surrendered, and we will not surrender to this ugly and violent smear campaign," he added.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said he ordered the Israeli military and Mossad intelligence agency to overthrow the Iranian regime.

"I instructed the Mossad and the IDF to bring down this regime, because that is the only way to put an end to this threat," he said, speaking at the pro-government Channel 14. Responding to a report of tension between him and the head of the IDF, Netanyahu said "someone is trying to stir up a conflict," citing warnings that "Iran is trying to interfere in the elections to bring me down."

Earlier on Sunday, Channel 12 reported a conflict between the military and the prime minister over fears that Netanyahu may use the Iran war for campaign purposes. IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir reportedly warned that a recent order might spark a confrontation with Iran, demanding that Netanyahu sign off on it in writing.

Speaking on Channel 14, Netanyahu said, "This supposed rivalry between us is all nonsense," attacking Channel 12 and saying, "I keep looking for the times when they report the truth. It almost never happens."

Yashar party chairman Gadi Eisenkot warned that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu would do whatever it takes to remain in power, urging voters not to take political change for granted at the party's final campaign rally Sunday.

"I see the polls, I know the data, and I know Netanyahu – he will stop at nothing, no lie and no extreme measure, to remain in power," Eisenkot said.

"Anyone who thinks change is guaranteed is mistaken and misleading," he said. "Until the polling stations close on Tuesday, each and every one of us must act knowing that change is in danger. The responsibility for making it happen rests with each and every one of us."

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu warned his voters that if the opposition wins the upcoming election on Tuesday, Israel will "give citizenship to hundreds of thousands of Palestinians."

Speaking on the pro-government Channel 14, Netanyahu said Israel will make the move if the opposition bloc led by election frontrunner Gadi Eisenkot (Yashar) wins the election, adding that it would be "the end of the Jewish state."

Netanyahu said the move will be the result of the new government, which he warned would include Arab parties, allowing the reunification of Palestinian relatives. The prime minister added that "this is exactly what happened" in the aftermath of the Oslo Accords.

The High Court of Justice rejected a petition seeking to prohibit election officials and party representatives from carrying firearms at polling stations, which argued that the presence of armed individuals could intimidate voters, heighten tensions and undermine free and fair elections.

The petition, filed by the Adalah Legal Center for Arab Minority Rights in Israel and the Joint List, followed announcements by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party and far-right Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich's Religious Zionism party that they would deploy some 2,000 election observers in Arab communities, as well as a police directive allowing licensed gun owners to carry their weapons at polling stations.

In its response to the petition, the government said that the decision is a police matter, since police "have the authority and responsibility to determine the security arrangements required on that day." The government said it received no information of "disruptions, disturbances or planned threats against voters."

Joint List chairman Yousef Jabareen said the court "has once again demonstrated that it is neither willing nor able to protect the rights of the Arab public from persecution by the racist right-wing government," adding that Netanyahu "is panicking over the rising voter turnout among Arab citizens."

The son of former Prime Minister Menachem Begin – who founded Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party and signed Israel's peace treaty with Egypt – urged Likud party members to vote Netanyahu out of office.

"Ten or fifteen years ago, I served in a government headed by Benjamin Netanyahu," former lawmaker Avinadav Begin said. "It was a good government that benefited all Israeli citizens. We held good, constructive, productive discussions – they were closed to the public but completely open to disagreements among colleagues," he said.

"But then bad things happened to Netanyahu, to the point that five years ago, I said about him from the Knesset podium, 'How much further can a person sink? How much lower can he drag himself?' ... Since I said those words, Netanyahu's tragedy has unfolded before our astonished eyes," the retired politician added.

"Yesterday, he threatened us with the destruction of Israel's economy, and today, he threatened us with an atomic bomb being dropped on Tel Aviv, accompanied by a frightening AI-generated image. A prime minister like this is a threat to us all."

Retired IDF general Ofer Winter, who leads the new far-right Amcha Yisrael party, said Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's post showing an AI image of a nuclear strike on Tel Aviv is "utter madness."

Winter, who endorsed Netanyahu in August, said Israel's leaders "need to stop scaring the public." Speaking to Army Radio, Winter added that his party has "the ability to get Israel back on track," adding that the country must "take more initiative" in defense matters.

‘I feel so happy!’: Ecstasy, relief among Brazil’s Lula supporters

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sign up now: Get ST's newsletters delivered to your inbox

Thousands of supporters spilled onto the streets celebrating President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva’s victory.

SAO PAULO – Joyful cheers of “Let’s go, Lula!” rose from the jubilant crowd again and again as Brazilians paraded down Sao Paulo’s Paulista Avenue on Oct 25, celebrating the leftist leader’s improbable election win.

Thousands of people spilled onto the streets following President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva’s victory over his right-wing rival Flavio Bolsonaro.

Lula, who turns 81 on Oct 27, joined the parade, leaning out of the sunroof of his car with a megaphone in his hand and his trademark rasp more hoarse than ever.

“I just had to tell you that I love you. Tomorrow, the fight continues,” he said.

He received 51.8% of the votes compared to 48.2% for former president Jair Bolsonaro’s son, who has refused to acknowledge the result.

“I feel so happy!” 43-year-old Anna Zanatta told AFP before bursting into tears of joy.

“Lula’s victory represents many things for us: freedom, our democracy, our history,” she said, waving a flag bearing the message “Antifascist Action”.

Zanatta viewed a hypothetical Bolsonaro victory as a “threat” to Latin America’s largest democracy.

“I was born under a dictatorship, and I promised my granddaughter that we Brazilians would never go through that again,” she said, referring to the military regime which ruled from 1964 to 1985.

Doorman Alfonso dos Reis, 64, carried a sign reading “Lula again with the strength of the people” alongside a photo of the President.

“We’re going to build a much better country,” he beamed.

Fireworks boomed and cars honked their horns as the crowd marched down Sao Paulo’s main avenue, which turned into a sea of yellow and green as the Brazilian flag covered every square inch of the place.

Lula appeared alongside lawmaker Andre Janones, who created the “Lula’s Army” WhatsApp channel which surged to 10.8 million followers during the second-round campaign.

Lula’s supporters attribute his comeback in part to the lawmaker’s digital battle.

“I owe this victory to the rebellious youth who rose up to defend democracy,” Lula said.

Mateus Freitas, a 33-year-old theatre actor, waved a flare that sent red smoke into the air, the colour associated with Lula’s Workers’ Party.

“No amnesty! No amnesty!” Freitas yelled at the top of his lungs.

He was referring to attempts to free Jair Bolsonaro, who governed from 2019 to 2022 and is serving a 27-year sentence for attempting a coup following his own electoral loss to Lula in 2022.

His son Flavio had promised to liberate him if elected.

“We stopped a government that would have been a fascist government, led by militias, criminals, racists, homophobes,” Freitas told AFP.

“This is a victory for minorities, for the people, for Brazilian workers... Lula’s government is inclusive,” he said.

The leftist victory has swerved Brazil away from a rightward turn, unlike a recent string of wins championed by US President Donald Trump across Latin America.

Flavio Bolsonaro came out on top in the first round of presidential elections, and polls had given him a slight advantage ahead of the run-off.

Those indicators had concerned Felipe Rodrigues, a 53-year-old architect taking part in the celebrations on Oct 25.

“I’m very glad to see that, despite the risks of having a far-right government, which seems to be a global trend, here in Brazil we have a president who continues his efforts to improve the lives of the poor,” he said. AFP

Россиянка зашла в супермаркет Италии и удивилась нескольким необычным продуктам

Фото: Mironov Vladimir / Shutterstock / Fotodom

Российская тревел-блогерша Елена побывала в Италии, зашла в местный супермаркет и удивилась нескольким необычным продуктам, многих из которых нет в продаже у нее на родине. Своими наблюдениями она поделилась в личном блоге «Лайк Трэвел Путешествия» на платформе «Дзен».

Первое место в списке автора публикации заняли маринованные артишоки. По словам россиянки, они сочные, мягкие и идеально дополняют как мясную нарезку, так и салат. «Итальянцы добавляют их везде, но больше всего мне нравится их вкус в пицце», — призналась она.

Второй продукт, о котором рассказала соотечественница, — фисташковая паста. В отличие от арахисовой, она более мягкая и сладкая, с ярко выраженным ореховым вкусом. Такую пасту намазывают на тосты или просто едят ложкой прямо из банки, добавила блогерша.

На третье место Елена поставила набор прошутто и сыра. «Идеальный вариант для ужина, если ждешь гостей. Итальянцы любят вкусный качественный сыр и вяленое мясо, если вы хоть раз ели прошутто, то знаете, что он не сравнится ни с какой колбасой», — подчеркнула она.

Кроме того, в итальянских супермаркетах продается порционный пармезан, каперсы в сухой соли, измельченные фисташки, конфеты и десерты со вкусом лимончелло, паштет из оливок в мягком тюбике, сухарики со вкусом брецеля, филе скумбрии и анчоусы в масле.

Ранее другая российская тревел-блогерша, переехавшая в Италию, сравнила стоимость жизни в Европе и на родине в 2026 году. Она заметила, что в России стоимость продуктов ниже, однако в Италии они более качественные.

¿Te has fijado en esos panes italianos que parecen tener agujeros gigantes en su interior y una corteza que suena al tocarla? Lograr ese resultado en casa puede parecer un reto digno de un maestro panadero, pero el verdadero truco no está en la fuerza de tus brazos, sino en la paciencia y en el uso de la biga. Esta técnica milenaria es la que marca la diferencia entre un pan blanco común y una auténtica obra de arte de la panadería de Lombardía.

La ciabatta, cuyo nombre significa literalmente «zapatilla» debido a su forma achatada, nació en los años 80 como una respuesta creativa para competir con otros panes europeos. Lo que la hace especial es su altísima hidratación y su estructura irregular, que la convierte en la compañera ideal para un chorrito de aceite de oliva virgen extra o para montar esos sándwiches gourmet que te dejan sin palabras.

Para entender la ciabatta, primero hay que comprender la biga. Se trata de un prefermento de consistencia dura o sólida, compuesto básicamente por harina, agua y una pizca de levadura. A diferencia del poolish, que es más líquido, la biga tiene una proporción de agua mucho menor (generalmente entre el 44% y el 50% respecto a la harina), lo que obliga a una fermentación más lenta y controlada.

Este proceso no es solo una cuestión de tiempo; la biga actúa como un andamiaje invisible para el pan. Durante sus horas de reposo, se predigieren los almidones y se generan compuestos aromáticos complejos que aportan notas lácticas y tostadas. Además, es fundamental porque permite desarrollar el gluten sin necesidad de un amasado agresivo, algo vital ya que la masa final es tan hidratada que sería imposible de manejar sin esta base de fuerza.

En la panadería, las cantidades no son sugerencias, son reglas. Para obtener esos alveolos gigantes, necesitas una harina de fuerza con alta proteína (W 300-350), ya que una harina débil no soportaría el peso del agua. La hidratación total suele oscilar entre el 80% y el 85%, aunque si eres principiante, empezar por un 80% es una decisión inteligente para no volverte loco con la masa.

Mezcla la harina, el agua y la levadura solo hasta que no veas restos secos. No busques un amasado perfecto; la biga debe quedar grumosa y compacta. Es crucial controlar la temperatura: lo ideal es que esté entre 18 y 20°C. Si se calienta demasiado, fermentará rápido y perderá esa potencia aromática que buscamos.

Deja reposar la mezcla una hora a temperatura ambiente y luego llévala al frigorífico entre 16 y 24 horas. Sabrás que está lista cuando haya triplicado su volumen y desprenda un olor dulce y ligeramente alcohólico. Si huele a vinagre, te has pasado de tiempo.

Para integrar la biga, trocéala en pedazos pequeños y mézclala con el resto de los ingredientes. Un truco de profesional es el amasado en doble adición: no pongas toda el agua al principio. Reserva un 10% y añádelo poco a poco una vez que la masa haya empezado a cobrar cuerpo. Esto evita que la estructura se rompa y te permite conseguir una masa brillante y elástica.

Debido a la gran cantidad de agua, el amasado mecánico no basta. Durante la fermentación en bloque (unas 2-3 horas), debes realizar series de pliegues cada 40 minutos. Humedece tus manos para que la masa no se pegue y estira los bordes hacia arriba doblándolos hacia el centro. Este proceso crea tensión y fortalece la red de gluten sin destruir las burbujas de gas que forman los agujeros.

Vuelca la masa sobre una superficie con abundante harina o semolín. Con una rasqueta, corta rectángulos con movimientos firmes pero delicados, evitando desgasificar la masa. Deja que reposen unos 40 minutos más tapados con un paño. El horno debe estar a tope, unos 240-250°C con vapor. El vapor es el gran secreto, ya que retrasa la formación de la corteza y permite que el pan se expanda al máximo (el famoso oven spring).

Hornea unos 15 minutos con vapor, retíralo y baja la temperatura a 220°C durante otros 20 minutos. Al sacar las ciabattas, es obligatorio dejarlas enfriar en una rejilla al menos 45 minutos; si las cortas calientes, la miga puede quedar gomosa porque el proceso de cocción termina con el calor residual.

Dominar la ciabatta requiere entender que el tiempo es un ingrediente más, donde la fermentación lenta de la biga, el manejo suave de la hidratación y un golpe de calor con vapor se combinan para transformar harina y agua en un pan artesanal con una corteza dorada y un interior lleno de aire.
