Most people in the US purchase iPhones from carriers, which means the devices are locked to that network. The easier approach is to buy directly from Apple, though there are some things you should know:

Connect to any carrier later: Choose this option for the most hassle-free experience. Choose a carrier: If you choose a carrier through Apple.com, you will get an unlocked iPhone, no matter which carrier you choose (except if you purchase with AT&T on an installment plan). Apple Upgrade: Buying directly from Apple also nets you access to the Apple Upgrade leasing option. Is it worth it? Broadly speaking, no. Most people do not need to upgrade to a new iPhone every year or two. It's expensive, and you must return your leased iPhone in good condition to avoid additional charges. AppleCare is not included, as it was with the discontinued iPhone Upgrade Program.

The iPhone's Charging Port Is USB-C, Not Lightning

The iPhone 15 range ditched the long-standing Lightning port for USB-C, and it's in every iPhone since. This is the same charging port on iPads, MacBooks, Android phones, Windows laptops, and many more personal tech gadgets. USB-C is often called the “universal port” because it can technically handle various responsibilities. For example, you can plug in external displays and extend the iPhone's screen or connect the same USB-C accessories you might be currently using on your laptop, like SD card readers. Sadly, it does mean your Lightning accessories won't work unless you get dongles.

Make-Ahead Mashed Potatoes

📅 Oct 16, 2018  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

Thanksgiving dinner can feel like a game of Tetris, but this make-ahead mashed potato recipe gives you one less thing to juggle. It lets you prepare the potatoes up to two days in advance, then reheat them just before serving without sacrificing their rich flavor or velvety texture.

The secret is in the technique: Simmer whole, skin-on Yukon Gold potatoes to keep them from absorbing excess water, then pass them through a potato ricer with plenty of butter. Adding the butter before the warm milk and cream coats the potato starches in fat, helping the mash stay smooth and creamy even after refrigeration. A little sour cream adds a welcome tang.

For extra flavor, try adding roasted garlic, finely grated Parmesan, or infusing the milk and cream, as we do in BA’s Best Mashed Potatoes, with bay leaves, thyme, or rosemary.

How far in advance can you make mashed potatoes?

Up to two days. Let the finished mashed potatoes cool slightly, then cover and refrigerate promptly. Need a longer head start? Learn how to freeze mashed potatoes.

How do you reheat mashed potatoes without making them gummy?

Gently warm them in a saucepan over medium-low heat with the additional milk and cream to restore the proper consistency. Stir often but gently to prevent scorching without overworking the potatoes, which can turn them gluey.

The potatoes may have absorbed too much water during cooking. For this recipe, simmer them whole, with the skins on, until very tender but not falling apart. A food mill fitted with a fine disk is your best alternative. A potato masher will produce a less uniform texture and requires peeling the potatoes first. Avoid a food processor or blender, which can overwork the starches and make the potatoes gluey.

We need a way to interpolate values. We know where the control points should be at 0% and 100%, but what about when the user is 25% scrolled through the content?

Best Microsoft Surface Laptop (2026): Which Models to Buy and Avoid

📅 Mar 14, 2018  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Microsoft’s Surface Line has a product to fit a variety of needs and budgets, including flexible 2-in-1 tablet-laptops. The company also recently announced new versions of its Surface Laptop and Surface Pro as follow-ups to its 2024 models, making it even trickier to pick the best model that matches your priorities.

When those 2024 Surface models launched, they were among my favorite devices of the year. Over the past couple of years, those models have held up, making them great buys—especially when on sale. But now, with the new Surface Laptop 8th Edition and Surface Pro 12th Edition (not to be confused with the Surface Pro 12), we finally have some next-gen silicon to pair with them. Just bear in mind that the new Snapdragon X2 chips come at much higher prices, bringing the devices’ value into question.

I weighed these details, among others, in outlining which Microsoft Surface device is best for every need.

Be sure to read our other buying guides for more, including the Best Laptops, Best Cheap Laptops, Best 2-in-1 Laptops, Best MacBooks, and our tips for choosing a laptop.

Updated October 2026: I've rewritten this guide with notes from my testing of the Microsoft Surface Laptop Ultra and updated pricing for other models.

Recommending a laptop from 2024 as my top choice feels embarrassing. Not for me—but for Microsoft. I adore this laptop, and I actually still find myself returning to it over newer, flashier options. The 3:2 aspect ratio screen is my favorite feature, and it can't be found almost anywhere else right now. The taller shape of the display is preferred if you spend most of your time on a laptop looking at documents, spreadsheets, or websites. Beyond that, it gets great battery life, performs well (in just about anything other than games), and has a gorgeous, high-resolution display.

But I hear you say, why not the 2026 model (also known as the 8th Edition)? Microsoft launched a refresh this year with new chips inside (the Snapdragon X2 lineup, if you care to know). I've tested these chips in other laptops, such as the Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5x, and they speed things up in meaningful ways—especially on the graphics front. Unfortunately, Microsoft made the poor decision not to send these out to reviewers for independent testing, so I've never had one to try myself.

The real problem, though, is the pricing. Like many laptop manufacturers this year, Microsoft has dramatically increased the price of its products. You can thank the ongoing memory shortage for that. Right now, you can still buy the 2024 Surface Laptop with 512 GB of storage for $999 on Amazon. It's only for the Dune color right now, whereas the Black and Silver models are $50 more. These are not quite as cheap as they were in 2025, but it's a great deal. It's especially affordable compared with the 2026 version. It's over $400 more for an otherwise identical configuration, and sometimes it jumps to the retail price of $1,600. That's just too much for what you're getting. I just don't think the performance difference is worth that much. It'd be a different story if the 2024 model were unavailable, but I have a hard time suggesting someone buy the newer one at the moment.

The Surface Laptop comes in two main variants: 13.8-inch and 15-inch screen sizes. Both are great, but I think the 13.8-inch model offers plenty of screen real estate for the average person, especially with this aspect ratio.

The Surface Laptop Ultra might bear some resemblance to the standard Surface Laptop, but it's a completely different animal under the hood. It's no thicker than the 15-inch Surface Laptop, despite offering far more performance. That's thanks to the Nvidia RTX Spark N1X chip inside, which delivers GPU performance typically found only in discrete graphics cards, like those in gaming laptops. In many ways, this is Microsoft's answer to the MacBook Pro—a laptop designed for content creators, media pros, and a burgeoning new demographic: AI enthusiasts. It can be configured with up to 128 GB of unified RAM, allowing you to feed that GPU tons of memory to access larger AI models—all running entirely on-device. I tested the 32-GB version of the Surface Laptop Ultra, which limits you from running larger AI models (such as 70-billion parameters and above). In reality, you need something with a more powerful GPU to handle foundation-tier models that really compete with the cloud-based services people are used to. I don't know if the case for running local AI models is fully made yet, but the Surface Laptop Ultra lets you dip your toe in.

It can play games, too, but don't be fooled by the “Nvidia” brand into thinking it will perform anywhere near a proper discrete graphics card in a gaming laptop. Some very popular multiplayer games still aren't compatible at all (such as PUBG and League of Legends), though Nvidia says it's working on it. The Surface Laptop Ultra is for someone who wants to dabble in content creation, gaming, and AI on their work laptop. And yes, if that sounds like the M5 Pro MacBook Pro, that's the point.

I’ve always liked the idea of a smaller Surface Pro, and in theory, the Surface Pro 12 (6/10, WIRED Recommends) checks the right boxes. It keeps the premium look and feel of the Surface Pro line, retaining the Snapdragon X Plus chip (with two fewer cores) while making slight changes to lower the price and make the tablet even thinner. I can live with the incremental downgrades in the webcam and display. My problem at launch, when I first reviewed the Surface Pro 12, was the pricing. Microsoft tried to slot this cheaper, smaller Surface below the Surface Pro 13.8, then immediately stopped selling the lower-tier configurations of that larger device to make room. But these days, the Surface Pro 13.8 has gotten so expensive that I no longer think it's worth buying. If you really want a 2-in-1 Windows tablet, the Surface Pro 12 is your best option.

Microsoft has even started selling a version of the Surface Pro 12 with only 8 GB of memory. That could work if your needs are fairly light. You wouldn't want to use that configuration as your primary work device, though. The Surface Pro 12 also doesn't include a bundled power supply, so you’ll need to pay an additional $70 to add one. Like every Surface tablet, you also have to pay extra for the corresponding keyboard or Surface Pen.

I’ve already mentioned that both the new Surface Laptop and Surface Pro are priced high for a standard, generational chip refresh. That already makes me doubt whether these devices are worth buying, but I only make official recommendations on products I’ve tested myself.

Another wrinkle: Microsoft isn’t running a typical media review program for these new devices. Unlike nearly every high-end laptop a major company releases, Microsoft isn’t sending review units to independent media for testing, according to a Microsoft spokesperson. That doesn’t necessarily make these devices inherently bad, but it’s a very uncommon choice and, frankly, not a good sign.

Microsoft Surface Laptop 13 (2026): Be careful with this one. It's named dangerously similar to the 13.8-inch model, but it's actually a midrange-type laptop. It has a more basic display, and frustratingly, it's not even in a 3:2 aspect ratio. And $950 for a laptop with only 8 GB of RAM is absurd. If you really want something cheap with only 8 GB of RAM, the Dell XPS 13 only costs $700 and is a far better laptop.

Microsoft Surface Pro 13 (2026): $1,500 for a tablet is really hard to justify. This is for the model with an IPS display, putting the upgraded OLED model at over $2,000. And don't forget: It doesn't come with a keyboard either. When the iPad Pro is cheaper, you know this is overpriced. This does tend to drop down in price on Prime Day or Black Friday, though you'll really need to do some price checks to make sure this is worth the money—even on sale.

Microsoft Surface Laptop 13 (2025): I haven’t tested this model yet, but it’s hard to recommend given the price alone. The 256-GB base configuration is sold out on Amazon, while Microsoft sells it for a whopping $950. That's way too expensive to even consider.

Microsoft Surface Go 3: These days, there isn’t a good reason to pick up any Surface device older than the 2024 models. Shockingly, though, you can still buy the Surface Go 3 new. It costs $700, and it comes with only 8 GB of RAM and 128 GB of storage. Even in 2026, that’s insanely overpriced for the specs.

Microsoft Surface Laptop for Business: I haven’t tested this model yet, either, but it’s currently the only way to get an Intel-powered Surface Laptop. It’s also the only way to get a Surface Laptop with 5G connectivity. This is primarily for commercial use, though anyone can currently buy one directly from Microsoft.

It’s been two years since the Snapdragon X platform launched alongside the Surface Laptop 7th Edition and Surface Pro 11th Edition. While I still wholeheartedly recommend these devices, I don’t know how much longer they will be available. Even so, now’s a great time to buy these, especially while they’re still on discount.

Qualcomm announced its Snapdragon X2 chips last fall, and you can buy the Surface Laptop 8th Edition and Surface Pro 12th Edition with those new chips inside. But they’re very expensive and weren't sent out for review. In most cases, they're still too expensive for me to recommend buying over the 2024 model.

The Surface Laptop Ultra just started selling, and it's among the first devices to use Nvidia's new RTX Spark platform.

There are a few things about Surface devices that may annoy you. With the Surface Pro, the keyboard is not included—and you’ll definitely want to buy one. While the tablet works fine detached for occasional use, Windows just isn’t as cut out for touch-only functionality as Apple’s iPadOS is on iPads. So, when you buy a Surface Pro, don’t expect Windows to help you navigate much without a mouse or touchpad.

Ports are a bit of a mixed bag for Surface devices. You’ll find USB-C ports throughout the Surface line, and the high-end models support Thunderbolt 4. Some models, like the Surface Pro 11th Edition and Surface Laptop 7th Edition, also use the magnetic Surface Connect charger, despite having USB-C ports available.
