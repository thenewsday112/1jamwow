Mum's long fight for breast cancer diagnosis as ACT misses screening target

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Alisha Henderson was surprised how long it took for her breast cancer to be diagnosed.

After finding a lump in one of her breasts, the Canberra woman sought medical advice but initial tests didn't identify anything malicious.

"It took seven months to get the diagnosis. The screening tools couldn't see through the density of my boobs," Ms Henderson said.

An initial ultrasound and mammogram did not detect possible signs of cancer and were followed by another ultrasound six months later.

"Again, nothing bad there, but I said to the breast surgeon at the time, 'it's like something's changed, it feels different'.

"And so, an MRI, at the seven month point, found it."

After being diagnosed with Stage 2B breast cancer, Ms Henderson underwent chemotherapy combined with targeted therapy, followed by a double mastectomy.

A year and half on from her diagnosis, she is cancer-free and supporting other women by volunteering as a National Breast Cancer Foundation community ambassador.

Ms Henderson's advice to other women is to know their own bodies and to seek medical advice and advocate for themselves if they feel something isn't right.

"Know what your normal is. But I don't think it's just breast cancer, right? Moles, check your poo, check your everything."

Ms Henderson was only 36 when she was diagnosed. Most breast cancers occur over the age of 50, which is why Australian women aged 50 to 74 are encouraged to have mammogram screening every two years.

In recent years, however, screening rates for Canberra women aged 50 to 74 have been consistently below the target of 60 per cent.

In 2025-26, only 56 per cent of Canberra women in the target age group underwent free screening, although this was higher than the national participation rate of 51 per cent in 2024-25.

The National Breast Cancer Foundation has warned that Australians cannot afford to be complacent about the disease. Despite breast cancer death rates falling more than 40 per cent over the past three decades, it still kills nine people in Australia every day.

For Ms Henderson, participating in screening seems like a "no-brainer". She has met women whose breast cancer has been caught at the earliest possible stage, allowing them to avoid the most serious treatments.

Another strong supporter of screening is Jodie Kirkness, who underwent a mastectomy when she was 17 after being diagnosed with breast cancer.

Ms Kirkness is now the health promotion and standards manager at BreastScreen ACT, where she leads a small team dedicated to encouraging women to undergo routine screening and to self-examine for possible early signs of breast cancer.

She tells time-poor women that they can usually be in and out of a routine screening appointment within 20 minutes.

"We screen asymptomatic women," she said.

"We're not expecting someone to come in and have a very serious malignant cancer. We are expecting we may pick up a very early stage cancer."

One of the team's priorities is working with women from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds who may be reluctant to visit a health centre unless they are feeling sick. Conversations with groups of women from these backgrounds have resulted in more people undergoing screening.

BreastScreen ACT recommends that women aged 50 to 74 participate in free screening every two years. Free screening is also available for participants from 40 to 49 and over 74.

Peter Jean is health reporter for The Canberra Times.

Agency spends $4 million on redundancies while increasing its spend on contractors

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Services Australia nearly doubled the amount it paid out for staff exits in the past financial year as the mega-agency juggled workforce realignments and a costly overhaul of its legacy telecommunications setup.

The agency spent $4.4 million in voluntary redundancy payouts to 28 staff, a jump from just five in 2024-25. On average, $157,000 was paid out per departing employee.

"In 2025-26 voluntary redundancies were used selectively as part of normal workforce management where workforce realignment was required and suitable redeployment was not available," a Services Australia spokesperson said.

The spokesperson said the low redundancy figures in 2024-25 were a result of heavy recruitment in frontline service delivery and the departures in 2025-26 aligned more closely to historical averages of 20 in 2022-23 and 33 in 2023-24.

The agency also forked out about $302 million in communications costs, an increase of $53 million compared with 2024-25.

The spokesperson attributed the increase in spending to rising supplier costs because supply chain inflation across postage and freight in addition to a move away from its legacy telecommunications platforms to a modern digital network.

External legal and contractor spending also increased, driven by what the spokesperson said was a "higher demand for specialist legal advice and support on complex matters".

An overhaul expected of ageing legacy systems is also expected soon so lawyers and contractors can expect to be extremely busy.

In the aftermath of a scathing audit of its cyber security processes, the Department of Parliamentary Services (DPS) has opened its cheque book.

The department awarded contracts worth $5.1 million and $2.38 million to software companies A23 and Informatech for "augmentation resources" for external information technology (IT) contractors.

External specialists were brought in after the Auditor-General found DPS's networks were outdated and vulnerable to attacks, with more than half the staff in the department's cyber security branch having been in their roles for less than a year.

"DPS takes the security, stability and functionality of the Parliamentary Computing Network seriously," a spokesperson said.

"We take a robust and strategic approach to the way we deploy resources to ensure we provide an IT system that meets the needs of the Australian Parliament and the Parliamentary Departments."

On the government's push to reduce contractor reliance, DPS said the splurge was necessary as the technical expertise was "not held within the department and/or may only be required for a short period of time."

For parliamentarians, staffers and press gallery folk hoping for answers about whether the multimillion-dollar contracts would address ongoing Wi-Fi and ParlView issues, DPS did not address network performance citing security protocols.

Parking fines in the Parliamentary Triangle hit $1.6 million last financial year, the National Capital Authority's annual report reveals.

Tickets and fines combined generated about $21 million in non-taxation revenue for the NCA, the statutory authority that collects revenue for the federal government (which theoretically gives it back to Canberrans through the Commonwealth Grants Commission).

But the NCA is still chasing a big chunk of fines owed - $782,000, to be exact.

For those drivers hoping to ignore their unpaid parking fines, the authority warned it would refer them to the ACT Magistrates Court for prosecution.

Nerds in Defence have been sent to drama school in a bid to improve their presentation style, a tender notice has revealed.

The department awarded a $25,300 contract to the National Institute of Dramatic Art (NIDA) which, it turns out, was for 20 bureaucrats in its finance team to brush up on their public speaking.

The lucky group took part in NIDA's Presenting with Confidence course, as part of a joint pilot program with the Australian Taxation Office for developing future finance leaders.

Bean counters were sent to the course to improve their delivery of technical financial data and strategic information to senior executives.

"NIDA was selected for its specialised expertise in executive communication and leadership development," the spokesperson said, adding the course was part of a broader professional development pathway across the APS.

If you're looking forward to your annual wall planner from the Bureau of Meteorology (BOM), it's been sent to the printers.

Public Eye inquired about a $106,126 tender BOM awarded to recruitment agency Ignite Limited.

A spokesperson said the contract was for a small customer service team to handle order processing, payment management and customer inquiries for the 43rd edition of the bureau's official calendar, which will feature amateur and professional photographs selected through a competition.

About 45,000 calendars are printed for sale each year, with an extra 4500 gifted to volunteer weather observers.

If one needed evidence that online scammers were becoming bolder, they should look no further than the website of Australia's corporate regulator.

The Australian Securities and Investments Commission's (ASIC) 2025-26 annual report shows it had to coordinate a takedown of a fake version of its own register portal, ASIC Connect.

The copycat site was part of a complex network linked to a fake trading platform called "Universal Group", which had links impersonating three overseas financial regulators to lend the air of authenticity.

It was just one instance of the exploding game of scam whack-a-mole. For the 2025-26 period, ASIC reported removing 19,400 investment scam and phishing sites, a massive 182 per cent surge from 2024-25.

Scammers were leveraging artificial intelligence and slick web designs to impersonate trusted brands. About 20 per cent of all Moneysmart investor alerts involved fake versions of ASIC-licensed businesses or registered companies.

To help consumers identify fake copycat sites, the regulator has begun publishing official website addresses directly on its Professional Registers Search.

Annual scam losses in Australia had hit $2.2 billion, the report said.

Former Berry staffer seeks declaration conduct was not corrupt

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The former ministerial chief of staff at the centre of a major Integrity Commission investigation has taken the watchdog to court, seeking a declaration his conduct was not corrupt.

Joshua Ceramidas has launched Federal Court action against the commission, arguing the findings of serious corrupt conduct in the handling of a primary school upgrade contract were not supported by evidence.

An amended originating application filed on behalf of Mr Ceramidas alleged the findings were affected by jurisdictional error because they were not supported by the evidence and were also irrational, illogical or unreasonable.

The commission's findings that Mr Ceramidas acted at the behest of or in the interests of the Construction, Forestry and Maritime Employees Union's (CFMEU) ACT branch, that he had a "close relationship with the union" and strongly backed the union's position and intended to help it succeed were among those Mr Ceramidas' claim said were not supported by evidence.

The claim also argued the Integrity Commission's finding Mr Ceramidas' actions constituted a criminal offence was wrong because the commission misconstrued or misunderstood elements of the offence and asked itself the wrong questions.

Mr Ceramidas was deputy chief minister Yvette Berry's chief of staff at the time the commission found he and the head of the Education Directorate, Katy Haire, "dishonestly provided a back door by which the strategic interests of the CFMEU were given priority" in the way a contract was awarded for a primary school upgrade.

Mr Ceramidas "acted in his own interest for the purpose of currying favour with the CFMEU", did not disclose his actions to Ms Berry and lied to the territory's corruption watchdog, the Operation Kingfisher report said.

Mr Ceramidas wants the court to declare the corruption findings were affected by jurisdictional errors and his conduct did not amount to corrupt conduct or serious corrupt conduct.

The case was listed for mention on October 16.

The acting head of the ACT public service, Russ Campbell, on Wednesday confirmed Mr Ceramidas was no longer a territory employee.

"It is not appropriate to comment further because of pending legal proceedings relating to his employment," Mr Campbell said in a statement.

"The territory is not funding any legal costs for Mr Ceramidas' challenge to the Integrity Commission's report."

Attorney-General Tara Cheyne last week told the Legislative Assembly she had received a request to intervene in the Federal Court matter but had declined.

The commission released its long-awaited Operation Kingfisher report in July, having also found the former head of the Education Directorate, Katy Haire, had engaged in serious corrupt conduct.

"Not only was the process itself improperly skewed, resulting in manifest unfairness and also, as it happened, substantially increased cost, but both Ms Haire and Mr Ceramidas undertook a calculated attempt to cover up the truth of what occurred," the report said.

"Accordingly, the corrupt conduct of each amounted to serious corrupt conduct."

Ms Haire resigned from her post as the director-general of the Education Directorate in 2024.

While the corruption watchdog cannot rule whether corruption amounts to a criminal offence, Integrity Commissioner Michael Adams KC's report noted Mr Ceramidas' actions could constitute the offences of abusing public office and conspiracy to defraud.

The findings prompted Yvette Berry, whom Mr Ceramidas worked for from 2016 before joining the ACT public service, to resign from cabinet. No findings were made against Ms Berry.

'Classic Canberra idea': architects to compete in 'missing middle' design contest

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Canberra's suburbs have long had a copy-and-paste quality about them, with government houses built over and over to pre-approved designs.

Now the ideas of a new generation of architects are being sought for the capital in a national design competition for a pattern book of "missing middle" house designs.

Planning Minister Chris Steel said the government was building on a "classic Canberra idea".

Much of Canberra had been built on pattern housing, when pre-designed homes were used to efficiently grow the capital in the post-war years, he said.

"First, we made it possible to build missing middle homes, now we're fast-tracking new homes through a pattern book approach," Mr Steel said.

"This pattern book will give Canberrans a range of top quality, sustainable, and affordable home designs that can be built without the need for a DA."

But unlike the Department of Works' 400 series designs for houses that gave mid-century Canberra swathes of small three-bedroom homes on big suburban blocks, the government wants innovation on a tighter scale.

The "missing middle" housing the government is working to encourage, sitting between existing detached housing and high-density apartment buildings, includes duplexes, dual-occupancies, townhouses and terraces.

Last week, planning law changes passed the Legislative Assembly to make smaller-scale subdivisions faster to approve, permit secondary residences on single-dwelling leases without a Crown lease variation, and streamline lease variations for minor changes.

The planning minister said the changes would deliver faster approvals for "missing middle" housing projects.

Mr Steel said the pattern book would demonstrate how well-designed "missing middle" housing could enhance Canberra's suburbs.

"It will provide practical examples of housing that responds to our unique climate, landscape and local character, while supporting a more compact and efficient city," he said.

"The competition represents an opportunity to harness the creativity of the design profession in pursuit of a shared goal: delivering more homes without compromising on quality."

ACT government architect Catherine Townsend said the competition would encourage creative and practical approaches to housing design and help shape more diverse, liveable and sustainable neighbourhoods.

"I am very pleased to launch the Expression of Interest for the Canberra House Pattern Book Design Competition, an important initiative to expand housing choice and support thoughtful, sensitive neighbourhood and precinct development," Ms Townsend, a Life Fellow of the Royal Institute of Architects, said.

"The competition seeks innovative, cost-efficient design solutions that respond to the evolving needs of families while reflecting the unique character and environmental context of Canberra, the Bush Capital."

The competition will be a two-stage process. First, expressions of interest will be invited from Australian architects, building designers and architecture students.

Next, about 16 selected professional entrants and about six student entrants will be tasked with submitting house designs, which will be publicly exhibited.

The winners will continue to a post-competition stage to focus on detailed design work and refinement to incorporate the plans in the Canberra House Pattern Book.

The book will serve as a library of pre-qualified housing designs that can be quickly approved for construction, the government said.

Expressions of interest for the design competition shut on December 4.

'Clipped and called a liar': Gallagher lays bare cost of 'roastings' as top bureaucrats flee APS

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Finance and Public Service Minister Katy Gallagher says the use of Senate estimates clips on social media has gone too far, warning that hard-working bureaucrats are being driven out of their careers by political attacks.

In an interview with The Canberra Times about the heightened safety risks experienced by politicians as political debate became more polarised, Senator Gallagher said she knew public servants who had quit because they had been ridiculed online.

"I've worked with a couple of very senior public servants who have retired or left because they will not come to Senate estimates," she said.

"They won't come because they've been called a liar, and that matters to them. Their professional career is being apolitical public servants, and so to be just roasted and then clipped and called a liar ... That is an issue that we should think about a bit more."

The minister and ACT Labor senator did not go as far as backing the Secretaries Board's call for a ban on the editing of videos clipped from estimates to post on social media for party political purposes, which Coalition frontbencher James Paterson has blasted as "an affront to democracy".

But, she said, while the public was entitled to "accountability and transparency" from the bureaucracy, the portrayal of estimates hearings "should be truthful".

"I don't think they should be clipped in a way that makes it tell a different story," she said.

Senator Gallagher shared her concerns while reflecting that the heat of political discourse online was spilling into real life, after the vehicle attack on independent Indi MP Helen Haines outside her electorate office shocked the nation.

The minister, whose evidence to estimates is regularly clipped by political rivals, said she had been accosted in public over how she was portrayed - such as when Nationals senator Matt Canavan posted his grilling her over a $30 billion federal budget overspend in February.

"I got absolutely inundated," Senator Gallagher said.

"This guy just comes up to me, I'm just at the supermarket, and starts abusing me and going, 'Where's our $30 billion? Where have you embezzled all this money?' In a really menacing way."

She acknowledged that being in government meant that "you're on the receiving [of criticism] and you're not giving it like the opposition".

But, she argued, lines of questioning at estimates were increasingly tailored towards "generating footage [to] make sure that you can whip up enough outrage with something and then move on."

"I believe in accountability and being accountable to the Parliament and trying to give as much information as we can, but we also have to protect ourselves and our institutions and the public servants that appear with us as well."

If public servants were expected to be "frank and honest," Senator Gallagher said, it was not helpful to "clip videos that make them look like fools, so that they get piled on."

"There's human consequences to this," she said.

After ACT independent senator David Pocock's social media post about Australia collecting more tax from beer than offshore gas exports went viral in February, she said, "the pile on that those public servants got was unbelievable."

"They have no right of reply," she said, citing a hearing where a public servant was "called a liar" and accused of having doctored a submission, which they later proved was untrue - but the damage was done.

"There's no consequence for senators and their behaviour. They can just throw this stuff out there and then move on as they do to the next video, and then meanwhile the public servant's left shattered, with their career ended."

Senator Pocock agreed that politicians "need to take care in what and how we ask our questions, show respect to public servants and disagree civilly."

"Senate Estimates hearings are publicly broadcast for good reason and are an essential part of a strong, open democracy," he said.

"Care does need to be taken in how clips from senate estimates are cut and shared on social media. It's definitely something my team and I are mindful of when using clips from estimates."

Senator Pocock's office does not believe his beer tax post, or any others, made a harmful contribution to the political debate.

Senator Canavan, who became Nationals leader weeks after his post about the $30 billion overspend, was scathing of Senator Gallagher's comments.

"I am sorry? Katy was described as a 'mean girl' by one of her own senators," he said, referring to the reported comments of the late Kimberley Kitching.

"Is she seriously throwing mud about other Senators doing their job and holding the government to account? We need more accountability, not less, otherwise people will become more frustrated than they are."

Voters had "legitimate frustrations" with the government and the bureaucracy that needed to be "responded to through the normal Parliamentary process," Senator Canavan said.

"Seeking to subvert or reduce Parliamentary accountability will not help our social harmony."

He stood by his social media clip about Senator Gallagher, which related to a $50 billion overspend on government programs.

"She could not account for $30 billion of that figure. She is the Finance Minister. Such a failure deserves to be exposed and Senate Estimates is precisely about doing that."

Acting opposition finance and public service spokesperson and Liberal Party deputy leader Jane Hume said politicians' and public servants' salaries "are paid for by taxpayers".

"It is entirely reasonable for taxpayers to see how their money is being spent and to hold those who spend it to account," Senator Hume said.

"Australians have every right to see their government being held to account. If any Minister doesn't like the questions being shared, perhaps they should start by answering them."

Greens deputy leader and democracy spokeswoman Steph Hodgins-May said Senate estimates existed "so the public can understand how political decisions are affecting the departments that serve all of us."

"It's about the transparent expenditure of public money, and we deserve to know how well that's working," Senator Hodgins-May said.

"Every senator at the table is asking questions on behalf of the Australian people. It's not about blaming public servants - it's about getting clear, honest answers, even when they can be hard to get, and holding ministers accountable for the decisions they make."

This department paid KPMG $115k while former partner sat on risk committee

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Education staff handed KPMG $115,000 after the firm's integrity scandal erupted, raising questions about the department's audit and risk committee.

One of the committee members is a former KPMG partner, who led its federal government account for 15 years.

Education staff signed two contract variations with KPMG in May: $26,000 on May 5 for "project assurance services" and $89,000 on May 18 for a CCTV in childcare evaluation.

These contracts and others meant Education spent more with KPMG ($2.23 million) than any other consultancy in 2025-26, according to its annual report published on Wednesday, October 21.

Federal agencies have faced scrutiny for their dealings with the firm since March, when allegations emerged that staff in its audit division misused client information to win new work.

KPMG leaders denied the allegations throughout April, saying they had not been substantiated through investigations.

Then on May 14 - days before Education signed the second contract variation - KPMG staff admitted that a senior partner improperly accessed and displayed a client's board documents.

The government banned agencies from starting new contracts with KPMG in June because of uncertainty about its controls on staff ethics and integrity, and that ban was extended in October.

Questions have now been raised about the department's response to the KPMG scandal, and the role of its audit and risk committee.

The committee met five times in 2025-26 to discuss financial and performance reporting, internal controls and systems of risk in areas such as fraud and corruption, cyber security and privacy.

Former KPMG partner Cath Ingram joined the committee in 2022, after leading the firm's federal government account for 15 years.

Ms Ingram has advised Attorney-General's, Canberra Airport and the Australian National University Foundation in committee and board roles since moving on from KPMG.

She attended all Education audit and risk committee meetings in 2025-26, but it is unclear whether there were any discussions regarding the department's external contracts at these meetings.

The Canberra Times does not suggest that Ms Ingram had any influence on decisions by Department of Education staff in relation to contracts with KPMG, or any other contracts.

A Department of Education spokesperson said it assessed value for money for all procurement activities in accordance with the Commonwealth procurement rules.

"The audit and risk committee is not involved in procurement decisions," the spokesperson said.

"The KPMG consultancy expenditure reported in 2025-26 relates to contracts awarded between October 2023 and December 2025.

"These contracts were established through open tender processes in which multiple suppliers were invited to respond.

"The audit and risk committee provides advice on the appropriateness of financial and performance reporting, systems of risk and systems of internal control.

"All audit and risk committee members are required to declare conflicts of interest annually and as they arise.

"Ms Ingram declared her previous roles at KPMG when she commenced as a member on the department's audit and risk committee."

When pressed on the audit and risk committee's role, the spokesperson said the committee had been updated on the department's actions in relation to KPMG contracts.

"The department follows whole of government guidance issued by the Department of Finance in relation to procurement," they said.

"Upon receipt of the procurement policy note the department sought assurances from KPMG in relation to active contracts.

"The audit and risk committee is not involved in the selection, procurement, or contract management of vendors; this work is undertaken by department delegates."

Liberal senator Richard Colbeck, who has advocated for more careful scrutiny of consulting spend, said the bar for transparency had become much higher since both the PwC tax leaks scandal and allegations involving KPMG.

"The department should proactively publish as much information as possible about the procurement process," Senator Colbeck said.

"That includes demonstrating that proper procurement rules were followed, that value-for-money assessments were undertaken, that any conflicts of interest were declared and managed appropriately, and that contract decisions were made independently of any advisory or committee roles.

"Where public money is being spent, the evidence should be available to support the decisions that were made.

"The lesson from recent consulting controversies is that transparency is not an optional extra; it is essential to maintaining public trust."

Regarding Ms Ingram's role on the audit and risk committee, Senator Colbeck said public confidence depended not only on actual integrity, but on the appearance of integrity.

"It is impossible to exclude away all conflicts of interest," he said.

"The most important thing is to ensure that there is a robust conflict of interest management system in place and that it is followed and reported upon.

"Transparency is the best safeguard against any doubt."

Greens senator Barbara Pocock, who has advocated for more transparency and integrity in government contracting, said the government had enacted a temporary freeze on KPMG contracts for good reason.

"KPMG has broken the community's confidence through its misuse of confidential information and its mistreatment of whistleblowers - it has proven it can't be trusted," Senator Pocock said.

"No department or agency should be contracting with KPMG, including variations and extensions on existing contracts, until they have been properly investigated and regulated.

Crisp manufacturers fear new EU rules could ban vegetarian snacks being described as meat-flavoured.

New EU rules have banned terms such as “steak” and “bacon” for vegetarian alternatives to avoid confusing consumers, while still allowing labels like “veggie burgers” and “meat-free sausages”.

However crisp manufacturers are now worried that flavours of crisps, such as “flame grilled steak” or “BBQ beef”, might fall foul of the rules.

Oliver Devaux, from the European Snacks Association - which represents Ireland’s Tayto and Sweden’s Estrella among others - told Politico that the legislation could impact meaty crisps.

He said: “Meaty names in the flavour description might be in scope”, adding that the association wanted clarity on whether the EU rules apply.

He is pushing for a specific exemption for flavourings to clear things up.

Under the rule changes, which were announced in May, EU lawmakers agreed to ban the use of 31 meat-related names to describe non-meat products. These included the terms bacon, beef, ribs, chicken and wing, among others.

The regulation establishes protection for the term “meat”, which has been defined as “edible parts of animals”.

However members of the European Parliament are pushing for more restrictions, and tabled new amendments to the legislation in August.

The European People’s Party, an influential centre-right party, do not support the proposals, and do not want terms such as “burger”, “sausage” and “nugget” to be banned from use on veggie products.

British crisp producers could end up falling foul of the rules if they sell into Northern Ireland, which still follows many EU rules as part of its ongoing relationship with the Republic of Ireland.

Though the new regulation entered into force on 18 August, there will be a three-year transitional period - meaning the ban will only take effect in August 2029.

Businesses will need to consider what they name their products before launch to ensure they comply with the ban.

La industria pone coto a completar el salario a los trabajadores de baja: así lucha el metal contra el absentismo

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

La mayoría de convenios provinciales del sector recogen límites a los complementos por incapacidad temporal

El diálogo social sobre la reforma de la gestión de las bajas laborales ha quedado paralizado tras el adelanto electoral. Ante esta situación y el continuo aumento de la incapacidad temporal, varios sectores han comenzado a actuar por su cuenta. Es el caso de la industria del metal, donde numerosos convenios provinciales han empezado a limitar los complementos salariales por incapacidad temporal que perciben los trabajadores durante una baja.

La tendencia no es exclusiva del sector metalúrgico, pero la amplitud de su negociación colectiva, que afecta a unas 220.000 empresas y a más de dos millones de trabajadores, convierte a sus convenios en un laboratorio de medidas para contener el crecimiento del absentismo.

Los convenios colectivos del metal están recortando los complementos por incapacidad temporal (IT) derivados de contingencias comunes para tratar de frenar el aumento de las bajas en un sector en el que cada día se ausentan más de 140.000 trabajadores de los 2,7 millones de ocupados que lo integran, según un estudio de Confemetal del periodo 2021-2024. El informe indica que el coste asociado a las ausencias por enfermedad común aumentó más de un 90%, hasta aproximarse a los 2.900 millones de euros en el último ejercicio del periodo analizado. Además, en 2024 se iniciaron 31 procesos de baja por cada 1.000 trabajadores del sector, frente a los 17 registrados tres años antes, lo que supone un incremento del 83%.

De esta manera, la negociación colectiva de la industria y los servicios del metal está tendiendo a restringir los complementos salariales que perciben los trabajadores durante una baja, especialmente cuando esta se debe a enfermedad común. En estos casos, la ley indica actualmente que la prestación por incapacidad temporal no cubre el 100% del salario: no se cobra nada entre el primer y el tercer día; se percibe el 60% de la base reguladora (equivalente en términos generales al salario) desde el cuarto hasta el vigésimo día, y el 75% a partir del día 21. En cambio, cuando la baja deriva de accidente de trabajo o enfermedad profesional, el trabajador cobra el 75% de la base reguladora desde el día siguiente al inicio de la baja.

Por este motivo, históricamente y en todos los sectores, muchos convenios colectivos recogen compensaciones salariales que, de forma habitual, complementan estas prestaciones hasta el 100% del sueldo del trabajador. Son estos complementos a los que el metal está poniendo coto, según se refleja en el análisis detallado que hace anualmente la patronal del metal Confemetal de todos sus convenios colectivos. En el caso del análisis correspondiente a 2025 (que incluye también acuerdos para 2026), la mayoría de estas normas pactadas por los empresarios y los trabajadores limitan, por ejemplo, a un periodo máximo de entre 100 y 200 días los complementos hasta el 100% del salario. Para ello, unos convenios rebajan el porcentaje de salario que complementa la empresa, y otros retrasan hacer esa compensación hasta que la baja empieza a tener una duración mayor.

Entre los ejemplos más representativos figura el convenio del metal de Burgos, que abona el 60% del salario durante los tres primeros días de baja y no complementa hasta el 100% hasta superar el día 30. En Castellón, la empresa deja de estar obligada a completar el salario a partir del día 70 de incapacidad temporal. En Ourense, el trabajador percibe el 60% del salario durante los tres primeros días de la primera baja anual por enfermedad común y no alcanza el 100% hasta transcurridos nueve meses de incapacidad temporal.

Otras fórmulas restringen el derecho al complemento cuando se acumulan varios procesos de incapacidad temporal en un mismo año. Es el caso del convenio de Málaga, que complementa al 50% los tres primeros días de enfermedad común en las dos primeras bajas del año y elimina esa mejora a partir de la tercera. En Segovia, el complemento se reduce al 75% durante los primeros quince días desde la segunda baja dentro del mismo año natural.

Estos son solo algunos ejemplos de decenas de fórmulas distintas que están limitando este tipo de complementos. Pero, además, junto a estas medidas, muchos convenios mantienen los denominados complementos o premios de asistencia, concebidos para incentivar la presencia efectiva en el trabajo y reducir el absentismo.

Aunque varias sentencias del Tribunal Supremo de 2025 y 2026 están estrechando mucho el margen para configurarlos. Según esta jurisprudencia, su legalidad depende no de la existencia en sí de un complemento de asistencia, sino de qué ausencias penaliza. Así, según explican fuentes de la patronal del sector, los negociadores de convenios pueden seguir vinculando esos pluses a cuestiones como la puntualidad, la ausencia de bajas injustificadas, la disponibilidad o el cumplimiento de objetivos de presencia efectiva. Pero la doctrina del Supremo considera, sin embargo, que estos complementos no pueden ligarse a determinadas ausencias que están protegidas por la ley, como las bajas por incapacidad temporal (independientemente de cuál sea su origen, laboral o enfermedad común) u otras relacionadas con permisos de conciliación, cuidados o maternidad.

Algunos ejemplos de este tipo de pluses en los convenios del metal son el provincial de Huelva, que fija el premio de asistencia y puntualidad más cuantioso: 223,47 euros mensuales si no hay ausencias. Si se falta una jornada se reduce a 167,63 euros; si se falta dos a 111,76 euros; y si se falta más de dos jornadas se pierde íntegramente. En el convenio del metal de Sevilla, se otorga un premio de 31,37 euros mensuales siempre que la persona trabajadora no tenga ninguna ausencia, parcial o total, en ninguna jornada laboral del mes. O el caso del acuerdo de Madrid, que vincula el complemento por mejora de la productividad (un 1,5% del salario convenio) a no acumular ausencias superiores a 8 horas laborales al mes.

Italien – Warum sich Salvinis Lega im Niedergang befindet

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Einige Elemente auf SRF.ch funktionieren nur mit aktiviertem JavaScript.

Die Lega ist die älteste Partei im kurzlebigen italienischen Parteiensystem. Vor 35 Jahren wurde sie gegründet und seit 13 Jahren hat sie den gleichen Chef: Matteo Salvini. Dieses Wochenende fand in Mailand der Parteikongress statt und Salvini wurde als Chef bestätigt. Das sieht nach viel Stabilität aus. Doch der Schein trügt. Die Lega steckt in einer tiefen Krise, für die Salvini die Hauptverantwortung trägt.

Vor ein paar Jahren erreichte die Lega ihren absoluten Höhepunkt: Bei der Europawahl 2019 kletterte sie auf einen Stimmenanteil von sage und schreibe 34 Prozent. Heute dümpelt die Lega gemäss aktuellen Umfragen nur noch bei fünf Prozent herum.

Der Absturz der Partei hat zwei Namen: Matteo Salvini und Roberto Vannacci.

Der heute in seinem Amt bestätigte Salvini hatte vor zwei Jahren eine Idee. Vor der Wahl ins EU-Parlament zauberte er einen bis anhin unbekannten Spitzenkandidaten aus seinem Hut: Ex-General Roberto Vannacci. Salvini erkannte in Vannacci einen Stimmenfänger, ein Zugpferd, das die Lega hätte vorwärtsbringen sollen.

Vannacci politisiert weit rechts, so weit rechts, dass in der Lega, die in der Lombardei und Venetien eine Volkspartei ist, viele murrten. Vannacci sparte dann auch nicht mit Provokationen: Er bezeichnete Benito Mussolini als Staatsmann, nicht als Diktator, und die spezifische Strafnorm bei Femiziden will er tilgen.

Und tatsächlich: Vannacci punktete am rechten Rand und mobilisierte Stimmen für die Lega. Dumm nur, dass der Ex-General kurz nachdem er für die Lega ins EU-Parlament eingezogen war, absprang und seine eigene Partei gründete: Futuro Nazionale. Diese neue Partei liegt nun seit Monaten vor der Lega, aktuell bei etwa 8 Prozent.

Vannacci treibt die Lega und das rechte Regierungslager mit noch rechteren Parolen vor sich her. Dass sich Salvini mit Vannacci krass verschätzte, ist aber nur eine unter mehreren Fehleinschätzungen und Niederlagen Salvinis. So versprach der Lega-Chef dem Land mehr Föderalismus und scheiterte damit. Als Infrastrukturminister versprach er mehrmals den Baubeginn der gigantischen Brücke nach Sizilien, auch das eine leere Versprechung. Oder er wollte die Lega zu einer nationalen Partei machen, mit Sektionen auch in Süditalien. Auch das ein Fehlschlag.

Und trotzdem wurde Salvini in seinem Amt als Lega-Chef bestätigt. Das ist kein Zeichen der Stärke. Im Gegenteil.

Italien wählt vielleicht schon im nächsten Frühjahr und die Lega scheute sich davor, im bereits laufenden Wahlkampf ihren Chef auszuwechseln.

Doch sollte die Lega bei der anstehenden Wahl tatsächlich hinter Vannaccis Partei zurückfallen, könnten die Tage Salvinis, der sich gern «Capitano» nennen lässt, gezählt sein. Und der Rechtskoalition unter Giorgia Meloni könnten entscheidende Sitze fehlen, um weiterregieren zu können.

Известный российский поэт, член Союза писателей Москвы и лауреат различных литературных премий Евгений Чигрин ушел из жизни. Об этом со ссылкой на его семью сообщило РИА Новости.

Чигрина не стало утром 25 октября, ему было 65 лет.

«Женя долго болел, у него был рак последней стадии. Он боролся три года с этой болезнью. Врачи от него уже отказались — соответственно, у него пошли осложнения, и сегодня он скоропостижно скончался», — добавили близкие поэта.

О дате и месте прощания пока не сообщается.

Евгений Чигрин с 2002 года проживал в Москве и подмосковном Красногорске. В числе его работ — поэтические книги Невидимый проводник, Неспящая бухта, Подводный шар и другие.

Стихи Чигрина публиковались во многих литературных журналах и альманахах, а также в престижных европейских и российских антологиях.

Кроме того, произведения поэта были переведены на английский, французский, польский, румынский, хинди, арабский, турецкий, сербский, македонский, чешский, азербайджанский, белорусский и украинский языки.

Ранее стало известно о смерти российского актера Владимира Курочкина, известного по работам в Мелиховском театре «Чеховская студия», а также фильму «Все по закону» и сериалу «С чего начинается родина».

María Corina Machado vuelve a Venezuela para impulsar la transición

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

La líder opositora y premio nobel de la paz 2025 ya se encuentra de regreso en Venezuela y reapareció este domingo en una Iglesia católica del estado Lara (noroeste) abrazada por los seguidores de su partido Vente Venezuela (VV) en medio de una eucaristía.

Rompiendo el veto que aseguraba tener para volver a Venezuela, María Corina Machado reapareció este domingo en el escenario nacional para medir el verdadero alcance de su liderazgo de calle, en un intento por forzar la transición democrática en su país y desafiar la diplomacia petrolera entre Delcy Rodríguez y Donald Trump.Después de diez meses fuera de su país, de donde salió para recibir el Premio Nobel de la Paz 2025 en Oslo, Machado regresa tras superar unos siete bloqueos aéreos y marítimos a su retorno desde Centroamérica e islas del Caribe, según la información que ha revelado su equipo y fuentes conocedoras del asunto."Estoy aquí. Con ustedes", dijo este domingo en un mensaje en X y minutos después su partido, Vente Venezuela (VV), difundió imágenes de la líder opositora entrando en una iglesia en la pintoresca localidad de Santa Rosa, estado Lara (oeste), arropada por sus seguidores. No hay detalles todavía sobre su regreso.Instalada en los últimos meses en Panamá -país al que considera una segunda casa por haberla acogido en momentos álgidos, como cuando resguardó las actas de votación que, según la oposición, daban el triunfo a Edmundo González Urrutia en las presidenciales de 2024-, Machado llega a Venezuela decidida a encabezar la lucha por unas nuevas elecciones presidenciales.La opositora, de 59 años, ha buscado el cambio político en su país desde sus inicios en 2002, cuando fundó la organización Súmate, dedicada a la defensa de los derechos políticos de los ciudadanos, pero solo logró convertirse en la líder máxima de la oposición en 2023, cuando arrasó en las primarias de ese año.Siempre defendió a Estados Unidos como un aliado y apenas 12 días después de la captura de Nicolás Maduro en enero pasado por parte de tropas de este país, Machado se reunió con Trump, a quien le entregó la medalla del Nobel y le expresó su deseo de volver a Venezuela.Entonces el mandatario anunció que podría "involucrar" de alguna manera a la opositora en su plan de transición para Venezuela, pero días después el secretario de Estado, Marco Rubio, -quien es más cercano a Machado-, declaró que el liderazgo de la dirigente no era realista en ese momento para iniciar una transición.En marzo, Machado se reunió nuevamente con Trump bajo total hermetismo y solo hasta el 5 de octubre volvió a referirse a ella como una persona "fantástica". El mandatario fue preguntado sobre si apoyaba el regreso de la líder opositora al país y se limitó a responder que "todo saldría bien".En todo este tiempo, Washington ha estado negociando acuerdos en materia de petróleo, minería y gas con el Gobierno de Delcy Rodríguez, una relación que ha crecido hasta el punto de calificarse mutuamente como socios y amigos, y que los llevó a firmar en agosto un convenio petrolero que permite a Estados Unidos acceder a la quinta parte de las reservas de crudo venezolanas.El pacto ha sido criticado por quienes consideran que Estados Unidos busca principalmente el petróleo venezolano y no impulsar la democratización del país. Machado tardó en reaccionar a este acuerdo y solo se limitó a decirle a la Administración de Trump que sus "enemigos" están en el poder en Venezuela.Bajo este escenario la relación Machado-Trump parece distante.Mientras, la urgencia por las elecciones de Machado no coincide con las declaraciones recientes de Trump, en las que sostuvo que aún es "demasiado pronto" para la celebración de las mismas.Sin embargo, el plan de transición del mandatario contempla un proceso de negociación entre el chavismo y la oposición representada por la exdiputada Dinorah Figuera para alcanzar un acuerdo que permita convocar a comicios presidenciales en el país suramericano.Figuera fue escogida por Estados Unidos como la líder de la delegación opositora bajo el argumento de que representa el Parlamento electo de 2015, cuando el antichavismo ganó la mayoría de curules.Machado no forma parte del diálogo, pero manifestó en sus inicios que no obstaculizará el proceso y reiteró su disposición a negociar con Delcy Rodríguez, como ya lo había indicado hace meses durante una reunión con la mayor coalición opositora, la Plataforma Unitaria Democrática (PUD)."Si es necesario para intercambiar en algún encuentro a los efectos de definir (un) cronograma de transición, pues se hará. Pero lo he dicho, cualquier proceso es sobre la base del reconocimiento del 28 de julio de 2024 y para una transición", expresó Machado en febrero pasado en un encuentro virtual con medios colombianos.Recientemente reiteró en redes sociales su disposición a negociar, lo que desató las críticas del ministro del Interior, Diosdado Cabello. Durante su programa de televisión, el dirigente oficialista -uno de sus principales detractores- también se mofó de las denuncias sobre los presuntos impedimentos a la opositora para retornar y, de hecho, descartó que ella intentara regresar por las fronteras marítimas o terrestres.Con todo, Machado se encuentra de vuelta en el país y ha reabierto el tablero político venezolano.
