Thousands of Handwritten Records Reveal Scale of Warming in the Congo

📅 Oct 12, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

A weather record kept in the National Institute for Agronomic Studies and Research in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Derrick Muheki

The Congo Basin is one of the few places on Earth where the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change has failed to track the long-term shift in extreme heat and rainfall. The reason is a lack of readily available data. To fill this gap, scientists have digitized thousands of handwritten weather records, which reveal the impact of warming in the region.

The records, recovered from archives in Belgium and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, span the years 1908 to 2023. To digitize the data, scientists had to scan more than 9,000 pages that together included more than a million observations.

“Seeing how fragile these paper archives were as I was visiting [the archive in the Democratic Republic of the Congo] made me realize how urgent this data rescue work is,” said Derrick Muheki, a PhD candidate at Vrije Universiteit Brussel. “Just months before my visit, part of the archive had been damaged by fire. We could have lost decades of irreplaceable data.”

The digitized data revealed a clear warming trend in the latter half of the 20th century. Between 1961 and 1990, scientists found, the average daily temperature in the Congo Basin rose by about 0.9 degrees F, while the average number of hot days per year nearly doubled. As the Congo warmed, scientists found, it also saw more extreme heat and more heavy rainfall. Their findings were published in Earth System Dynamics.

The trends observed in the Congo mirror changes seen in Europe, North America, and elsewhere. But unlike those regions, the Congo lacked the climate data needed to study changes over the long term.

Now, combining the newly digitized records with other, previously rescued data, scientists have assembled one of the largest climate datasets for the Congo Basin. The data not only offers a fuller picture of weather history but could be used to improve projections of future climate change, scientists say.

“Our work further highlights the urgent need for more data rescue projects, especially in data-sparse regions, to close knowledge gaps on climate trends,” said Wim Thiery, a professor at Vrije Universiteit Brussel, who was involved in the new research. “This is particularly important for regions in the Global South that are severely impacted by climate change already today.”

The lack of weather data is an ongoing problem, scientists say. In Africa, a scarcity of weather stations is making it harder to warn people about upcoming extreme weather events or to determine the role of climate change after the fact.

Pluralistic: How labor rights rescued Ultima from a monopolist (12 Oct 2026)

📅 Oct 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Pluralistic: Daily links from Cory Doctorow

If you work in the media, you have doubtless had a lifetime of encouragement to treat copyright as your primary source of economic protection, and doubtless you've been told that this protection arises from the fact that copyright is a property right – specifically, it's a subspecies of "intellectual property":

https://locusmag.com/feature/cory-doctorow-ip/

Property rights are capitalism's holy sacrament, so of course, it's natural to treasure any property right you can lay claim to. But this is a trap. Creative workers aren't asset speculators, we're workers. When we bargain with a media company to sell them our copyright, we're just bargaining to sell our labor, not our property.

This is a distinction with a difference. Property rights are alienable. When you negotiate to sell your property to someone else, you propose to transfer the title to that property. If you sell your house, you don't get to move back in if you change your mind later. Sell your car, and you must not get in and drive it away.

Nominally, creative workers only bargain away some of their copyright, because copyright is also divisible. You can sell US English print rights to a novel, a one-month license to synch a song to an advertisement, or the right to display a painting for a year.

But here's the thing about property rights: the most reliable predictor of how many property rights you must give up in a sale isn't how many property rights you started with – it's how much bargaining power you have. For 50 years, governments have expanded copyright in every way, making it last longer, cover more works and restrict more uses, all backstopped by ever-steepening penalties. 50 years later, media companies – our bosses – are larger and more profitable than they've ever been, and we're poorer than we've ever been. The share of the money generated by our labor has declined, even as our rights over the product of that labor have expanded.

How to make sense of this? It's easy, once you factor in power. In a world of five publishers, four studios, three labels, two app companies and a single ebook/audiobook company, getting more copyright to bargain with just means getting more copyright to bargain away. When copyright's term expanded from "life plus 50 years" to "life plus 70 years," our bosses simply demanded the entirety of the new term, and didn't offer us a dime more.

This is the thesis of Chokepoint Capitalism, the book on Big Tech, Big Media, and creative labor markets Rebecca Giblin and I published in 2022:

https://www.beacon.org/Chokepoint-Capitalism-P2020.aspx

Chokepoint Capitalism is broken into two parts: the first half of the book is a series of pitiless, plain-language dissections of the baroque scams that media companies have used to make themselves richer from our work, from Spotify to Amazon to Livenation and beyond. From reader feedback, I can tell you that these chapters will make you very angry, because once you strip away all the deliberate obfuscation, all you're left with is one screw-job after another, each of which makes the creators you love poorer even as you pay more for their work.

But then there's the second half of the book. This section is devoted to "shovel-ready" policy solutions, each of which is designed to change the "distribution" of the spoils of the media industry, putting more money in workers' pockets at the expense of our bosses. None of these are things you can do as a "conscious consumer." The monopolistic destruction of creators' livelihoods is a systemic problem and you can't solve it by shopping better.

The point of these chapters is to lay out what we must demand from our policymakers when (inevitably), they propose to "solve" the problem of worsening straits for creative workers – and to help creative workers understand that when our bosses demand more copyright, that's not there to benefit us.

One of these systemic solutions is "termination of transfer," a simple and radical idea that has a proven track-record of making creative workers richer at our bosses' expense. Simply put, "termination of transfer" is the statutory right to void any contract you've signed to transfer your copyright to someone else. In the USA, the 1976 Copyright Act introduced a 35-year termination right. 35 years after you sign any copyright contract, you can file some paperwork with the US Copyright Office and get that copyright back, no matter what your contract says.

Now, most creative works don't have a commercial life after 35 years (very few works are commercially viable even after the original US copyright term of 14 years), but for those that do, termination of transfer is a reliable way to address the extremely common situation in which a creator signs away their rights for a low price, not knowing that it will become a lasting hit. Once you've scored that hit, you can probably get a better deal from your next work, but the deal for that hit is done and can't be altered.

Termination solves this. From The Babysitters Club to the early Stephen King novels to George Clinton's entire catalog, termination of transfer has put lots of money into creative workers' pockets at the expense of corporations that ripped them off at the start of their careers.

From the start, there was no question whether termination of transfer would make it into Chokepoint Capitalism. Rebecca is one of the world's leading experts on the subject, having co-authored the definitive study of termination:

https://pluralistic.net/2021/09/26/take-it-back/

In the book, we note that – unlike longer copyright terms – termination has a proven track record of putting groceries on creators' tables, braces on their kids' teeth, and a roof over their heads. We go over the legislative history of termination in the 1976 Act, noting that it was originally supposed to be automatic and kick in after just 20 years. If we want to make creative workers richer, changing the 35-year administrative right into an automatic 20-year termination right is a no-brainer amendment to copyright law.

How did an automatic right that kicked in after 20 years turn into a right you have to apply for after 35 years? Simple: our bosses hate termination. How much do they hate it? Well, take Mitch Glazier, the head of the Recording Industry Association of America, where he draws a $1.3m annual salary to represent the record labels.

Before Glazier got the RIAA gig, he was an obscure Congressional staffer, but he was forced out of the job after he snuck a clause into an otherwise unrelated bill that killed termination of transfer for recording artists. This was so outrageous that Congress actually convened a special session so they could rip out Glazier's amendment, and Glazier got a new job as a millionaire lobbyist for the recording industry:

https://pluralistic.net/2026/03/03/its-a-trap-2/#inheres-at-the-moment-of-fixation

Termination is hugely useful and quite radical. Last year saw Gary K Wolf, creator of the novel Who Censored Roger Rabbit? (later adapted by Disney as Who Framed Roger Rabbit?) claw back his rights to the book and its characters, in order to continue the series:

https://pluralistic.net/2025/11/18/im-not-bad/#im-just-drawn-that-way

In 2023, Bill Willingham terminated the transfer of his wildly successful graphic novel series Fables from DC and released the copyright into the public domain:

https://pluralistic.net/2023/09/15/fairy-use-tales/#sampling-license

And now, Richard Garriott, AKA "Lord British," has claimed back the rights to the groundbreaking Ultima series of computer role-playing games, which he sold to Electronic Arts in 1992:

https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/richard-garriott-is-officially-regaining-control-of-the-ultima-series/

On the Techjess podcast, Garriott marveled at how easy the process was: "there’s a really weird United States copyright law that says if you are the original holder of a copyright and you assign it or sell it to any publisher or any other entity, after 35 years, you can just take it back":

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2El7VtPuSzs

EA killed off Ultima in 2000, and it has languished since, despite an ardent fandom. Garriott has vowed to reissue the old games and is mulling new ones.

All this brings me back to property rights. Property rights are uniquely unsuitable to this kind of arrangement. You don't get to move back into the house your parents sold 35 years ago, and if you did, the photographer who took the family portrait in the hallway wouldn't be allowed to break in and take it away 35 years after you sat for it.

Termination of transfer isn't an "IP right" – it's a labor right. It belongs to the large class of rights that are far more important and useful than mere property rights, such as privacy rights and human rights:

https://pluralistic.net/2026/09/28/cost-of-everything/#value-of-nothing

If copyright were just about property rights, then termination of transfer would be inconceivable. Termination is a vestigial labor right at the very periphery of copyright. It protects artists, and it can only do so because it is not a property right.

Philippines Digital Nomad Visa 2026: What EO 86 Says, and Why You Can’t Apply Yet

📅 Oct 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

If you have a foreign partner, a client abroad or an expat friend, you have probably been asked this already. They read that the Philippines now has a digital nomad visa, they picture a month in Siargao with a laptop, and they want to know where to apply.

The honest answer, as of 11 October 2026: nowhere, yet. The law exists. The application form doesn’t.

The Philippines digital nomad visa was created by Executive Order No. 86, signed on 24 April 2025. Nearly eighteen months later, most guides online still contradict themselves, calling the visa “fully operational” in one paragraph and admitting the fee and income rule are unknown in the next. This guide is for Filipinos who keep getting asked about it, for Fil-Ams and former Filipinos thinking of working from home for a year, and for foreign partners and friends planning a long stay here. We go through what the order actually says, line by line, what is still missing, who can realistically qualify, and what people do instead while the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) finishes the job.

This is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Rules change, so confirm your own case with the DFA, the Bureau of Immigration or a licensed immigration lawyer before you act on it.

Here is where things stand as of 11 October 2026, checked against government sources on that date.

If you only read one line: the Philippines has authorized a digital nomad visa, but as of this check there is no public way to apply for it.

The order is short. Section 1 lets the DFA issue a Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) to non-immigrant foreigners who want to enter and stay temporarily “for the purpose of working remotely using digital technologies”, where their clients or employers are outside the Philippines. Section 2 lists eight conditions. The DFA’s own embassy in The Hague publishes the same eight conditions on its DNV page, word for word, which is the closest thing to an official checklist right now.

Section 3 says a DNV holder may stay up to one year and may renew for the same duration. Section 4 tells the DFA to set the grounds for revoking a DNV, and says those grounds must include, among others, taking a local job, fraud or misrepresentation in the application, and breaking immigration law. Section 5 puts the DFA in charge, working with the Department of Justice, the Department of Tourism, the Bureau of Immigration and the Bureau of Internal Revenue, and orders a pilot within 60 days. Section 7 orders implementing guidelines within 30 days.

That is it. No fee, no income figure, no form, no processing time.

Search “Philippines digital nomad visa requirements” and you will see USD 24,000 a year everywhere. It is not in EO 86. Trace it back and it leads to consultancies and nomad websites, never to a DFA notice. At the BSP reference rate of PHP 62.90 to the dollar (9 October 2026), that is about PHP 1.51 million a year, or PHP 125,800 a month, and nobody official has said it.

None of this means those figures will turn out wrong. It means nobody can quote them as the rule yet. When the guidelines do appear, the income figure and the fee will be the first things to check, and we will update this post with the DFA’s own numbers.

If someone offers to “process” a Philippine DNV for a fee today, ask them which DFA circular they are filing under. There isn’t one we can find.

This is the part almost every guide skips, and it is the most Filipino part of the whole order.

Section 2(f) says the applicant “must be a national of a country that offers DNVs to Filipinos”. So whether a foreigner can get the Philippine nomad visa depends on how their own country treats Philippine passport holders. The DFA has not published a list. But we already track which nomad visas Filipinos can actually get, so the mirror image is easy to sketch.

Countries whose nomad or remote-work visas were open to Philippine passports in our September 2026 check: Thailand (DTV), South Korea, Spain, Portugal, Hungary, Croatia, Estonia, Malta, the UAE, Malaysia (DE Rantau), Indonesia and Colombia. Taiwan’s Gold Card is also open to Filipinos, but it is an open work permit rather than a nomad visa, so we leave it off. The deadlines in the order passed long ago. EO 86 takes effect on publication, and gave 30 days for guidelines and 60 days for a pilot. Both windows closed in mid-2025.

Without a DNV, long-staying foreigners use the routes that already work.

The visa-free entry plus extensions route. Nationals of visa-free countries get an initial 30 days on arrival, according to the Bureau of Immigration. They can extend at any BI office or through BI e-services online. DFA posts describing the BI’s Long-Stay Visitor Visa Extension say a stay can be extended in blocks of up to six months, to a maximum of 36 months in total for visa-free nationals and 24 months for nationals who need a visa. Stays past 59 days also need an ACR I-Card, which we explain in our guide to getting an ACR I-Card in the Philippines.

One honest caveat. A tourist stay is not a work status, and we could not find any BI guidance saying remote work for a foreign employer is fine on one. Plenty of foreigners do it quietly. The DNV was created to close that gray area, which is why its delay matters.

The balikbayan route, for Fil-Ams, OFWs and former Filipinos. Under Republic Act 6768 as amended by RA 9174, a balikbayan is a Filipino citizen who has lived abroad continuously for at least a year, an OFW, or a former Filipino naturalized abroad. A former Filipino gets a one-year visa-free stay on arrival, unless they hold the passport of a restricted nationality. The foreign spouse and children of any balikbayan get the same one-year stay, as long as they travel together with the balikbayan. Bring an old Philippine passport or PSA birth certificate, plus the marriage or birth certificate for family members. For most of our US readers, this already beats the DNV: one year, no application, no fee. We cover the details in our guide to the one-year visa-free balikbayan stay for foreign spouses and children.

Dual citizenship. A former Filipino who reacquires citizenship under RA 9225 is simply Filipino again, with no stay limit and no visa question at all.

Since the DNV has no published fee, the real numbers today belong to the tourist and balikbayan routes. Conversions use the BSP reference rate of PHP 62.90 to USD 1 on 9 October 2026.

Siargao and La Union have the surf and the community, with more brownouts and slower internet. Is the Philippines digital nomad visa open for applications? Not that we can confirm. As of 11 October 2026 it is not on the DFA e-Visa portal, and we found no published implementing guidelines.

How much income do you need for the Philippines digital nomad visa? EO 86 says only “sufficient income” earned outside the Philippines. The widely quoted USD 24,000 a year is unofficial.

How long is the Philippines digital nomad visa valid? Up to one year, renewable for the same duration, under Section 3 of EO 86.

Can Filipinos apply for the Philippines digital nomad visa? No, and they don’t need to. It is for foreign nationals. Filipino citizens and dual citizens live and work in the Philippines without a visa.

Can Americans get the Philippines digital nomad visa? On a strict reading, no, because the US offers no digital nomad visa to Filipinos. The DFA has not confirmed how it will apply the rule.

Can a digital nomad visa holder work for a Philippine company? No. Section 4 says the grounds for revocation must include local employment.

My foreign husband works remotely. What should he use? If you have lived abroad continuously for at least a year, are an OFW, or are a former Filipino, he can enter together with you under the balikbayan privilege for a one-year visa-free stay, as long as he is not a restricted national. If you live in the Philippines already, the balikbayan rule does not apply, so visa-free entry plus BI extensions is the usual route.

The Philippines digital nomad visa is real on paper and missing in practice. Until the DFA publishes guidelines, a form and a fee, the useful routes are the old ones: visa-free entry with extensions, the balikbayan privilege for Filipinos abroad, former Filipinos and their families, or dual citizenship. Verschwörungstheorien: Wie prüfe ich, was stimmen könnte?

📅 Oct 12, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Verschwörungen gibt es und wird es immer geben. Die Frage ist, wie sich absurde von wahrscheinlichen Verschwörungstheorien unterscheiden lassen. Dabei hilft ein gewisses Maß an Misstrauen - auch sich selbst gegenüber.

"Birds aren't real", Vögel gibt es nicht. Das kritzelte der US-amerikanische Student Peter McIndoe auf ein Stück Pappe und mischte sich 2017 bei einer Demonstration unter die Anhänger des US-Präsidenten Donald Trump.

Auf seinen Slogan angesprochen erzählte er: Die US-Regierung habe alle Vögel umbringen lassen und sie durch Überwachungsdrohnen ersetzt. Vögel säßen auf Stromleitungen, um sich aufzuladen, Vogelkot sei ein flüssiges Ortungsgerät.

Das ist eine Verschwörungserzählung. Eine andere geht so: Der Ölkonzern ExxonMobil wusste bereits seit den 1970er-Jahren von der Bedrohung durch den Klimawandel. Vom Konzern bezahlte Forschende haben den globalen Temperaturanstieg ziemlich präzise eingeschätzt.

Die internen Analysen von ExxonMobil standen allerdings immer in heftigem Kontrast zu ihren öffentlichen Erklärungen, in denen der Klimawandel kleingeredet, Mythen verbreitet und Unwissenheit vorgetäuscht wurde.

Peter McIndoe erzählte der New York Times 2022, dass seine "Birds aren't real"-Verschwörung Satire gewesen sei. Er habe damit darauf aufmerksam machen wollen, wie schnell Menschen an erfundene Verschwörungserzählungen und Desinformationen glauben.

ExxonMobils Machenschaften wurden 2015 durch investigative Journalisten aufgedeckt, die Zugang zu internen Dokumenten und Forschungsberichten hatten. 2020 reichte der Generalstaatsanwalt von Washington D.C. Klage gegen ExxonMobil und weitere Ölkonzerne ein. Eine 2023 im Fachmagazin Science erschienene Studie wertete die internen Forschungsergebnisse des Unternehmens aus und bestätigte die Verschwörungserzählung: Exxon wusste seit Jahrzehnten von den Folgen des Klimawandels.

Es gibt also beides: wenig plausible und plausible Verschwörungserzählungen. Die Frage ist nur, wie sich die eine von der anderen unterscheiden lässt. Es gibt ein paar Prüfkriterien, die dabei helfen können.

Eine Voraussetzung dafür ist: innehalten und nachdenken. Nichts tun, außer sich selbst ein paar kritische Fragen zu stellen.

Eine dieser Fragen ist die nach der Logik, sagt Lotte Pummerer, Sozialpsychologin und Postdoc an der Universität in Mannheim, wo sie zu Verschwörungstheorien forscht.

Wie viele Menschen müssten eingeweiht sein und welche Motive sollten sie haben, die Verschwörung geheim zu halten? Um bei dem Beispiel mit den falschen Vögeln zu bleiben: Neben den Drahtziehern müssten alle Ornithologen und Vogelschützer Bescheid wissen und dichthalten. Und das seit Jahrzehnten. Ohne einen einzigen Whistleblower. Wie logisch ist das?

Auch die eigenen Motive sollten einmal unter die Lupe genommen werden, sagt Pummerer. "Kann es sein, dass ich gerade bewusst nur in eine bestimmte Richtung nach Informationen suche? Gibt es einen Grund, dass ich eine schnelle, befriedigende Antwort will oder wäre es okay für mich, zu dem Ergebnis zu kommen, dass ich diese Antwort aufgrund meiner derzeitigen Informationen nicht bekommen kann?"

Solche Fragen sollen mögliche kognitive Verzerrungen (cognitive bias) überprüfen, für die wir alle anfällig sein können. Es handelt sich dabei um systematische Denk- und Wahrnehmungsfehler, die unsere Entscheidungsfindung, Urteilsbildung und Wahrnehmung beeinflussen und zu falschen Annahmen führen.

Ein Beispiel für eine kognitive Verzerrung ist der sogenannte Bestätigungsfehler (confirmation bias): Wir bevorzugen tendenziell Informationen, die am besten in unser Weltbild passen und unsere ohnehin vorhandenen Annahmen bestätigen.

Aufgrund dieser kognitiven Verzerrungen sei es wichtig "mal ganz bewusst den Gegencheck zu machen", sagt die Sozialpsychologin. Und sich zu fragen: "Bin ich bereit, mich überzeugen zu lassen, wenn die wissenschaftliche Evidenz in eine andere Richtung weist?"

Pummerer nennt 9/11 als Beispiel. Die Verschwörungserzählung, dass die Anschläge auf das World Trade Center und das Pentagon 2001 von der US-Regierung inszeniert waren, hält sich hartnäckig. Die Türme können nur gesprengt worden sein, sie wären sonst nie zusammengefallen. Oder hätten zur Seite kippen müssen.

Umfangreiche Ermittlungen vieler unabhängiger Expertinnen und Experten haben ergeben: 9/11 war ein islamistischer Terroranschlag. Es war der Einschlag der beiden Flugzeuge, der die Türme zum Einsturz gebracht hat. "Da sind viele Leute, die in den entsprechenden Bereichen viel mehr Expertise haben als ich, angefangen von Bauingenieuren, Menschen, die Flugschreiber und Flugradare auslesen, bis hin zu Investigativjournalistinnen. Warum sollte ausgerechnet ich, die ich nicht dafür ausgebildet bin, zu der entscheidenden Erkenntnis kommen?", fragt Pummerer.

"Es gibt einen Wissensunterschied zwischen Leuten, die Experten sind und einem selbst, wenn man kein Experte ist", ergänzt Marlene Altenmüller, ebenfalls Sozialpsychologin und Juniorprofessorin für Wissenschaftsrezeption am Leibniz-Institut für Psychologie in Trier. Es sei wichtig, diesen Unterschied anzuerkennen, denn "wir leben in einer komplexen Welt und können gar nicht alles selbst durchdringen und verstehen."

Bedeutet auch: Ohne Vertrauen geht es nicht. Das heißt aber nicht, dass wir blind vertrauen sollten, im Gegenteil. Misstrauen sei wichtig und konstruktiv, sagt Altenmüller. "Misstrauen und Zweifel sind zentraler Bestandteil wissenschaftlicher Prozesse."

Sie bezeichnet Wissenschaft als "institutionalisierten Zweifel". Studien werden immer wieder von unterschiedlichen Forschenden kritisch geprüft und oft auch wiederholt, um frühere Ergebnisse abzusichern und besser zu verstehen. Aus diesem Grund, sagt Altenmüller, sei Wissenschaft "die beste Annäherung an die Wahrheit, die wir haben".

"Wissenschaftlicher Konsens" bedeutet, dass sich die Argumente durchgesetzt haben, deren Datenbasis einer wiederholten, kritischen Prüfung standgehalten haben. Der menschengemachte Klimawandel ist ein Beispiel für wissenschaftlichen Konsens.

"Dieses ständige Hinterfragen und der produktive Prozess des Misstrauens, der dann zum Konsens führt, sollte viel überzeugender sein als eine Einzelperson mit einer Meinung", sagt Altenmüller.

Es liegt in der Natur wissenschaftlicher Erkenntnisse, dass sie nie als absolut unumstößlich angesehen werden, sondern immer mit einer gewissen Unsicherheit behaftet sind. Ein Umstand, den sich ExxonMobil in seinen öffentlichen Stellungnahmen zum Risiko durch die globale Erwärmung zunutze gemacht hat: Unsicherheiten in Bezug auf die Klimawandelforschung wurden besonders betont und übertrieben.

Die Wissenschaftlerin Lotte Pummerer wünscht sich an dieser Stelle ein bisschen mehr selbstkritisches Hinterfragen und weniger Doppelstandards: Wenn von einer politischen oder wissenschaftlichen Institution eine lückenlose Beweiskette verlangt werde, wie könne dann gleichzeitig irgendeiner Person auf einem Sofa in einem YouTube-Video geglaubt werden, die beim Schminken was von geheimen Machenschaften erzähle?

Hay ciertos alimentos que son el motor de la cocina mediterránea. El aceite de oliva virgen extra es, sin duda, el oro líquido; esencial para sofreír, aliñar en crudo o dar el toque final a un plato. Junto a él, el ajo y la cebolla forman el dúo dinámico que aporta personalidad y aroma a casi cualquier guiso, sopa o salsa que nos planteemos hacer.

De igual forma, la pasta seca es el recurso más socorrido cuando no tenemos tiempo, permitiéndonos crear platos deliciosos en cuestión de minutos con un poco de queso y ajo.

No podemos olvidarnos de las patatas, ese alimento humilde pero potente que se adapta a todo: al horno, fritas, en puré o cocidas. Por otro lado, las legumbres (como garbanzos, lentejas o alubias) son tesoros nutricionales cargados de fibra y minerales. Mezclarlas con cereales garantiza una proteína vegetal completa, ideal para mantener el cuerpo fuerte y saludable.

El vinagre, ya sea de manzana, sidra o balsámico, aporta la acidez necesaria para equilibrar los sabores y aliñar ensaladas frescas.

Si queremos añadir un toque dulce, la miel de abeja natural es una alternativa fantástica al azúcar, además de tener propiedades antiinflamatorias. Hablando de dulzor, el azúcar sigue siendo el rey de los postres y un corrector de acidez muy útil para las salsas de tomate. Para los amantes del cacao, el chocolate negro es un aliado perfecto tanto para repostería como para snacks antioxidantes.

En el congelador o la nevera, el pollo y el cerdo son las carnes más versátiles por su sabor y precio. El pescado blanco, como el bacalao o la merluza, aporta ligereza y salud. Para los momentos de máxima rapidez, el atún en conserva es un recurso práctico que encaja en ensaladas o bocadillos.

Los lácteos también tienen su lugar: la leche para salsas y desayunos, el queso para gratinar o dar sabor, y el yogur natural como base para aderezos saludables. Para cerrar el círculo, los frutos secos (nueces, almendras o avellanas) aportan grasas buenas y una textura crujiente que realza cualquier ensalada o tarta.

Tener una despensa abastecida con estos elementos, priorizando siempre los productos de proximidad y de temporada, nos permite cocinar de forma eficiente, económica y creativa. Al combinar estos básicos con ingredientes frescos de la tierra, aseguramos una alimentación equilibrada que cuida tanto de nuestro organismo como del medio ambiente, evitando que la falta de previsión nos obligue a recurrir a opciones menos saludables.

No importa si te lanzas al ayuno intermitente o eliges otro método; si no quemas más de lo que comes, la báscula no se moverá.

Cómo hacer el arroz blanco perfecto: trucos y recetas fáciles

📅 Oct 11, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

El arroz blanco es, sin duda, uno de esos pilares fundamentales en la cocina de medio mundo. Mientras que en nuestra tierra solemos verlo como el acompañamiento ideal para un guiso o una carne, en las culturas asiáticas es la fuente principal de energía y el centro de su dieta diaria, funcionando casi como nuestro pan. Lograr que quede en su punto exacto, ni apelmazado ni crudo, es un arte que parece sencillo pero que tiene su miga.

A veces nos complicamos la vida buscando métodos rebuscados, cuando la clave reside en unos detalles técnicos muy precisos: la variedad del grano, la cantidad de agua y, sobre todo, el respeto absoluto por los tiempos de reposo. Si alguna vez has sentido que tu arroz parece un puré, no te preocupes, que con unos cuantos trucos de abuela y técnicas profesionales vas a conseguir que cada grano quede suelto y sabroso.

Antes de encender el fuego, hay pasos que marcan la diferencia entre un arroz mediocre y uno profesional. El primer gran truco es lavar el grano concienzudamente. Al pasar el arroz por agua fría varias veces hasta que esta deje de salir turbia, eliminamos el exceso de almidón, que es el principal culpable de que el arroz se pegue. Este paso es vital si buscas esa textura ligera típica de las ensaladas o el arroz asiático.

En cuanto a la elección del producto, aunque existen muchas opciones, el arroz bomba es una apuesta segura por su capacidad de absorción. No obstante, si prefieres aromas más exóticos o texturas distintas, el basmati o el jazmín funcionan de maravilla siguiendo procesos similares. Recuerda que factores externos como la altitud de tu ciudad o la dureza del agua pueden variar ligeramente el tiempo de cocción, así que no tengas miedo de ajustar un minuto más o menos según veas el grano.

Para que no haya pérdida, la regla de oro en las medidas es utilizar un vaso como referencia: calcula unos 100 gramos de arroz por persona y añade el doble de agua (dos vasos de agua por cada vaso de arroz). Si buscas un resultado más específico, algunos prefieren usar una proporción de 1,5 tazas de agua por cada taza de arroz de grano corto.

Si quieres salirte de lo convencional y darle un toque gourmet a tu guarnición, existen diversas formas de potenciar el gusto. La combinación clásica de ajo, laurel y aceite es infalible, pero puedes experimentar con una cebolla claveteada (pinchando clavos de olor en una cebolla pelada) sumergida en el agua, lo que aporta un aroma sofisticado y profundo.

Para quienes buscan sabores más internacionales, añadir un chorrito de salsa de soja al agua de cocción o una pizca de pasta de miso disuelta transforma el plato por completo. Otras alternativas efectivas incluyen sustituir el agua por un caldo casero, usar mantequilla en lugar de aceite para ganar untuosidad, o incluso añadir un chorrito de limón para aportar frescura y brillo al grano.

Una vez que tienes tu arroz perfecto, las posibilidades son infinitas. Es la base ideal para un arroz a la cubana con plátano frito, salteados de verduras estilo chino, o como acompañamiento para unos calamares en su tinta. También es excelente para preparar arroz tres delicias o rellenos de pimientos.

Si has cocinado de más, no pasa nada. El arroz blanco se conserva estupendamente en la nevera durante cuatro o cinco días, siempre que esté bien tapado para evitar que se seque. Solo tendrás que calentarlo ligeramente o usarlo directamente en ensaladas frías.

Dominar la cocción del arroz depende de equilibrar el lavado del grano, el rehogado previo en aceite aromatizado y el respeto estricto a los tiempos de cocción y reposo sin abrir la tapa. Ya sea optando por la sencillez del ajo y laurel o experimentando con caldos y especias, la clave está en controlar el almidón y la hidratación para lograr esa textura suelta y profesional que convierte una guarnición básica en el centro de la mesa.
