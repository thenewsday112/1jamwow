Basler Theaterleitung wandert nach München – Verlust für Kulturszene

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Basel erlebt einen erschütternden Verlust: Die gesamte Schauspielleitung des renommierten Theater Basel zieht nach München und hinterlässt eine Lücke in der Identität der Stadt als Kulturmetropole. Der Schritt des langjährigen Intendanten Benedikt...

Basels Theaterlandschaft erlebt einen erschütternden Einschnitt: Die gesamte Schauspielleitung des renommierten Theater Basel zieht nach München. Dieser unprecedenteden Schritt hinterlässt eine Lücke, die nicht nur personeller Art ist, sondern die fundamentale Identität der Stadt als Kulturmetropole infrage stellt. Dass die Basler Intendanz den Sprung in den bayerischen Freistaat wagt, wird als gewaltiger Verlust für die heimische Szene gewertet, während München von einem sofortigen Qualitätszuwachs profitiert.

Der Abschied der Führungsetage ist Ergebnis jahrelanger Spannungen. Benedikt von Peter, seit Jahren das Gesicht des Theater Basel, bestätigte den Schritt und verwies auf die Suche nach „neuen kreativen Strukturen“ im Münchner Umfeld. Die Basler Zeitung analysierte die Ursachen als Ergebnis einer „perspektivlosen“ Finanzsituation. Es geht um mehr als nur um Personalien: Es ist der Kampf um die Existenzberechtigung eines traditionsreichen Hauses in einem Umfeld, in dem die finanziellen Rahmenbedingungen immer enger werden. Die Entscheidung spiegelt einen strukturellen Wandel wider, der nicht nur Basel, sondern die gesamte deutsche Sprachbühne betrifft.

Betroffen sind 15 feste Positionen, darunter die künstlerische Leitung und fünf Senior-Dramaturginnen und -Dramaturgen. Dieser personelle Massenabgang hinterlässt an der Basler Theaterstraße sofort eine Lücke. Die Vertretung übernimmt zunächst kommissarische Funktionen, doch die Unsicherheit bei den verbliebenen Schauspielern und Mitarbeitern ist groß. Verträge laufen aus, Engagements sind plötzlich ungewiss. Die Belegschaft muss mit der Sorge konfrontiert werden, ob das Haus auch ohne seine intakte Führungsspitze noch die gewohnten Produktionen auf die Bühne bringen kann. Dies trifft gerade in einer Zeit hart, in der die Theater nach den Pandemiejahren um jede Zuschauerzahl und jede Förderung kämpfen.

Der Exodus folgt einem gut beobachtbaren Trend: Immer mehr deutsche Schauspiel- und Regietalente suchen ihr Glück in München. Die bayerische Landeshauptstadt genießt den Ruf, eine der stärksten Subventionslandschaften für darstellende Kunst zu besitzen. Während Basel mit Sparzwängen und knappen Haushalten hadert, punktet München mit stabilen Mitteln und der Möglichkeit, langfristige Projekte zu wagen. Dieser „Subventionswettbewerb“ verschiebt nun die Gewichte: München gewinnt nicht nur ein erprobtes Ensemble, sondern auch das nötige Know-how, um eigene Spitzenproduktionen zu entwickeln, ohne von vorne beginnen zu müssen. Basel hingegen muss befürchten, dass der Ruf als Zentrum der deutschen Sprachbühne an Glaubwürdigkeit verliert.

Für die Basler Kulturpolitik bricht eine kritische Zeit an. Es gilt, so schnell wie möglich kommissarische Nachfolge zu regeln, um einen kompletten Saisonabbruch zu verhindern. Kulturpolitische Experten warnen davor, dass der Verlust der Führungsetage das Ansehen Basels als Dreh- und Angelpunkt für deutschsprachiges Theater nachhaltig trüben könnte. Die Stadt steht vor der Aufgabe, nicht nur Ersatz zu finden, sondern ein Konzept zu entwickeln, das die Attraktivität für künstlerisches Personal langfristig sichert. Andernfalls droht ein „Brain Drain“, der die Basler Szene über Jahre hinweg schwächt.

Derweil profitiert München von dem Wechsel. Die Theatern der Stadt erhalten nicht nur erfahrene Führungskräfte, sondern auch ein Ensemble, das bereits zusammenarbeitet und etablierte Strukturen mitbringt. Das spart Einarbeitungszeit und ermöglicht einen sofortigen Start in anspruchsvolle Produktionen. Für die bayerische Landeshauptstadt ist der Fall Basels ein Gewinn, der jedoch das kulturelle Gleichgewicht in der Region verschiebt. Die Basler müssen nun aktiv werden: Ob durch erhöhte Fördermittel, neue Kooperationsmodelle oder eine Neuausrichtung des künstlerischen Profils – die Entscheidung in München zeigt schmerzhaft auf, was auf dem Spiel steht. Es bleibt abzuwarten, ob Basel die Talente halten kann oder ob der Exodus als Präzedenzfall für weitere Abgänge in die bayerische Metropole gelten wird.

Russia’s Sevmash Shipyard presents nuclear sub building innovative solution

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

MOSCOW, October 30. /TASS/. A representative of the Sevmash Shipyard, a subsidiary of the United Shipbuilding Corporation (USC), has presented an innovative solution for the construction of modern nuclear submarines at an international research and practical conference in Nizhny Novgorod, the shipyard’s press service reported.

The new solution will save an estimated 81 million rubles ($1.004 mln), it said.

"Sevmash Shipyard representative Igor Kalemintsev demonstrated an innovative development for the construction of fourth-generation nuclear submarines with an economic savings of over 81 million rubles at the international workshop titled ‘Modern Technologies in Shipbuilding and Aviation Education, Science, and Production.’ The event is taking place in Nizhny Novgorod from October 28-30," the press service said.

It specified that Kalemintsev, head of a department at the Sevmash Design Bureau, presented modern technologies for installing equipment and structures using polymer materials. The development includes a unique method for securing mechanisms to the foundation, protected by a Russian patent. He demonstrated practical solutions aimed at improving the efficiency of equipment installation, introducing modern materials, and optimizing production processes in the construction of fourth-generation missile-carrying submarines.

The press service emphasized that the practical application of this design and technological solution has already been confirmed by its successful use in the construction of Yasen-M and Borei-A class nuclear submarines at the shipyard. The technology has been approved by the USC’s Malakhit and Rubin design bureaus, as well as by the Defense Ministry’s Shipbuilding and Ship Repair Technology Center.

"This development significantly reduces the labor required for installation work and ensures the reliability of the structures. The innovative solutions implemented can also be applied to the construction of other vessels in the domestic shipbuilding industry. The assembly unit, which involves securing mechanisms to the foundation using flowable plastic, has been registered as an invention by the Federal Service for Intellectual Property and has been granted a patent. Labor savings per order range from 800 to 2,200 standard hours," the company stated.

Sevmash added that, due to the reduction in the labor intensity of installation work, the total economic benefit from using these components and technology in the construction of nuclear submarines exceeds 81 million rubles.

Canadian astronaut Joshua Kutryk successfully completes first of two spacewalks

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Astronaut Joshua Kutryk spent more than seven hours hovering in the vacuum of space on Thursday, making him the fifth Canadian to perform a spacewalk.

The International Space Station was some 420 kilometres above the Earth when Kutryk and NASA astronaut Anil Menon stepped out.

The duo placed their suits on battery power at 7:57 a.m. EDT, marking the official beginning of the spacewalk. They were back in the station at 3:08 p.m., after a total of seven hours and 11 minutes.

The astronauts successfully installed seven of eight new solar panel systems needed to upgrade the station’s power capacity.

Following the walk, fellow Crew 13 members Luke Delaney and Jessica Watkins helped Kutryk and Menon out of their equipment and checked their biomedical data.

After taking off his helmet, Kutryk wiped his face and Menon smiled. Kutryk is scheduled to speak to media Friday morning.

NASA mission control in Houston said the spacewalk was “essentially flawless.”

Before the spacewalkers left the airlock, their crewmates wished them luck.

“From Luke and I on the other side of the hatch, good luck. We’re proud of you and we’ll see you on the other side,” Watkins, a NASA flight engineer, told them.

Kutryk is on his first mission in space and his trip outside the space station is expected to be his first of two spacewalks.

Joshua Kutryk is on his first mission in space and his trip outside the space station was expected to be his first of two spacewalks.Supplied

Before Kutryk, the last Canadian to perform a spacewalk was David Saint-Jacques in 2019.

Saint-Jacques answered questions from schoolchildren at the Montreal Science Centre as a livestream showing the spacewalk was projected.

He described the feat as “one of the most exciting and one of most dangerous things we do” and said Kutryk was in “very good spirits” when they last spoke ahead of the spacewalk.

“It’s kind of the peak of the career for an astronaut to get a chance to put on your own miniature spacecraft that we call a space suit and go outside to do some maintenance work,” he said.

Saint-Jacques said spacewalkers train for thousands of hours in a giant pool and a full-size replica of the space station in Houston. They learn to operate the space suit and study the procedures and equipment needed for their mission.

They also receive step-by-step instructions in real time.

Canadian astronaut Joshua Kutryk shares first impressions from space

The crew’s mission is crucial, Saint-Jacques said, as the station is nearly 30 years old and its original solar panels have lost efficiency. According to the Canadian Space Agency, the new panels, paired with previously installed arrays, will help increase power generation capacity on the station by up to 30 per cent. The space station is fully electric, said Saint-Jacques.

While astronauts can face technical challenges, such as a stuck bolt or trouble plugging in a connector, the veteran astronaut said he didn’t doubt Kutryk’s abilities.

The advice he gave the spacewalker? To really take in the view.

“Give yourself the chance to look around, let it all sink in, and come back with some emotions as well, because that really is what you bring back for yourself,” he said. “It’s very easy to get focused in the work, to want to do a good job.”

Canada also played an important role in the mission from the ground.

From left: Astronauts Anil Menon of NASA and Kutryk of Canadian Space Agency.Supplied

While Watkins and Delaney, also a NASA flight engineer, assisted the spacewalkers with the Canadarm2 robotic arm, a Canadian Space Agency team was monitoring activities in real time from Longueuil, Que.

Joshua Kutryk’s successful flight to the ISS shifts focus to Canada’s next moves in space

Canadian astronaut Jenni Gibbons was supporting the spacewalkers from NASA’s Johnson Space Center in Houston. The Canadian Space Agency described her as “the voice bridging two worlds” as she was the main link between experts on Earth and the space crew.

Gibbons was giving the astronauts directions as they carried out the maintenance work. She also served as the capsule communicator during the Artemis II lunar flyby in April.

“Canada delivers. And so I’m very, very proud of our space sector,” said Saint-Jacques. “It’s a long history of success that we should all be very proud of . . . at the forefront of exploration, breaking silos, innovating.”

Kutryk and Menon departed from the Quest airlock module on the space station to install solar panels on the portside truss structure of the orbital outpost.

The truss serves as the station’s structural backbone and supports major systems, including solar wings, radiators, power, data and temperature-control equipment.

According to NASA, Thursday’s mission was the 285th spacewalk in support of the station’s assembly and maintenance. It was the United States’ 100th spacewalk.

Menon had performed three spacewalks before his mission with Kutryk.

The crew’s second spacewalk is scheduled for Nov. 5.

Trump celebrates Quebec separatist, B.C. Conservative election wins in social media posts

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

B.C. Conservative interim Leader Lorne Doerkson celebrates on stage during provincial election night in Vancouver, on Oct. 24. Doerkson has criticized Trump’s tariffs and called him a bully.ETHAN CAIRNS/The Canadian Press

U.S. President Donald Trump drew criticism Wednesday for celebrating election victories by a Quebec separatist and a British Columbia Conservative, days after his administration accused Canadian politicians of interfering in U.S. elections.

With U.S. midterm elections approaching, Trump shared an article portraying the Quebec victory as a setback for Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and hailed the Conservative win in British Columbia as evidence of the party’s growing strength.

But neither election was a referendum on Carney, whose federal Liberal Party is separate from British Columbia’s provincial Conservatives. Analysts say both results reflected provincial politics, not declining support for the prime minister.

“Congratulations to Paul St-Pierre Plamondon on his WIN!” Trump wrote Tuesday on Truth Social, more than three weeks after the Quebec separatist leader’s Oct. 5 election victory. Trump linked to an article headlined “Separatists win Quebec provincial election in blow to Mark Carney.”

Trump also congratulated British Columbia’s premier-designate, Lorne Doerkson, on his “Big Victory,” adding: “Conservatives are moving strong in Canada!” The post came one minute before his congratulations to Quebec’s separatist leader.

In some U.S. states, Trump’s trade war has made Canada an election issue

The posts come amid Trump’s trade war and repeated threats to make Canada the 51st U.S. state. St-Pierre Plamondon has criticized Trump’s tariffs and ruled out holding a Quebec independence referendum while Trump remains president.

Trump’s trade war with Canada has become an issue in the Nov. 3 midterm elections, particularly in Michigan and Maine, where Republican candidates have faced pressure over tariffs hurting local businesses. Michigan Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers has called for an end to the tariff war.

“It is highly unusual for a U.S. President to comment on Canadian provincial politics or to congratulate provincial election winners,” said Daniel Béland, a political science professor at McGill University in Montreal.

Parti Québécois Leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon speaks to supporters after winning the provincial election in Montreal on Oct. 5. Plamondon has criticized Trump’s tariffs and ruled out holding a Quebec independence referendum while Trump remains president.Graham Hughes/The Canadian Press

Béland said neither result should be interpreted as a rejection of Carney, whose popularity has been bolstered by his response to Trump.

Nelson Wiseman, a University of Toronto political scientist, said Trump misunderstands Canada’s Conservatives, noting that Doerkson has criticized Trump’s tariffs and called him a bully.

Doerkson spoke with Carney on Tuesday, with both leaders emphasizing co-operation on economic growth and investment.

“Carney’s popularity is at a stratospheric level,” Wiseman said. “The more Trump attacks Canada and Carney, the more popular Carney becomes.”

The U.S. Embassy in Ottawa last week accused Canadian politicians of seeking to influence the Nov. 3 midterms. It cited Industry Minister Mélanie Joly’s comments about targeting Republican states with retaliatory tariffs and Ontario Premier Doug Ford’s U.S. advertisement featuring former President Ronald Reagan criticizing tariffs.

The embassy also cited 11 Canadians charged with illegally voting in U.S. elections between 2012 and 2026, without linking the cases to the Canadian government.

Carney rejected the allegations, saying they had “no merit,” and expressed disappointment with U.S. Ambassador Pete Hoekstra.

Asked Wednesday whether the Trump administration was setting up Canada to be the fall guy if Republicans suffer heavy losses in the U.S. midterms, Carney laughed.

“That’s an interesting way of framing it,” Carney said, before adding that Canada would pursue a trade deal with Washington only when it served Canadian interests.

Béland warned that the accusations could foreshadow a “Blame Canada” narrative if Republicans suffer losses in the midterms.

“It is absurd for President Trump to comment on Canadian provincial politics while the U.S. embassy in Canada is falsely claiming that country is interfering in the U.S. midterms,” Béland said.

Doerkson vows to prioritize B.C.’s finances, work toward West Coast pipeline as next premier

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

B.C. premier-designate Lorne Doerkson arrives for a post-election news conference in Vancouver on Sunday.DARRYL DYCK/The Canadian Press

British Columbia’s next premier, a former rodeo rider and newspaper publisher who many people hadn’t heard of five weeks ago, says his first priorities will be taking an in-depth look at the province’s deeply indebted finances, and working with Alberta and Prime Minister Mark Carney to jump-start the planned oil pipeline to the West Coast.

BC Conservative Leader Lorne Doerkson won a large majority government Saturday in a snap election called two years early by NDP Leader David Eby.

“British Columbians voted for change last night and for whatever reason, they liked the platform that we put forward,” Mr. Doerkson said at a news conference Sunday. “I intend to get to work right away.”

The loss has the BC NDP bitterly regretting the high-stakes gamble of calling the election, the timing of which appeared aimed at taking advantage of the disarray in the Conservative caucus. The Conservatives had elected a deeply unpopular leader in the spring, but she resigned two days before Mr. Eby made the election call.

Mr. Doerkson was named interim leader and was soon thrown into a campaign, uniting a fractious caucus behind him.

Lorne Doerkson didn’t plan on leading the BC Conservatives, but he’s trying to meet the moment

NDP MLA and cabinet minister Adrian Dix, who was re-elected, called Saturday’s result one of the most disappointing nights in the modern history of the BC NDP. After nine years of government under the leadership of John Horgan and Mr. Eby, the party is heading into opposition.

“Clearly, given the results, it was a mistake to call the election,” Mr. Dix told reporters after the results came in.

Mike Farnworth, who has served as B.C. transportation minister and was also narrowly re-elected, agreed calling the snap election was a bad idea.

“In hindsight, I don’t think it’s rocket science to say we probably shouldn’t have,” he said in an interview Sunday.

He said the caucus and the party will have to take time to reflect on the outcome and what comes next.

B.C. Conservatives win majority government, Lorne Doerkson to become premier

Mr. Eby resigned as party leader in a somber speech Saturday night, accepting responsibility for the outcome of the vote. He said he would continue to represent his riding.

“This is obviously something I do with a very heavy heart,” he said to supporters at his party’s gathering in Vancouver. “But it is time for new leadership within our party.”

The results with 99 per cent of the polls reporting showed 64 Conservatives elected, 27 for the NDP and two for the Greens. The NDP lost 20 seats compared to the party’s result in the last election in 2024.

At his news conference Sunday, Mr. Doerkson listed some of his first priorities.

He said briefings with the public service will begin in coming days with “some urgency” so the government will be able to swiftly advance its agenda once it’s sworn in.

He said after being sworn in, he would immediately launch an independent review of the province’s finances, citing the record-high $14-billion deficit and the need to find a way to bring it to balance eventually.

Other initiatives include an expert commission on hospital emergency rooms and wait times, as well as a tax break aimed at returning $1,000 to every British Columbian.

He said he hopes to engage with Mr. Carney and Alberta Premier Danielle Smith to get the Pacific Link pipeline, between Alberta and B.C., moving.

Without being specific, Mr. Doerkson said he has been “thrilled” about recent announcements by the Prime Minister. “We’ll work very closely with him, and of course, our neighbours.”

Mr. Doerkson said Sunday that he hopes to use a session of the B.C. legislature next spring to repeal an Indigenous rights law passed unanimously by the legislature in 2019, the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act, or DRIPA. Critics worry the province is at risk of many lawsuits if Indigenous groups believe the province’s laws are not in alignment with DRIPA.

“I am still committed to the repeal of DRIPA. It has not worked in this province, and, frankly, it has caused more problems to date than, I think, potentially anything else,” he said.

B.C. law on Indigenous rights has increased conflict and uncertainty, municipalities group hears

The premier-designate said he looks forward to working with First Nations communities in the province, noting that he has received complimentary messages from some of those communities.

He said he had not ruled out holding a session of the legislature this fall.

Former Alberta premier Jason Kenney said Mr. Doerkson’s win offers lessons that other Conservatives should take to heart.

“Yet another reminder to Conservative parties: ignore the crazy fringe of the hard populist right. Don’t let the tail wag the dog. Let them stay in their little fringe parties,” Mr. Kenney posted on social media Saturday night.

“Instead, propose bold, thoughtful policy reforms rooted in conservative principles that appeal to the broad mainstream,” Mr. Kenney wrote.

Manitoba NDP Premier Wab Kinew said the B.C. results should be a warning for other politicians and consultants looking to eke out tactical advantages without considering voters themselves.

“It is a serious responsibly that isn’t to be played with. There’s no partisanship in these comments, it’s all about reminding people that we have a serious obligation to take our democracy and our duties with the utmost of care.”

Voter turnout appears to have played a significant role in Saturday night’s outcome.

Elections B.C. reported Sunday afternoon only 48 per cent of eligible voters cast a ballot, a record low.

While the Conservatives took nearly half of all the votes cast, they netted fewer actual votes than in the 2024 election, as did the NDP.

B.C. election turnout slumps to new low as fewer than half of voters cast ballot

The Conservatives recorded about 5 per cent fewer votes than in 2024, while the Greens took home roughly 13 per cent more. But the NDP took by far the biggest hit, with 274,000 fewer votes, a drop of 29 per cent.

Stewart Prest, a political science lecturer at the University of B.C., said that trend suggests a big chunk of the NDP’s base stayed home, and it cost the party the election.

Mr. Eby had tried at first to frame the election around the need to repudiate what he described as a Conservative Party too closely aligned with the U.S. politics of culture war issues and division, Mr. Prest said.

But “voters generally don’t like being told what to do,” Mr. Prest said. “Being told you have to show up two years after you cast a vote to do it again to prove a point, I think that was a difficult sell with NDP supporters.”

B.C. election turnout slumps to new low as fewer than half of voters cast ballot

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Voters arrive at a polling place in Vancouver on Saturday. Turnout for B.C.'s provincial election was about 48 per cent, according to preliminary results.Justine Beaulieu-Poudrier/The Globe and Mail

Voter turnout in B.C. hit a new low in Saturday’s provincial election as fewer than half of registered voters cast a ballot, an indication, observers say, that many voters rejected the rationale for the snap vote.

According to preliminary results from Elections BC, about 48 per cent of voters cast a ballot, which would be the lowest level recorded going back as far as 1928. In 2024, voter turnout was about 58.5 per cent, and in 2020, it was about 53.9 per cent.

The stunning electoral turnaround that saw the Conservatives, led by interim party leader and now premier-designate Lorne Doerkson, sweep the incumbent NDP government from office was a remarkable rebuke for outgoing Premier David Eby.

With two years left in his mandate and with an opposition party that was without a permanent leader and in disarray, Mr. Eby called the vote on Sept. 22 with his party leading in the polls. Voters, however, did not seem persuaded that an election was necessary, despite Mr. Eby’s efforts to make it about winning a new mandate to tackle the U.S. trade war.

On Saturday, they punished the government, electing a sizable Conservative majority and ending a nine-year run in charge for the NDP that began in 2017 under premier John Horgan, who died in 2024.

Geoff Meggs, who was chief of staff to Mr. Horgan, said it appears that many of the party’s voters simply stayed home.

“Dave Eby’s campaign angered them. They were never persuaded by his arguments in favour of an election,” Mr. Meggs said.

He said in some ways it’s not surprising that the party’s support took a hit. The NDP campaign, when it eventually pivoted away from the trade argument for the election, proposed complex policies focused on affordability, such as one to regulate grocery prices, that didn’t capture the imagination of voters.

And while Mr. Eby raised fears about what the Conservatives would do if they won power, he didn’t clearly underline what his government had accomplished, according to some political observers.

“He didn’t campaign on the very real achievements of the NDP over the years since 2017 and proposed things that didn’t resonate with British Columbians,” Mr. Meggs said.

The NDP received about 274,000 fewer votes than in the previous election, while the Conservatives got about 49,000 fewer. The Green Party increased its tally by about 23,000.

Gary Mason: Political hubris claims another victim

The low turnout for the provincial election is in contrast with relatively strong turnout during the recent municipal elections. In Vancouver, municipal voter turnout was up seven percentage points for the Oct. 17 vote at 43.4 per cent, compared with 36.3 per cent in 2022. Across the province, municipal-election turnout was 32.8 per cent, according to the Union of B.C. Municipalities, up by about three percentage points from four years ago.

Allan Tupper, a professor emeritus of political science at the University of British Columbia, said the timing of the two elections, just one week apart, was odd and may have had an impact on how many votes were cast. With two sets of signs on local lawns and campaigns competing for often overlapping pools of volunteers, citizens may have experienced some election fatigue.

“It just seemed there was no energy for this thing,” Prof. Tupper said. “I think Eby didn’t size it up right. The opposition organized itself very quickly.”

In the Quebec provincial election of Oct. 5, turnout was a relatively high 67 per cent, while the Alberta referendum of Oct. 19, which asked whether to hold a vote on separation, had preliminary turnout of more than 62 per cent.

Duff Conacher, co-founder of Democracy Watch, a non-profit that works on democratic reform, said studies show that voter turnout is high when voters believe something important is at stake, when the leaders appear viable and competent and the race appears close.

Turnout was above 70 per cent in B.C. in all the elections held from 1983 to 2001 and hasn’t been higher than about 62 per cent since.

Mr. Conacher said it’s a worrying sign that voter participation appears to have dipped below 50 per cent this time, but it’s not surprising given recent trends.
