Dating app users should first vet the platforms they register with, making sure they agree with how data is collected and processed [File] | Photo Credit: REUTERS

Earlier in October, U.S. lawmakers revealed how users of dating apps were being exposed to serious financial and physical risks. They pinned this to the outdated AI stack in these applications.

In an investigation centred around the Match Group, the lawmakers found that the algorithm matched U.S. users of dating apps like Tinder and Hinge with scammers. The report, titled ‘Outmatched: Tinder, Scammers, and Match Group’s Efforts to Catch Up’, highlighted that users connected with nearly 1.5 million scammers on these two popular dating applications in the first nine months of 2025.

At the core of these disturbing findings were outdated machine learning models installed to detect bad actors. These hadn’t been updated in years, per the report.

This information came from internal documents that the Match Group shared with the committee, led by senator Maggie Hassan. As the accuracy of scam detection dropped, Tinder began depending on other measures that it knew were often ineffective.

“In fact, Match Group knew that Tinder was more likely to show a profile if it belonged to a scammer or other bad actor rather than a regular user,” stated an official press release about the report.

However, the investigation acknowledged that the Match Group later put in place new safety measures, such as an AI model in Tinder this year that was designed to detect scammers and bad actors.

The report included statements from victims who described how romance scammers cheated them out of tens of thousands of dollars after building a relationship with them on the dating apps. A common trend was to cite a major emergency in order to pressure the victim into sending over money. Using the victims’ phone number and/or email ID, the scammers would proceed to harass them online until the victim sometimes paid them in the hopes of de-escalating their aggression.

Another type of scam involved the same malicious user operating as different people on the same dating app, to ensnare more victims.

The report pointed to an inconsistent dating app AI strategy, where patchy machine learning systems led to heavy caseloads and “overwhelmed” human moderators who were unable to focus on visibly risky accounts. Per a document from the Match Group itself, Tinder could have better tackled dangerous accounts by building better AI signals or reducing its reliance on manual rules.

“In practice, these moderation lapses meant that bad actors that reached moderation were not immediately banned and instead may have continued to interact with and harm legitimate users until the platform gathered additional evidence,” explained the report.

A bouquet of dating apps come under the Match Group, including Tinder, Hinge, Match (formerly Match.com), Plenty of Fish, and more such offerings catering to users in diverse countries and cultural communities.

In other words, a security lapse in one app comes with serious implications for the users of other Match Group-owned apps. Tinder and Hinge are popular in India as well, claiming the top ranks in terms of downloads via app marketplaces.

According to the Match Group, Hinge is expected to reach $1 billion in revenue in 2027, while Tinder is expanding its in-person events that are already live in the U.S. and Europe.

Indian users often register with apps that suggest profiles and tout AI-powered matchmaking as a selling point. However, as the U.S. committee report noted, cutting-edge AI tools require constant upkeep, testing, and upgrades in order to ensure customers remain shielded from emerging threats.

Regulators must also play a larger role in monitoring dating app services.

Dating app users should first vet the platforms they register with, making sure they agree with how data is collected and processed. Users should also be aware of the reporting options they have in case an online or offline interaction turns unsafe. Customers can research the apps’ founders, investors, and brand ambassadors, to make sure there is an alignment of values.

Dating app users should promptly report fraudulent user profiles and cease interacting with them. AI-generated user photos and videos, even when posted as a joke, often violate platform policies.

Another red flag is a dating app user who becomes intimate too quickly or is eager to discuss finances early on. Furthermore, scammers often pressure dating app users to contact them on external sites, so they can view the user’s phone number or harvest other personal details.

Users must withhold personal phone numbers, email IDs, home addresses, private social media channels, and employer information until they meet a match in-person and feel comfortable proceeding.

Updated - October 26, 2026 11:37 am IST - Beijing

Chinese President Xi Jinping. File | Photo Credit: AP

China’s President Xi Jinping opened a key meeting of Communist Party elites in Beijing on Monday (October 26, 2026), state media said, which observers will be closely watching for clues as to the country’s direction and future leadership.

Roughly 300 members of the ruling Communist Party’s Central Committee in attendance at the meeting, called the fifth plenum, will be looking to tighten discipline within their ranks, according to state media.

That includes measures to reduce corruption and make cadres more effective and loyal.

Mr. Xi opened the session on Monday (October 26, 2026), presenting a draft decision by the Central Committee that “sets out strategic plans for strengthening full and rigorous Party self-governance”, state news agency Xinhua reported.

Few details from the closed-door gathering, held under tight security, will be made known until it concludes on Thursday (October 29, 2026), but the timing of the meeting is crucial.

The fifth plenum comes ahead of a major party congress next year, where 73-year-old Mr. Xi is almost certain to secure a historic fourth term and oversee a leadership reshuffle.

Previous plenums have represented key moments for Mr. Xi to cement his authority.

The Chinese leader has waged a 14-year campaign against corruption that has purged thousands of officials, including dozens of the Central Committee.

Yet he remains frustrated with cadres he deems are not implementing his orders.

State media published an article ahead of the plenum, based on a speech Mr. Xi gave in January in which he railed against “two-faced” officials who appear to comply with central directives but “inwardly resist”, pursue “individualism” and are “politically opportunistic”.

Analysts told AFP that Mr. Xi is trying to maximise efficiency and loyalty among cadres by imposing more supervision and rules.

But as a result of his expansive purges, this year’s plenum is expected to have the lowest turnout since former leader Mao Zedong’s death in 1976.

Eight of the Central Committee’s seats lie vacant, with another 15 members under investigation and at risk of being replaced.

Mr. Xi’s anti-corruption drive has also targeted the military, toppling five of the seven members of the Central Military Commission (CMC), the party’s top armed forces body.

Of 44 military members appointed to the Central Committee in 2022, only two have not been purged or disappeared, according to the Asia Society Policy Institute.

The plenum could see new members appointed to the CMC; the party could wait until next year’s congress to replenish it; or Xi might even restructure party control over its vast military.

The talks also come at an unsteady time for the world’s second-largest economy, which has struggled to address persistently sluggish domestic spending and a protracted crisis in the crucial property sector.

Turbulent political and economic ties with the United States are also weighing on China’s leaders.

Authorities are expected to release a summary of the outcomes after the meeting concludes on Thursday (October 29, 2026).

Israel's markets have soared despite three years of war and political turmoil, but ​some investors see further upside if the Opposition defeats Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in Tuesday’s (October 27, 2026) election, potentially easing regional tensions and political risk.

Opinion polls point to a ‌tight contest, with neither Mr. Netanyahu's ruling Likud bloc nor an opposition alliance centred on former military chief Gadi Eisenkot expected ​to secure the 61 seats in the 120-seat Knesset needed to govern.

That raises the prospect of lengthy coalition negotiations or even ⁠another election. Since the October 7 Hamas attacks in 2023, the Tel Aviv stock market has soared 130%, far outpacing Wall Street's 80% gain, while the shekel has climbed 25%, one of the world's strongest currency performances.

Analysts attribute some of that strength to Israel's tech sector and the global AI boom, while defence spending, which ‌has nearly doubled to around 8% of GDP, has also provided a boost.

With both Mr. Netanyahu and Mr. Eisenkot vowing to maintain pressure on Hamas and Iran, markets are focused on their broader policy differences. JPMorgan estimates the shekel could swing as much as ‌3% depending on the result.

An opposition victory that improves relations with Western allies and halts a judicial overhaul that sparked mass protests in ‌2023 ⁠could strengthen the currency, JPMorgan said, while another Netanyahu-led government could weaken it.

Billionaire investor Bill Ackman, while stopping short of backing ⁠a candidate, said a government without far-right ministers Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich would signal that Israel was "turning a new page”, potentially helping rebuild its international credibility and expand the Abraham Accords with its neighbours.

IBI Investment House also expects an Opposition victory to lift the markets. "From a capital markets perspective, this would likely be reflected in a decline in Israel's risk ​premium, appreciation pressure on the shekel, and a positive reaction in the ‌local market," IBI's Chief Economist Rafi Gozlan said.

"The most significant issue is what the result will mean for the region," said Equity Bank's chief economic adviser Charlie Robertson. "If Netanyahu is reelected it might increase the chances of further escalation with Iran, whereas a defeat might be seen as lessening the tensions."

But the prospect of gains under an Opposition government does not mean investors expect Israeli assets to suffer if Mr. Netanyahu ‌remains in power. Stocks and bonds have proved remarkably resilient through years of war and political upheaval, and some investors remain confident ​in the economy regardless of the election outcome.

Union Investment's Sergey Dergachev has been buying Israeli bonds ahead of the election, betting the economy and companies he favours will remain fundamentally strong. "We are very constructive on Israeli credits going into the election," Mr. Dergachev ⁠said, adding that bonds were less prone to major swings and the region's intertwined conflicts should eventually ease.

Despite the rally, foreign investors remain cautious. Copley Fund Research data show the share of global funds holding Israeli equities is at a record low, having fallen sharply since 2023.

T. S. Lombard's Christopher Granville cited ‌central bank estimates that the war had cost the economy more than $55 billion by last year, equivalent to 8.6% in cumulative lost GDP growth.

Israel's fiscal position is another focus. Public debt has risen to nearly 70% of GDP, around 10 percentage points higher than in 2022, while the budget deficit is expected to remain around 4.5% of GDP, versus less than 2% before the conflict.

Central bank head Amir Yaron stressed last week the need to "preserve adequate fiscal policy space" given the Iran war.

Like stocks, Israeli government bonds have rallied, returning about 30% over the last two years, more than double the main emerging market indexes.

The main risk for investors is an inconclusive result that prolongs political uncertainty, delaying budgets, reforms and investment, some investors ‌believe.

That uncertainty can produce sharp short-term swings. Tel Aviv Stock Exchange research covering the last 10 elections found volatility in leading share indexes rose by as much as ​45% in the weeks after voting compared with a typical week. But initial losses were typically recovered within a month, with the indexes posting an average gain of about 1.7%.

Over a longer horizon, the TA-125 returned an average 16.4% in the year ⁠following elections, compared with a long-term annual average of 11.5%, the research found. Political deadlock often weighed on stocks before voting, but the effects typically faded within months, ⁠said Nissan Avraham, an analyst at the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange's research unit.

That record supports the view that elections matter less for Israeli companies than many investors assume.

Lior Yochpaz, chief investment officer at insurance company Menora Mivtachim, said businesses have continued to thrive despite ‌years of political upheaval, judicial overhaul proposals and regional conflict, helped by a relatively business-friendly environment.

For some investors, however, the more attractive election trade lies outside Israel. TT International's Jean-Charles Sambor said an Opposition victory or weakened Netanyahu government could ease regional tensions and benefit Lebanese bonds.

"The best way ​to play it for us is through other West Asia countries, and for us Lebanon is a good proxy," Mr. Sambor said.

İran: Mekke İttifakı üyesi ülkelerin üst düzey isimleri Tahran'ı ziyaret edecek

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

İran Ulusal Güvenlik Yüksek Konseyi Genel Sekreter Yardımcısı​​​​​​​ Ali Bakıri, İran televizyonuna yaptığı açıklamada, Batı Asya'da yerel güvenlik düşüncesinin uygulanması kapsamında Mekke İttifakı üyesi Suudi Arabistan, Pakistan ve Türkiye'den üst düzey yetkililerin Tahran'ı ziyaret edeceğini ileri sürdü.

Tahran'daki toplantıya Suudi Arabistan Savunma Bakanı Prens Halid bin Selman, Pakistan Genelkurmay Başkanı Asım Münir ve üst düzey bir Türk yetkilinin katılacağını öne süren Bakıri, söz konusu isimlerin İran Ulusal Güvenlik Yüksek Konseyi Genel Sekreteri Muhsin Rızai ile görüşme gerçekleştireceklerini söyledi.

Görüşmede bölgesel güvenlik konularının ele alınacağını dile getiren Bakıri, İran'ın dış güçlerin bölgeye müdahalesine ve nüfuzuna karşı olduğunu ifade etti.​​​​​​​

Bakıri, görüşmenin tarihine ilişkin bilgi vermezken, İran basını ise bugün gerçekleştirileceğini yazdı.
