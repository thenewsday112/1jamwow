Met Police won't enforce breaches of some tag conditions as it struggles to cope with early release scheme

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Published: 16:38 GMT, 25 October 2026 | Updated: 16:43 GMT, 25 October 2026

Britain's largest police force will not enforce some breaches of criminals' tagging conditions as it struggles to cope with Labour's early release scheme.

The Metropolitan Police is running a pilot scheme under which officers would ignore when some offenders breach their probationary conditions unless the criminal is deemed to pose a risk to the public.

More than 4,500 inmates, including violent criminals, will be eligible for early release over the next ten months under Labour's soft-justice programme.

As well as thieves and fraudsters, they include domestic abusers and criminals convicted of certain violent or sexual offences – raising fears for the safety of victims.

Serious criminals can now be released in half of their sentence, as opposed to two-thirds under previous rules in a government bid to deal with prison overcrowding.

Now, the Met is expected to reduce the level of enforcement for tag breaches by more than 15 per cent.

This means that if an offender breaches their conditions, by getting home after their curfew for example, police may not do anything about it.

Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley previously said that he was 'deeply worried' about the early release of thousands of convicts onto Britain's streets.

He said that Met officers were now policing over 100 tag breaches a day as more criminals stream out of prison early.

The force's pilot scheme has made clear that breach notifications should still be reviewed, but that action would only be taken if there was a risk to the public.

The move was done in an effort to reduce pressure on the Met's resources, according to Sky News.

But while some breaches can be explained by an offender going out for a cigarette or going to hospital, some offenders brazenly ignore their bail conditions.

In other cases, lags in the tagging system mean that authorities aren't notified for a number of hours after a breach has occurred – in which time a crime could be committed.

The Government said this pilot applies to offenders, or alleged offenders awaiting trial, that have been bailed by the courts.

The news came as it was revealed that tents are being handed out to early release prisoners after the Probation Service ran out of housing.

Around 100 tents were reportedly given to prisoners who were released but had nowhere to live as the number of released offenders surged under Andy Burnham's early release scheme.

It was also reported that probation officers had asked charities for tents so they could give them out to released prisoners.

Berkshire-based charity The Inside Out Trust worked with probation services to provide tents, cooking facilities and sleeping bags, it was claimed.

Prisoners who are at risk of homelessness can be offered up to 12 weeks of temporary accommodation.

But probation officers have been under increased pressure to find places after more prisoners were released after Mr Burnham restarted Labour's early release programme.

A probation officer reportedly said that they would usually have a year to find an offender a place to live but that this had been cut down to a few weeks as a result of the scheme.

Almost one in six prisoners are left homeless as they are released and the number of released offenders entering rough sleeping has more than doubled since 2022-23.

Nick Timothy, the shadow justice secretary, told The Telegraph: 'Labour's shambolic early release scheme is putting thousands of criminals back on to the streets, and their chaotic lack of planning means many released prisoners are now sleeping in tents.

'Angela Rayner promised to get all rough sleepers off the streets by Christmas, but early release will make the problem even worse.'

He added: 'We know rough sleeping makes reoffending more likely, so this once again shows that Labour's early release crisis is putting public safety at risk.'

A Met spokesman said: 'We understand the pressures across the criminal justice system. To best protect the public, those pressures cannot always fall on policing and certainly not without prioritisation. Offenders who are high-risk, such as domestic abusers are a priority.

'The Met continues to respond to more than 100 tagging breaches a day, many of which are low-level breaches. In the type of cases where breaches have previously not resulted in any consequence through the court system, we are working with partners to explore the most appropriate penalty or consequence, so that police time can be focused where it has the greatest impact.

'That means freeing up officers to focus on work that better protects the public, including pursuing offenders responsible for high-risk breaches. Where offenders have briefly stepped outside for a cigarette, or police are notified 19 hours later that they were 15 minutes late returning home, tying up several officers for hours is not the best use of resources. Where there is a risk to the public, particularly if someone is breaching conditions designed to protect a victim, we will absolutely respond.'

A Government spokesman said: 'We inherited a prison system on the brink of collapse. If we had failed to act, the police would have been forced to halt arrests, putting communities at untold risk.

'We will never jeopardise public safety. That is why we are putting thousands more Neighbourhood Police officers onto our streets, upping police funding to £18.5 billion and taking investment in the probation service to record levels.

'It is not Government policy to provide tents to people being released from prison who are at risk of being homeless. Tents are not, and should never be, an acceptable housing solution. We're helping those leaving prison to secure accommodation and move into work, as we work to halve prison leaver homelessness by the end of this Parliament, reduce reoffending, and keep the public safe.'

Labour's early release scheme started in September 2024 with the aim of easing overcrowding in jails

Last month it was revealed that fitting tags on offenders was taking up to five months, according to a report by His Majesty's Inspectorate of Probation (HMIP).

Only 55 per cent of the ankle tags that track location were fitted on time, with some criminals going unmonitored for five months.

Andy Burnham said in the Commons in September that he understood the 'anguish' bereaved families faced as a result of changes to the early prisoner release programme, but he stopped short of apologising to them.

A total of 27,847 people were given tagging orders with equipment in England and Wales as of June 2026.

Experts warned earlier this month that ankle tags were 'too slow' to protect domestic abuse, sexual assault and stalking victims from their attackers.

Currently, if a criminal wearing an ankle tag breaches their exclusion zones, probation officers are just sent an email – meaning nothing will be done until the following day if the alert is sent out of working hours.

As thousands of convicts are due to be set loose early from October under Labour's early release scheme, watchdogs have said these alerts could come 'too late' for those living in fear of their abusers.

In the last six months, suspects breaching their tag conditions have already rocketed by nearly 50 per cent in London alone, from 2,977 in January to 4,408 in July.

Martin Jones, chief inspector of probation for England and Wales, warned that 'expanding tagging programmes without addressing their weaknesses risks overpromising and underdelivering on public protection'.

Mr Jones added: 'At present, the electronic monitoring is too clunky and reactive. We need a shift to smoother, proactive management.

'I know the Metropolitan Police have voiced very similar concerns around the need for faster real-time information.

'If it is to command public confidence, we need electronic monitoring systems that can be used to provide faster responses where they are needed.'

Unter britischer Führung: Bericht: Airbus prüft Einstieg in internationales Kampfjet-Projekt

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Nach dem Scheitern des FCAS-Projekts prüft Airbus laut einem Medienbericht Kooperationsgespräche mit dem britischen Rüstungskonzern BAE Systems. Ziel ist die gemeinsame Entwicklung eines Tarnkappen-Mehrzweck-Kampfflugzeugs.

Der europäische Luft- und Raumfahrtkonzern Airbus lotet einem Medienbericht zufolge eine Beteiligung am internationalen Kampfjet-Projekt GCAP aus. Dazu sollen sich Führungskräfte von Airbus mit Vertretern des britischen Rüstungskonzerns BAE Systems treffen, wie die Zeitung "Financial Times" unter Berufung auf mit der Angelegenheit vertraute Personen berichtete. Airbus und BAE kommentierten den Medienbericht bisher nicht.

Das europäische Rüstungsprojekt Future Combat Air System (FCAS) wurde im Juni 2026 nach langjährigen Differenzen zwischen Deutschland, Frankreich und Spanien in seiner ursprünglichen Form beendet. Die Entwicklung des gemeinsamen bemannten Kampfjets der sechsten Generation wurde offiziell aufgegeben. Während Teilbereiche wie die digitale Vernetzung und Begleitdrohnen fortgeführt werden, prüfen die Partnerländer nun alternative Allianzen wie das britisch-geführte Konkurrenzprojekt GCAP. Dabei wäre auch eine Zusammenarbeit mit Schweden und Saab vorstellbar.

Bei dem Airbus-Treffen sollen dem Bericht zufolge auch alternative Bereiche für eine Zusammenarbeit erörtert werden. Dazu zählten autonome "kollaborative Kampfflugzeuge" sowie die sogenannte "Combat Cloud". Dieses digitale Rückgrat soll den Datenaustausch zwischen Jets, Drohnen und Sensoren wie Bodenradar ermöglichen.

Das Rüstungsprojekt GCAP (Global Combat Air Programme) wurde 2022 von Italien, Japan und Großbritannien ins Leben gerufen. Ziel ist die gemeinsame Entwicklung eines Tarnkappen-Mehrzweck-Kampfflugzeugs, das um das Jahr 2035 in Dienst gestellt werden soll.

Der britische Premierminister Andy Burnham hatte Anfang des Monats erklärt, Großbritannien sei offen für einen Beitritt Deutschlands zu dem von BAE, Leonardo und Mitsubishi Heavy Industries geleiteten Programm. Im Juli war Kanada dem Projekt als Beobachter beigetreten, was den Weg für eine tiefergehende Beteiligung ebnen könnte. Auch Saudi-Arabien wird als möglicher Partner gehandelt.

England lose to Pakistan after improbable last-wicket stand

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Haris Rauf (back) and Shadab Khan (front) shared a record 10th-wicket partnership to defeat England

England 241 (46.4 overs): Duckett 105 (111), Shaheen 4-46

Pakistan 243-9 (47.1 overs): Shadab 113* (96), Overton 3-43

England somehow lost another one-day international from a seemingly unbeatable position thanks to an improbable last-wicket stand by Pakistan in Lahore.

Three days after conceding 430 to lose to Sri Lanka, Harry Brook's side were beaten despite reducing Pakistan to 154-9 in pursuit of 242.

Shadab Khan was the engineer of Pakistan's unlikely win, striking a quite brilliant unbeaten 113 - the highest score by a number eight in an ODI.

He was supported by Haris Rauf, Pakistan's number 11 contributing seven to the unbroken 10th-wicket partnership of 89 - comfortably a new record for a last-wicket pair to win an ODI.

In barely believable scenes, Shadab was given out lbw to Gus Atkinson on 97, with 21 runs still required.

The players shook hands before Shadab called for a speculative review. DRS found a tiny inside edge and, reprieved, he watched Rauf belt debutant Henry Crocombe down the ground for the winning boundary with 17 balls to spare.

It means England are all but out of the tri-series - their exit will be confirmed if Sri Lanka beat Pakistan on Tuesday.

A second implosion in as many matches is hugely concerning for England, who looked to be on course for victory despite being bowled out for only 241.

Ben Duckett made a fine 105 - only the opener, Sam Curran and Gus Atkinson passing 20 in blameless conditions.

Nevertheless, England were well on course thanks to a collectively relentless display from their bowlers. Jamie Overton bowled his 10 overs in one spell for 3-43.

Then came Shadab and another baffling defeat for England, who could be out before they play their final game against Sri Lanka on Thursday.

Unbelievable last-wicket stand beats England

With less than a year to go to the World Cup, this tri-series was designed to give England experience of the pressure and permutations of tournament play.

In a matter of days, they have gone through two extreme situations, both ending in crushing defeats. What linked both matches was England's powerlessness to salvage the situation once the momentum turned against them.

If anything, the finish to this match was more dramatic than Sri Lanka pulling off the second-highest chase in ODI history.

Even Shadab was convinced he was out when he was hit on the shin by Atkinson's low full toss - he called for the hopeful review as he was walking off the field. When it came back in his favour, he put his head in his hands and was embraced by Rauf.

In the heat of the tight finish, Rauf had to be pulled away from a confrontation with England's Will Jacks when a Sam Curran bouncer was not called wide, while Rauf could also have been run out by Tom Banton's flick from mid-on when 18 were still required.

Shadab expertly farmed the strike - Rauf faced only 19 deliveries in a stand that lasted 80 balls.

Rauf defended his first 18 balls then cut loose to his 19th, hammering Crocombe for four then running away and throwing his bat in the air to begin the joyous celebrations.

What must England do to win a match in this tri-series? They have lost all three of their matches, the past two in extraordinary circumstances.

Yes, England are without key players Jofra Archer, Jos Buttler and Jacob Bethell on this tour, while Adil Rashid has been omitted from the XI and Josh Tongue missed out with a toe injury.

Still, what they are learning is they do not respond well in tight situations. On Thursday, the batters broke records, only to be let down by the bowlers. On Sunday, the bowlers were on course to mask the deficiencies of the batters, then wilted when it mattered.

This loss means encouraging performances from Duckett and Overton will be forgotten. For Duckett, this was a much-needed score at time when questions have been asked about his position. Overton was superb, the first England seamer to bowl 10 consecutive overs in an ODI since 2017.

But, when England needed inspiration to take the last wicket, they were found wanting. Maybe the pace of Archer or Tongue, or mystery of Rashid would have made the difference.

They crowded the bat for Rauf, tried to defend against Shadab and held countless meetings. Heads dropped, plans failed and England were not good enough.

'We've been given two good lessons' - McCullum defiant after England loss

To focus only on England does a disservice to Shadab, who played a knock for the ages, one of the greatest by a Pakistan batter in an ODI.

The all-rounder had been left out of the first two matches of this series. After bowling seven overs with his leg-spin, he arrived to bat when his team were in disarray at 103-6, then left as a hero.

Shadab was unflappable in the face of such a monumental task, supported by the fiery Rauf. In a 96-ball stay, Shadab expertly picked his moments to attack, pouncing when England erred. He hit five sixes and 13 fours.

Rauf batted for 70 minutes, more than two times his previous longest stay in an ODI. Only once before had he faced more deliveries.

As tension rose, so did the tempers - Joe Root had to step in to guide Rauf away from Jacks.

Rauf had the last word, but this will be remembered as Shadab's match.

Зампред комитета Госдумы по физкультуре и спорту Сергей Бурлаков оценил то, что Москва примет чемпионат мира (ЧМ) по тяжелой атлетике в 2030 году. Его цитирует «Подъем».

Бурлаков назвал решение правильным и выразил уверенность, что Россия способна принимать соревнования и по другим олимпийским дисциплинам. По его мнению, страна открыта для всех, кто не ставит себя выше других, а представители большинства государств приедут в Москву и смогут убедиться в ложности стереотипных представлений о ней.

При этом другой зампред комитета, Роман Терюшков, оценил решение как значимое, но осторожное из-за большого временного запаса до 2030 года. По его мнению, международные спортивные структуры демонстрируют готовность возвращаться к деполитизации спорта и восстанавливать сотрудничество с Россией, однако право на проведение турнира еще предстоит защитить от возможного давления.

Ранее Международная федерации тяжелой атлетики объявила, что Москва примет чемпионат мира по тяжелой атлетике в 2030 году. Россия впервые с 2022 года получила право проведения чемпионата мира по олимпийскому виду спорта. Эти соревнования станут первым квалификационным стартом для тяжелоатлетов на пути отбора на Олимпиаду 2032-го.

Перечислены главные ошибки при выборе бюстгальтера

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Фото: Kostikova Natalia / Shutterstock / Fotodom

Дизайнер бренда нижнего белья Curvy Kate Джене Лучани Сена назвала главные ошибки при выборе и носке бюстгальтера. Ее рекомендации перечислены в материале Daily Mail.

В первую очередь эксперт заявила, что нельзя ориентироваться на один размер у всех брендов, так как сетка может различаться не только у производителей, но и у моделей одной марки. Особенно заметными различия могут быть между американскими и европейскими брендами.

Кроме того, важно не покупать бюстгальтер, который удобно сидит только на самых тугих крючках. «Новая модель должна надежно и комфортно фиксироваться на самом свободном ряду застежек. При носке и стирке пояс постепенно растягивается, и оставшиеся крючки позволяют скорректировать посадку. Если сразу застегивать его максимально туго, такого запаса не останется», — пояснила она.

Не следует сильнее затягивать бретели, если бюстгальтер перестал поддерживать грудь. Основную нагрузку несет пояс: если он растянулся, белье пора заменить. Подтягивание бретелей проблему не решит, но может усилить давление на плечи, добавляет эксперт

По словам специалистки, одна из главных ошибок — не учитывать форму и расположение груди. Отмечается, что на посадку влияют не только размер, но и расстояние между молочными железами, их полнота, выступание вперед и распределение объема. Поэтому следует примерять бюстгальтеры с разной формой чашек и конструкцией центральной перемычки.

Сена также призвала не застегивать бюстгальтер спереди, а затем проворачивать его вокруг тела. Вместо этого она рекомендует уделять внимание правильному размещению груди в чашках: слегка наклониться вперед, а затем руками аккуратно направить ткани груди от боков к центру.

Что касается спортивного бюстгальтера, выбор слишком тесного бра тоже является ошибкой: для йоги и бега требуется разная степень поддержки. При этом избыточное сдавливание может мешать свободно двигаться и дышать, а недостаточная фиксация — не обеспечивать нужную поддержку при интенсивных нагрузках.

В заключение собеседница издания посоветовала не сушить бюстгальтер в сушильной машине. Такая сушка быстро разрушает ткань и снижает эластичность белья.

В июне также были названы способы продлить срок службы бюстгальтера.

Former Panorama producer accuses BBC of bias over Gaza coverage claiming a 'more radical generation' has taken over

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Published: 16:30 GMT, 25 October 2026 | Updated: 16:58 GMT, 25 October 2026

A former senior Panorama producer has accused the BBC of bias in its coverage of the conflict in Gaza.

Phil Craig claimed a 'new, more radical generation' had taken over, suggesting the broadcaster had hired staff with 'partisan views'.

Mr Craig, who spent two decades there before moving on to Discovery Networks International and Australian broadcaster ABC, suggested output from the BBC was 'insufficiently policed'.

Speaking at the Battle of Ideas festival last Saturday, the TV veteran said: 'I'm afraid as I saw what played out in Gaza, my career-long faith in the BBC just ebbed away. In its fairness and objectivity.'

Mr Craig added at the discussion and debate event in Westminster: 'Of course, news makes mistakes. We all know news coverage, it can be faulted. There are wars, people spin them, both sides spin, and there's propaganda.

'But all the mistakes in this particular war seem to be in the same direction. Mistakes of hiring people with incredibly partisan views or maybe even hiring people who were actually part of Hamas. The most outrageous of all, the October hospital moment.'

Mr Craig was referring to the criticism the BBC faced for its coverage of an explosion at the al-Ahli Arab Hospital in Gaza, when a correspondent said that the Israelis had been contacted and were investigating, but added: 'It's hard to see what else this could be really given the size of the explosion other than an Israeli air strike or several air strikes'.

Western intelligence agencies later concluded that a misfired rocket from the Palestinian militant group Islamic Jihad from within Gaza was to blame.

The BBC apologised, admitting that 'even in this fast-moving situation it was wrong to speculate in this way about the possible causes'.

Mr Craig said: 'It really worried me. I suspect the thing that caused all this is group-think. It's a new generation, perhaps, a new, more radical generation, young people, activists, people insufficiently policed, insufficiently guided, old principles not being enforced in the way they have been on us.'

Mr Craig joined the BBC's trainee scheme in 1982, aged 23, rising to become a senior producer on Public Eye and Panorama before leaving to work for Brook Lapping. As an independent producer co-running the London-based firm he made numerous programmes for the BBC.

Recalling his production company's work on a documentary for the broadcaster in 1998, titled: The Fifty Years War: Israel and the Arabs, Mr Craig said they had been held to 'super high standards' of balance and fairness.

He later told The Telegraph he had watched the BBC's coverage of the alleged hospital strike with 'mounting alarm and dismay, and noted how it helped fan fires all around the world, including literal fires at synagogues'.

He added: 'Anyone who has ever worked for the corporation's news operation knows its global influence, and in this case it was deployed in entirely the wrong way.'

Last week a US judge ordered the US President to answer questions under oath in his defamation lawsuit by December 11.

A BBC spokesman said: 'The BBC is fully committed to reporting the Israel-Gaza war impartially, with no agenda and to the highest standards of journalism.

'We have delivered comprehensive coverage of the conflict with breaking news, ongoing updates on developments and in-depth analysis. BBC News has a duty to our audiences to provide a range of perspectives on the conflict.

'When mistakes are made, we are transparent and accountable in acknowledging them and making corrections.'
