Oct 25 - Borr Drilling's Prospector 5 rig sank late on Saturday off the coast of Mayumba, Gabon, killing one employee and leaving four others missing among the 115 people on board, three sources familiar with the matter said on Sunday.

An Indian national, one of the five initially unaccounted for, has been found dead. The remaining four missing include three Gabonese nationals and one other expatriate, the sources said.

The government has not confirmed the death, instead saying that five people remain missing and that search efforts were ongoing.

"Preliminary information indicates that the rig experienced a punch-through event during preload activities, resulting in loss of stability and the subsequent sinking of the rig," the offshore drilling contractor said.

The Prospector 5 oil and gas rig, owned and operated by Borr, is contracted to support BW Energy's drilling operations off Gabon. The rig had not yet begun drilling.

Prospector 5 is located at the Dussafu Marin permit, a licence off the Gabon coast. BW Energy holds a 73.5% working interest in the licence, with Panoro Energy holding 17.5%.

Gabon's Ministry of Petroleum and Gas said it had activated a crisis unit and ordered the navy to reinforce search and rescue operations at sea. The ministry also ordered a technical investigation, Petroleum and Gas Minister Clotaire Kondja said in a statement.

The crisis unit brings together the ministry, the navy, state oil company Gabon Oil, BW Energy and Borr Drilling, Kondja said.

The accident occurred at approximately 9:40 p.m. (2040 GMT) on Saturday in the Dussafu Marin permit area near the Mabomont production facility, the ministry said.

"We are deeply concerned for those who remain unaccounted for and everyone affected by this incident," said Borr Drilling Chief Executive Bruno Morand.

"Our immediate priority is supporting the ongoing search and rescue efforts and the welfare of all personnel involved."

Panoro Energy said production had been halted, adding that there were no reports of damage or accidental discharges.

No discharge of harmful substances has been observed, the ministry said, adding that pollution-response resources remained deployed to protect the coastline and nearby Mayumba National Park. REUTERS

Town CEO Jean-Denis Greze on why most people still don’t know what to ask AI

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

AI agents are powerful, but most ordinary people don’t yet use them. Town wants to change that. Led by CEO Jean-Denis Greze, playfully referred to as Town’s mayor, the company announced a $108 million Series B on October 19, co-led by Forerunner Ventures and Menlo Ventures, at a $1 billion valuation.  

Greze cofounded the San Francisco startup with Tony Vincent in late 2024 after serving as CTO of Plaid, where he built up the engineering team from about 20 people to 350.

Town’s betting it can make AI more accessible and practical for small-business owners and mid-market firms still looking for day-to-day AI benefits. 

Fast Company spoke with Greze about why most people still treat AI like a fancy search engine, where AI assistants can be most helpful, and why he’s working harder than he ever has before. The conversation has been edited for length and clarity.

ChatGPT and Claude can already connect to other apps. Why do people need stand-alone AI assistants like yours?

Could Claude or ChatGPT do most of what Town does? I think the answer is, most of it, yeah, they can. But most people who use ChatGPT or Claude aren’t using those tools to do what people in tech describe as agentic work, which just means using them to actually do things for you as opposed to researching things for you. They use it more as a Google Search replacement.

I think the biggest reason is that most people don’t really know what to put in the chat box. Putting someone in front of an empty box and asking them to write what to do, especially when the capabilities are changing all the time—that’s not onboarding.

No one’s figured out how you take a mainstream person and get them to use these products to their full potential. That’s the market opportunity. And we may be wrong, by the way. Maybe distribution just wins, and Google, Microsoft, and OpenAI end up winning. As a startup, that’s the risk you’re taking on.

What’s a use case that surprised you?

There’s a small travel firm at a vacation destination where people rent cars and gear, like a bike, for the day. The business is totally run over email. If something’s damaged, the employee takes pictures and sends an email to the central processing person.

Now there’s a little workflow that at the end of the day takes all the emails with the pictures, looks at the CRM for who the people are, and drafts the emails. They were drafting 10 to 12 of these emails every day, and it wasted 25 minutes; now it takes them 5 minutes. No one’s building SaaS for that small business. 

What should we call this category of tool? An AI assistant? A chief of staff?

We call our product a Townie. If you use the word assistant or the phrase chief of staff, you have a human mental model for what an assistant does. I think what this technology does today and what it’ll do in five years will probably defy these expectations. And no one knows what a chief of staff does outside of CEOs of Fortune 500 companies.

I actually think the right analogy for what we’re building is more like a browser or a device. When the App Store came out, suddenly a percentage of what you used to do in your web browser, you started to just do in your phone. Adding AI assistance is the same. 

The percentage of the time you interact with information in a browser or your phone will just go down now forever, and the percentage that you do by talking to an AI will go up. The future, I think, is voice for sure.

What’s next for Town?

I’m very excited about live voice. You could imagine in the morning you’re commuting to work and you have in your ear a world-class executive assistant that’s telling you, “Hey, you got seven emails overnight. There’s two of them that are urgent. On this one, if you tell me this, I can help draft the reply. The other one is going to require you to move a meeting later today. Do you want me to come up with some options?” It’s just talking to you, and you can interrupt it.

I’m also excited about what we call agent to agent. You could say, “Who at the company is really close with someone doing R&D at this other company?” Your agent will talk to other agents at your own company, and it’ll learn that Bob plays tennis with someone there. But it won’t tell you that. It’ll just be like, “Hey, Bob appears to be closely connected to that person. Do you want me to ask them if they can do the intro?” Leveraging the network of everyone within a company in a way that’s privacy-preserving is huge.

Should people connect their financial accounts to an AI agent?

If you’re a consumer, it is very likely that you connect everything about you, your medicals, your financials, to the agents. It’s just too beneficial. It’ll tell you that your credit card rate’s too high, that you should apply for a different one. It’s free money.

But the privacy concern is real. I don’t think you want to use an assistant where you’re not convinced it’s making decisions that are in your best interest. These products only work in a world where you think they truly have your best interest.

Can you trust those services?

If you’re paying for it, you can. If you’re not paying for a service, you’re not the customer. You’re being monetized in some other way.

We made the choice very early: We like consumer use cases, and they’re subsidized by business use cases. We charge businesses, and it’s not a cheap product; it’s not $5 a month. Our main plan is $50 a month.

What’s the hardest part of running Town?

The world’s too fast. I’ve never seen anything like it. It’s nerve-racking. Software had high gross margins because there was a really high fixed cost to copying it, and there was a real advantage to being early in market. By the time someone could copy you, you’d have built the next feature.

With AI, competition moves so fast and good ideas are copied so quickly. Things that would take nine months to copy are copied in a month or two.

So the pressure you feel to execute and to work a lot—I’m 47, but I’ve not worked this hard [before]. Psychologically, it’s tough, because you’re either ecstatic because you shipped something before OpenAI did, or you’re in fear that there’s a tweet from Sam Altman that puts you on your back foot. So that’s super stressful.

How do you use Town outside of work?

I’ve told Town to research food blogs and newspapers that write about food in San Francisco. I have a date night with my wife every Sunday night. So every Monday night, it looks at the next month and gives me four tables that I can make a reservation at that are classics, and then finds four that are new that have been mentioned in food blogs. I like food. I just don’t have the time [to do the research myself].

Najzdrowszy sposób jedzenia wołowiny. Eksperci rozwiewają mity o czerwonym mięsie

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Wołowina przez lata uchodziła za kontrowersyjny składnik diety, często łączony z chorobami serca i nowotworami. Najnowsze badania rzucają jednak nowe światło na jej spożycie, a dietetycy podkreślają: liczy się nie tylko ilość, ale przede wszystkim sposób przygotowania mięsa. Sprawdzamy, jak jeść wołowinę, by była częścią zdrowego stylu życia.

Przez długi czas czerwone mięso, w tym wołowina, miało złą sławę w świecie dietetyki. Liczne badania wskazywały na związek między jego nadmiernym spożyciem a chorobami serca, udarami czy nowotworami. Jednak najnowsze analizy przynoszą zaskakujące wnioski. Okazuje się, że umiarkowane spożycie wołowiny nie zwiększa ryzyka udaru, a także nie wpływa znacząco na zachorowalność na choroby serca czy nowotwory.

Specjaliści zwracają uwagę, że kluczowe znaczenie ma często to, w jaki sposób spożywamy czerwone mięso — innymi słowy, jak jest przygotowywane oraz ile ma kalorii, tłuszczu i soli. To właśnie sposób obróbki i dodatki decydują o tym, czy wołowina będzie sprzymierzeńcem zdrowia, czy wręcz przeciwnie.

Dietetycy podkreślają, że chuda wołowina to prawdziwa skarbnica wartości odżywczych. Chuda wołowina to dużo wartości odżywczych w stosunkowo niewielkiej porcji - zwraca uwagę dietetyczka Nichola Ludlam-Raine, cytowana przez „Daily Mail”.

W 100 gramach ugotowanej chudej wołowiny znajduje się około 2,5 mg żelaza, 5-6 mg cynku i 2,5 mcg witaminy B12 – składników kluczowych dla utrzymania energii, odporności i prawidłowych funkcji poznawczych.

Wołowina, nawet ta najchudsza, zawiera tłuszcz – zwykle mniej niż 10 gramów na 100 gramów produktu. Choć tłuszcze nasycone wciąż budzą obawy, warto pamiętać, że mięso to dostarcza również zdrowych tłuszczów nienasyconych, w tym kwasów omega-3.

Niektóre kawałki wołowiny, jak polędwica, mają mniej tłuszczów nasyconych niż kurczak ze skórą. Na przykład stek z polędwicy zawiera około 2 gramy tłuszczów nasyconych, podczas gdy trzy skrzydełka lub udka z kurczaka mogą mieć ich nawet czterokrotnie więcej.

Ekspertka radzi, by przed przygotowaniem steku odciąć widoczny pasek tłuszczu – pozwoli to zaoszczędzić nawet 100 kalorii i 3 gramy tłuszczów nasyconych.

Najzdrowszą opcją są świeże lub mrożone, chude kawałki mięsa, takie jak polędwica czy rostbef. Zawierają one mniej tłuszczów nasyconych niż antrykot, z którego trudno usunąć tłuszcz. Najmniej korzystne dla zdrowia są natomiast przetworzone produkty: kiełbasy, salami, boczek czy konserwy mięsne. Zawarte w nich konserwanty – azotyny i azotany – mogą przekształcać się podczas obróbki w związki zwiększające ryzyko nowotworów.

Potrawy z wołowiny mielonej, takie jak burgery, chili czy spaghetti bolognese, mogą być zdrową opcją, jeśli wybierzemy mięso o niskiej zawartości tłuszczu. Domowy burger z 125 gramów wołowiny to około 170 kalorii i 2-3 gramy tłuszczów nasyconych. W połączeniu z warzywami i pełnoziarnistą bułką stanowi pełnowartościowy posiłek.

Trzeba jednak uważać na gotowe burgery i dania z mielonego mięsa, które zwykle zawierają więcej tłuszczu, soli oraz dodatki chemiczne.

Najlepiej wybierać gotowanie, duszenie lub pieczenie w niższych temperaturach. Grillowanie jest korzystniejsze niż smażenie, ponieważ pozwala na odprowadzenie nadmiaru tłuszczu. W przypadku smażenia część tłuszczu można odsączyć papierowym ręcznikiem.

Unikajmy przypalania mięsa na otwartym ogniu – powstające wtedy związki, tzw. heterocykliczne aminy (HCA), mogą zwiększać ryzyko nowotworów. Badania wykazały, że zawartość HCA w dobrze wysmażonym steku jest nawet 3,5 razy wyższa niż w steku krwistym. Te związki powstają najczęściej, gdy mięso jest mocno zwęglone, przypalone lub wystawione na działanie płomieni - ostrzega Ludlam-Raine.

Wolne gotowanie sprawdzi się szczególnie przy twardszych kawałkach mięsa, pozwalając zachować wartości odżywcze i kruchość.

Choć tatar czy stek krwisty mają swoich zwolenników, spożywanie surowego mięsa wiąże się z ryzykiem zatrucia bakteriami, takimi jak E. coli czy Salmonella. Szczególnie powinny go unikać kobiety w ciąży, dzieci, osoby starsze oraz z obniżoną odpornością.

Jednak, jak podkreślają eksperci, ryzyko zatrucia jest niewielkie w przypadku całych kawałków mięsa, które zostały obsmażone z zewnątrz. Większe zagrożenie niesie tatar, gdzie bakterie mogą być rozprowadzone w całym produkcie podczas siekania.

Eksperci są zgodni: odpowiednio przygotowana wołowina może być cennym składnikiem zdrowej diety, dostarczającym wartościowego białka, żelaza, cynku i witaminy B12. Kluczem jest umiar, wybór chudych kawałków i właściwy sposób obróbki termicznej.

Qui sont les "citoyens palestiniens d'Israël" et pourquoi pourraient-ils détenir la clé pour destituer Netanyahu ?

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Aux heures de pointe, à Nazareth — la plus grande ville à majorité arabe d'Israël —, des étudiants distribuent des tracts en bord de route, incitant les automobilistes à aller voter lors des élections législatives très disputées de la semaine prochaine. Les sondages suggèrent que les partis arabes pourraient jouer un rôle déterminant dans l'accession au pouvoir.

Par le passé, le mécontentement a été un facteur clé de la faible participation électorale des Arabes israéliens, qui représentent environ un cinquième de la population et se définissent souvent eux-mêmes comme des citoyens palestiniens d'Israël.

Toutefois, à l'occasion de ce scrutin, une volonté marquée se fait sentir de défaire la coalition la plus à droite de l'histoire du pays : une alliance entre le Premier ministre de longue date, Benjamin Netanyahu, et des partis d'extrême droite ainsi que des formations ultra-orthodoxes.

"J'estime qu'il est de notre devoir de chasser ce gouvernement extrémiste, qui nous traite comme des citoyens de seconde zone", déclare Arkan Badran, 23 ans, présent parmi les étudiants qui distribuent les tracts.

"J'appelle tous les jeunes à voter pour les listes arabes, qui nous représentent, car il en va de notre avenir", ajoute-t-il.

La plupart des sondages indiquent que ni Netanyahu et ses alliés, ni le "Bloc du changement" — coalition d'opposition menée par l'ancien général de centre-droit Gadi Eisenkot — ne disposent d'une voie claire vers une majorité parlementaire.

Si ces prévisions se confirment, les partis opposés à Netanyahu qui souhaiteraient former une coalition gouvernementale devront, au lendemain du scrutin, soit revenir sur leurs promesses de ne jamais collaborer avec le Premier ministre, soit conclure un accord avec les listes à majorité arabe.

Un point de friction majeur réside dans le fait qu'Eisenkot a, jusqu'à présent, exclu toute participation à un gouvernement incluant des partis arabes, estimant que ces derniers ne partagent pas les mêmes valeurs.

La vie politique et la société israéliennes ont été bouleversées par l'attaque brutale et inédite menée par le Hamas contre le pays le 7 octobre 2023, ainsi que par la guerre qui a suivi à Gaza. Aujourd'hui, il existe une méfiance et une hostilité croissantes à l'égard de la minorité arabe du pays.

Des informations vérifiées à portée de main

"Après le 7 octobre, nous avons été traités comme des ennemis de ce pays", affirme Suha Salman Mousa, du Centre Mossawa à Nazareth, qui défend l'égalité des droits pour les citoyens arabes.

"La liberté d'expression a été drastiquement réduite. Il y a eu des arrestations et des licenciements en masse pour avoir publié des contenus sur les réseaux sociaux ou manifesté contre la guerre à Gaza", souligne-t-elle.

Mousa accuse les ministres de pratiquer une "discrimination systématique" et de se livrer à une "incitation" à la haine contre les Arabes israéliens.

De leur côté, des organisations de la société civile affirment que les budgets alloués aux communautés arabes ont subi des coupes disproportionnées.

Le gouvernement soutient que ces réductions sont nécessaires pour financer les dépenses de défense et de sécurité en temps de guerre.

Des campagnes politisées ont également visé des médecins arabes travaillant dans des hôpitaux israéliens, tandis que des chauffeurs de bus arabes ont été la cible d'attaques racistes de la part d'Israéliens juifs.

Certaines campagnes électorales ont cherché à tirer profit de l'aggravation des divisions sociales.

Bezalel Smotrich, homme politique d'extrême droite et ministre des Finances, a promis de "judaïser" certaines zones de la Galilée (au nord) et du Néguev (au sud) — régions où réside la majeure partie de la population arabe — comparant cette initiative à l'expansion des colonies en Cisjordanie occupée.

La plupart des Arabes israéliens sont des descendants de Palestiniens restés sur le territoire qui constitue aujourd'hui Israël, durant la guerre qui a suivi la création de l'État en 1948 — une période où des centaines de milliers d'autres ont été expulsés ou ont fui leurs foyers.

Le gouvernement israélien actuel les présente souvent comme une menace pour l'identité juive de l'État. Récemment, le Premier ministre a affirmé que ses rivaux envisageaient de s'allier aux Arabes pour former un "gouvernement dangereux".

Pour appuyer cet argument, sa campagne a déployé des panneaux publicitaires, notamment à un carrefour situé à l'entrée principale de Nazareth.

Ces affiches suggèrent un choix entre "La droite ou la Palestine", en montrant des figures de proue de l'opposition — y compris des personnalités politiques de droite — dépeintes comme des défenseurs de la seconde option plutôt que de la première.

L'équipe de campagne de Netanyahu a également eu recours à des vidéos générées par intelligence artificielle, montrant ses opposants en train d'enlacer des dirigeants arabes.

À Radio Nas, une station arabophone populaire de Nazareth, je rencontre le présentateur du journal, Mohammed Magadli, qui est également analyste pour la chaîne israélienne Canal 12.

Son père, Raleb Magadli, a été le premier ministre musulman d'Israël entre 2007 et 2009, sous les couleurs du Parti travailliste.

Je demande au jeune Magadli ce qu'il pense du refus des dirigeants du "Bloc du changement" d'unir leurs forces à celles des partis arabes.

"En résumé, c'est du racisme. Mais si l'on cherche à en expliquer la raison, c'est à cause des intérêts politiques de Benjamin Netanyahu", estime-t-il.

"Il tente constamment de saper la légitimité des partis arabes, simplement parce qu'ils sont arabes, parce qu'ils sont palestiniens. Cela a une incidence sur la plupart des partis juifs en Israël", explique-t-il.

Les sondages de cette semaine suggèrent que le bloc d'Eisenkot, tout comme le bloc nationaliste religieux de Netanyahu, pourraient chacun obtenir environ 50 sièges sur les 120 que compte le Parlement israélien, tandis que 13 à 14 sièges pourraient revenir à des formations à majorité arabe.

La "Liste arabe commune" — une coalition composée du Hadash, du Balad et du Taal — ainsi que la "Liste arabe unie" — un parti islamiste plus modeste dirigé par l'homme politique Mansour Abbas — devraient recueillir ces sièges.

Bien que le parti d'Abbas ait été initialement créé en tant que branche du Mouvement islamique en Israël — qui prône des valeurs socialement conservatrices —, son dirigeant s'est fait connaître en nouant des alliances avec des partenaires juifs.

Il donne la priorité à la résolution des problèmes des Arabes israéliens et s'exprime relativement peu au sujet des Palestiniens de Cisjordanie et de Gaza.

Abbas a déjà goûté au pouvoir. En 2021, il est devenu ministre au sein d'un précédent gouvernement israélien, de courte durée, dirigé par Naftali Bennett — aujourd'hui membre de la droite au sein du bloc d'Eisenkot.

Pour ces élections, il a pris une initiative inattendue en accordant la deuxième place de sa liste à Yoav Segalovitz, un ancien policier juif et député centriste.

Abbas estime qu'Eisenkot et d'autres seront plus enclins à une alliance avec son parti après les élections, et il insiste sur sa volonté de faire partie du prochain gouvernement.

"Nous sommes arrivés à la conclusion que la représentation parlementaire ne suffit pas pour servir la société arabe. Nous avons décidé d'assumer nos responsabilités, de participer à la résolution de nos problèmes et d'offrir de l'espoir, tant aux Arabes qu'aux Juifs", déclare-t-il.

"C'est pourquoi notre démarche a été guidée par la volonté d'accéder à une position d'influence, et non de simple représentation", explique-t-il.

Par ailleurs, la "Liste commune" — composée de partis laïcs qui prônent l'égalité des droits pour les Arabes israéliens, la fin de l'occupation militaire israélienne des territoires palestiniens et une solution à deux États au conflit israélo-palestinien — entend jouer un rôle influent au sein de l'opposition.

Il est question d'offrir un "filet de sécurité" électoral à ceux qui pourraient remplacer le gouvernement Netanyahu, en échange d'un soutien sur des dossiers politiques majeurs.

L'un des enjeux majeurs de ces élections pour les Arabes israéliens est la criminalité organisée, qui sévit de manière endémique.

Insolvent businesses reacquired by their previous management in “pre-pack” administrations are much more likely to fail again and tend to repay far less to creditors, research for the Guardian suggests.

The study is the latest to raise questions about the practice of “phoenixism” – accounting’s controversial art of liquidating companies to allow directors to return with a new entity, free of debts.

The process tends to involve pre-pack administrations, where a supposed corporate rescue is agreed before commencing insolvency proceedings. These orchestrated deals often leave unsecured creditors nursing large losses. HM Revenue and Customs estimates that phoenixisms cost taxpayers hundreds of millions of pounds a year.

Cases have included owners extracting millions of pounds in dividends and loans from businesses they subsequently arrange to buy back from administrators.

The study was commissioned by the Guardian from Turnstone Intelligence, which used AI agents to read official documents relating to more than 1,700 administrations during 2019. The research company noted much poorer outcomes when a business’s assets were reacquired by “connected parties”, such as former directors and shareholders.

It found that 44% of pre-packs had resulted in a second insolvency by July 2026 when assets were bought by a connected party. Just 26% of pre-packs produced a subsequent insolvency when the acquirer was unconnected to the failed company.

Ben Sims, managing director of Turnstone Intelligence, said: “I was surprised how big the gap remains. These results support what many in the industry have long suspected and will feed the continuing debate as to whether saving these businesses is worth the impact on taxpayers and creditors.”

The data showed that connected parties appeared far more likely to be permitted to defer payments in pre-packs. A total of 198 out of 303 connected-party administrations – 65% – allowed former owners and directors to reacquire the assets via future instalments. For unconnected pre-packs, 28% of buyers used deferred payments.

Unsecured creditors, who typically include HMRC, received a return in only 23% of pre-packs involving a connected party deferring payments, with those deals paying out just 1.58p in the pound. When the buyer was unconnected, 36% of pre-pack administrations resulted in repayments to those creditors, at a rate of 7.86p in the pound.

“What was interesting to us was the sheer scale of losses,” Sims said. “You would hope these deals would provide better outcomes to creditors, but we see them getting almost nothing. Of course, the biggest loser is often the taxpayer.”

The findings appear to closely mirror results from two well-known reports in the field: research by the University of Wolverhampton for the government in 2014 (Graham review) and a European Union-funded 2018 study. Both pointed to poorer outcomes in connected pre-pack administrations. Those issues were compounded when the assets were reacquired by connected parties through deferred payments.

Riz Mokal, an honorary law professor at University College London who co-authored the EU study, said: “These are striking findings and are consistent with our earlier work. They reinforce a wider concern that connected-party pre-packs can allow non-viable ‘zombie’ businesses to shed their unsecured liabilities and continue trading, harming not only their creditors, including HMRC, but also competitors that continue to pay their way.”

The methodology used by the latest researchers was broadly consistent with the earlier, EU-funded study. They used AI agents to read filings submitted over the past seven years for administrations commenced in England and Wales in 2019 – which Turnstone said was “long enough for creditor returns to settle and buyer failures to show”.

Ninja releases kitchen appliances so often that it’s hard to keep up. Here’s whether or not the new MultiPot XL is a true kitchen essential.

When I think of kitchen brands dominating home kitchens and wishlists alike, Ninja immediately comes to mind.

Beyond the company’s viral social media videos and massively popular product releases, like the Crispi air fryer and Creami frozen dessert maker, the brand’s popularity may also be rooted in its rapid product launches. Ninja releases products fast, and it releases a lot of them.

In the last couple of months alone, we’ve seen the release of the Prepi, CrushBoss, NeverQuit cookware and now, the MultiPot XL. With colder weather setting in, the 12-in-1 cooker caught my eye. Theoretically, it can reduce kitchen clutter by replacing numerous other countertop appliances, but is it truly a worthy replacement for your Instant Pot, Crockpot and even the classic Dutch oven?

With so many new releases, how do you separate want from need? We cooked numerous meals with the new MultiPot XL to see whether the shiny new appliance has what it takes to earn a place on your countertop.

The Ninja MultiPot XL has 12 functions and the option to cook in the “Megazone,” a 7.5-quart pot, or in two smaller pots, each 3.5 quarts. All of the pots are ceramic-coated.

The first meal I cooked in it was homemade ramen, and I learned there are a few settings where the MultiPot excels, including the sear/sauté function.

The first ingredient I sautéed was mushrooms with butter. The MultiPot quickly preheated and sauteed the mushrooms just as well as a non-stick pan on a stovetop would. When the lids are shut, the pot quickly gathers steam to help speed up the cooking process too.

Next, I placed the chicken thighs in the already heated pot, and they took only a few minutes to cook all the way through. The best part was that the chicken developed a crust, which added extra flavor to the dish.

I used the same function for a handful of additional ingredients, combined everything in the pot with chicken broth and then set the MultiPot to its hot pot setting until everything came to a boil.

Because the pot was already hot, it didn’t take long to boil. When I was ready for it to stop, I turned off the appliance and lifted the pot off the hot plates so it wouldn’t overcook. Over the next couple of weeks, I continued to use the appliance for various meals, but sauté/sear and hot pot remained among my favorite functions.

Despite its ability to sear and sauté just as well as a non-stick pan on the stovetop, one setting was less impressive. I used the slow cooker setting to cook a small batch of beef short ribs in one half of the cooker, cooking them on low for over 8 hours. I’ve cooked this meal before in a slow cooker, and by the end of the 8 hours, the meat is usually falling apart and my whole house smells like the dish.

With the MultiPot, though, although the meat was still fully cooked through and had a great flavor, it wasn’t fall-off-the-bone cooked. I’m not entirely sure why that is, but it made me wish I’d cooked that dish in my Crockpot instead.

On the other side of the MultiPot, I cooked brown rice, and it cooked just as well as on the stovetop.

In my experience, the only place where the MultiPot has a leg up on a slow cooker is the fact that you can sauté or sear directly in the MultiPot, then turn the pot to the slow cooker setting, instead of having to sear in a separate pan on the stovetop, then transfer the ingredients to a slow cooker. So, if you’re looking for convenience, the MultiPot offers it.

If you have limited countertop space for appliances and want just one that can handle many cooking tasks, the MultiPot XL would be a great option. With 12 settings, it has more versatility than many competitors, but keep in mind that the “keep warm” function is included in the 12, and some functions won’t be used as much as others, such as sous vide.

At $250, it’s more expensive than standard slow cookers and Instant Pots, but it could be a good option if you often cook large meals for a group. If you’re looking for a cheaper multicooker, but want to stay in the Ninja family, the 8-in-1 Ninja PossibleCooker may be a smarter buy since it’s less than half the price but still has the essential functions. However, I haven’t tested the PossibleCooker myself.

The MultiPot XL can definitely be a great choice for some, but I wouldn’t put it in the must-have category, even if the rose quartz option is one of the cutest appliance color schemes I’ve seen.
