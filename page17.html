Serbian election winners plan gas talks with Russia

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Serbia’s incoming leadership plans to hold talks with Russia to negotiate a long-term natural gas supply agreement, prominent Student List candidate Radomir Saicic has said.

The student-backed movement, which defeated the coalition of longtime Serbian leader Aleksandar Vucic in a parliamentary election this month, previously indicated that Belgrade would not join Western sanctions against Moscow.

Vucic himself, however, has warned that the Student List would renege on its position, while some high-profile members of the movement have suggested that Serbia’s relations with Russia could be reconsidered.

The Student List secured around 52.5% of the vote in Sunday’s snap election. The movement emerged from mass protests triggered by the November 2024 collapse of a railway station canopy in Novi Sad, which killed 16 people and sparked widespread accusations of government corruption.

While the authorities launched an investigation into the tragedy and made several major concessions, the protests continued. Vucic eventually called snap parliamentary elections and resigned the presidency in September to seek a return to power as prime minister. The Serbian leader denounced the protests as an attempted Western-backed ‘color revolution’, but pledged to respect the election results and avoid bloodshed, ultimately conceding defeat.

Moscow, which had shared Vucic’s concerns about possible foreign interference, acknowledged the election results. Earlier this week, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov praised Vucic’s contribution to bilateral ties, expressing hope that the incoming leadership would continue developing constructive relations with Russia. He stressed that cooperation with Moscow was in Serbia’s interests, adding that “it takes two to tango.”

Speaking to Serbian weekly NIN in an interview published on Wednesday, Saicic, a founding signatory of the Student List who is reportedly viewed as a possible prime minister, outlined ties with Russia as one of the incoming government’s priorities.

“In the first 30 days… energy supply, which includes oil, gas and the functioning of the Electric Power Company of Serbia, will be ensured. To this end, our delegations will hold talks in Washington (expiration of OFAC’s decision on the temporary suspension of NIS sanctions) and in Moscow (gas arrangement), in order to conclude longer-term arrangements,” he said.

Saicic also previously reaffirmed the movement’s opposition to sanctions against Russia over Ukraine despite pressure from the EU, stressing that “problems should be resolved through dialogue, negotiations, and agreements, not sanctions.”

Another Student List representative, university professor Jovo Bakic, gave similar assurances in an interview with RT Balkan on Monday, saying that “we respect Russia both as a great power and as our historically important partner and, in many cases, protector.”

At the same time, Ilija Srdanovic, another prominent member of the movement, delivered a different message, signaling in an interview with US-funded RFE/RL that all options remain on the table, saying that “there will certainly be a dialogue within our society.”

Russia is Serbia’s dominant natural gas supplier, accounting for up to 90% of its imports in 2025, with most deliveries arriving through the TurkStream pipeline via Türkiye and Bulgaria. However, the two countries have yet to negotiate a new long-term gas contract since their previous three-year agreement expired in May 2025, relying instead on successive short-term extensions. The current gas agreement is valid until December 31.

The overall talks have been complicated by the uncertainty over the fate of NIS, which operates Serbia’s only oil refinery and faces US sanctions over its majority Russian ownership. Hungarian energy company MOL has been negotiating to acquire the Russian stake, while Washington has issued temporary waivers to keep the company operating.

Moscow said that long-term contracts were complicated by EU energy policies, Serbia’s European integration ambitions, and uncertainty over gas transit through Bulgaria. Nevertheless, Russia has repeatedly stressed it is open to supplying gas to any interested European customers.

Russian President Vladimir Putin and Vucic discussed energy cooperation and NIS in late September before the elections, with the Serbian leader saying at the time he was confident that Russian gas deliveries would continue at favorable prices – €318 ($356) per 1,000 cubic meters, compared with a European market benchmark of more than €800.

Spot gas price in Europe surpasses $1,200 per 1,000 cubic meters first since December 2022

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

MOSCOW, October 30. /TASS/. The spot price of gas on the exchange in Europe surpassed $1,200 per 1,000 cubic meters during Friday trading for the first time since December 2022, according to TASS calculations based on data from major trading hubs in Europe.

Specifically, day-ahead prices at the TTF hub reached $1,204 per 1,000 cubic meters, or around 104 euros per MWh.

Meanwhile the price of December gas futures contracts at the TTF hub in the Netherlands has risen to around $1,190 per 1,000 cubic meters, or 102.76 euro per MWh, according to data from London's ICE exchange.

Gas prices in Europe have been climbing amid historically low storage levels ahead of the upcoming winter and the ongoing conflict in the Middle East.

Gazprom chief executive Alexey Miller has said that the price of gas in Europe exceeded the $1,200 per 1,000 cubic meters mark, adding that it could even climb to $2,500 if winter temperatures are low.

Russian President Vladimir Putin has not ruled out the possibility that gas prices in Europe could rise to $1,500 per 1,000 cubic meters.

The CAS ruling resolves the matter, as it is final and cannot be appealed. A challenging task lies ahead in working with the International Ice Hockey Federation to implement the CAS ruling and ensure the reinstatement of Russian teams in competitions held under the IIHF’s auspices," the minister added.

On July 30, the International Ice Hockey Federation announced that it would extend Russia’s suspension from international tournaments until 2027. The IIHF stated that: "Following the IIHF Disciplinary Board Decision, the IIHF Council re-analyzed the Russian Ice Hockey Federation’s request that it be permitted to enter a Russian Team in each of the following IIHF Championships: IIHF Men’s World Championship 2027; IIHF Women’s World Championship 2027; IIHF World Junior Championship 2027; IIHF U18 Men’s World Championship 202; and IIHF U18 Women’s World Championship 2027."

A Ukrainian drone strike has killed a worker while he was traveling to the Zaporozhye Nuclear Power Plant, the Russian facility’s management reported on Friday morning.

The statement described Thursday’s attack as “barbarism” and part of a “hunt” targeting plant personnel.

“Strikes such as this one not only affect particular people. They are designed to intimidate plant workers, seed panic and tensions among those who ensure the atomic site operates safely every day,” it said.

Russia’s nuclear energy agency Rosatom mourned the death of the worker, who was employed as a welder and was traveling to work on an e-scooter when he was targeted.

The incident was first reported on Thursday by Maksim Pukhov, the mayor of the city of Energodar, near which the Zaporozhye NPP is located. He identified the victim as a man in his early 40s but did not mention his connection to the plant.

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) condemned the incident, saying any attack on the facility is “completely unacceptable” and urging “maximum military restraint” around the Zaporozhye plant.

Moscow has repeatedly criticized the UN nuclear watchdog for failing to explicitly blame Ukraine for attacks on Europe’s largest nuclear power plant, including strikes targeting its personnel.

Rosatom head Aleksey Likhachev told an IAEA conference in September that 20 people, including nine plant employees, had been killed since Ukrainian forces intensified drone and artillery strikes against the facility in late April.

Among the victims were chief engineer Aleksandr Yakovlev and driver Dmitry Filippov, who were killed in July when a drone struck a car carrying the senior employee.

Russian management took control of the Zaporozhye NPP in 2022. Residents of Zaporozhye Region voted to join Russia in a referendum later that year, which Kiev rejected as a “sham” vote. The IAEA established a permanent on-site observer mission at the plant in September 2022.
