Michael Carrick insisted he was not saying farewell to Manchester United after their 3-0 defeat to Bournemouth but admitted he does not know if he will be sacked.

United have made their worst start to a season for 40 years and lost their last two games by three goals, following Wednesday’s 4-1 thrashing by Como, and Carrick was taunted by Bournemouth supporters with chants of “sacked in the morning”.

He was still serenaded by United fans as he went down the tunnel but claimed: “It wasn't a farewell from me.”

He said he had only had a short conversation after the game with the United hierarchy and is not sure if chief executive Omar Berrada and sporting director Jason Wilcox will dismiss him.

“Just briefly like always after a game, but nothing out of the ordinary,” he added. “I will see what happens. I totally understand the situation.

“I'm not expecting anything in particular, to be honest. I am carrying on as normal. I love this place. I'll always do what I feel is going to be the best for the football club, whatever decision is made on anything really. So that's kind of it. You know, I'm not expecting anything either way.”

Carrick insisted the United players are hurting as their nosedive continues but admitted he has not spoken to them all.

“There's a lot of emotion in the changing room right now, there's a lot of disappointment, there's a lot of the boys quite hurt, disappointed,” he said.

“It's not like they don't care, or they want to be in this position. The ones I've spoken to - I've not spoke to everyone individually, the ones I have spoken to, it's affecting them a lot.”

Carrick said he was left with a variety of feelings as he thanked the United fans for their backing. “Mixed emotions for the supporters to respond in that way,” he said. “To feel the connection and appreciation from the supporters, I feel it and appreciate it a lot.”

The Guardian view on Oxford Street’s pedestrianisation: cities should be designed around people, not cars | Editorial

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

The transformation of Europe’s busiest shopping street should be a case study in making cities greener, less congested and more inviting places to live

Sadiq Khan has been trying to pedestrianise Oxford Street since he first became the mayor of London in 2016. On Monday, after numerous setbacks, his plan will finally come to fruition. The busiest stretch of the nation’s famous shopping street will be closed to traffic in a scheme designed to improve the area and make it more attractive to pedestrians. It should be an emblematic case study in making cities greener, less congested, more inviting places to live and visit.

The Greater London Authority hopes it will rejuvenate a high street fallen on hard times. Since the pandemic, the number of vacant shops on Oxford Street has more than quadrupled. It estimates that the scheme will generate £82m of value a year in the area by increasing footfall and encouraging visitors to linger. This impact remains to be seen, but research from other cities suggests that shops experience higher sales once an area is pedestrianised. Given the prime minister’s focus on rejuvenating British high streets, whose dilapidation is correlated with support for Reform UK, he should be watching with interest.

The benefits of pedestrianisation are far greater than increased shopping. Domestic transport remains the largest contributor to greenhouse-gas emissions in Britain. This sector’s emissions have fallen more slowly than others largely because too many people still rely on cars. Mr Khan’s city is an outlier: 59% of all journeys in Britain were made by car in 2024, while in London cars accounted for 34%. This is hardly surprising when the Treasury spends twice as much on London’s transport system as it does on other regions in England, and the capital’s bus fares are cheaper. In Leeds, which has no mass-transit system, nearly half of people drive to work. Car use cannot be reduced without simultaneously improving public transport.

The critics of pedestrianisation – including Westminster council – suggest it merely displaces traffic elsewhere. But the evidence on this is more nuanced. One study from the University of the West of England suggests the closure of strategic roads causes some traffic to disappear permanently, as people switch to public transport or walk. Low-traffic neighbourhoods (LTNs), the pedestrianisation schemes that were rushed through in the capital during Covid-19 and have since been introduced in Manchester and Birmingham, were fiercely criticised for causing a buildup of road traffic elsewhere. Yet one study of 46 LTNs in London suggested their impact on traffic elsewhere was minimal.

Car-free areas should be accompanied by other policies that reduce pollution on surrounding roads, such as the low-emissions zones introduced in London and Oxford. Mr Khan was criticised by the then leader of the opposition, Keir Starmer, for the expansion of Ulez in 2023, and also suffered racial abuse. He has since been vindicated by research from Imperial College suggesting Ulez has significantly improved the health of primary school children in the capital, whose lung growth had been stunted by pollution.

That Transport for London estimates the pedestrianisation of Oxford Street will have only a minimal impact on nitrogen dioxide emissions, which were already decreasing as a result of Ulez, is testimony to the policy’s success. In 2014, Oxford Street had the highest concentration of nitrogen dioxide pollution in the world. Its pedestrianisation should be an inspiration to other cities to embrace policies that work for people, not cars.

Römisches Reich: Forscher rekonstruieren Farbtrick des Lycurgus-Bechers

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Auf das Licht kommt es an: Von innen beleuchtet strahlt der 1600 Jahre alte Kelch rot

Der Lycurgus-Becher kann, was sonst unter anderem Chamäleons vorbehalten ist: Er wechselt seine Farbe. Scheint Licht von innen in das Glas, leuchtet es von außen betrachtet rubinrot. Bei normalen Lichtverhältnissen hingegen wirkt er jadegrün. Bereits vor rund 1600 Jahren gelang den alten Römern in der Antike das Meisterwerk, das Historiker und Glasmacher seit Hunderten Jahren fasziniert. Jetzt ist es Forschern der University of Sheffield dem »Guardian« zufolge  gelungen, den Farbtrick präzise nachzuahmen.

Das Projekt sei eine Herzensangelegenheit gewesen, sagte Lisa Hollands, Technikerin an der Fakultät für Chemie-, Material- und Bioingenieurwesen der University of Sheffield dem »Guardian«. Andere hätten zuvor bereits versucht, den Effekt nachzubilden, allerdings nur mit mäßigem Erfolg. »Für mich ist es Magie«, sagte sie.

Um den Effekt zu kopieren, galt es bei der Herstellung Gold- und Silbernanopartikel unter den richtigen Bedingungen in das Glas einzubringen – jedes davon tausendmal kleiner als ein Sandkorn. Der Erfolg hing unter anderem vom Platz im Brennofen ab. Als Vorlage diente den Forschenden ein Fragment des römischen Originals. »Wenn die Römer mich beim Experimentieren hätten beobachten können, hätten sie wahrscheinlich gelacht und gefragt: ›Warum weiß sie nicht, wie man das macht?‹«, sagte Hollands.

Die Gold- und Silbernanopartikel sorgen dafür, dass nur rote und orangefarbene Anteile des Lichts das Glas durchdringen können, blaue und grüne Anteile blockieren sie. Aus diesem Grund wirkt das Glas von außen rot, wenn es von innen bestrahlt wird. Fällt hingegen Licht auf die Oberfläche des Glases, wird dieses reflektiert und durch die Partikel gestreut, wodurch eine grüne Farbe entsteht.

Das Projekt soll helfen, zu verstehen, wie den römischen Künstlern Werke wie dieses allein aufgrund ihrer handwerklichen Fähigkeiten gelingen konnten, ohne Mikroskope, Labore und Wissen um die physikalische Theorie.

Der luxuriöse Becher, der im 4. Jahrhundert nach Christus gefertigt wurde, zeigt eine Szene aus der griechischen Mythologie. Darin triumphiert der Weingott Dionysos über König Lycurgos, der von einer Weinrebe gefangen gehalten wird. Ian Freestone, emeritierter Professor für archäologische Materialien am University College London, hält den rot-grünen Farbverlauf des Glases laut »Guardian« nicht für einen Zufall: Einer Theorie zufolge könnte er die Reifung der Traube symbolisieren.

Vervollständigt wird das Glas, das im British Museum bewundert werden kann , von einem silbernen Fuß, verziert mit Weinblättern. Knapp 16 Zentimeter ist der Becher hoch, knapp 700 Gramm schwer. Und seit 2022 zählt er zu den sieben Glaswundern der Welt.

Mardi prochain se tiennent des élections législatives déterminantes pour l'histoire d'Israël. Face au Premier ministre sortant Benyamin Nétanyahou, un homme entend bien jouer les trouble-fêtes : Gadi Eisenkot, un militaire de carrière récemment entré en politique.

Gadi Eisenkot n'a pas la même aisance, le même bagout que Benyamin Nétanyahou. D'ailleurs, il fuit les médias étrangers, lui qui reconnait ne pas maitriser très bien l'anglais. Âgé de 66 ans, il est en politique depuis seulement quatre ans, après un long parcours dans les rangs de l'armée. De petite taille et plutôt corpulent, Eisenkot, souvent habillé en noir, fait de sa sobriété sa marque de fabrique.

Israël : à quelques jours du scrutin, des électeurs aux attentes opposées

D'origine très modeste (ses amis d'enfance racontent qu'ils devaient se partager les paires de sandales pour aller à la mer), il a grandi dans les quartiers d'Eilat, au sud du pays. Ses parents sont des immigrants juifs du Maroc. S'il devenait Premier ministre, il serait le premier à être issu de la communauté juive séfarade (les Ashkénazes, les juifs d'Europe centrale, dont fait partie Nétanyahou, dominent eux les cercles de pouvoir depuis la création d'Israël en 1948).

Militaire de carrière, Gadi Eisenkot a connu une véritable ascension, d'abord au sein de la brigade Golani, connue pour son implication au Liban, où il fut simple soldat avant de devenir, après avoir franchi tous les échelons, commandant en chef. En 2015, il atteint le sommet de la hiérarchie militaire, devenant le 21ᵉ chef d'état-major de l'armée israélienne, nommé à ce poste par... Benyamin Nétanyahou !

En 2022, il s'engage en politique, sous les couleurs de l'alliance centriste de Benny Gantz, son prédécesseur à la tête de l'armée ; il est élu député à la Knesset. Après le massacre du 7 octobre 2023, Gadi Eisenkot décide de rejoindre le gouvernement Nétanyahou, au nom de l'unité nationale. Ministre sans portefeuille, il participe au cabinet de guerre et au pilotage de l'offensive à Gaza. C'est d'ailleurs lors d'une réunion de ce cabinet qu'il apprend la mort de son plus jeune fils, Gal Meir, 25 ans, mobilisé comme réserviste dans l'enclave palestinienne. Gadi Eisenkot finit par démissionner en juin 2024, accusant Benyamin Nétanyahou d'avoir délibérément saboté les accords de libération des otages, sous la pression de ses alliés d'extrême droite.

Il crée alors son propre parti, au centre toujours, qui a pour nom Yashar ("intègre" en hébreu). Un parti tout récent (fondé il y a un peu plus d'un an) et avec seulement six personnes au départ. Aujourd'hui, Yashar a grandi au point de devancer le Likoud de Nétanyahou dans les sondages, mais aucun des deux partis n'est en position d'atteindre la majorité des sièges. Ce sera tout l'enjeu des jours qui suivront l'élection : trouver des alliances pour former une majorité. Eisenkot refuse, tout comme Nétanyahou, l'alliance avec les partis arabes, pourtant faiseurs de roi.

"Provoquer le retrait de Nétanyahou de la vie politique israélienne. C'est là que commencera la guérison de l'État d'Israël".

dans une interview au quotidien "Times of Israël"

Eisenkot n'a pas de mots assez durs contre Benyamin Nétanyahou. Et s'il a des points de convergences avec ce dernier (refus de la solution à deux États, reconnaissance d'une annexion de facto de la Cisjordanie et volonté de mettre fin à la domination du Hamas à Gaza), il se distingue sur de nombreux points de l'actuel chef de gouvernement.

"La sécurité" plutôt que "la paix" : dans la campagne pour les législatives en Israël, la question palestinienne est "largement marginalisée"

Il veut ainsi abroger la réforme judiciaire votée par Nétanyahou, s'engage à installer une commission d'enquête sur le 7-Octobre, et promet de mettre un terme à l'exemption du service militaire pour les ultra-orthodoxes. Gadi Eisenkot s'oppose, par ailleurs, au projet de colonie E1 qui couperait la Cisjordanie en deux. Face aux violences des colons en Cisjordanie occupée, il prône une politique répressive (il a parlé à propos de la Cisjordanie de "terrorisme juif"). Il s'était d'ailleurs rendu au chevet d'Heidi Levine, la photojournaliste du Washington Post récemment rouée de coups par des colons. Enfin, il veut faire adopter une loi interdisant à toute personne poursuivie au pénal de former un gouvernement, visant directement Nétanyahou, dont le procès pour corruption est toujours en cours.

Cornell fraternity member seeks dismissal from Jane Doe’s sexual assault lawsuit

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

One of the men sued by a former Cornell University student identified as Jane Doe has asked a judge to dismiss the claims against him, arguing that her allegations do not support her broader accusations of rape and sexual assault.

Diego Sarabia filed the motion Friday seeking dismissal of all claims against him in the lawsuit Doe filed Sept. 16 in New York state court. Sarabia, who was a member of the Chi Phi fraternity, was one of seven men originally sued by Doe for allegedly participating in a gang rape at the Chi Phi house from Oct. 19 to 20, 2024.

Sarabia denies the allegations against him, according to the filing. His attorneys argue that even if the complaint’s factual allegations are accepted as true for the purposes of the motion, the claims against him were filed too late, are legally insufficient, or both.

James C. Knox, an attorney for Sarabia, wrote in the motion that as far as the allegations against his client, “this is essentially a lawsuit about a forced kiss.” Knox argued that the conduct Doe attributed to Sarabia did not qualify for the extended statute of limitations she cited for certain sexual offenses.

Doe accused Sarabia of grabbing her face, kissing her and snorting ketamine off her body with other fraternity members during a drug- and alcohol-fueled encounter in what was known as the “alumni room” of the fraternity house.

“Throughout a sprawling complaint approaching 600 paragraphs, plaintiff repeatedly, collectively and without differentiation accuses seven student defendants of ‘sexual assault and rape,’” Knox wrote in his filing. “Those legally conclusory statements do not substitute for pleaded facts.”

Knox argued that the complaint did not allege that Sarabia “supplied her with drugs or alcohol, participated in any of the alleged sexual encounters described elsewhere in the complaint, or was even present for most of the conduct that forms the basis of plaintiff’s broader allegations.”

The attorney said that the assault, battery and intentional infliction of emotional distress claims were barred by a one-year statute of limitations and the allegations against Sarabia did not qualify for extensions cited in the complaint.

Doe’s attorney Thomas Giuffra declined to comment on Sunday. Giuffra previously agreed to withdraw claims against the acting president of the fraternity’s chapter at the time.

The lawsuit inspired calls for a reckoning at the Ivy League institution, where past and current students and community members have accused the administration of not doing enough to protect women from sexual assault.

Cornell’s Board of Trustees hired former Deputy Attorney General Sally Yates “to lead a comprehensive independent review of how the University prevents and responds to sexual harassment, sexual assault and related misconduct on campus.”

Doe’s allegations were investigated by Cornell University Police. An internal civil rights investigation led to the expulsion of two students and disciplinary action against others. On social media, I've documented trying one new thing a week. It's gotten us out to meet people and have some interesting experiences.

There's a risk in moving abroad. You have to get yourself over the hump, and you have to be brave.

Believe in yourself. If there's something you want to try, like moving overseas, going on a solo trip, or taking a cooking class, take a chance on yourself. A graphic on the wall on the way into their dressing room at the StoneX Stadium talks of finding the perfect blend, ascribing an area of rugby to the grapes that make up a brilliant Bordeaux. It will have displeased Venter and Joe Shaw, the head coach, greatly that each failed to pass muster here, with their defence and set piece (Cabernet Sauvingnon) matched in mediocrity by an attack that had nothing like the smoothness or silkiness of Merlot.  “You can’t sugar-coat it,” Shaw said. “If you come and you play as individuals and you don’t play to the best of your ability, you are going to get punished. We didn’t do what we had planned to do. We just weren’t good enough today.”

For Harlequins, though, this was an afternoon to toast. It is Saracens that have of late hit out at what they feel is undue bad press but the south west Londoners have copped their fair share of flak over these last 12 months. Credit where credit is due, then: this was a real statement performance, starting with a bang and seldom letting up until a professional job was decisively done. Plenty had written obituaries after Harlequins had taken a shellacking at Gloucester in round two but Jason Gilmore’s side had been pleasingly spiky and spunky against both Bath and Sale and unfortunate not to come away with more. It feels a facile point but what a difference their England stars make. Playing together for the first time, their three contracted internationals Marcus Smith, George Furbank and Fin Baxter were each prominent and potent in an intoxicating first 40 minutes, meaning the game was virtually beyond doubt by the time that Baxter – carefully managed after nearly a year out with a complex foot injury – departed at the interval.

Harlequins had begun fast in their most recent Prem outing against Sale and again registered in the third minute. Smith and Furbank, striking up a productive partnership as magician and his high-class assistant, were prominent throughout a sweeping score finished off by the former, celebrating his 200th appearance with a try. What a difference they make; so, too, Baxter, a most welcome returnee laying on the second for Seb Driscoll with two thunderous carries married to a contrastingly deft offload.

Saracens had conceded the first five penalties of the match inside 10 minutes and soon had more to worry about. Even a fine finish from Rotimi Segun in the left corner hadn’t really got them going, and Harlequins marched up the field again. Ben Earl’s hauling down of a tryline-bound maul earned he a stint a sin bin and the hosts seven more points.

That should have been cancelled out immediately. Segun, the visitors’ bright spark, trod a touchline tightrope with dancing hands and feet and set up Max Malins for a certain score. All the full-back had to do was ground the ball – he dallied and failed, with Smith hustling back to knock the ball from his grasp to rather sum up the two performances. The steak served in the Saracens dressing room as an accompaniment to the bold Bordeaux bottles was excellent; this was butchery of an all together different kind.

It felt a pivotal moment. Earl returned but Saracens were soon returned to 14 men, Fergus Burke with a double whammy of a yellow card and a failed head injury assessment following a clash with Rodrigo Isgro. Cadan Murley converted the player advantage in the opposite corner to secure a bonus point before half time. To Saracens’ credit, they edged the action thereafter, tries from Theo Dan and Nick Tompkins bettering a fabulous solo scamper from Will Porter. It mattered not, though, as Harlequins retained their defensive shape and strength to banish any thoughts of a proper fight-back, with young Driscoll and Lucas Schmid emergent back-row forces.

After beating Saracens last year, they were nilled at Exeter the next week. Their wretched record against their rivals has been decisively overturned but the real challenge is what comes next to build from here. Not that victory will taste any less sweet.
