Eating 30% Less Food Has an Unexpected Effect on DNA, Study Finds

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Eating fewer calories may do more than slow certain aspects of aging. Scientists have found that mice consuming 30 percent less food accumulated fewer DNA mutations across several tissues, suggesting that diet can influence how genetic damage builds up over time.

The study, published in Cell, examined mutations across a large portion of the genome rather than focusing on individual genes. The results showed that calorie restriction affected different organs and regions of DNA in surprisingly different ways.

DNA mutations develop throughout life as cells experience genetic damage or make mistakes while copying and repairing their DNA. Most mutations are harmless, but some interfere with normal gene function and can contribute to cancer and other diseases. Although animal studies have established that calorie restriction can extend lifespan and slow certain effects of aging, its broader influence on mutation accumulation has remained unclear.

To investigate, researchers led by NYU Langone Health and University Hospitals Cleveland worked with scientists at the University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center and the National Institutes of Health. Using highly accurate DNA sequencing technology, they examined genetic changes in mice fed 30 percent fewer calories than animals allowed to eat freely.

The restricted diet reduced several types of mutations, including substitutions, which occur when one DNA letter is replaced by another, and insertions and deletions, which add or remove DNA letters. However, the reductions differed across tissues, with liver cells showing a stronger response than kidney or brain cells.

“While links between diet and lifespan have been established in animals, our results show that diet and mutations across the genome are also linked,” said co-corresponding study author Gilad D. Evrony, MD, PhD, a researcher at NYU Langone Health and associate professor at NYU Grossman School of Medicine. “Mice on a calorie-restricted diet are healthier for longer, but this diet is too challenging for humans. Nevertheless, considering the role mutations play in cancer and many other diseases, it’s exciting to have found a way to decrease mutations, which we can now work to understand.”

The researchers also discovered that calorie restriction did not affect all regions of DNA equally. In liver and kidney cells, the greatest reductions in mutations occurred in the least active portions of the genome. These regions contain either no genes or genes that are not being used by the cells.

One possible explanation involves differences in DNA repair activity. Regions containing actively used genes already undergo frequent DNA repair, which may limit how much additional protection they receive if calorie restriction reduces DNA damage. Less active regions, where repair occurs less frequently, could experience a larger reduction in accumulated mutations. The researchers have not yet established whether this mechanism explains the pattern.

“We didn’t expect to find this asymmetry across the genome,” said co-corresponding author Jonathan Shoag, MD, an associate professor in the Department of Urology at University Hospitals Cleveland and Case Western Reserve University School of Medicine. “By profiling more dietary interventions and mutations in additional tissues and cell types, we hope to learn what drives the connection between diet and mutations.”

The findings establish a connection between calorie restriction and lower mutation levels in mice, but they do not demonstrate that people would experience the same benefits. Sustained, substantial calorie restriction is also difficult to maintain and can carry health risks.

Researchers now hope to identify the biological processes responsible for the reduction in mutations. Understanding those processes could help scientists investigate approaches that protect DNA without requiring people to follow severely restricted diets.

“Understanding these mechanisms could eventually reveal new ways to reduce mutations – without the challenges and risks of an extreme diet—to counter diseases of aging,” said first study author Marta Grońska-Pęski, PhD, a postdoctoral fellow in Evrony’s laboratory.

The research relied on a specially established group of mice used to study the biological effects of calorie restriction. “We were fortunate to have collaborated with Dr. Victoria Acosta-Rodríguez, Dr. Joseph Takahashi, and Dr. Carla Green, who established this mouse caloric restriction cohort,” said Dr. Evrony.

Reference: “Caloric restriction modulates genome-wide somatic mutation in mice” by Marta Grońska-Pęski, Victoria Acosta-Rodríguez, Amoolya Srinivasa, Amanda Garrido, Shany Picciotto, Carla B. Green, Joseph S. Takahashi, Jonathan E. Shoag and Gilad D. Evrony, 9 September 2026, Cell. DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.013

Mourinho geht auf die Knie: Slapstick und Traum-Kombination: Mbappé-Doppelpack schockt Barcelona

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Dank eines Doppelpacks von Superstar Kylian Mbappé hat Real Madrid den Clásico gegen den FC Barcelona für sich entschieden. Die Königlichen gewannen bei Barça mit 2:1 und sorgten damit für noch mehr Spannung in der spanischen Fußball-Liga. Durch den Auswärtssieg beträgt der Rückstand von Real als Tabellendritter (24 Punkte) auf Spitzenreiter Barcelona (27) nur noch drei Zähler. Zweiter ist Atlético Madrid mit 25 Zählern. Für Barça unter Trainer Hansi Flick war es die erste Pleite in der Liga in dieser Saison nach zuvor neun Siegen zum Auftakt.

Real ging durch Mbappé kurz vor der Pause in Führung (41. Minute), als er im Slapstick-Stil den Ball ans Knie geschossen bekam. Nachdem Raphinha für die Hausherren ausgeglichen hatte (61.), sorgte Mbappé in der Schlussphase für den viel umjubelten Siegtreffer der Gäste (87.). Zuvor behauptete der ehemalige Dortmunder Jude Bellingham mit einem traumhaften Dribbling den Ball im Mittelfeld und passte per Hacke auf Eduardo Camavinga. Der Franzose überrannte in beeindruckender Manier Barcelonas Pau Cubarsí und passte zentimetergenau auf den Doppeltorschützen Mbappé.

Reals Coach José Mourinho musste in dem Topspiel auf den in der Liga gesperrten und aktuell formschwachen Vinícius Júnior verzichten. Das Tor zum 2:1 feierte der Trainer (fast) wie in seinen besten Jahren rutschend auf den Knie.

Zudem fiel für Real Torwart Thibaut Courtois wegen eines Infekts aus. Flick musste derweil schon in der ersten Hälfte den ersten Wechsel vornehmen. Für den verletzten Xavi Espart kam in der 30. Minute Alejandro Balde in die Partie.

Zwischen den beiden Schwergewichten in Spanien entwickelte sich von Beginn an eine ausgeglichene Partie. Richtig gefährlich wurden beide Teams aber nur selten. Das 1:0 der Madrilenen durch Mbappé war eher ein Zufallsprodukt. Nach einer Hereingabe des Ex-Leipzigers Yan Diomande schoss Barcelonas Gerard Martín bei einer Abwehraktion Mbappé an.

Nach dem Ausgleich durch Raphinha in der zweiten Hälfte drängten die Katalanen auf den Sieg. Dann aber sorgte Mbappé für die erneute Führung der Madrilenen - und den Sieg im Clásico.

Serbia: crowds celebrate in Belgrade after Vučić admits defeat

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Supporters of the student-backed opposition movement celebrated the announcement, marking a major political turning point after nearly two years of demonstrations against his government. Earlier, election officials had begun counting ballots at polling stations across the capital as voting closed. Preliminary figures released by Serbia's election commission indicated the country's highest parliamentary election turnout in more than three decades, reflecting the scale of public participation in a closely watched contest between the governing party and a broad opposition movement. The election followed a prolonged wave of protests that began after the collapse of a concrete canopy at Novi Sad railway station in November 2024, killing 16 people. The disaster triggered widespread anger over alleged corruption and failures of public oversight, with students emerging as the driving force behind demonstrations that spread across Serbia. Their movement gradually brought together people from different political backgrounds, united in demanding accountability and political change. Vučić's concession on Sunday brought a dramatic conclusion to the electoral contest, with supporters of the student-led movement gathering in Belgrade to celebrate the defeat of the party that had dominated Serbian politics for years.

Deeply divided Israel braces for pivotal election

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Watch: Why Israel's election is one of the most important in its history

After three years of regional war, this is a battle Israel always knew was coming.

The general election on Tuesday hangs over a country that's deeply divided and ready for a fight.

Polls published on Friday put the governing coalition and the main opposition bloc almost neck-and-neck, with half a million first-time voters due to cast their ballot and some 70,000 Israelis reportedly flying in from abroad specifically to vote.

On Saturday night, vast crowds of opposition voters gathered in central Tel Aviv to demand a change of government.

"We're optimistic," 21-year-old Omri told me. "I want peace, I want security, and I want to end corruption and get the religious fanatics out."

This campaign has been energized by new tactics - including AI videos designed to grab the headlines - and also by a new face.

Gadi Eisenkot is, perhaps, not the man you might expect to take on the flashy, aggressive, media-savvy brand of Israel's longest-serving prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu.

Relatively new to politics, with a working-class Moroccan background, Eisenkot is a former military chief of staff who is renowned for his calm manner and low-drama approach. He's been dubbed by some as a "non-politician".

But he and his centrist Yashar party have regularly pipped Netanyahu and his Likud party to the top spot in recent polls, the last of which was published on Friday, in line with Israeli election law.

Israeli politics is the art of coalition-building, rather than the task of any one party winning power, but with the predicted result too close to call, Eisenkot's rising profile has sharpened a race many Israelis already see as existential for the future of their country.

I watched him slip past the cameras at a community centre for Ethiopian Jews in the city of Ramle last week, through a gaggle of security personnel and advisors.

One of those who had come to hear him speak was David, a smartly dressed man in a freshly-pressed shirt and navy jacket, who said he had met Eisenkot before.

"He looks you in the eye, not like a leader looking down at someone," David told me. "And he listens. He's very sympathetic."

Eisenkot's image as a "non-politician" has proved popular

Eisenkot's appeal is often put down to his personal manner and profile. A softly-spoken military man whose son was killed during the Gaza War, he has presented himself as coming "from the periphery", not from Israel's wealthy urban elite.

His Moroccan background became election headlines earlier this month when a pro-Netanyahu speaker used a racist slur to refer to Eisenkot during a TV debate, referring to him as a Moroccan "monkey".

"These elections are about many issues," Eisenkot told the crowd gathered at the Ramle community centre. "They are about security, economy, education...but primarily, they are about the identity of the State of Israel, and what kind of Israel we want to see. A Jewish state, a democratic state, a liberal, tolerant, egalitarian state."

This is music to opposition ears, who say Benjamin Netanyahu's government is a threat to Israel's democracy, pointing to his efforts to curb the powers of the judicial system, and his refusal to establish a state commission of inquiry into the 7 October 2023 attacks on the grounds that the country's Supreme Court would be biased against him.

Eisenkot, along with other opposition leaders, has attempted to draw voters away from Netanyahu's right-wing Likud party by promising such a state inquiry, which a recent survey suggests is supported by more than 70% of Jewish Israelis.

Protesters gathered in Tel Aviv on Saturday to demand a state commission of enquiry into the October 7 attacks

He has also vowed to enforce court rulings on drafting ultra-Orthodox Jews into national military service - another measure that is popular with right-wing voters and opposition alike.

Benjamin Netanyahu has hit back with a campaign focused on Israel's security, and a core campaign slogan of "No Surrender".

He has repeatedly cast his rivals as soft on security, playing on the fears of some right-wing voters that Jewish opposition parties might ally with Israel's Arab parties to secure a majority, and suggesting this could compromise national security.

Around one-fifth of Israel's population is Arab - many of whom describe themselves as Palestinian citizens of Israel.

These are the first elections since the 7 October attacks, in which Hamas-led gunmen stormed through Gaza's border fence, killing almost 1,200 people and taking 251 hostage.

It was Israel's worst security failure, but three years on its prime minister is again running for re-election as the only man who can keep Israel safe.

"If the army had woken him in time, it wouldn't have happened," Shlomi Bashar told me, one of several die-hard Netanyahu fans drinking in a basement cafÃ© in Jerusalem's Mahane Yehuda market. "He does bear a certain responsibility, but after the 7 October, he changed the entire Middle East."

Netanyahu has been hammering home the message that Israel's wars in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran, in the years after the 7 October attacks, have reduced existential threats to the country.

"We need him to be re-elected so that he can finish this work," said Shlomi. "In my opinion, he is one of the best leaders in the world. Most of the world loves him and respects him, because he is a strong man and his ideas are correct."

Shlomi Bashar is a long-time Netanyahu supporter

The prime minister has traditionally relied on a stable base of right-wing voters, but is projected to see a thinning of support in this election. Some voters may be slipping away to opposition parties like Eisenkot's. Others, including some die-hard Likud voters we spoke to, said the 7 October attacks had pushed them towards parties further on the right.

Lt Col (Res) Eyal Hajbi, head of the regional civil defence unit in the Netanyahu stronghold of Sderot, right on the Gaza border, lost several friends and colleagues in the attacks. He lights candles for them every day in his office, and told me he would be thinking about them when he cast his ballot on Tuesday.

Standing on a ridge overlooking the ruins of northern Gaza, he gestured to the wasteland where Israel's army has completely flattened the neighbourhoods of Beit Lahia and Beit Hanoun.

"As far as I'm concerned, after 7 October, we should wipe them all out," he said. "I've changed my understanding of Gaza. Before, I wouldn't have said we should destroy it."

He told me he and his family had always voted Likud, but that now he was voting for the far-right party of Itamar Ben Gvir, Jewish Power.

"My family has become more extreme than it ever was before," he said. "If once we deliberated over it, today we don't think twice; we vote."

Polls suggest that less than 10% of Israelis are undecided and that very few of those are considering switching between government parties and opposition ones.

Ben Gvir, National Security minister in Netanyahu's coalition, has been reaching out to younger voters through headline-grabbing social media campaigns.

A poll for one of Israel's leading newspapers, Yedioth Ahronoth, published on 9 October, suggested that more than 16% of first-time voters in Israel would choose far-right parties like his.

In Sderot, a group of 18 year olds from a nearby Jewish religious college told me their political views had been affected by the 7 October attacks far more than their parents. All four teenagers were planning to vote for another far-right party, Religious Zionism, led by Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich.

"Today, the whole younger generation understands that peaceful paths will not work," Aviad Elmahach, one of the teenagers, told me. "Older adults, who are set in a fixed mindset, find [it] harder to change, but the youth understand that we need to be here with strength and an iron fist, which is the only way to win."

The lesson of 7 October, he said, was that Israel needed to be as forceful as possible in its relations with the Palestinians, more than 74,000 of whom have been killed by Israel in Gaza since then, according to the Hamas-run health ministry, whose figures are considered reliable by the UN.

"The youth have a fresh new perspective that has not yet become entrenched in politics, and they understand very well why we're voting for Smotrich - it means holding the land with strength and giving [the Palestinians] nothing through peaceful gestures."

Israel's military campaign in Gaza has left much of the territory in ruins

A little further south along the Gaza border are the kibbutzim that were overrun by gunmen during the Hamas attacks. They were renowned for decades as bastions of the Israeli left.

Among them is Nir Oz, where 47 people were killed and 76 taken hostage that day.

Three years on, residents are moving back. Twenty-six-year-old Gal Pauker has opened a Friday wine cafÃ© at the edge of his small vineyard next to the Gaza border - a few wooden tables set out under sheets of beige canvas to shade customers from the strong desert sun. His grandfather Gideon was killed during the 7 October attacks.

I asked Gal, and his mother Galia, whether the those attacks had shifted their political views.

Nir Oz was one of the worst hit kibbutzim on 7 October

"We need a change - we can't live with this government anymore," Galia said. "And I'm not talking about security, I'm talking first about humanity."

Having voted for centrist parties in the past, Galia says she's decided to vote for Eisenkot this time around, because "he's an ordinary guy and I hope he'll continue the way he's talking".

Her son Gal said he was deciding between Eisenkot and the left-leaning Democrats party of Yair Golan, another former army general.

"I'm not naÃ¯ve. I know there are a lot of more militant voices from young generations," he said. "I don't know if Eisenkot will be better than Golan in terms of security, but I think we have bigger problems: economics, social problems, education, health. We didn't get new neighbours on October 7 - it was a major event, but didn't change how we see ourselves."

Threats to security versus threats to democracy - these are old political tramlines for Israeli voters, deepened by the 7 October attacks, and by four years of the most right-wing government in Israel's history.

But on some of the biggest international concerns, the differences between government and opposition are not as wide as it might seem.

Gadi Eisenkot has pledged to end Hamas rule of Gaza within 18 months, either through a US-led peace plan, or through "military and operational capabilities".

All the main opposition leaders have voiced support for some form of Israeli settlement in the occupied West Bank, which has expanded massively under the current government and is illegal under international law. None have openly committed to the idea of full Palestinian statehood, something Benjamin Netanyahu has vowed to block. Eisenkot has spoken about creating the conditions for "autonomy or state-minus" under ultimate Israeli security control.

Tuesday's vote could well end in deadlock, with neither government nor opposition able to form a majority without the support of Arab parties, which polls project could win enough seats to be kingmakers. All but one of Israel's Jewish opposition parties have so far rejected working with either of the two Arab party blocs.

With Benjamin Netanyahu ramping up warnings of fresh threats from Iran, opposition voices warning of potential disruption to the ballot, and Israeli rights activists warning of a further spike in violence by settlers in the occupied West Bank, political instability at home could leave Israelis facing both a fresh test of their democracy, and fresh security threats.

Las dos compañías se han reunido con el Ministerio de Industria para plantear la posibilidad de lanzar un programa de modernización militar específico para el proyecto.

Seat, propiedad del grupo alemán Volkswagen, está abierta a diversificar su negocio y una de las opciones que tantea es entrar en la industria de la Defensa, una posibilidad que ya ha abordado con varias empresas en los últimos meses.

Según ha reconocido su presidente, Markus Haupt, la empresa ha sido contactada tanto por el Gobierno como por el Ejecutivo catalán para poner sus fábricas al servicio de las necesidades militares, un sector que está afrontando unos severos cuellos de botella en las entregas de pedidos, ya que hay una gran demanda y no tanta capacidad industrial disponible de manera inmediata.

Una de las propuestas en firme que tiene Seat sobre la mesa en estos momentos es la del grupo tecnológico catalán Aggity, que, como adelantó EXPANSIÓN la semana pasada, está conformando un consorcio de drones civiles y militares y busca un socio con capacidad para poder fabricarlos en serie.

Fuentes al tanto de la situación aseguran que Seat ha catalogado el proyecto, que contemplaría la producción de drones en la planta de la empresa en Martorell (Barcelona), de "estratégico", aunque sigue evaluando las sinergias que pueden existir entre la industria automovilística, acostumbrada a volúmenes muy altos de producción, y la industria de Defensa, con cifras más reducidas y con unas fuertes barreras de entrada en forma de homologaciones, permisos y controles de seguridad.

Mientras, las conversaciones avanzan. Las dos compañías mantuvieron la semana pasada un encuentro con el Ministerio de Industria para plantear la posibilidad de acceder a financiación pública a través de un Programa Especial de Modernización (PEM) de Defensa específico para impulsar este proyecto, que tendría carácter dual (civil y militar).

Para articular esta iniciativa se vislumbran dos consorcios. El primero, liderado por Aggity, combina el software y la infraestructura necesaria para la operación de los drones y del mismo forman parte Cargo Drone Solutions (operadora certificada), Bluenest (encargada de los vertipuertos), XRF (compañía participada por la OTAN que aporta tecnología de mando y control) y Aggity con su software.

Seat entraría en un segundo consorcio, en el que aportaría su capacidad industrial con una nueva línea dedicada a la fabricación de los drones en su planta de Martorell.

De hecho, ya habría un primer pedido de 500 drones por parte del Ministerio para la Transición Ecológica de cara a la temporada de incendios del año que viene, si bien se trata de una solicitud verbal que todavía no ha sido trasladada a un contrato público, señalan las fuentes consultadas por EXPANSIÓN.

En el marco de las conversaciones de Seat y Aggity con el Ministerio de Industria para captar la financiación pública necesaria para impulsar y poner en marcha el proyecto, uno de los principales escollos tiene que ver con la cercanía de las elecciones generales del 29 de noviembre, dado que un cambio de signo en Moncloa supondría la llegada de nuevos interlocutores ministeriales.

La cartera liderada por Jordi Hereu ha manifestado interés en el proyecto, pero ha indicado a las empresas que el proyecto debe pasar el filtro del Ministerio de Defensa, que recibe multitud de propuestas de este tipo debido al auge del sector.

Las fuentes consultadas apuntan que Aggity ha mantenido diferentes encuentros con el departamento que encabeza Margarita Robles y que una de las cuestiones que se le habría trasladado al grupo catalán es si la presencia de Seat es excluyente. Además, apuntan que Aggity lleva varios meses en conversaciones con Seat y que es con la empresa con la que quiere sacar adelante la propuesta. El proyecto abriría la puerta a Seat a un negocio en auge como es el de los drones y facilitaría que pudiese obtener las certificaciones aeronáuticas para el diseño, fabricación y mantenimiento de estos equipos.

Además de Seat, el Gobierno también tanteó a Ford para saber hasta qué punto podría contar con su capacidad industrial para futuros programas militares. También para saber cuáles son las capacidades nacionales inmediatas ante necesidades productivas. Sin embargo, la opción de usar las instalaciones de Ford en Almussafes es ahora más complicada debido al acuerdo cerrado con la china Geely.

Las fuentes señalan que, de momento, no hay ningún proyecto confirmado, pese a las reuniones "preliminares", que han sido de perfil técnico y que se suman a otras muchas que está manteniendo Seat con empresas del sector.

En caso de que el proyecto no consiga la financiación pública necesaria en España, Aggity tiene otra "oferta sólida" para sacar adelante su negocio de drones.

Se trata de una gran empresa india, aunque no se ha desvelado el nombre ni el sector al que se dedica la compañía.

En esta búsqueda de financiación pública, se espera que esta semana haya un encuentro entre Aggity y la Generalitat de Cataluña para abordar cómo impulsar este proyecto.

Las fuentes recalcan que el proyecto sería inviable sin financiación pública debido al volumen de inversión necesaria para ponerlo en marcha.

Estimated at £1.9bn (ranging from £1.6bn to £2.1bn by the Cyber Monitoring Centre), the cyber-attack was the most economically damaging in UK history. In June, a New York Times investigation linked the attack to a Russian hacking group.

Al Carns, former armed forces minister, made the comments in July at an event at the Chatham House think tank. He was asked about how the UK could deter “threats in the absence of political leadership”, according to a report in The Telegraph.

Mr Carns reportedly replied: “We have all the detail. We know all of the information, we know all of the threats. Sometimes it comes out sporadically, but it’s not a centrally coherent national conversation between government and society.

Mr Carns told The Telegraph that his comments were not based on an official Ministry of Defence assessment, adding: “It would be highly unlikely for an attack of that scale to emanate from anywhere without the host state knowing about it”.

Momentum is building in Illinois to form a new state, with more counties ready to vote on leaving because of Chicago’s influence.

Eleven counties are set to vote on non-binding referenda this year to secede from Cook County. If all 11 vote to leave they would join 33 counties that have already approved similar measures.

Cook County is home to Chicago, but people in the state and nation have criticized it for its crime rate, cost of living, Democratic leadership and oversized influence on state politics.

Loret Newlin, director of Illinois Separation, said Illinois leadership does not take concerns from other parts of the state into consideration.

“This is our lived experience. We are ignored. We are not taken into consideration when they’re making a lot of the laws that hurt us,” she told NBC Chicago.

Iroquois County, just 70 miles south of Cook County, is one of 33 counties that have voted to leave the state. About 73 percent of its voters backed the non-binding proposal.

The state’s 8th most populous county, Madison County, also joined the movement.

“Having Madison and Iroquois on the map, we are no longer rural only and we are not southern only,” Newlin told NBC Chicago. “Now with adding several counties on the western side, that’s equally far north as Iroquois, that’s shoring up that we’re not just for southern Illinois anymore.”

Madison County approved the vote in 2024 and one of the organizers said it’s more than just a symbolic measure.

“I think this movement has a lot more teeth to it than the governor has given it credit for. He's kind of pooh-poohing us and not taking us very seriously,” Dave Stopher, who helped get the measure before voters in Madison County said to NPR. “We're hoping that this sends a very distinct and clear message to Springfield and to Cook County.”

Newlin said the votes are the first step to creating a new state. The group’s plan is based on Article IV of the U.S. Constitution, which was used to create West Virginia during the Civil War.

The state’s General Assembly would have to approve the counties leaving the state, then the U.S. Congress would need to approve the new state.

Gov. JB Pritzker has previously referred to the movement as a “stunt.”

If the 11 counties approve the measure this year, it would bring the total to 44 of the state’s 102 counties that want to leave. Newlin said other counties have also asked her about getting the measure to their voters.
