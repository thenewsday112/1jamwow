Popular cannabis sleep gummies may have a hidden dark side

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Cannabinol (CBN) has become a popular ingredient in sleep supplements, often promoted as a natural, gentle way to relax and get a better night's rest. However, new research from the University of Connecticut suggests that this widely available cannabis compound may have risks that consumers should know about.

Scientists found that repeated exposure to CBN caused mice to develop signs of physical dependence. The findings, published in the November issue of the journal Drug and Alcohol Dependence, raise questions about the safety of using CBN regularly, particularly over long periods.

"CBN is everywhere right now. Gummies, tinctures, capsules, and it's marketed as a safe, herbal sleep supplement," says Steven Kinsey, professor in UConn's Elisabeth DeLuca School of Nursing. "Our findings suggest consumers should be aware repeated use may carry risks."

Popular Sleep Aid Shows Effects Similar to THC

CBN is a naturally occurring compound in cannabis, along with THC (tetrahydrocannabinol) and CBD (cannabidiol). Although CBN is generally considered less psychoactive than THC, the researchers discovered that it affects some of the same biological systems and can produce several key signs associated with drug dependence.

In the experiments, mice received CBN twice a day for three consecutive days. As treatment continued, the animals became less responsive to the compound, indicating that they had developed tolerance. When CBN was withdrawn, the mice also displayed physical symptoms resembling withdrawal, including head twitching and paw fluttering. These movements are comparable to the shaking that people can experience during drug withdrawal.

Another experiment revealed a surprising connection between CBN and THC. Mice previously trained to recognize and respond to THC's psychoactive effects reacted similarly when given CBN. This suggests that the two compounds produce overlapping subjective effects, at least in mice.

Together, the results indicate that CBN, despite its reputation as a milder cannabis compound, interacts with some of the same receptor systems that contribute to THC dependence.

Scientists Raise Concerns About CBN Sleep Supplements

The findings are particularly relevant because CBN products have become increasingly common, even though scientists still have limited information about their long-term effects.

The lead author of the study, Olivia Vanegas '26 Ph.D., notes "The marketing of minor cannabinoids is pretty wild right now. There are so many therapeutic claims about these drugs, but we really don't know much about their potential risks, especially with repeated use."

CBN is now widely promoted as a sleep aid, yet important questions remain unanswered. Researchers do not fully understand how CBN affects sleep quality or whether taking it repeatedly at doses commonly found in supplements could lead to addiction in humans.

There is also a historical reason for caution. Over the past century, several medications initially promoted as helpful sleep treatments were eventually found to carry serious risks of dependence and habit formation.

Potential Benefits Come With Unanswered Safety Questions

The researchers also identified potentially useful properties of CBN. The compound demonstrated anti-inflammatory effects, including at lower doses that did not produce psychoactive effects.

However, the experiments revealed another possible concern. During a simulated bacterial challenge, CBN reduced the body's ability to defend itself. This weakening of immune protection could make it easier for an infection to develop, similar to how bacteria can enter through a cut on the foot and cause illness.

These findings suggest that CBN may have medical applications, but scientists need a clearer understanding of its benefits, potential side effects, and appropriate doses before determining how it can be used safely.

Importantly, the experiments were conducted in mice, so the findings do not establish that CBN causes dependence or withdrawal in people. Additional studies are needed to understand how the compound affects humans, especially when taken frequently, at higher doses, or over extended periods.

"CBN may still have therapeutic value," he says. "But until we know more about dosing and long-term effects, the message for consumers is simple: buyer beware."

SÉRIE. Mythes, légendes et croyances de Tarn-et-Garonne. Pourquoi nous aimons nous faire peur ?

Article rédigé par Géraldine Jammet

l'essentiel À l’approche de la Toussaint, octobre est le mois idéal pour se plonger dans les histoires les plus mystérieuses du Tarn-et-Garonne. Mais derrière les récits de créatures fantastiques, les sources miraculeuses et les maisons prétendument hantées, que se cache-t-il vraiment ? Tout au long du mois, notre série "Mythes, légendes et croyances" remonte le fil des histoires qui nourrissent l’imaginaire local. Entre folklore, mémoire populaire et faits historiques, nous avons enquêté sur ces récits parfois lugubres, souvent étonnants, qui traversent les générations et continuent d’entretenir le mystère. Pour ce troisième opus, retour à la réalité avec l’abbé Georges Passerat, historien médiéviste qui décrypte les mécanismes de ces récits populaires et explique comment géographie, histoire et imaginaire se mêlent pour donner naissance aux légendes.

Il a un CV long comme le bras. Prêtre du diocèse de Montauban, historien médiéviste, occitaniste spécialiste de l’histoire religieuse du Midi et président de la Société archéologique et historique de Tarn-et-Garonne, l’abbé Georges Passerat a toujours été bercé par les contes et récits populaires de Comberouger. D’ailleurs, selon une croyance populaire, le nom de son village proviendrait du massacre des moines de Grandselve, qui auraient été indélicats avec des femmes du canton. À Montauban, il paraît que le musée Ingres-Bourdelle (MIB), bâti sur les vestiges du château des comtes de Toulouse, abrite un tunnel qui mène jusqu’à Montbeton. Histoire de s’échapper en cas d’attaque. Et un peu plus loin, sur le carreau de la place Nationale, une dalle est réputée pour figer net qui la foulera. « Ah celle-ci, je ne la connaissais pas ! », rigole Georges Passerat en feuilletant le livre du Tarnais Daniel Loddo, « Legendari d’Occitània », dans lequel il recense notamment les légendes, mythes et contes du Quercy*.

« À Bioule, près de Caussade, Gargantua est partout. On lui attribue notamment les buttes de Montalzat et Puylaroque dont on raconte qu’elles ont été formées parce que le géant avait de la terre collée à ses souliers. Et en les tapant, ça aurait créé ces deux collines », poursuit-il.

Pour le prêtre qui a enseigné plus de trente ans à l’Institut catholique de Toulouse (ICT), point de géants à l’horizon. Ni de dames blanches, de ponts du diable, de plantes maléfiques ou de trésor enfoui comme à Saint-Vincent d’Autéjac où des habitants ont confié aux folkloristes de La Talvera, basés à Cordes-sur-Ciel, qu’une peau de bœuf était remplie d’or dans un tunnel. « Cet informateur pensait que ce trésor avait un rapport avec les seigneurs du lieu-dit La Bastide ou Calvignac », écrit Loddo dans son ouvrage.« Les légendes constituent un thème que je connais depuis toujours, notamment grâce à la tradition orale. C’est toujours lié à la géographie à laquelle se greffe une histoire, qui dépasse vite la réalité. Les lacs, montagnes, sources ou grottes sont autant d’invariables dans ces récits types. Si on y ajoute la présence d’un château, d’une abbaye ou encore mieux, des Templiers, vous pouvez être sûrs que les gens vont chercher un trésor. Tout ceci provient de la mythologie populaire, des fantasmes liés à l’imaginaire », analyse l’abbé Passerat.

Légendes de fondation, de cloches enfouies, de guerres, de revenants, de métamorphoses, de lieux maudits et bien sûr, de saints : il existe un nombre incalculable de mythes et croyances qui ont traversé les siècles – et bien au-delà – grâce à la transmission orale et au travail documenté de spécialistes.

« La plupart de ces récits évoquent la vie de saints mais ce sont des contes, pas des documents historiques. D’ailleurs, le terme de legenda renvoie à la page que les moines doivent lire chaque jour. Toutes les grandes légendes de l’humanité ont pour origine la religion. »

Il faut reconnaître qu’il est bien difficile de recenser toutes les histoires de Vierges noires, comme celle de Castelferrus qui aurait été trouvée dans la cavité d’un arbre par un jeune vacher. « Toute la Bible est fondée sur le même schéma fonctionnel. J’ai fait de l’exégèse toute ma vie notamment pour comprendre comment les grands mythes se retrouvent dans les textes religieux et ailleurs. »

Pour Georges Passerat, ces récits transposables à l’infini « marchent à tous les coups car on y retrouve toujours un héros qui affronte des obstacles avec à la fois des adjuvants et des opposants ». « Ils permettent une évasion salutaire de l’esprit et de conjurer des peurs ancestrales ». D’ailleurs, « le catholicisme n’a rien inventé ». « La pierre, la roche, l’eau : ce sont des archétypes du Néolithique. En soi, il n’y a rien d’original mais ce qui en fait la saveur, c’est quand on a eu la chance de les cueillir directement sur les lèvres de conteurs. C’est pour cela que le travail de collecte ethnographique et de mémoire orale Al Cantou, mené en Aveyron puis en Tarn-et-Garonne, est précieux. Recueillir ainsi les récits à la source, ça se fait depuis les frères Grimm. Sauf que de nos jours, il y a un travail de relecture et de réinterprétation, notamment à la lumière des sciences du langage qui permet de déceler les couches successives de la tradition orale », précise le spécialiste.Mais il existe aussi une autre fonction à toutes ces histoires que l’on entend parfois depuis qu’on est gamins : « effrayer les enfants ».

« Lorsque nous allions tourner autour du puits, on nous criait que la Jambe crue allait nous manger. Il s’agit là d’un vieux conte lomagnol sur des parents très pauvres qui firent manger à leur enfant la jambe d’un mort. Après, on se servait de la légende de la Jambe crue pour empêcher les enfants de faire des bêtises. Quand on y réfléchit, c’est comme pour l’Ogre, ça renvoie à l’anthropophagie. Ça va loin quand même ! »* Georges Passerat animera une conférence intitulée « Les légendes populaires de notre Quercy » le samedi 7 novembre, de 16 h 30 à 18 heures, dans l’immeuble des Récollets, à Caussade. Entrée gratuite.

Les folkloristes et ethnologues Jean-François Bladé et Antonin Perbosc ont largement travaillé sur le sujet, en se basant sur des récits qu’on leur a rapportés à l’oral. ILLUSTRATION DDM MANU MASSIP

C’est une vieille créature du folklore occitan, présente jusque dans la mémoire populaire de Tarn-et-Garonne. Les archives et les travaux des ethnologues permettent d’établir une chose : des récits de Drac, dont l’étymologie renvoie au terme dragon, ont bien été recueillis sur le territoire. Direction Poupas, au XIXe siècle. Dans ses Contes populaires de la Gascogne, publiés en 1886, le folkloriste Jean-François Bladé raconte l’histoire d’un Drac lié à un maquignon du canton de Lavit. En échange de l’usage exclusif d’un mystérieux fouet de soie doté d’un manche en bois de Perpignan, le Drac s’occupe seul des basses besognes. « Il lui soignait si bien ses chevaux et ses juments, qu’on n’en aurait pas trouvés de si beaux aux foires d’Agen et de Toulouse », écrit Bladé. Mais le commerçant finit par utiliser le fouet interdit.

Dès la nuit suivante, des claquements et des hennissements résonnent dans l’écurie. Les chevaux sont en sueur. Le manège se répète chaque nuit. « Au bout de sept mois juste, on n’entendit plus rien. Peut-être le Drac fut-il chassé par les conjurations d’un grand devin que le maquignon fit venir des Landes. Peut-être aussi le Drac se trouva-t-il assez vengé, et s’en alla-t-il de lui-même quelque autre part », observe Bladé. L’identité du marchand reste inconnue. L’auteur précise seulement que le récit lui a été dicté par Pierre Lalanne, un habitant de Lectoure (Gers).

Né en 1861 à Labarthe, Antonin Perbosc a lui aussi consacré une grande partie de sa vie à recueillir la littérature orale du Quercy et des régions voisines. Selon lui, le Drac était plus malicieux que véritablement dangereux.

Dans une étude publiée en 1941, intitulée « Mythologie populaire : Le Drac, l’Étouffe-Vieille et le Matagot d’après les traditions occitanes », Perbosc indique avoir « recueilli personnellement à Comberouger (Tarn-et-Garonne) » trois récits mettant en scène le démon. Dans l’un, une bûche devient de plus en plus lourde avant de se redresser dans l’âtre et de disparaître en riant. Dans un autre, un mouton rencontré la nuit se met à parler. Une habitante emporte aussi un chat dans son tablier, mais le trouve si lourd qu’elle l’abandonne au pied d’un griottier. L’animal l’avait invitée, en patois, à le rejoindre pour grignoter les fruits. « Mais la femme détala au galop, sans jamais se retourner : elle comprit, raconta-t-elle, que ce chat était un diableteau », écrit Antonin Perbosc.

S’il y a bien quelqu’un qui a inspiré l’abbé Georges Passerat, c’est Antonin Perbosc qui a officié comme instituteur à Comberouger, de 1893 à 1908. « Il a adopté une méthode unique qui consistait à se servir de ses élèves comme de folkloristes en culotte courte. C’est ainsi qu’il les a mandatés pour recueillir, à la main et en gascon, tous les contes du coin directement auprès de leur grand-mère ou arrière grand mère. De cette manière, il a permis d’éviter les intermédiaires. Les récits étaient racontés de manière naturelle sans le truchement de la présence d’un inconnu », explique l’abbé Passerat. Tous ces récits, conservés au fonds Perbosc de la bibliothèque municipale de Toulouse, ont abouti à un premier recueil, « Contes de la vallée du Lambon », paru dès 1914. Il a fallu attendre plus de cent ans, grâce au travail de Josiane Bru et Jean Eygun, pour accéder à l’ouvrage complet intitulé « Au país de la gata blanca - Contes de Comberouger ».

À Saint-Sardos, le Drac s’est glissé dans une autre histoire : celle du trésor de Grand-Selve. Sur son site, Tarn-et-Garonne Tourisme raconte que l’abbaye, présentée comme l’une des plus riches de Gascogne, aurait possédé d’importantes richesses. Un abbé nommé Alexandre II Farnèse aurait caché un coffre rempli d’or et de biens dans les bois alentour. La légende affirme que le trésor n’a jamais été retrouvé. Sauf peut-être par le Drac, « l’espiègle lutin d’oc », qui aurait détenu l’une des pièces d’or. En 2010, une chasse au trésor organisée autour du lac de Saint-Sardos évoquait déjà les « traces du trésor du Drac ».

Mais l’origine de cette association entre le Drac, Grand-Selve et le trésor reste à éclaircir : dans les sources folkloriques anciennes consultées, nous n’en avons retrouvé aucune trace. La légende est bien vivante mais son histoire reste à écrire.

"Une femme à la tête d'un garage, ça peut surprendre" : elle a repris le garage automobile de ce petit village du Lot et compte bien le développer

La cheffe d'entreprise préfère se recentrer sur son activité à laquelle se joindra totalement son mari. Une entreprise qui semble prendre la bonne direction sous les ordres du couple.

Independent candidates sense a moment. Some Democrats hope they're right.

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Pins and bumper stickers supporting Dan Osborn's candidacy for U.S. Senate sit on a campaign table on Sept. 3, 2026 at Pals Brewing Company in North Platte, Neb. Osborn, a 51-year-old Navy veteran, is running as an independent. Austin Johnson/AFP via Getty Images hide caption

BURWELL, NEBRASKA – Tucked in the rolling Sandhills, the largest sand dune formation in America, sits Morgan Ranch, a 15,000-acre, family-run operation home to 1,000 cows and thousands of offspring.

"There's a lot of mouths to feed — both animals and two-legged," laughed Dan Morgan. His ranch has one of the largest privately-owned Wagyu herds in the U.S.

He said his job has given him a front row seat to what he calls the "uncertainty" of President Trump's policies.

"To raise a piece of meat takes me three years," Morgan said. "Well, look at the changes in the past two years: costs, tariffs, reciprocal tariffs because half of our business is export — it's just hell," he said.

He's a Democrat — a self-described small blue dot in a sea of red, in a county and state that President Trump dominated two years ago.

There's no Democrat running for the Senate in Nebraska, but Morgan is excited by independent candidate Dan Osborn.

"Osborn makes the case that somebody needs to represent the working family," Morgan said. "And he can effectively do that."

Osborn is one of four independents running for Senate in states President Trump won handily, including Idaho, South Dakota and Montana. Buoyed by the president's sinking approval ratings and public frustration over affordability, independent candidates argue they bring a winning case to voters.

It is an argument that some Democrats hope will resonate with voters as the party looks to retake control of Congress, betting that these candidates can help topple Republicans in parts of the country where their own brand is hurting.

"I'm a partisan Democrat. I believe in the Democratic Party. I also believe in building coalitions so we can win elections and then do the work of the people," said Jane Kleeb, the chair of the Nebraska Democratic Party. "And if that means creating coalitions with independents in some of our states, then that's exactly what we need to do."

But while these independents are generally unified in their political philosophy that the two-party system is toxic and has led to legislative intransigence, the challenges they face in their respective races are different, in large part because of the role the Democratic Party has decided to play.

Seth Bodnar, a former Green Beret running for Senate in Montana, said voters he talks to are fed up with politics as usual — and that he offers a viable alternative as an independent.

"When I go to Washington, D.C., I'm not going to put on a red jersey with an 'R' or a blue jersey with a 'D.' I'm going to have a jersey with an 'M' on it, and I'm going to work for Montana," Bodnar said in an interview from the homecoming parade at the University of Montana, where he used to be president.

Like Osborn, Bodnar said if elected, he won't caucus with either party. The current two independents in the Senate, Bernie Sanders of Vermont and Angus King of Maine, both caucus with Democrats.

His theory of the case is that in a closely-divided Senate, an independent willing to work with either party — but beholden to neither — actually holds significant strength.

Seth Bodnar, an independent candidate for a U.S. Senate seat in Montana, speaks to supporters and the press outside the Yellowstone County Courthouse, Aug. 7, in Billings, Mont. Mathew Brown/AP hide caption

"I think there is real power in an independent or multiple independents that can work together, even with people who maybe don't leave their caucus but form an independent-minded bloc, that can move forward common sense solutions that this country so desperately needs," Bodnar said.

The race has been marked by frustration among Democrats — at either Bodnar for not running as a Democrat himself, or at the Democratic nominee, Alani Bankhead, for not dropping out and creating a glide path for Bodnar.

"Had Bodnar run as a Democrat, he'd be leading in this election," said former Democratic Gov. Brian Schweitzer.

"At the end of the day, there will be a lot of people that look in the mirror and say, 'Oh, my God, if we would have just run a Democrat without an independent, we would have won this election,'" Schweitzer said.

Many prominent Montana Democrats — like former Sen. Jon Tester and former Gov. Steve Bullock — have endorsed Bodnar, setting up a divide between Democrats supporting their own party's nominee and those supporting the independent.

"It's just been really, really contentious and quite a bitter thing within the state party," said Rob Saldin, political science professor at the University of Montana.

Saldin said unlike states like Idaho, which has deep Republican entrenchment, Montana Democrats aren't as "beat down" and look at the election as a chance to rebuild the brand in the state.

"They want somebody who, for years into the future, is going to be able to help bring money into the state Democratic Party, to help down-ballot candidates for the legislature, to be the face of the party in the state," he said. "And when you've got an independent candidate who's saying, 'a pox on both your houses,' this is not the guy who's going to be able to do that work in the trenches for the party."

There was an effort to get Bankhead to exit the race before the August deadline. Bankhead said she and her family were harassed. She also said "white gloved politicians" were subtly trying to tell her to drop out of the race.

Alani Bankhead, the Democratic candidate for Senate in Montana, is seen preparing for a rally in East Helena, Mont. on Aug. 10, 2026 — the last day that ballots could be changed. Bankhead verified she was not dropping out of the race. Clayton Murphy/Montana Public Radio hide caption

"They were very polite. They didn't ask me to drop out, but it was subtle — like, 'Well, what's the goal and what kind of job do you want down the road?'" she said, adding she heard from a sitting senator, sitting governor and former cabinet official. "Nobody from the [Democratic National Committee] was like, 'drop out' but that was certainly the nudge."

Bankhead claimed in an interview with Montana PBS that she was offered a DNC job if she dropped out of the race. The DNC denies this.

Bankhead said if Democrats want to support independents — there's a better way to do it.

"Nebraska was very transparent, right? The Democratic nominee said they would step aside in favor of the independent if the people voted for them. And that's very transparent — that's great."

Kleeb, the Nebraska Democratic Party chair and a DNC vice chair, said Nebraska Democrats were very conscious about being transparent regarding their intentions to support the independent on the ticket.

Your customer service problem isn’t a customer service problem

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A few years ago, one of our team members came down with a big health problem—a splitting headache that just wouldn’t let up. She visited several doctors, who recommended various treatments and medications to alleviate the pain, none of which worked. The pain was so intense that it made it difficult to carry out basic tasks, affecting both her personal and professional lives.

After a few days of nonstop pain, she decided to try a different route and visit a chiropractor. The chiropractor listened, asked probing questions about her headache, then examined her from head to toe—leading to a surprising discovery. As it turned out, our team member’s jaw was out of alignment. The chiropractor proceeded to treat the misalignment, and almost immediately, the pain started to evaporate. The headache, though disruptive, was not the core problem to be solved. Instead, it was a symptom of a problem somewhere else in her body, specifically her misaligned jaw.

Over the years, our agency has been asked by many a customer to help them solve a major brand-related headache—poor customer experience. In much the same way our team member’s headache turned out to be a symptom rather than a root cause, we found in each and every one of these client engagements that the poor customer experience was a symptom of a much bigger problem—poor employee engagement. It wasn’t that the employees of these companies didn’t know what great customer experience looked, smelled, and tasted like. The problem was that they didn’t care enough to deliver that level of service.

The link between employee engagement and customer experience

When it comes to customer experience delivery, engaged employees will always outperform disengaged ones. As Timothy R. Clark, an author and organizational consultant, rightly notes, “Highly engaged employees make the customer experience. Disengaged employees break it.”

There is significant data to back up this claim. Research by Gallup showed that teams scoring in the top quartile on employee engagement enjoyed 10% higher customer loyalty/engagement. And research conducted by the Institute of Customer Service found that every 1% improvement in employee engagement leads to a 0.41 point increase in Customer Satisfaction Index scores. Based on these insights, it is clear that poor customer experience is the headache, while poor employee engagement is the misaligned jaw that needs to be treated.

Treat the cause, not just the symptom

I’ve spent nearly 20 years helping clients build brands that are loved by their employees and customers (in that order). Over that time, I’ve observed frustrated senior leaders with the best intentions trying to improve their poor customer experience delivery by commissioning customer service training, without doing anything about the actual cause of their subpar customer experience. If you want to get rid of the headache of poor customer experience, you have to treat the cause—not just the symptom. This means developing a sturdy employee engagement program that addresses the issue of poor engagement head-on.

Here are the steps I recommend my senior leadership clients take to elevate employee engagement to the same level of importance as other internal programs designed to strengthen organizational performance and ultimately deliver better business outcomes.

1. Make employee engagement a strategic priority

The first step in elevating the importance of employee engagement in your workplace is to make it a strategic priority rather than a “nice to have.” If customer experience is important enough to be discussed at senior leadership meetings or in the boardroom, so is employee engagement. If you don’t track employee engagement scores across the workplace, start doing so—today!

Make improving employee engagement scores a permanent agenda item for your senior leadership or board meetings. Begin treating it as a nonnegotiable priority, giving it the same discipline, attention, and urgency as any other core business metric.

2. Form your engagement unit and assign accountability

Your next step should be to form a team of experts with the skill sets needed to improve your employee engagement, and hold them accountable for doing so.

In modern workplaces, the responsibility of developing a culture where employees are highly engaged lies with Human Resources. However, some companies choose to form an employee engagement team that sits outside of HR on the organizational chart, while others may hire a specialized third-party employee engagement agency to do the heavy lifting. No matter which option you run with, hold your engagement team accountable by setting and documenting clear employee engagement goals, metrics, and key performance indicators for that team to aim for.

3. Specify desired outcomes

The most effective employee engagement programs are specific and measurable. Be sure to clearly articulate and document your employee engagement program’s goals, objectives, and success metrics so that your employee engagement team is aware of what they should be aiming for and how you define success.

4. Let employees cocreate your employee engagement programs

One of the biggest mistakes organizations make is designing employee engagement programs for employees rather than with employees. In doing so, they risk investing time, energy, money, and other resources in programs that don’t address the workplace issues employees actually care about. If employee engagement is the cumulative result of the experiences employees have within the workplace, then employees themselves are uniquely qualified to help identify what is working, what is not, and what needs to change in the workplace. 

Savvy business leaders involve their employees in the design, implementation, and evaluation of engagement initiatives. Empowering employees to cocreate your engagement programs increases the likelihood that employees will embrace those programs and that they’ll make a positive impact on the workplace environment.

5. Measure, monitor & refine

Chances are that as your engagement unit builds out your employee engagement programs, they’ll make some mistakes along the way. Be patient with your engagement team, and accept that some elements of your engagement programs may initially fall short of their desired impact.

With this in mind, be prepared to measure and monitor your employee engagement programs so you can refine and fine-tune them and, in the long run, develop programs that best fit your unique employee engagement needs.

Excellent customer experiences start with excellent employee experiences

If you have a customer experience headache, remember that poor customer experience is a symptom. If you want to eliminate the headache, you need to treat the cause—not just the symptom.

Invest resources in developing programs that engage employees with the same vigor as you engage customers. Then, follow up with customer experience training that your newly engaged employees will appreciate and benefit from. Only then will you see an improvement in your organization’s level of customer experience.

Melindres Gallegos: Secretos y Recetas del Dulce Tradicional

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Si hay un bocado que transporte a cualquiera directamente a las romerías y ferias de Galicia, son sin duda los melindres. Estos pequeños tesoros de la repostería gallega, con su forma de rosquilla y ese toque anisado tan particular, son mucho más que un simple postre; representan la esencia de las reuniones familiares y las fiestas populares de la comunidad.

Aunque se disfrutan en diversas comarcas, tienen un arraigo especial en Ferrolterra y sobre todo en Melide, donde se han convertido en un símbolo local. Es curioso cómo una receta aparentemente sencilla, basada en ingredientes básicos, puede variar tanto según el maestro pastelero o la zona, guardando cada familia su propio secreto para lograr esa textura seca que se deshace en la boca.

La historia de este dulce está profundamente ligada al Camino de Santiago y a la zona de Melide. Se dice que fueron los monjes de los monasterios cercanos quienes perfeccionaron la receta para crear un alimento energético y resistente que los peregrinos pudieran transportar sin que se estropeara durante su camino hacia Santiago de Compostela. Con el paso de los siglos, lo que empezó como un sustento para el viajero pasó a ser una tradición doméstica transmitida de abuelos a nietos.

En la actualidad, el melindre es el protagonista absoluto del segundo domingo de mayo en Melide, donde se celebra una fiesta gastronómica dedicada a la repostería tradicional. En este evento se rinde homenaje a la maestría de los obradores locales, destacando el melindre junto a otras delicias como el rico y el almendrado, manteniendo vivo un legado artesanal que resiste el paso del tiempo.

Para preparar unos melindres auténticos, no hace falta buscar ingredientes exóticos, ya que la magia reside en la calidad de los productos básicos. La base común suele incluir harina de trigo de repostería, huevos (especialmente yemas) y mantequilla o aceite, dependiendo de la variante. El elemento indispensable que define su identidad es el licor de anís, que aporta ese aroma tan nostálgico.

En cuanto al glaseado, que es donde reside gran parte de la dificultad, se utiliza una mezcla de azúcar y agua para crear un almíbar. Algunas versiones tradicionales añaden una clara de huevo montada al almíbar para conseguir un acabado blanco y opaco, mientras que otras buscan una capa más traslúcida y cristalina que deje ver el dorado del dulce.

Esta versión es ideal para quienes buscan un resultado más ligero y sencillo de ejecutar. Para empezar, se deben batir las yemas con la mantequilla derretida y el anís, incorporando la harina tamizada poco a poco hasta obtener una masa homogénea. Es fundamental amasar durante unos diez minutos hasta que la pasta esté lisa y no se pegue a las manos, dejándola reposar bajo un film durante una hora para que la masa se asiente.

Para darles forma, se crean pequeños cilindros que luego se enrollan sobre sí mismos formando un nudo o un aro. El horneado se realiza a una temperatura alta, generalmente entre 200ºC y 220ºC, durante unos 10 minutos, hasta que estén ligeramente dorados. Una vez fríos, se bañan en un almíbar de agua y azúcar calentado a fuego medio. El truco aquí es ser muy rápido con el glaseado, ya que el azúcar solidifica en cuestión de segundos al entrar en contacto con el dulce.

Para los amantes de los sabores más intensos, existe la versión frita, muy común en las ferias. En este caso, la masa puede llevar levadura química (Royal) y aceite de girasol u oliva en lugar de mantequilla. La preparación implica mezclar los huevos, el azúcar y el anís, añadiendo la harina y la levadura hasta formar una masa consistente. En lugar de nudos complejos, a veces se forman bolitas con un hueco en el centro hecho con el dedo.

La fritura debe hacerse en abundante aceite caliente pero sin llegar a humear para evitar que se quemen por fuera y queden crudos por dentro. Tras escurrirlos en papel absorbente, se procede al glaseado. Una técnica muy efectiva es montar una clara a punto de nieve e incorporarla al almíbar hirviendo, batiendo enérgicamente para crear una glasa blanca y espesa donde se sumergen los melindres rápidamente antes de dejarlos secar en una rejilla.

Si utilizas una Thermomix, puedes optimizar la mezcla de aceites y aromas, pero recuerda que para cantidades grandes es mejor terminar el amasado a mano para controlar la consistencia.
