German special forces preparing for war with Russia – commander

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Germany’s elite special forces are preparing for a potential large-scale war with Russia, the unit’s commander told Bild. The remarks come amid tensions between Moscow and NATO over the Ukraine conflict, with German officials warning that the armed forces must be ready for a confrontation by the end of the decade.

The highly secretive unit, known as Kommando Spezialkrafte (KSK), is comparable to Britain’s SAS or the US Green Berets. According to a report from its base in southwestern Germany published on Monday, the commandos are adjusting their training to counter Russian forces equipped with tanks, artillery, drones, and electronic warfare capabilities, including in extreme cold.

“We are orienting ourselves very specifically toward the adversary Russia,” KSK Commander Brigadier General Andreas Kuhne said, adding that his soldiers would have to be prepared to “remain in the field much longer,” potentially “with no end in sight,” if war broke out.

Kuhne also said the unit is preparing to carry out missions before conventional German forces enter combat. “When the 45th Armored Brigade in Lithuania starts its engines in a real emergency, we want to have already completed our job,” he told Bild.

According to the newspaper, only around a third of the KSK’s 1,500 personnel are commandos, while the rest serve in administrative and support roles.

The unit has been rocked by a series of far-right extremism scandals in recent years. In 2017, an investigation was launched following reports that soldiers performed Nazi salutes and listened to far-right music, among other things.

In 2018, investigations exposed an underground far-right network involving German military and security personnel, some of whom allegedly discussed plans to kill political opponents in the event of a breakdown of public order.

In 2020, police discovered a large cache of weapons, ammunition, explosives, and Nazi memorabilia at the property of a KSK sergeant major. Some of the ammunition was suspected of being stolen from German military stockpiles.

The scandals prompted then-Defense Minister Annegret Kramp-Karrenbauer to disband one of the KSK’s four commando companies, the 2nd Company, and introduce sweeping reforms. The special forces command itself remained operational.

Speaking to Bild, Kuhne said disciplinary and conduct-related incidents within the unit have become “extremely rare,” but have not disappeared entirely, insisting that misconduct would no longer be tolerated or covered up.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz has vowed to transform the German armed forces into Europe’s strongest conventional military force in response to what he describes as threats from Russia. His government has prioritized defense spending and military assistance to Ukraine despite acknowledging that the German economy is in a “structural crisis.”

In July, The Telegraph and Die Welt reported that Berlin was preparing a secret plan to override potential resistance from regional governments to the deployment of NATO troops toward Russia’s borders.

Merz’s military ambitions come amid declining public support for his government. According to an INSA poll published by Bild this week, the chancellor ranked last among 20 prominent German politicians. The surging opposition Alternative for Germany (AfD) won state elections in Saxony-Anhalt and Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania in September, while the socialist Left Party (Die Linke) emerged as the largest party in Berlin’s election.

Russia has denied that it plans to attack NATO countries. In August, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov dismissed reports that Moscow intends to attack NATO members as “scare stories” that have “nothing to do with reality.”

Alberta Premier fends off leadership challenge and looks to squelch separatist discord

Alberta Premier Danielle Smith has asked members to accept the result of the referendum, warning that an internal civil war over separation would sink the party ahead of next year’s provincial election.Darren Calabrese/The Canadian Press

Alberta Premier Danielle Smith appears to have fended off a leadership challenge and is looking to smother discord within her United Conservative Party by requiring those seeking a seat on its provincial board to pledge they will respect the province’s majority vote against holding a separation referendum.

In an e-mail sent to UCP members on Tuesday evening, party president Rob Smith said at least 65 of the 87 constituency associations have passed motions in support of the Premier, whose authority has been questioned by pockets of the party’s membership following last week’s referendum and ahead of next year’s provincial election. At least 22 associations would need to vote in favour of a leadership review in order to force one.

“There will be NO UCP leadership review before our next general election,” wrote Mr. Smith, who holds no relation to the Premier. He added that Ms. Smith will be the leader for the foreseeable future.

With the possibility of a leadership review set aside, Ms. Smith confirmed the UCP is now requiring those wishing to serve on its 18-seat provincial board to pledge that they will respect the result of last week’s referendum on separation.

Alberta’s vote has softened the rhetoric, but peace and quiet are not guaranteed

The requirement is contained in a mandatory declaration form that candidates applying to run for the party board must complete ahead of the UCP’s annual general meeting in late November.

The declaration asks candidates to agree they “recognize, respect, and will abide by the majority decision made by Albertans on Question #10 of the 26/10/19 referendum,” referring to the ballot question that asked residents whether they wish to remain in Canada or have a second, binding secession vote.

The pledge is the first evidence that Ms. Smith and the UCP are taking steps to prevent separatist agitators from attempting to control the party apparatus.

Factions within the independence movement have threatened to take over the party’s governance structures in the wake of the Oct. 19 vote, in which roughly 75 per cent of Albertans rejected the prospect of a future independence referendum.

Ms. Smith has asked members to accept the result, warning that an internal civil war over separation would sink the party ahead of next year’s provincial election. Those wishing to turn the party into a vehicle for separation would not have a home in the UCP, she said.

She had been unclear, however, about what measures she was willing to take to snuff out the separatist fire.

Asked on Tuesday at an unrelated news conference about the new declaration requirement for board candidates, Ms. Smith said: “If you’re going to be a member of our party, you have to accept what Albertans told us.”

Ms. Smith recently said the party had sold roughly 6,900 tickets to its AGM, which would break the party’s attendance record when nearly 6,100 party members showed up in Red Deer two years ago to reassert the Premier’s leadership.

The latest bid to defy her call for party unity and challenge her leadership happened on Monday night, when the Bonnyville-Cold Lake-St. Paul UCP constituency association in northern Alberta narrowly voted in favour of a leadership review. It was the fourth association to do so in the past two months.

Prominent separatist leader Mitch Sylvestre, who’s been vocal about his bid to stack the UCP board with pro-independence allies, is the association’s president.

The upcoming AGM and board elections are now the more pressing issues for Ms. Smith as advocates like Mr. Sylvestre push for like-minded members to vote for pro-independence candidates.

Ms. Smith reiterated her position that promoting separation is an incompatible position for those who speak on behalf of the UCP.

“If anyone wants to advocate for independence, we are not an independence party. That’s contrary to what Albertans told us,” she said.

“There’s lots of range for people to be able to advocate on policy, but just not that.”

Even so, people who emerged as prominent independence advocates are applying for board seats, indicating they support the Premier’s direction.

Mr. Scott said that while he still believes independence is “the only way to achieve the sovereignty Alberta needs,” the referendum result was decisive. “This is an opportunity where both sides can set the nationhood thing aside for now and focus on the sovereignty things that we should be doing if we’re ever to prepare to be a nation anyhow,” Mr. Scott said.

Drawn lot decides mayor of Lytton, B.C., after tied election

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Main Street in Lytton, B.C., in August, 2025.DARRYL DYCK/The Canadian Press

The new mayor in Lytton, B.C., a tiny village razed by a raging wildfire in 2021, has been determined by the drawing of lots after a tied election.

The village says Jennifer Thoss is the winner in a process conducted in front of a judge, resulting in the defeat of incumbent Denise O’Connor.

Both candidates received 45 votes in the Oct. 17 election and the random draw was conducted after a judicial recount at the Kamloops Provincial Court produced the same tally on Tuesday.

A similar process determined the mayor of Peachland, B.C., in 2018.

Thoss and O’Connor have expressed differing views on the village’s recovery from the wildfire that killed two people and destroyed most of Lytton’s structures, including its health centre, bank and municipal office.

Thoss, who was elected as a councillor in Lytton in 2022, has criticized the handling of the recovery and rebuilding process with O’Connor at the helm.

She told The Canadian Press last spring that more than $50 million in planned infrastructure funded largely by Ottawa would ultimately “bankrupt” the tiny village, where the shrunken tax base wouldn’t be able to sustain the operations costs.
