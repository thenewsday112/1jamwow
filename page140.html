Amgen says drug combo cuts risk of death by 58% in patients with aggressive lung cancer

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A drug from Amgen could give patients with a notoriously aggressive form of lung cancer significantly more time to live when added to an immunotherapy from AstraZeneca, a study found.

Full results from a late-stage trial released Saturday show that adding Amgen's immunotherapy Imdelltra to AstraZeneca's Imfinzi drug slashed the risk of death by 58% in patients with extensive-stage small cell lung cancer, a fast-growing disease that has spread beyond the lung where it began.

The drug combination extended their median overall survival to 21.1 months compared with 12.7 months with AstraZeneca's drug alone, the trial found.

That means half of patients in the group that took the combination regimen were alive for at least roughly 21 months. The trial tested the combo as a maintenance therapy in patients who received initial treatment with Imfinzi and chemotherapy, and whose cancer had not progressed.

"I think there are moments in medicine when you see a piece of data that is so profound that it's going to transform the standard of care for patients, and I think, honestly, we're at that moment," said Dr. Paul Burton, Amgen's chief medical officer, in an interview.

The combination "has the potential to take on one of the most aggressive cancers that we deal with today, and almost convert it into a chronic disease," he said, adding that "these data say that's a possibility."

The results also suggest a greater commercial opportunity for Imdelltra, which posted $288 million in sales during the second quarter, up 115% year over year. The drug already generated $627 million in global sales in 2025 and appears to be on its way to becoming a blockbuster medicine, or hitting the $1 billion sales threshold.

Small cell lung cancer remains difficult to treat because, even if chemotherapy initially works well for most patients, the disease often returns or progresses within months, said lead trial investigator Dr. Jacob Sands, who is the associate chief of the Lowe Center for Thoracic Oncology at Dana-Farber Cancer Institute.

He said immunotherapy has helped a small subset of patients achieve years of disease control, but most have not experienced similarly durable benefits, highlighting the need for better treatments. There are roughly 31,000 new small cell lung cancer cases in the U.S. each year, and an estimated 60% to 70% of those people are diagnosed with extensive-stage disease.

Sands said the benefits in the trial were seen across subgroups of patients, including those whose cancer spread to the brain or liver. Disease that has spread to those organs is associated with poorer treatment outcomes.

He added that with longer-term follow-up data on the patients, the 21-month figure "may evolve further and hopefully will substantially improve," similar to what Amgen saw in a Phase 1 trial on the immunotherapy combination. In that early-stage trial on a smaller number of patients, a long-term follow-up showed the benefit was sustained for years.

Amgen said the findings also suggest that using Imdelltra earlier in a patient's treatment could meaningfully alter the trajectory of the disease. Imdelltra is currently approved for patients whose small cell lung cancer has progressed while they are on, or after, platinum-based chemotherapy.

Researchers specifically tested whether adding Imdelltra could help patients live longer than continuing Imfinzi alone, which is already approved as a first treatment for this type of lung cancer. Amgen said nearly 80% of patients who started taking both drugs were alive at one year compared with 53% receiving Imfinzi alone.

Imdelltra's "big impact" on controlling the disease and ultimately how long people live highlights the importance of including it in first-line maintenance treatment, Sands said. That refers to ongoing therapy given after an initial heavy treatment, such as chemotherapy or surgery.

Burton added that it positions Amgen's drug to be an "indispensable backbone therapy" for small cell lung cancer, regardless of the other immunotherapy that can be paired with it.

Imdelltra binds to DLL3, a protein on cancer cells, and CD3, a protein on T cells, which are a type of immune cell that can kill cancer. By binding to both, Imdelltra brings T cells into close contact with tumor cells, helping the immune system destroy the cancer.

Imfinzi, made by AstraZeneca, is an immunotherapy that blocks a protein called PD-L1, which cancer cells use to evade the immune system. The drugs work together in complementary ways: Imfinzi helps remove the brakes on the immune response, while Imdelltra brings T cells directly to cancer cells and helps activate them to attack the tumor.

The study, which was presented at a major cancer meeting called the European Society for Medical Oncology Congress, did not test whether the combination was better than other first-line treatment approaches.

Still, the findings suggest Amgen's drug could be another option offered to small cell lung cancer patients earlier in their treatment. Kate Brenan, a 44-year-old mother of two from Denver, sees that as a win on its own.

She was diagnosed with extensive-stage small cell lung cancer in early January 2024. Brenan underwent six rounds of a common combination chemotherapy, followed by chest radiation and later whole-brain radiation after the cancer spread.

In September 2024, she started Imdelltra and has had no evidence of disease ever since.

"I think that it's remarkable how quickly things are changing in the world of small cancer treatment options," Brenan told CNBC, adding that Imdelltra potentially becoming an earlier treatment option "can only be a good thing for the patients moving forward, and I'm really excited about that."

Symbol of Nazi 'Insanity' Goes up in Flames After Suspected Arson...

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

About 200 firefighters deployed to tackle blaze at former Nazi mountain retreat in remote part of southern Germany

Police have arrested a 33-year-old German citizen after a fire ripped through Adolf Hitler’s former mountain retreat in Bavaria, leaving the historic Nazi meeting place – known as the Eagle’s Nest – largely destroyed.

About 200 firefighters were deployed on Friday night to tackle the blaze at the Nazi dictator’s remote, purpose-built Kehlsteinhaus residence, a protected building on the peak of Obersalzberg in southern Germany.

Flames rose high into the air as firefighters approached the scene to find the building “completely ablaze”, police said, adding that nobody was injured in the fire.

Lisa Maier, a police spokesperson, said firefighters struggled to tackle the blaze due to its location at an altitude of 1,834 metres (6,000ft).

The building, which was one of the few undamaged monuments of the Hitler era, was only accessible with “heavy breathing apparatus”, she said.

Built by the Nazis and formally inaugurated by Hitler on his birthday in 1939, the Eagle’s Nest has been preserved as a monument to “the insanity of the regime”, according to its website.

The building is only accessible via a road cut into the mountain through previously impassable terrain. Nazis would enter a tunnel and take a 124-metre brass lift buried in the mountain to get inside the building.

Authorities were alerted to the fire when a smoke detector triggered a fire alarm at about 9.15pm, police said. When firefighters arrived on the scene at about 9.50pm, the entire building was engulfed in flames.

Fire departments from six municipalities sent emergency personnel to tackle the blaze and the Bavarian Red Cross, the local mountain rescue service and the police also attended.

No reason for the fire was given, but German media reported that investigators suspected it was the result of arson. Firefighters found no one inside the building.

Efforts to fight the fire were hampered by a strong wind and rain, which turned into the year’s first snow, and firefighters had to pull out overnight after several hours.

The blaze was reportedly still burning at 7am on Saturday but appeared to be out later in the morning, although smoke continued to rise from the building.

A police spokesperson told the German tabloid Bild: “It is damaged to a large extent. The house is unlikely to be saved.” Officers blocked access to the road leading to the retreat, according to an Agence France-Presse photographer.

Maier said police were still investigating and would hold a news conference later on Saturday.

The 33-year-old suspect arrested by the police was a German citizen who had ties to the area but did not live there. Prosecutors said he showed signs of mental illness and after being questioned he was being sent to a psychiatric facility.

The possibility of a political motive for the blaze would be investigated, but police said there was nothing to indicate anyone else was involved.

The Eagle’s Nest was constructed between 1937 and 1938 and forms part of the toxic legacy of Hitler’s Third Reich, which was responsible for the deaths of millions of Jews as well as Roma, LGBTQ people and communists in the Holocaust.

It became legendary in the postwar period and was often depicted as an essential meeting place in US war films, although in reality Hitler rarely visited the complex.

Since 1952 the building has housed an exhibition by the Institute for Contemporary History recounting its Nazi history, and a restaurant.

Officials indicated that the building, which is owned by Bavaria’s state government, would be restored after discussions with local officials, historians and conservators.

“We’re not going to leave a ruin there,” Joachim Herrmann, Bavaria’s interior minister, told reporters, adding that it was important to continue to do “the work of remembrance”.

Faktencheck: Wie Flávio Bolsonaro Zweifel an Brasiliens Demokratie sät

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Hört man dem rechten Präsidentschaftskandidaten zu, könnte man glauben, der amtierende linke Präsident Lula habe Brasiliens Demokratie an den Rand des Abgrunds gebracht. Wie viel Wahrheit steckt hinter der Rhetorik?

Am 25. Oktober entscheiden die Wählerinnen und Wähler in Brasilien zwischen dem linken Präsidenten Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva und dem rechten Kandidaten Flávio Bolsonaro, Sohn des ehemaligen, inhaftierten Präsidenten Jair Bolsonaro.

Im stark polarisierten Wahlkampf in Südamerikas flächen- wie bevölkerungsmäßig größtem Land hat Flávio immer wiederein dunkles Bild von Brasiliens Demokratie unter Lula gezeichnet.

Behauptung: Bolsonaro zufolge hat die Lula-Regierung "ein institutionelles Ungleichgewicht geschaffen. Sie hat sich entschieden, gemeinsam mit einer Fraktion des Obersten Bundesgerichts zu regieren."

Vor Journalistinnen und Journalisten erklärteder 45-Jährige nach der ersten Wahlrunde Anfang Oktober: "Ist Brasilien eine vollwertige Demokratie? Nein." Lula stelle eine Gefahr für die Demokratie dar.

Unter Lula hat sich der Zustand von Brasiliens Demokratie nicht verschlechtert – sondern vielmehr verbessert im Vergleich zu seinem Vorgänger Jair Bolsonaro, Flávio Bolsonaros Vater.

Das zeigen Demokratie-Indizes wie Varieties of Democracy (V-Dem)und der Bertelsmann Transformation Index (BTI), die den Zustand demokratischer Institutionen weltweit vergleichen. Bei BTI heißt esetwa: " Nach den Herausforderungen und Bedrohungen für die brasilianische Demokratie durch die Regierung von Jair Bolsonaro hat sich die politische Lage seit 2023 etwas stabilisiert."

Die Indizes beschreiben Brasiliens demokratische Institutionen als allgemein funktionsfähig, das Wahlsystem des Landes und die unabhängige Justiz als widerstandsfähig.

Der rechtsextreme Jair Bolsonaro war von 2019 bis 2022 Präsident Brasiliens, der ehemalige Militäroffizier machte aus seiner Sympathie für Brasiliens Militärdiktatur keinen Hehl und hatte für die Spielregeln der Demokratie nicht gerade viel übrig.

Dies bestätigt auch die Politikwissenschaftlerin Marjorie Marona: "Mit der Bolsonaro-Regierung haben wir erlebt, wie eine Strategie systematischer Angriffe auf demokratische Institutionen in der Praxis aussieht. Er griff den Wahlprozess an und drohte, Entscheidungen des Obersten Bundesgerichts nicht umzusetzen. Er setzte die Presse und die Kontrollorgane unter Druck. Und all das mündete, wie wir wissen, in einen Putschversuch.“

Am 8. Januar 2023 stürmten Tausende Anhänger von Jair Bolsonaro Regierungsgebäude in der Hauptstadt Brasília, weil sie das Ergebnis der Präsidentschaftswahl 2022 nicht anerkennen wollten. Bolsonaro wurde später wegen der Vorbereitung eines Putsches zu mehr als 27 Jahren Haft verurteilt.

Mit Lula war dagegen laut Marona, die an der Bundesuniversität des Bundesstaates Rio de Janeiro (UNIRIO) lehrt, "eine Umkehr jenes Autokratisierungsprozesses" zu beobachten: "Die Angriffe auf das Wahlsystem hörten auf, und das Verhältnis zwischen den Staatsgewalten funktioniert wieder deutlich weniger konfrontativ.“

Flávio Bolsonaros Behauptungen, Brasiliens Demokratie stehe am Abgrund, zielen nicht nur auf den amtierenden Präsidenten ab, sondern auch auf den Obersten Gerichtshof STF und insbesondere STF-Richter Alexandre de Moraes.

Laut Bolsonaro habe dieser Brasiliens "Demokratie gekidnappt"und verfolge gezielt Politiker aus dem rechten Lager. Zwar kritisieren Expertinnen und Expertentatsächlich schon lange, dass der Oberste Gerichtshof Macht bei einzelnen Richterinnen und Richternkonzentriert.

Auch Marona sagt: "Der Oberste Gerichtshof hat durchaus strukturelle Probleme, die nach Reformen verlangen. Ein Richter am Obersten Gerichtshof kann eigenmächtig ein Gesetz per einstweiliger Verfügung wegen Verfassungswidrigkeit aussetzen. Er kann ein Verfahren bisweilen monatelang blockieren – indem er Akteneinsicht beantragt –, selbst, wenn es bereits eine klare Mehrheit gibt."

Aber was Bolsonaro tue, sei keine legitime Kritik, sondern eine ständige Darstellung von sich und seiner Familie als politisch Verfolgte – Moraes war der federführende Richter im Prozess gegen Ex-Präsident Jair Bolsonaro.

Viele aktuelle Vorwürfe Bolsonaros gegen Moraes beziehen sich auch auf den Banco-Master-Skandal. Gegen den früheren Eigentümer der Bank, Daniel Vorcaro, ermittelt die Bundespolizei wegen milliardenschwerer Finanzdelikte und Korruptionsvorwürfen.

Es wurden auch Nachrichten Vorcaros an STF-Richter Alexandre de Moraes bekannt sowie ein Vertrag von umgerechnet 24 Millionen Euro zwischen der Bank und der Anwaltskanzlei von Moraes' Ehefrau. Moraes weist Vorwürfe des Fehlverhaltens zurück.

Dass Flávio Bolsonaro selbst auch in den Fokus der Banco-Master-Ermittler geraten ist – es gab Treffen und er hat den mittlerweile festgenommenen Bänker um finanzielle Unterstützung für den Film "Dark Horse" über seinen Vater gebeten – hielt ihn nicht davon ab, Moraes lautstark zu kritisieren. Auch Flávio Bolsonaro bestreitet Unregelmäßigkeiten.

Der Banco-Master-Skandal ist ein Beispiel für weitreichende systemische Korruption – diese wird auch von den Demokratie-Indizes als großes Problem in Brasilien beschrieben, das die Bewertung verschlechtert. So wird das Land etwa beim Democracy Index der Economist Intelligence Unit (EIU) in die Gruppe der "Mangelhaften Demokratien"eingeordnet.

Zu den chronischen Mängeln von Brasiliens Demokratie gehört unter anderem die extreme politische Polarisierung, zu der Flávio Bolsonaro paradoxerweise selbst mit seinen Behauptungen beiträgt.

Expertinnen und Experten warnen, dass Institutionen allein eine Demokratie nicht dauerhaft tragen können, wenn große Teile der Gesellschaft Angriffe auf demokratische Institutionen und Regelbrüche der Rechtsordnung normalisierten und Politiker anderer Parteien als Feind betrachteten.

Marco Antonio Carvalho Texeira, Politikprofessor an der Päpstlichen Katholischen Universität von São Paulo, meint: "Wir haben das Verständnis verloren, dass Politik etwas Konstruktives ist und nicht nur ein Schlagabtausch oder eine Strategie, um den anderen auszuschalten."

Misstrauen gegenüber demokratischen Institutionenzu säen ist eine Strategie, die auch Rechtspopulisten anderer Länder erfolgreich einsetzen. Sie dient nicht nur der Mobilisierung von Wählerschaft, sondern kann auch den Grundstein dafür legen, demokratische Kontrollmechanismen nach der Wahl zu schwächen.

Marona sagt: "Ich erkenne da ein Muster, man denke etwa Viktor Orban in Ungarn oder Donald Trump in den USA. Am Anfang steht immer das Narrativ, diese Institutionen seien korrupt oder marode, um genau darauf aufbauend eine Strategie zur Änderung der Spielregeln in Gang zu setzen."

Viele Brasilianerinnen und Brasilianer fürchten sich vor einem solchen Szenario nach der entscheidenden Stichwahl am 25. Oktober.

Most e-readers are squarish and hard to hold without something like a PopSocket, and they can feel too big for reading while lying down. This was a big plus for me because when I’m reading in bed at night, I don’t want to disturb my sleeping husband with the light. While you can lower the brightness on a smartphone, the Palma 2 Pro can be dimmed to an exceptionally low level. Even when reading in complete darkness, I could see the screen clearly without straining my eyes or disturbing my husband.

Not only was the Boox Palma 2 Pro easier on my eyes, but it was also noticeably lighter than my iPhone, weighing just 175 grams and making it even more comfortable to hold.

And since it’s pocket-sized, I ended up carrying it everywhere, from the doctor’s office to the airport, especially since it fits in my small, everyday purse that isn’t big enough for a Kindle. So, in moments when I would normally scroll on my phone, I was getting some reading in instead.

Since it’s not a locked-down e-reader, you can download apps like Kindle, Kobo, and Libby, as well as apps for audiobooks, podcasts, read-it-later services, and email. This means that you don’t have to use the built-in reader app, and can choose to read on your preferred app.

Access to apps turns the compact E Ink device into a versatile tool for reading, research, and productivity. It’s worth noting that while scrolling through these apps, animations and some interfaces may feel sluggish because not all apps are optimized for E Ink. And while the device is capable of displaying color, images will definitely look less vibrant than they would a smartphone screen.

For me, the Palma 2 Pro became a dedicated device for reading and listening to audiobooks, which allowed me to enjoy the best of both worlds without the usual distractions that come with a smartphone.

The Boox Palma 2 Pro also features a rear camera for scanning documents, a built-in speaker for listening to audio without headphones, and a hybrid SIM slot for cellular data on the go.

The device is compatible with a stylus, so you can take notes and mark up PDFs. However, I can’t speak to that experience since I didn’t receive a stylus with my review unit.

As for battery life, the device lasted about 5–7 days, which worked for me but could be a downside for people who are used to Kindles, which can last for weeks on a single charge. Additionally, if you want to get more juice out of the device, you can just turn off Wi-Fi.

One downside I found, especially for an e-reader at this price point, is that there’s quite a bit of ghosting. Text from a previous page can linger on the screen when you turn to the next page or return to the home screen. There’s a refresh button in the navigation bar that quickly clears it, but it may get annoying for some users to repeatedly have to do that.

I think the Boox Palma 2 Pro is a great device for a very specific type of reader: someone who wants a smartphone-sized device for comfort, portability, as well as access to apps. If you’re someone who loves your Kindle, I don’t think this device is for you. But if, like me, you’re tired of reading on your phone and dealing with eye strain or headaches, the Boox Palma 2 Pro is a great option.

Of course, the price is quite steep for an e-reader, so you’ll have to decide whether the added versatility and portability are worth the extra cost. It’s worth mentioning that Boox is launching a non-Pro Palma 3 soon for $340 that features a black and white display and replaces the Palma 2.

Here are the top AI agents that can live in your text messages

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Rather than downloading another app, a growing number of agents can simply be texted like an ordinary person.

You text it what you need, and it can remember context, connect to the apps and services you already use, and complete tasks on your behalf. That can mean scheduling an appointment, organizing a calendar, researching a trip, sending an email, making a reservation, shopping online, or reminding you about something days later.

While Instinct is one of the buzziest AI agents at present, thanks to its $10 billion valuation after its latest funding round of $1 billion, there are many others also making a play for this space.

Below are the most notable options so far, from general-purpose personal assistants to agents designed for families, travel, and work.

Ask Proxy’s biggest differentiator is that it “thinks like a woman,” meaning it aims to anticipate your needs before you even ask, the company explains on its website.

It works like a chatbot you can text like a friend, whether you need gift ideas, help planning an event, or recommendations for things to do that weekend. You can ask it to add important dates to your calendar, set reminders, or help with everyday decisions. You can also send it photos, like an empty table, to figure out how to decorate the table for a dinner party.

Unlike a typical chatbot, Ask Proxy also takes a proactive approach. It can reach out when it notices something you might forget or when it comes across a new activity, restaurant, or experience you might enjoy.

There’s also a work-focused version, which the company explains “gets employees back to work faster, while keeping personal data off company systems.”

The personal version costs $1 per day. For businesses, pricing starts at $15 per employee per month for large companies and $23 per employee per month for small teams with fewer than 49 employees.

Ask Proxy works over SMS. You can text “start” to 820-732-2625.

Caddy is an AI assistant that turns the information scattered across your phone into things you can actually act on. It lives in iMessage for iPhone users and RCS for Android users, so there’s no separate app or inbox to constantly check.

For instance, an email contains an appointment that needs to be added to your calendar or a friend sends a list of things to pick up. Instead of worrying about all the details getting buried, Caddy connects to your calendars and conversations, then identifies things that may require action. It can add events to your calendar, set reminders, keep track of follow-ups, and even do research for you.

Caddy has been available in public beta since April 2026.

Comma is designed to manage tasks across both work and everyday life. Comma carries tasks through to completion, checking its own work and determining when a task is finished.

Completed work is surfaced for review when a decision or approval is needed. It also proactively notifies users and recommends the next steps.

It can work across a user’s browser, computer, and files, while remaining accessible remotely through messaging apps such as Signal, Telegram, and WeChat. Comma maintains context and memory across devices and conversations, and can deliver daily briefings, flag urgent matters, and summarize recorded calls.

Fambot is an AI-powered “chief of staff” for families, designed to bring together the many moving parts of family life, including school communications, sports activities, meal planning, calendars, and other day-to-day responsibilities, and turn them into an organized plan.

The service is designed to work across both an app and text messages. Fambot is available on iOS devices, Android devices, and the web, and families can also interact with it directly over SMS. Every night, Fambot automatically sends a summary of the following day, including upcoming events, to-dos, and details such as school uniform or packing requirements. Parents can reply to those messages to ask questions, check information, or make changes to their calendar.

Fambot currently connects to Gmail, Google Calendar, Outlook, and WhatsApp, with support for Apple Calendar planned for the future.

The company launched in beta in early September 2026 and has raised $3.5 million in pre-seed funding. The service is currently free during the beta period, but Fambot expects to eventually charge roughly the equivalent of a Netflix subscription.

Folk is designed to be an AI assistant you can reach through iMessage, WhatsApp, and Telegram. It can remember personal context, manage reminders, research topics, track flights, handle email, and perform real-world tasks such as restaurant reservations.

One distinction is that Folk runs on its own private cloud computer. It can also run code and execute multi-step tasks rather than merely responding conversationally.

Folk launched its beta in May 2026, and it’s available for free but also offers a Pro subscription for $8.33/month if you want unlimited tasks running in the background.

Instinct first made headlines after raising $350 million at a $2.5 billion valuation. In September 2026, it raised $1 billion, valuing it at $10 billion.

The always-available agent can take actions on your behalf, rather than simply answer questions. Users can communicate with Instinct by text or voice and connect it to the apps and services they already use, such as email, calendar, Google Workspace, and more. The company says early users have used Instinct for tasks ranging from planning trips and buying groceries to booking tickets and canceling subscriptions.

In September, Instinct began giving users dedicated email addresses for their assistants. The idea is to let Instinct create and manage accounts on a user’s behalf without filling the user’s personal inbox. The agent can use its address to sign up for services, contact businesses, follow up on requests, and handle tasks that require an email account. (However, this level of autonomy has also raised concerns about privacy and security.) It’s also begun rolling out support for placing calls.

Powered by Hermes Agent, Iris is designed to let users delegate tasks to an AI assistant directly through iMessage. Users can connect the apps they use and hand over a task with a simple message. The agent then works through the required steps, while retaining relevant context from previous interactions. It’s also designed to follow up with users proactively rather than waiting for another request.

Martin is a general-purpose personal assistant reachable through SMS as well as phone, WhatsApp, email, Slack, and its own iOS app.

It can help manage calendars, email, reminders, tasks, notes, and communications. It can also act on behalf of users by sending messages or making phone calls, while providing daily summaries of what needs attention.

It’s a bit on the pricier side, with subscriptions starting at $21 per month.

Maverick describes itself as an “AI companion who lives in your texts,” acting as a personal assistant that helps manage everyday tasks directly from your messages.

It can fix typos, cancel subscriptions, schedule meetings from voice notes, and help group chats settle on a date for an event. One notable feature is that Maverick asks for your permission before taking action.

The platform also offers hundreds of integrations, including Discord, Gmail, and Slack, allowing it to connect with the apps you already use.

It’s currently in beta and free to use on iMessage. There’s also a Pro version for €27 per month (or approximately $30.25 USD), which includes 500 tasks a month,  20 connected apps, scheduled tasks, and memory.

Miso takes the personal-assistant concept and applies it specifically to travel.

It operates through iMessage and combines AI trip planning with support from a dedicated travel team. Users can arrange flights through messaging while Miso takes into account their travel preferences, loyalty points, and other priorities. Miso also gives travelers access to trip details and flight updates, along with personalized recommendations.

Ohai is another family assistant focused on helping households manage the constant stream of information and tasks involved in everyday family life.

Users can send messages, forward emails, or make voice requests, and the assistant can transform them into schedules, reminders, and plans. It can also coordinate calendars, household chores, and meals across multiple members of a family.

It includes a free basic option, but also offers different subscriptions based on family size, starting at $9.99 per month.

Similar to the other family-focused agents, the idea behind Ollie is that families already have the information they need, but it lives across school messages, calendars, task lists, and group chats. Ollie aims to bring those pieces together and keep them in sync. It can keep track of calendars, emails, reminders, tasks, and other household details, then deliver relevant information to users through text.

Its biggest differentiator from other agents is that Ollie is one of the first mainstream, family-focused AI assistants to achieve SOC 2 compliance, a widely used security standard.

Launched in June 2026, Ollie offers a free tier, as well as paid plans starting at $25 per month for 150 messages. A $100-per-month plan includes up to 1,000 messages.

Orbits also has an AI assistant that is focused on the household.

It works through both the Orbits app and text messaging, and can bring together calendars, lists, and conversations while also carrying out tasks for you. That can include searching the web, making purchases, booking restaurants, changing appointments, and requesting quotes for household services.

Additionally, Orbits can remind family members about chores, communicate with service providers, and help coordinate responsibilities beyond the immediate household.

Orbits is backed by investors like Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) Speedrun fund, N49P, and Garage Capital.

Users can interact with Pally as they would with a friend while giving it access to the apps they already rely on. The assistant can connect information spread across services such as WhatsApp, Gmail, calendars, and Google Drive, helping users keep track of plans, receipts, dates, files, and other details.

There’s a free plan available. It works over iMessage, RCS, and Telegram.

Poke launched in March 2026 as a general-purpose assistant that you can reach through text messaging. It can handle tasks involving calendars, planning, health and fitness, smart-home controls, photos, and other everyday activities.

Poke became particularly notable in June 2026 when Apple approved Poke as the first AI agent on the Apple Messages for Business platform. A month later, parent company The Interaction Company of California was acquired by AI coding startup Cognition in a deal valued in the low nine figures.

Rene is an AI personal assistant designed to be a user’s “best contact” rather than another productivity agent. It’s text-based and can turn natural-language conversations into tasks, reminders, summaries, and other follow-up actions. Rene also maintains context across conversations, allowing it to remember details and surface things that may need attention later.

Additionally, it has a browser, can write code, shop for you, as well as create slides and images.

Rene is currently available on iMessage, Telegram, and WhatsApp.

Instead of waiting for users to open an app, Skye is an “agentic homescreen” for the iPhone that analyzes information from connected services and surfaces what it thinks is relevant through contextual cards, suggestions, and daily briefings. The assistant can generate audio briefings, prepare information ahead of meetings, surface reminders, and identify potentially useful information.

The company, Signull Labs, raised about $3.6 million in seed funding in 2025. It entered private testing and beta in April 2026, and hasn’t yet announced a public release date.

Stanley is an AI content assistant aimed at content creators rather than general personal productivity. Available through iMessage, Telegram, and the web, it acts as a kind of AI head of content, helping users come up with ideas, turn them into posts, and maintain a consistent publishing schedule.

For instance, Stanley can connect to a user’s Instagram account, using that context to suggest content based on what the user has actually been doing. Users can also send voice notes, which Stanley turns into scripts, captions, and other social assets. It includes a content calendar and can schedule posts, with the broader goal of making content creation a more continuous, automated process rather than something users have to sit down and plan from scratch.

Szn is designed to coordinate tasks and follow-ups through a single conversation thread. Users can send text messages, voice notes, photos, or forwarded emails, and the assistant takes action on their behalf, including making calls, sending emails, and managing follow-ups.

According to the company’s website, Szn has served more than 7,500 businesses across 16 countries, completed over 10,000 jobs, and handled 95 hours of calls.

Tab allows users to delegate everyday tasks through familiar messaging platforms. Users can send requests to the assistant via iMessage or WhatsApp, ranging from ordering groceries to finding birthday gifts, and Tab handles the process on their behalf.

The company emerged from stealth in early October with a valuation of $300 million. Its investors include SV Angel, Valar Ventures, and American Spirit.

The biggest differentiator with Tomo’s personal assistant for iMessage is that, instead of being a private assistant that only you interact with, Tomo can become part of group conversations.

Also, like other agents, it’s designed to help with goals, accountability, scheduling, and everyday organization. It also has the ability to send reminders, search the web, work with calendars and email, watch videos, and edit photos.

The basic plan starts at $19.99 per month.

Town is aimed more at professional life. It gives users a “Townie,” an AI assistant designed to learn how they work and handle recurring professional tasks. It can work with email, calendars, documents, Slack, and other platforms.

In June 2026, the company raised a $55 million Series A led by Andreessen Horowitz and Forerunner.

Underdog is a privacy-focused AI assistant that launched an invite-only beta in early October 2026. Unlike cloud-based AI services, the model runs entirely on the user’s device, keeping personal data on hardware they already own. The initial release supports Mac and Windows computers, with Linux, iPhone, and Android versions planned for the future.

The company plans to offer the app for free initially and has stated that it will never include advertising.

Wajo’s first consumer agent, Fo, can call businesses, send emails, join group chats, make bookings and purchases, and coordinate with people to complete everyday tasks.

A key part of Wajo’s approach is giving Fo its own identity. The agent has its own email address, phone number, and payment card, allowing it to interact with businesses without requiring users to hand over their own credentials.

Notably, when Fo encounters something it cannot handle on its own, Wajo says it can bring in a human assistant to finish the task.

Fo launched publicly in September 2026 with a free tier, while heavier workloads and custom agents are available through a paid Pro offering.
