Auftakt der RN-Wahlkampagne: Le Pen spricht von "Wiedergeburt" Frankreichs

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Die rechtspopulistische französische Präsidentschaftskandidatin Le Pen führt derzeit in den Umfragen. Zum Auftakt der Wahlkampagne ihres Rassemblement National warb sie für einen politischen Neuanfang.

In der ersten großen Rede ihrer Kampagne zur Präsidentschaftswahl hat die rechtspopulistische Politikerin Marine Le Pen für einen politischen Neuanfang Frankreichs plädiert. Unter dem Motto der "Wiedergeburt“ ("Renaissance") sprach sie sich vor den 8.500 Delegierten des Parteitags des Rassemblement National (RN) in Orléans unter anderem gegen Einwanderung aus, die französische Städte "überschwemmt". Auch will sie kriminelle Organisationen bekämpfen.

Sie wolle Frankreich seine "Macht, seine Größe und seine Ausstrahlung" zurückgeben, so wie es am Ende des Mittelalters der Fall gewesen sei, sagte Le Pen. Dabei schlage sie keine Rückkehr in die Vergangenheit, sondern den Beginn einer neuen Ära vor.

Ihre Rede war von Optimismus und Zuversicht geprägt. "Wir werden den öffentlichen Raum schützen, damit er ein Ort des Lebens, der Gelassenheit, des gegenseitigen Respekts bleibt", erklärte Le Pen weiter. Höflichkeit sei der sicherste Weg, "um Gewalt in unserer Gesellschaft zurückzudrängen", sagte Le Pen. "Wir werden wieder an die französische Lebensart anknüpfen, die unser Land einst zum 'lieblichen Frankreich' machte."

Nach zehn Jahren unter Amtsinhaber Emmanuel Macron kämen Begriffe wie Ruinen, Niedergang und Unruhen in den Sinn, sagte die 58-Jährige. Sie wolle "den Rechtsstaat wiederherstellen" und Kinder und Familien schützen. Ausländische Straftäter sollten automatisch und konsequent abgeschoben werden. Mit Blick auf mehr Kaufkraft für die Menschen wolle sie Energiesteuern senken.

In der Rentenpolitik forderte sie, dass junge Menschen früher anfangen zu arbeiten. Le Pen betonte zudem, dass sie Soziales nicht gegen Wirtschaft, Rechte nicht gegen Linke ausspielen wolle. Sie griff dazu nach ihren Angaben auf ein Sprichwort zurück: "Ich bin weder der rechte Flügel noch der linke Flügel. Ich bin der Vogel."

Die RN-Politikerin kandidiert zum vierten Mal für das Präsidentenamt. Die Wahlen finden im kommenden Frühjahr statt. Umfragen sehen sie derzeit im ersten Wahlgang klar auf dem ersten Platz, auch in der Stichwahl würde sie sich derzeit gegen jeden anderen Kandidaten durchsetzen.

Le Pen betonte, sie werde am nächsten Tag den Vorsitz der RN-Fraktion in der Nationalversammlung niederlegen, um zu unterstreichen, dass sie nicht als Vertreterin einer Partei antrete.

Die Veranstaltung wurde von Protesten begleitet. 3.000 Sicherheitskräfte waren laut Präfektur im Einsatz. Während der Rede von Le Pens Schützling Jordan Bardella, der aus ihrer Sicht Premierminister werden soll, gab es Unruhen im Publikum. Auf der Plattform X bekannte sich dazu der französische Verband jüdischer Studierender. Bardella sieht sich derzeit nach Medienrecherchen mit schweren Antisemitismusvorwürfen konfrontiert.

Bardella stellte in erster Linie eine harte Ordnungspolitik in Aussicht. "Die Regierung, der ich vorstehen werde, wird alle Mittel nutzen, um Ordnung herzustellen", sagte er. "Polizisten und Gendarmen sind die wichtigsten Schutzschilde der Gesellschaft", betonte er vor dem Hintergrund der jüngsten gewaltsamen Ausschreitungen am Rande von Massenprotesten im ganzen Land.

Bardella kündigte an, er wolle die Strafmündigkeit auf 16 Jahre absenken und zusätzliche Plätze in den Gefängnissen schaffen. "Wer einen Polizisten angreift, schläft noch am selben Abend im Gefängnis." Ausländische Straftäter sollten umgehend abgeschoben werden, die Einwanderung solle insgesamt reduziert werden.

I know many of you are mourning the loss of the Arc browser, and I get it. It felt like the first app in a long time to really offer a new approach to browser design. Atlassian bought the company last year and shifted its focus to developing Dia, its even more AI-focused browser. And soon enough, Arc will officially be no more, leaving dedicated users in a lurch. But I abandoned Arc quite some time ago for the Zen Browser and haven’t really missed it. If you’re willing to deal with some minor inconveniences, I think you’ll find Zen is the Arc replacement you’re looking for.

Zen unabashedly rips off all the best bits of Arc. Visually, they’re almost indistinguishable. It has the launcher-like pinned sites, tab bar on the left, siloed spaces, and mods that let you customize the browser’s UI to your liking. It takes the bits that made Arc’s interface feel fresh, then strips out everything that made it bloated and drove me into the arms of another.

There’s no agent in a sidebar hounding me to let it handle tasks. I’m not worried about Zen training an LLM on my browsing activity. It’s just a web browser.

Zen prides itself on its privacy features: “Beautifully designed, privacy-focused, and packed with features. We care about your experience, not your data,” it proudly proclaims. Now, I’m no fool. I know that nothing I do on the internet is really private, but with Arc, it always felt like my data was an integral part of the business plan. That’s not the case with Zen. It’s a free and open-source project that relies on volunteers and donations.

In addition to Zen Mods that can do anything from tweak the look of an active tab to overhaul how PiP videos are handled, you can also install Firefox extensions. Yes, you can install Chrome extensions in Arc, but anecdotally, I’ve found that Zen handles extensions better. Both Zen and Arc are different enough from a UI perspective that not all extensions display properly, but Zen offers more workarounds.

Zen also just kind of lets me focus on the task at hand. Links open in “glance” view, which is a temporary pop-over tab, which is really great when you’re doing some window shopping or light research. If I want to open it in a proper tab, it’s just a click away. Zen also has a floating bar that pops up whenever you press Command+T or CTRL+T. From here you can launch a new tab, switch to an existing tab, perform a search from the search engine of your choice, and even trigger an extension.

Zen isn’t perfect. I love its clean UI and organizational features, but it can’t play DRM-protected video content right now. That means Netflix, Spotify, HBO, Disney+, and more do not work in Zen. The Zen Team is working to obtain a Widevine license from Google. In the meantime, I use Chrome when I want to watch a movie, which is fine. Zen is built on Firefox, and Firefox’s Web MIDI support is lacking. So I have to fire up Chrome semi-regularly anyway.

I also miss Arc’s Boosts in some cases, but I’ve learned to accept the web as it is. Zen implemented a similar Boost feature in May, but it’s not super obvious and, if there is an official, easily browsable repository of installable Boosts, I’ve yet to find it. Instead, you have to tweak individual sites’ appearance yourself.

If you tried Zen a long time ago and were turned off by its buggy early builds, rest assured it’s come a long way. Look, if you are all in on Arc’s AI features, Dia is probably more your speed. Personally, I always loved Arc’s interface, but never really dug the vibes. Zen just feels right.

Amtsinhaberin Jotowa vor Wahlsieg in erster Runde der Präsidentenwahl in Bulgarien

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Die Wahlbeteiligung fiel auf ein Rekordtief. Die GERB-Partei verzichtete erstmals auf eigenen Kandidaten, auf ihre Wahlempfehlung könnte es in der Stichwahl ankommen.

Amtsinhaberin Ilijana Jotowa hat am Sonntag die erste Runde der bulgarischen Präsidentenwahlen gewonnen, muss aber in eine Stichwahl. Laut Exitpolls des Meinungsforschungsinstituts Alpha Research kommt Jotowa auf 35,3 Prozent der Stimmen. Für Andrej Gjurow haben demnach 32,4 Prozent gestimmt. Auf Platz drei reihte sich der Nationalist Kostadin Kostadonow mit 12,6 Prozent ein. Jotowa und Gjurow treten am 1. November in einer Stichwahl gegeneinander an.

Die Wahlbeteiligung war erwartungsgemäß niedrig, erreichte allerdings mit knapp 22 Prozent ein Rekordtief. Jotowa wurde von der allein regierenden Mitte-links-Partei „Progressives Bulgarien“ (PB) des russlandfreundlichen Ministerpräsidenten Rumen Radew sowie der traditionell russophilen sozialistischen Partei BSP unterstützt. Ihr Herausforderer, der 50-jährige prowestliche Ex-Interimsregierungschef und früherer Vizechef der Zentralbank Andrej Gjurow, wird von der oppositionellen Reformpartei „Wir setzen den Wandel fort“ (PP) und dem liberal-konservativen Bündnis „Demokratisches Bulgarien“ (DB) unterstützt.

Die Präsidentschaftswahl stand symbolisch für die Zerreißprobe, in der sich das EU- und Nato-Mitglied Bulgarien seit Jahren befindet: ein ständiges Tauziehen zwischen Westanbindung und historisch tief verwurzelten prorussischen Reflexen.

Der Ukraine-Krieg gehörte zu den entscheidenden Wahlkampfthemen. Erst vor wenigen Wochen waren vor der bulgarischen Schwarzmeerküste vier Handelsschiffe mutmaßlich von russischen Drohnen getroffen worden. Zudem wurden unweit von Sofia Antennen des Satelliteninternetdienstes Starlink in Brand gesetzt, dessen Netz wichtiger Bestandteil der ukrainischen Verteidigung im russischen Angriffskrieg ist. Während Jotowa gegen Waffenlieferungen für die Ukraine ist und eine schnelle Friedenslösung fordert, lehnt Gjurow eine Entscheidung über den Köpfen der EU und der Ukraine ab. Beide bekennen sich zur EU- und Nato-Mitgliedschaft Bulgariens.

Auffällig bei der Wahl am Sonntag ist, dass die einst mächtige konservative GERB-Partei des Langzeitpremiers Bojko Borissow erstmals seit ihrer Gründung im Jahr 2009 auf einen eigenen Kandidaten verzichtete. Wen die im April abgewählte GERB-Partei in der Stichwahl unterstützen wird, gilt daher für die politischen Beobachter in Sofia als die entscheidende Frage. (APA)

Two men have been killed by falling trees and hundreds of thousands of homes have been left without power as Storm Boelo battered the country with gusts reaching 84mph.

A Met Office amber weather warning for wind remains in place for Scotland and much of northern England until 9pm, having been activated at 9pm on Saturday, hours after the first named storm of the season – Storm Austen – petered out from the south west. Boelo poses a danger to life from flying debris and crashing waves and people should stay indoors as much as possible, the forecaster said.

A 60-year-old man was killed by a falling tree in Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland, with police called to the scene at around 10:30am on Sunday. The man was pronounced dead at the scene, Police Scotland said.

A man in his 20s was also killed after a tree fell onto his moving car in north London. Chief Inspector Guy Ellwood, from the Metropolitan Police’s North West Command Unit, said: “This is a deeply tragic incident and our thoughts are with the loved ones of the young man who lost his life.”

A neighbour told the Metro that the man’s family had gathered at the scene in Mill Hill following the tragic incident. She said: “I believe he was on his own in the car. I spoke with his sister and gave her a hug. Then I see a young lad standing looking confused so I went over to speak to him and he said he was the victim’s best friend.

“I came home upset but couldn’t settle so I had to go back and offer my support”.

Sunday began with heavy winds in northern parts of the UK – widely reaching 60 to 70mph and at 84mph in Argyll and Bute, 83mph in Snowdonia and 82mph in the Inner Hebrides, the Met Office said, while video footage showed floodwater coursing through Largs in North Ayrshire.

The business which shared the video on Facebook claimed that the street flooded “at its highest level ever”.

The storm caused widespread power cuts in Scotland and England’s north west. Engineers have reconnected 344,900 customers to power but 82,600 were disconnected on Sunday afternoon, the Energy Networks Association said.

Many A roads in Scotland were closed due to fallen trees, while National Rail said those wishing to travel by train also faced severe disruption, with many of the UK’s major operators impacted.

Network Rail shared images of a trampoline beneath a train and a shed by the tracks, in a call for those living near rail lines to secure their garden furniture, while a fallen tree blocked a line between Liverpool and Manchester and several lines in western and northern Scotland closed as a result of the strong wind and heavy rain warnings across the country.

Network Rail Scotland shared an image of flooding from the tracks to the platform at Dalmarnock station in Glasgow. The floodwater submerged the tracks between the platforms overnight, overwhelming its water pumps for a time.

The train operator said the station is now clear, but the line is closed between Partick and Rutherglen for planned engineering work.

Bridges across Scotland are facing closures and warnings over the high wind speeds.

The Queensferry Crossing, M90 and the Clackmannanshire Bridge A876 have been closed to high sided vehicles on Sunday, and a 30mph speed limit has been imposed on other traffic.

A number of flights to and from the UK and Ireland, particularly those across the Irish Sea, were cancelled.

Some 17 flights at Belfast International were cancelled by Sunday lunchtime, while some services were diverted from landing at Leeds Bradford Airport and others were cancelled.

An airport spokesperson thanked customers for their patience, adding: “We appreciate that this is a challenging situation. Our teams are working in very difficult conditions to support passengers as best they can.”

Many ferry services across Scotland have also been cancelled or are facing disruption.

Of CalMac’s 30 services, 24 have been cancelled, some of them until Monday.

Stena Line also cancelled services from Belfast to Liverpool and Cairnryan, while Western Ferries’ services remained suspended and DFDS cancelled crossings between Dover and Calais.

The Environment Agency had issued six warnings that flooding is expected for spots in northern parts of England, as well as 50 other alerts where it is possible. The Scottish Environment Protection Agency said 17 flood warnings and 11 flooding alerts were active on Sunday afternoon.

Storm Boelo follows Storm Austen, the first named storm of the season, which brought strong winds to southern parts of the UK on Friday.

Gusts of 76mph were recorded at St Mary’s Airport in the Isles of Scilly, with 74mph at Aberporth in south Wales and 71mph at Culdrose in Cornwall.

Met Office meteorologist Craig Snell said it was hard to compare storms because they affect different areas and “if a branch falls on your car in one, then you’re probably going to feel like that one was the worst storm ever”.

Storm Amy brought a gust of about 92mph to similar parts of the UK last year. Storms often happen in quick succession, he said, pointing to storms Bert, Conall and Darragh appearing in about one week in 2024.

“If the atmosphere is ripe for storm, it tends to mean it’s ripe for a couple of storms, so it is a fairly common thing.”

Forecasters are monitoring another low pressure system moving in on Monday and Tuesday.

It does not appear as potent as storms Austen and Boelo and there are no current plans to name it, but it will bring further wet and windy weather.

The country can expect a return to more dry and settled conditions after that, Mr Snell said.

NFL fans visiting Paris in high spirits for Saints vs. Steelers game, undeterred by protests

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

PARIS — American football fans brought high energy, chants and smiles to Stade de France on Sunday to watch the Pittsburgh Steelers face the New Orleans Saints. Some said the mass protests in the city were not an issue during their weekend trip.

The NFL’s first regular-season game in France came a day after more than 400,000 people around the country took to the streets to protest educational funding and the high cost of living. At least 110,000 of those protesters were concentrated in Paris, according to the French Interior Ministry.

Although the demonstrations were largely peaceful, some clashes broke out, prompting French security forces to use tear gas and water cannons in several cities. Paris prosecutors said there were 64 arrests in the nation’s capital on Saturday, mostly for suspected weapons offenses or participation in a group preparing violence or property damage.

But American visitors in town to cheer on their teams told NBC News that everyone they’ve encountered had been welcoming.

Lifelong friends and Saints fans Sindhu Pandit, Shruti Menon and Pramod Menon said they never felt the game would be canceled. Pandit said the key to their own movements around the city was to stay informed.

Pramod Menon added that locals were “incredibly hospitable” and considerate of the fact that they were visitors. The friends said they came to support both the country and their team, a sentiment his wife shared.

“We’re so excited to be here in Paris, and what a perfect place for New Orleans, the home of the French,” Shruti Menon said. “And so it’s wonderful to be here.”

Sean Warner, a Steelers fan since 1974, may not share the Menons’ taste in teams, but he also told NBC News that he and his wife, Saroja, were having a “wonderful” time in Paris.

“We were concerned about the riots and everything that we had heard about and read about, but quite honestly, it’s been amazing,” he said. “The experience has been fantastic. ... Paris has really tried to work hard to, you know, make people feel safe and comfortable, and then enjoy the experience.”

The Washington, D.C.-based couple, who have visited Paris several times, spent Saturday walking around the city. Saroja Warner said it was a bit sad to see some businesses with boarded-up windows, but any clashes seemed “localized.”

“We walked around Paris all day till about 6 o’clock at night ... and we didn’t see any unrest, any crazy stuff,” she said. “It just felt like a lot of folks trying to have a nice time on a rainy day in Paris.”

The Steelers beat the Saints 16-10 at France’s national stadium, just outside the heart of Paris. The game is the latest in a series of international games the NFL has scheduled in recent seasons to grow the sport outside of its domestic audience.

NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell told reporters Wednesday that he was not concerned about the Paris protests ahead of the game and was comfortable with the security preparations the league had made in coordination with French authorities.

“Protests are not unusual in France, as you know, and that’s something that we will be aware of and make sure that the appropriate arrangements are made,” Goodell said.

Figures from the Interior Ministry indicate Saturday’s demonstrations produced significantly fewer problems than those the week before. There were about 1,300 arrests nationwide, roughly half the previous week’s total. Only 53 members of the security forces reported injuries, compared with 1,144 the week before, according to the ministry.

The unrest began weeks ago, when students at a high school in Créteil, a suburb of Paris, began protesting a lack of resources and poor conditions at their school. Students at other schools in the poorer suburbs surrounding Paris soon joined, and the protests grew into a movement of students across France.

The teenagers blocked entrances to their schools and demanded that the French government invest in their educations by increasing staffing, repairing aging buildings and providing more resources to the public education system. Their protests caught the attention of many across the country who were struggling with the high cost of living.

Protest organizers agreed to the plans coordinated with the French government on Saturday, the Interior Ministry said Sunday. But the ministry warned that high-risk groups, largely made up “of radical far-left and ultraleft activists,” had infiltrated demonstrations in medium- and large-sized cities. It blamed those groups for “fomenting attacks” against law enforcement, businesses and other people.

“While the level of violence from these radical elements was the same as last week, their attempted actions — which triggered systematic intervention by law enforcement in line with the clear instructions of the Minister of the Interior — were generally thwarted,” the ministry said.
