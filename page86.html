Présidentielle 2027 : à Orléans, Marine Le Pen promet une "renaissance" sans "rupture brutale" et esquisse les contours de sa présidence

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Article rédigé par La rédaction de La Dépêche du Midi

l'essentiel À six mois du scrutin présidentiel, Marine Le Pen entend préparer les Français à une éventuelle arrivée du RN au pouvoir. À Orléans, ce dimanche 25 octobre, elle a défendu son projet de "renaissance", promettant de transformer le pays sans provoquer de "rupture brutale".

Pas d'attaques frontales ni de grandes annonces. Pour son premier grand discours de campagne, ce dimanche 25 octobre à Orléans, Marine Le Pen a défendu son projet de "renaissance" sans "rupture brutale". Sous haute surveillance policière, la cheffe de file du Rassemblement national a esquissé les contours de sa présidence, que les sondages annoncent comme une possibilité à six mois du scrutin.

Malgré les manifestations de la veille et l'attentat raciste survenu vendredi à Trappes, "la France a de beaux jours devant elle", a-t-elle lancé en ouverture de son discours. Dans une arène réunissant 8 500 militants chauffés à blanc, qui scandaient régulièrement "on va gagner", la quadruple candidate à l'Élysée avait un seul mot à la bouche, celui de la "renaissance".

Convaincue que "la bataille du constat est gagnée" et que "tout le monde en France est d'accord avec nous", Marine Le Pen s'est attachée à rassurer ceux qui redoutent son arrivée au pouvoir. Les sondages la placent largement en tête au premier tour et la donnent gagnante dans les différents scénarios de second tour. La cheffe du RN a toutefois évité d'évoquer l'épée de Damoclès judiciaire qui pèse sur sa campagne : la Cour de cassation pourrait rendre définitive sa condamnation pour détournement de fonds publics dans l'affaire des assistants parlementaires européens, à quelques semaines du premier tour prévu le 18 avril.

Cette "renaissance" ne serait ni une "rupture brutale" ni une "révolution destructrice", a-t-elle assuré durant son heure d'allocution. Elle y voit au contraire une manière de "s'élever vers le beau". Pour y parvenir, elle mise sur "la puissance" et sur la fermeté d'un programme déjà connu, notamment en matière de sécurité et d'immigration.

Les déclarations les plus martiales ont été laissées à son dauphin, Jordan Bardella. Le président du RN a promis qu'une fois au pouvoir, il "utilisera tous les moyens légaux pour rétablir l'ordre et protéger les honnêtes gens". Marine Le Pen a, de son côté, confirmé son intention de le nommer Premier ministre en cas de victoire, à la tête d'un gouvernement "d'union nationale". Leur allié Éric Ciotti venait justement de défendre cette "union pour la France", appelant ses "anciens amis" des Républicains à "revenir à la maison".

Jordan Bardella et Éric Ciotti ont copieusement fait huer les adversaires du RN. Marine Le Pen, elle, s'est abstenue de "donner des noms" en pâture. Elle n'en a pas moins désigné les ennemis de sa "renaissance", réunissant dans une même liste les "obscurantismes" de l'islamisme, les antifascistes, les Insoumis et les écologistes.

Les opposants au RN avaient été tenus à distance du meeting par un dispositif de 3 000 policiers et gendarmes déployés tout le week-end à Orléans, où les autorités craignaient des débordements. La veille, les forces de l'ordre avaient ainsi encadré les 13 000 à 26 000 manifestants venus dénoncer la tenue du congrès du parti à la flamme. Dimanche, elles ont également empêché une centaine de militants antifascistes d'approcher du lieu du meeting.

Une poignée d'activistes est néanmoins parvenue à entrer dans la salle et à déployer une banderole visant Jordan Bardella, réélu la veille à la tête du parti : "Le racisme et l'antisémitisme ça ne dérange pas le RN, au contraire". Une référence aux révélations de Mediapart sur des écrits antisémites de jeunesse attribués au dirigeant nationaliste, qui a porté plainte pour diffamation.

L'action, revendiquée par l'Union des étudiants juifs de France (UEJF), a rapidement dégénéré en altercation avec des participants, avant une intervention musclée du service d'ordre du RN. Celui-ci a également fait sortir de la salle un journaliste de Mediapart qui se trouvait à proximité.

Déjà pris à partie la veille pendant le congrès, les journalistes du site d'investigation ont finalement quitté les lieux, estimant que leur sécurité n'était "pas assurée dans cet endroit". L'UEJF a annoncé de son côté son intention de porter plainte pour violences en réunion. Jordan Bardella a réagi en accusant "des militants d'ultragauche, qui se revendiquent de l'UEJF" d'être "venus accompagnés de Mediapart pour agresser et attaquer des militants".

L'incident n'a pas détourné Marine Le Pen du message qu'elle était venue défendre. Pas même lorsqu'elle a fait l'éloge de la "civilité", autre pilier de sa "renaissance". Présentée comme un remède à "l'ensauvagement", elle serait, selon elle, "le chemin le plus sûr pour faire reculer la violence dans notre société".

Ministerstwo Spraw Zagranicznych Libii wezwało amerykańskiego charge d’affaires, by oficjalnie zaprotestować przeciwko słowom prezydenta USA Donalda Trumpa. Podczas sobotniego wiecu w Perry w stanie Georgia, Trump określił Libię mianem „g...nianego” kraju „przeżartego terroryzmem”, co wywołało natychmiastową reakcję libijskich władz.

Libijski resort spraw zagranicznych w oficjalnym oświadczeniu podkreślił, że odrzuca wszelkie wypowiedzi godzące w godność obywateli Libii lub umniejszające ich pozycję na arenie międzynarodowej.

Zarówno uznawany przez społeczność międzynarodową rząd w Trypolisie, jak i siły generała Chalify Haftara z Bengazi, zgodnie potępiły słowa Trumpa. Oba ośrodki władzy podkreśliły, że Libia nie zamierza być wykorzystywana jako element amerykańskiej debaty wyborczej.

Premier Abd al-Hamid ad-Dubajba w specjalnym komunikacie przypomniał, że Libia jest suwerennym państwem, które nieustannie podejmuje wysiłki w walce z terroryzmem w regionie. Podkreślił, że relacje z USA muszą opierać się na wzajemnym szacunku, a nie na wyborczej retoryce.

Libijski przywódca zaapelował o powstrzymanie się od obraźliwych komentarzy i przypomniał o znaczeniu partnerskiej współpracy.

Portal zaapelował również, by Libia nie była zmuszana do oferowania kontraktów naftowych czy współpracy wojskowej tylko po to, by zasłużyć na elementarną uprzejmość ze strony USA.

Der Angreifer, der in zwei Pariser Vorstädten aus mutmaßlich rassistischen Motiven einen Menschen getötet und fünf weitere verletzt hat, leidet offenbar an Schizophrenie und stand während der Tat unter Drogeneinfluss. Nachdem der Polizeigewahrsam des 39-jährigen Kevin T. am Samstag aufgehoben worden war, wurde er am Sonntag in die Psychiatrie eingewiesen, wie die Staatsanwaltschaft mitteilte.

Der Angreifer hatte am Freitag in den Pariser Vorstädten Trappes und La Verrière aus seinem Auto heraus mit einer Maschinenpistole auf Menschen geschossen. Dabei tötete er einen 26 Jahre alten Franzosen und verletzte mehrere. Ein zwölf Jahre altes Mädchen wurde von acht Kugeln getroffen und schwebt weiter in Lebensgefahr. Der vorbestrafte 39-Jährige wurde schließlich im nahe gelegenen Coignières festgenommen.

Nach der Tat am Freitag waren laut Staatsanwaltschaft Spuren von Cannabis und Kokain im Blut von Kevin T. nachgewiesen worden. Wie die Nachrichtenagentur AFP berichtet, erfuhr sie aus Ermittlerkreisen, dass der 39-Jährige unter Schizophrenie leide.

In der Wohnung des Angreifers im Ort Plaisir fanden die Ermittler Gasflaschen, Zünder und einen Zeitschaltmechanismus. Zudem stießen sie auf eine Ausgabe von Adolf Hitlers »Mein Kampf« sowie eine Bibel und einen Koran. Seine Eltern und zwei weitere Menschen aus seinem Umfeld kamen ebenfalls in Polizeigewahrsam.

Frankreichs Staatschef Emmanuel Macron sprach nach dem Angriff von einem »rassistischen Terroranschlag«. Die französische Anti-Terror-Staatsanwaltschaft leitete ein Ermittlungsverfahren wegen »Mordes im Zusammenhang mit einer terroristischen Vereinigung« ein. Dabei werde berücksichtigt, dass die Tat »aufgrund der tatsächlichen oder vermeintlichen Zugehörigkeit des Opfers zu einer bestimmten angeblichen Rasse, ethnischen Gruppe, Nation oder Religion begangen wurde«.

In einem Trauermarsch gedachten Hunderte des 26-jährigen Todesopfers. Die Familie des Todesopfers hatte kritisiert, dass der Polizeigewahrsam des mutmaßlichen Schützen nach einer psychiatrischen Untersuchung aufgehoben worden war. Der Anwalt der Familie des Getöteten erklärte, es müsse geprüft werden, ob Behördenversagen vorliege. Schließlich sei der Täter der Polizei und der Justiz bekannt gewesen und habe offenbar »Kriegswaffen« besessen.

Moscow will host the 2030 World Weightlifting Championships, marking the first time Russia will host any world championships in an Olympic sport since it invaded Ukraine in 2022.

The International Weightlifting Federation (IWF) announced its decision on Sunday, with Ahmedabad in India awarded the 2029 edition at the same time.

Russia’s status as a pariah state in the sporting world is slowly coming to an end, with Russian and Belarusian weightlifters eligible to compete in IWF events since the International Olympic Committee provisionally lifted its suspension of the Russian Olympic Committee in July.

The IOC Executive Board, when it lifted the suspension, said athletes should not be held responsible for the actions of their governments.

No decision has yet been ⁠taken on whether Russian athletes will compete under their national flag, colours and anthem at the Olympic Games.

The National Olympic Committee of Ukraine has appealed to the Court of Arbitration for Sport against the IOC's decision.

The ROC was suspended in October 2023 after recognising regional Olympic councils in the Russian-occupied Ukrainian territories of Luhansk, Donetsk, Kherson and Zaporizhzhia, following Russia's ‌invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

The 2025 World Aquatics Championships were originally awarded to the Russian city of Kazan but were later moved to Singapore.

This will be the first time since 1983 that Moscow has hosted the World Weightlifting Championships.

“All the bids we received were excellent and we are sure that the Championships in ⁠your respective countries will be a huge success," said IWF General ​Secretary ⁠Jose Quinones.

Russia's return to international competition also comes against the backdrop of one of the biggest doping scandals in Olympic sport.

The country ⁠has been under scrutiny since a 2015 World Anti-Doping Agency-commissioned report ⁠found evidence of systematic doping in Russian athletics, while subsequent investigations uncovered a state-backed cover-up ‌linked to the ​2014 Sochi Winter Olympics.

The IOC said all Russian athletes now returning to international competition would have to meet relevant anti-doping requirements.

Pressure grows on Sage as fans turn on Palace boss

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Highlights: Osula and Barnes give Newcastle victory at struggling Palace

"Oliver Glasner achieved some amazing things, and now I have to do the same," Pierre Sage said after replacing the man who delivered an FA Cup, a European trophy and Community Shield during two years in charge at Selhurst Park.

Four months into a three-year contract, the Frenchman is already under big pressure after a fifth defeat in eight Premier League games left the Eagles in the bottom four.

Palace have failed to win their past four games and Sunday's 2-0 home defeat by Newcastle was greeted with boos from home fans, who had turned up hoping to see a positive response after last weekend's 5-0 humiliation at fierce rivals Brighton.

"There is no noise inside the club," said Sage when asked about his future in his post-match media conference.

"The boos are normal because we don't have the result.

"The noise is the consequence of the result of the team, and at the moment we don't have what we expect.

"Inside, we are just really focused on solutions. How to be better, how to create the conditions to compete, and we will continue our work, and there's no noise inside."

Asked about boos from home fans during the match and at full-time, Sage accepted it was an expected response after failing to win.

"It's normal that people lose patience when you lose, because everybody wants to win. Today it was not the case."

Sage was named Ligue 1 manager of the year after leading Lens to a second-place finish as well as a French Cup triumph.

But could time already be running out for the 47-year-old?

Sage struck a defiant tone during his post-match media conference as he addressed questions about his future for 20 minutes.

"I don't have to convince the people around the club. I just have to convince the players to do their best to compete and to continue to improve," he added when asked further about his job.

"After that, this kind of decision is for the chairman, to the owners, this kind of person.

"For me, the noise around the team, the noise around the club, it's only noise."

Palace have just seven points from their first eight league games in the post-Glasner era.

After losing to Everton and Manchester City in their first two matches, Palace won at Fulham before losing at home to newly promoted Ipswich.

They secured a rare clean sheet in a goalless draw at Leeds before coming from behind to beat Nottingham Forest at Selhurst Park on 11 October.

They have since lost at his former club Lyon in the Europa League, and Brighton, before drawing 2-2 against Besiktas in Istanbul on Thursday.

However, patience is clearly wearing thin after fans reacted to the latest defeat by Newcastle.

In addition, Palace have the worst defensive record in the Premier League after conceding 19 goals in eight games.

Their next Premier League match is at improving Tottenham next Saturday, before hosting Liverpool on 8 November going into the international break.

Palace's first game back is at fellow strugglers Coventry City on 21 November.

French football expert Julien Laurens said Sage needed to be more ruthless.

"He's a lovely guy. He's maybe a little bit too nice at times," Laurens, speaking to BBC Radio 5 Live, added.

"Remember when Crystal Palace beat Fulham earlier in the season? That was Palace's first win of the season and he felt sorry for Fulham.

"You don't feel sorry in this league. This Premier League is ruthless. It's the top, top level. This is the best league in the world.

"You can't feel sorry for the opposition you have just beaten, especially when this is your first win and you needed it badly.

"But this is the kind of guy that he is."

Sage's Lens side were built on aggressive pressing and a willingness to attack quickly once they won the ball back.

The Opta numbers showed a team intent on regaining possession high up the pitch.

Lens made 426 high turnovers during the 2025-26 season, frequently turning those moments into chances - producing 69 shots and seven goals.

However, Palace are clearly struggling to fire under Sage's leadership.

"He needs to do things slightly differently," added Laurens. "There is pressure everywhere in the Premier League."

Former Manchester United and England forward Dion Dublin said Sage needed to be tougher if he is to have a long-term future at Palace.

"It's no good being lovely with everything," added Dublin, also speaking to BBC Radio 5 Live.

"Things can't be lovely when you are a Premier League manager. You will just lose games and get sacked."

Former Leeds United forward Lucy Ward told 5 Live: "He has proved himself at Lyon and Lens. Being a little bit too nice doesn't help."

Why Patrick Gorman says public service tech barriers will fall ... eventually

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Old government computer systems are here to stay - for now - and the full-throttle pace of tech evolution means the right replacements for some may not have been invented yet, Assistant Minister for the Public Service Patrick Gorman says.

Mr Gorman praised public servants' "enthusiasm" for new technology such as artificial intelligence (AI), but said agencies need to remain sensible about funding upgrades and avoid falling victim to "first iteration" technology in favour of long-term solutions.

"The first iteration of a technology that people might really want doesn't end up being the lasting technology that is used for very long at all," Mr Gorman told The Canberra Times.

"This year is effectively 20 years since Blackberries were rolled out in the public service. You can't get a Blackberry today. They've come and gone," he said.

Public servants cited technology used inside government agencies as a "great or very great" barrier to peak work performance 28 per cent of the time in the latest APS census - 35 per cent said it was no barrier.

Mr Gorman, who deputises Minister for the Public Service Katy Gallagher and is also assistant minister to the Prime Minister and the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations, said the statistics spoke to improving tech-savvy within the service.

"The enthusiasm for using technology at work, I find really encouraging," Mr Gorman said, but the government "do hear the message" that public servants are struggling with tech barriers.

"I get why that is annoying to people, but I also understand that not every device on every workstation needs to have every type of software or needs to have every capability," he said.

Wholesale replacement of government legacy tech would be a $15 billion hit to the federal budget, Home Affairs officials have said, while a widely cited 2025 report penned by Microsoft and Mandala found 71 per cent of agencies use outdated IT accounting for 40 per cent of tech budgets.

Mr Gorman urged discernment as agencies hunt for system replacements to ward off the cyber security threats posed by AI after new directives issued by Home Affairs late last month.

"Some of the systems that public servants need to use are large secure systems that have had significant investment in them and it doesn't always make sense to be doing tech investment every single budget cycle for every single agency," he said.

Mr Gorman said the success of programs like the APS Digital Profession had also demonstrated "the ability to get a lot of knowledge sharing happening without large investments in platforms or soft infrastructure".

Just shy of 20,000 (more than one in 10) public servants have joined the Digital Profession - which connects government tech workers to help share skills and capabilities - since 2021, according to figures provided by Mr Gorman's office, or a 30 per cent membership increase since July 2025.

The Digital Profession's annual report is due in December.

Mr Gorman said he was "encouraged" by the latest APS census results despite a sharp decline in satisfaction with wages, job security and the ability of the service to manage change as agencies struggle to reduce spending on external consultants and chase voluntary redundancies.

Public servants are expected to reject a three-year, 9 per cent pay rise offer put forward by the government on Tuesday.

"I like that public servants do, when we give them the opportunity to, tell us exactly what they think," he said.

"I'll get that again in the [2027 State of the Service] Road Show," said Mr Gorman. "And I do appreciate when people actually say this is what's working and this is what's not."

But he would not be drawn on future changes to the Strategic Commissioning Framework - which required agencies to set targets to reduce external contractor spending and in-house core work.

A recent report by the Australian National Audit Office found agencies were using "narrow" language in the Framework to claim savings from jobs returned to within the service while still spending more on external contractors.

Mr Gorman insisted the Framework was a success that had been "embedded into the core day-to-day business of the public service", which the government would "continue to improve it over time".

Senator Gallagher last month told this masthead the government was not done in its attempts to cut external agency spending, saying the public service had grown to a size where it could absorb work that would otherwise be outsourced.

"There's more to be done and we will do it," said Senator Gallagher.

The government spent $20 billion on its external workforce over the past financial year and more than $75 billion on employment and superannuation overall.
