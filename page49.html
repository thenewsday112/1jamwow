Shortly after his daughter Meghan married Prince Harry, Thomas Markle was the subject of a cover story on America's most notorious supermarket tabloid, The National Enquirer.

Published in October 2018, it gave a compelling insight into why his absence from that summer's royal wedding might, in the great scheme of things, turn out to have been a blessing in disguise.

The article was based on a tape recording of the retired TV lighting director discussing his time in Hollywood, where he'd won three Emmy awards.

Two excerpts stood out. In one, Markle brazenly talked about having dabbled in Class A drugs, confessing: 'In the industry I worked in back in the 70s and 80s there was cocaine around a lot. And I'm sure that I had an occasional sniff of cocaine.'

In the second, he gave a bizarrely equivocal answer when asked if he'd ever consorted with prostitutes in South-East Asia, where he'd holidayed on a number of occasions.

'Have I ever paid for a sex worker? Uh, there was a Playboy Club in the top floor of a hotel in the Philippines [but] I never paid for sex. I would go upstairs and enjoy myself in the Playboy Club, but… not for sex. I didn't get sex out of it,' Markle said.

But at the weekend he admitted to the Mail on Sunday that he had paid for sex in the Philippines.

The tape recording turned out to have been secretly made by Markle's son, Thomas Jr.

The Duchess of Sussex has been estranged from her father, Thomas Markle Sr, 82, since 2018. Pictured: The couple together on the day of Meghan's marriage to film producer Trevor Engelson in 2011

Roslyn Loveless, the first wife of Meghan Markle's estranged father Thomas, pictured outside her home in Albuquerque, New Mexico

Meghan Markle's half-brother Thomas Markle Jnr pictured hand-in-hand with a woman outside the apartment building where he lived with his father in the Philippines on May 10, 2025

In a separate interview, around the same time, he discussed his father's love life, claiming that in between divorcing his mother, Roslyn Loveless, in 1975, and marrying Meghan's mother, Doria Ragland, in 1979, he'd enjoyed a lengthy relationship with a lady from the Philippines named Marty.

'There's a certain type of woman he likes. He likes Asian women a lot,' said Thomas Jr, before adding enigmatically: 'He likes to go on vacation to Thailand, Vietnam. He likes their culture a lot.'

Fast-forward eight years and Meghan's father, now 82, is once more the subject of a toe-curling sex scandal. The principal setting is, again, the Philippines.

And the most explosive details are, as previously, being made public by Thomas Jr, one of two children from whom he appears to be irreparably estranged.

The whole thing kicked off at exactly 10.04pm on Thursday, when it emerged that Filipino police were investigating Thomas Sr over a huge stash of child sex images that had allegedly been found at his home in Cebu, a tourist island some 350 miles south-east of the capital Manila.

It had purportedly been discovered by Thomas Jr, who told The Sun newspaper that he'd found 'hundreds if not thousands' of images in four cardboard boxes that had been shipped there from Rosarito in Mexico, where father and son had lived before moving out to the Philippines for a 'fresh start' in January 2025. 'When I found this material, I was horrified and I had no option but to report my dad,' he said.

'I have supported, cared for him and helped him for years – but that stops now. I won't speak to my father ever again unless I'm called to give evidence against him.'

Thomas Jr, 60, claims to have come across the material in July, while searching for a mobile phone handset he could give to the father of his fiancee, a 28-year-old Filipino woman named Rose.

Mr Markle faces child sex allegations which he denies and told The Mail on Sunday he was 'not buying' the idea that his first wife, who died two years ago, wrote the letter to the Met Police in early 2019

He says he charged up an old LG device, which had previously belonged to his Dad, only for explicit video of 'naked young girls' to flash up on the screen.

Other images, of what The Sun described as 'victims appearing to be aged no older than ten or 11,' were discovered on a Lenovo ThinkPad laptop.

There were also alleged to be explicit DVDs and photographs, some of them secreted in the pages of a couple of coffee table books that belonged to Thomas Sr. One was called Lewis Carroll Photographs Of Children: Four Nude Studies, the other Hollywood Lolitas: The Nymphet Syndrome In The Movies.

According to Thomas Jr, he promptly reported the discovery of the material to the FBI, hoping they would launch an investigation into his father.

Thomas Sr had moved back to the US in May after suffering serious health difficulties, including a stroke and a blood clot, which resulted in the amputation of his left leg below the knee.

Yet three months passed, without an FBI probe ensuing. So Thomas Jr instead decided to report the matter to police in the Philippines.

On Friday morning, he transported the four cardboard boxes to the police headquarters in Cebu City in a Toyota HiAce people-carrier under the watchful eyes of a Sun reporter.

Thomas Jr, who had until now sided with his father in various public spats with Meghan, then used the newspaper to issue a mea culpa to his estranged half-sister, saying yesterday: 'I'm so, so sorry… I now know I have been protecting the wrong person for the past nine years.'

The Duchess of Sussex had by then set a second hare running. On Friday, she issued a statement declaring herself 'deeply distressed at the news that has come to light in the last 24 hours'.

Meghan added that in 2018, the Metropolitan Police had shared with her details of a letter which 'made troubling and disturbing allegations against my father'.

She was 'given assurances that these were being investigated', and subsequently decided to prevent her children from having contact with their grandfather, in order 'to protect them'.

It later emerged, the letter had in fact been sent in early 2019, shortly after The National Enquirer's revelations about Thomas Sr's previous exploits in an 'X-rated jiggle joint' in the Philippines. The letter's author was Thomas Sr's alcoholic ex-wife Roslyn Loveless, who died in 2024.

She alleged that he had admitted going to the Philippines and Thailand to abuse children, according to sources who have seen the letter's contents.

The Met is now 'reviewing its handling of the matter' and may yet refer itself to the police watchdog.

One odd wrinkle is that the letter seems to have ended up not with officers specialising in child sex abuse but with the Force's 'Fixated Threat Assessment Centre', a unit frequently referred to as its 'stalker and obsessive unit', which deals with people who threaten royals, celebrities and other public figures.

It's unclear whether this means officers concluded that claims made by Ms Loveless suggested she was a potential threat to Meghan, or whether they were instead concerned about Thomas Sr. Either way, an informed source said last night that the Met would almost certainly have had no jurisdiction to carry out further inquiries since Mr Markle was not in the UK and neither was his alleged offending nor the purported victims.

Another tricky factor for the law enforcement agencies might be the fact that the age of consent in the Philippines was only upgraded to 16 in 2022. Prior to that point it was just 12.

Quite how seriously the Duchess of Sussex took the 2019 letter from Ms Loveless is, at this stage, unclear. In February 2019, shortly after it arrived, Meghan authorised five friends to brief her favourite outlet, People magazine, about her estrangement from Thomas Sr. The resulting article suggested the Duchess wished to repair the relationship.

Later, in a 2022 Netflix documentary, Meghan declared that she had been 'a daddy's girl my whole life'. She only appears to have categorically cut him off in late 2025, when Thomas Sr was gravely ill in hospital in the Philippines.

At that point, a representative was dispatched to hand-deliver a letter in a scene witnessed by the Mail's Caroline Graham.

None of which is, of course, to suggest that the Duchess is anything but a victim of this week's appalling developments. One might argue, in fact, she is the only member of her troubled American family who emerges from recent developments with her reputation enhanced.

Thomas Jr's motives are, to this end, very much open to question.

A twice-divorced window-fitter, with two grown-up sons (one is a marijuana farmer in Oregon, where the activity is legal), his chaotic personal CV includes an arrest for allegedly putting a gun to a girlfriend's head during a drunken row (charges were filed in 2017 but later dismissed after they reconciled) and a court appearance for drink-driving.

He also appeared on Australia's version of Celebrity Big Brother in 2022, alleging that he and Meghan had once been close, but that 'money changed her' after she achieved fame.

In recent years, the 60-year-old has been almost entirely financially supported by his father. The duo moved out to the Philippines together in January 2025, and Thomas Jr remains there.

Thomas Sr, for his part, returned to the US in May to recuperate from the health crisis that saw his leg amputated.

He left behind his most recent girlfriend Rio Canedo, a local nurse who he'd met when she treated him in hospital.

At 46, she is three and a half decades his junior.

Meghan's father initially spent time in Pennsylvania but has lately been in a nursing home in Lakeland, Florida, where he is being supported by Samantha Markle, the Duchess's somewhat outspoken second half‑sibling, who is the eldest child of Ms Loveless.

Samantha, a former actress and model who has given a number of unflattering interviews about Meghan over the years, remains very much in her father's camp.

She told the Daily Mail at the weekend that her brother's allegations about their father are 'disgusting', saying they have 'no merit in them whatsoever'.

She described Thomas Jr as a 'liar' who is fabricating the claims to make cash.

Furthermore, she has alleged that his original complaint to the FBI in July followed a family row over money: shortly after returning to the US in May, Thomas Sr changed passwords to a bank account his son had access to.

'My brother first came out with these lies days after the cash from my Dad was cut off. He has used Dad as a cash cow for years, and once the money stopped he made up the most outrageous lies he could to make money from the media,' was how Samantha put it.

She further described her brother as a 'delusional alcoholic' and said the devices said to contain illicit material had been untouched by her father since he'd arrived in the Philippines, where they had been kept in storage.

'Dad had no access to those computers for two years. Who knows who could have had access to them? Anyone could have set Dad up. There is no merit in these disgusting allegations whatsoever and my brother is disgusting for making them up.'

In remarks which may do little to improve her father's reputation in the court of public opinion, Samantha added that one of the photographs at the centre of the investigation, which purportedly shows her father on a bed with a young-looking Asian woman, 'may be one of his girlfriends'.

She went on: 'Dad dated a wonderful Japanese woman called Carolyn who I loved. She was in her 30s but she looked like a teenager. Asian women have great skin and are small and petite. They look much younger than they are.

'Dad had another girlfriend called Gail. She was Asian and gorgeous but looked very young.'

And what of Thomas Sr? He is purportedly in severe ill health, but has declared himself prepared to do 'whatever it takes' to clear his name, saying that the computers and phones at the centre of this week's events haven't been in his possession for more than two years so: 'If there is anything bad on there it was put there by someone else.'

As we have seen in an interview with the Mail on Sunday, he admitted that when he was single he travelled to the Philippines at least twice and 'slept with prostitutes', adding, perhaps ill-advisedly, that: 'I like Asian women but that is not a crime'.

Parlamentswahl in Serbien – Studentenbewegung triumphiert – Vucic gesteht Niederlage ein

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Zwar lägen noch keine endgültigen Ergebnisse vor, auf Basis von Hochrechnungen sei jedoch klar, dass seine Partei die Wahl verloren habe, sagte Vucic. Die Studentenliste habe die Wahl für sich entschieden und werde zwischen 135 und 140 Sitze im Parlament erhalten, während seine eigene Partei auf 105 Mandate komme.

«Liebes Serbien, heute Abend findet die Stimme des Volkes Gehör», sagte Radomir Sajcic, ein Professor an der Universität Belgrad, der im Namen der Studentinnen und Studenten sprach. «Heute Abend feiern wir den Sieg, morgen gestalten wir die Zukunft.»

Die Wahlbeteiligung lag bei fast 70 Prozent und war damit so hoch wie seit dem Zerfall Jugoslawiens im Jahr 1992 nicht mehr. Lange Schlangen hatten sich am Sonntag vor den Wahllokalen in den serbischen Städten und Gemeinden gebildet.

In den Strassen der serbischen Hauptstadt Belgrad brach Jubel aus. Autokorsos rasten hupend durch die Stadt.

Kurz nach der Ansprache von Vucic veröffentlichte die Wahlforschungsgruppe CRTA das Ergebnis ihrer Projektion. Demnach habe die Studentenliste 51.5 Prozent der Stimmen auf sich vereint, sagte Programmdirektor Rasa Nedeljkov auf einer Pressekonferenz. Die SNS kam der Projektion zufolge auf 40.4 Prozent. Keine weitere Partei habe den Einzug ins Parlament geschafft. Für den Einzug gilt in Serbien eine Drei-Prozent-Hürde beziehungsweise eine Ein-Prozent-Hürde für die Parteien der ethnischen Minderheiten.

Auch in der Schweiz konnten serbische Wahlberechtigte ihre Stimme abgeben. Dafür standen Wahllokale in Bern und Zürich zur Verfügung.

Vor dem serbischen Konsulat in Zürich bildete sich zeitweise eine bis zu einem halben Kilometer lange Warteschlange. Manche Wählerinnen und Wähler mussten nach Angaben vor Ort bis zu acht Stunden warten, bevor sie ihren Wahlzettel einwerfen konnten. Wer sich vor Ablauf der Frist noch in der Warteschlange der Wahllokale befand, durfte seine Stimme noch abgeben.

Die Abstimmung wurde weithin als Referendum über die Führung des Landes durch Vucic aufgefasst. In wechselnden Funktionen bestimmt der 56-Jährige seit 2012 die Politik im Alleingang. Kritikerinnen und Kritiker werfen ihm Korruption und Verbindungen zur organisierten Kriminalität vor. Er war Ende September von seinem Amt als Staatspräsident zurückgetreten, um im Falle eines Wahlerfolgs seiner Partei Regierungschef zu werden.

Herausgefordert hat den starken Mann Serbiens die Liste der neuen Studentenbewegung. Sie entstand aus den Protesten nach dem Einsturz des Bahnhofvordachs in der nordserbischen Stadt Novi Sad im November 2024. Dabei waren 16 Menschen ums Leben gekommen. Kritiker führten das Unglück auf die Korruption und Schlamperei der Vucic-Regierung zurück. Die anschliessenden Proteste der Studierenden mobilisierten breite Schichten der serbischen Bevölkerung und weckten in ihnen den Wunsch nach Veränderung.

Auf der Studentenliste stehen nahezu keine Studierenden. Aufgestellt hat sie eine Bewegung, die über keine Führungsgremien verfügt und ihre Entscheidungen in basisdemokratischen Versammlungen trifft. Die Liste besteht vor allem aus Lehrkräften der Universitäten, aus Ärztinnen und Ärzten sowie Künstlern. Angeführt wird sie von dem angesehenen Kardiologen Ilija Srdanovic aus Novi Sad. Das knappe Elf-Punkte-Programm verspricht die Wiederherstellung von Rechtsstaat und Demokratie sowie die Verfolgung von Korruption und Kriminalität.

Die Wahl am Sonntag fand in einer angespannten Atmosphäre statt. CRTA berichtete von zahlreichen Indizien für einen Stimmenkauf und eine Wählereinschüchterung im ganzen Land. In 48 Prozent der Wahllokale seien Stimmzettel fotografiert worden. Auch über gewalttätige Angriffe auf Wahlbeobachtende und Journalistinnen und Journalisten berichteten die Medien.

Serbien verhandelt seit 2014 über einen EU-Beitritt. In den vergangenen Jahren gab es dabei keinerlei Fortschritte. Einer der Gründe dafür ist Beobachtern zufolge der Abbau der Demokratie unter Vucic.

Architect is struck off after calling her neighbours racist during row 'because they cheered for England and occasionally flew a Union Jack flag'

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Published: 01:53 GMT, 26 October 2026 | Updated: 01:57 GMT, 26 October 2026

An architect was struck off after making a string of false allegations against her neighbours - including accusing one of being racist because they cheered for England and occasionally flew a Union Jack flag.

The Architects Registration Board struck Karin Elliott off for a minimum of two years after finding her guilty of unacceptable professional conduct by sending inappropriate communications.

The tribunal found she used 'inflammatory language, making a series of misleading and potentially damaging accusations' in 2024 against her neighbours 'with scant regard to their accuracy'.

The board said the Irish-American architect's actions were 'misleading, lacking integrity and dishonest'.

Ms Elliott accused one neighbour of 'racial harassment' after falsely claiming that he wielded a chainsaw in her garden in Norwich, the tribunal heard.

The architect's partner, Francois Girardin, told the tribunal that none of the neighbours said 'anything directly racist'.

However, Mr Girardin, who is French, made an 'inference' of racism from how 'sometimes a Union Jack would appear' and 'a wall had been constructed after the US President had talked about building a big wall to keep out immigrants', the tribunal heard.

Mr Girardin also said whenever England played sport, a neighbour would 'leave their doors open, and – especially when England played France – their family would cheer for England'.

The Architects Registration Board struck off Karin Elliott (pictured) for a minimum of two years after finding her guilty of unacceptable professional conduct by sending inappropriate communication

Ms Elliott also accused a neighbour of gender violence for driving his 'phallic symbol' of a car across her driveway.

During cross-examination, Ms Elliott said from 'a psychoanalytic perspective' her neighbour repeatedly driving across her driveway with the 'phallic symbol' of his car was a manifestation of gender violence.

The tribunal found this 'underscored how tenuously she constructed serious accusations against other people, and how casually she dispensed them'.

The architect had made a planning application for the installation of CCTV cameras on her property in December 2023 following disputes with her neighbours.

She wrote a letter to local residents in February 2024 asking for support for her application and made serious allegations about her neighbours that the tribunal considered to be 'dishonest' and 'misleading'.

In the letter to residents, she claimed one neighbour had 'smashed down several of our historic walls, chopped down many of our historic trees, and built on our property, all without our consent'.

Discussing another neighbour, Ms Elliott wrote: 'He calls us either a "piece of s***", "disgusting" and/or exclaims "the stench" regularly.'

In another allegation, the architect wrote: 'He threatened to hit one of us over the head with a hammer at one stage, after he was asked to desist.

'He had been starting to build a wall on our property illegally on a Sunday, following Trump's declaration that he would "Build a big wall" to keep out immigrants.

'It is very upsetting. We are Irish/American and French.'

Discussing claims about a third neighbour, Ms Elliott accused her of building an 'ugly concrete foundation' and using influence at the local council 'to escape justice'.

She also accused her neighbour of singing Tragedy by the Bee Gees 'from the enormous balcony they built overlooking our home' after the death of her father.

Discussing the three neighbours, Ms Elliott wrote: 'We hope that the CCTV might deter them all from further trespassing and further destroying our listed garden, and from any further gender violence and racial harassment.'

In cross-examination, the architect said: 'I think I'd give this letter a serious editing if I did it again.'

Ms Elliott made further allegations in a planning appeal form to Norwich City Planning Portal in March 2024.

Discussing one neighbour in the planning form, the architect wrote: 'I begged him to turn off his chainsaw and explained that I considered it to be racial harassment.'

One neighbour told the tribunal that the architect's 'bizarre accusations of threatening behaviour and the racist accusations have been a source of acute embarrassment and personal discomfort'.

Another said the allegations were 'very depressing and upsetting' and they do not spend time in their garden because Ms Elliott and her partner are 'always looking at us', adding that the architect is an 'absolute disgrace to her profession'.

The tribunal found Ms Elliott's conduct occurred after she had previously been found guilty of unacceptable professional conduct in 2021 for calling neighbours 'racists and Nazis' as well as 'making offensive references to their physical characteristics and mental health'.

Anti-social behaviour like drinking in public and riding e-bikes could be stopped under new crackdown

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Published: 01:51 GMT, 26 October 2026 | Updated: 03:06 GMT, 26 October 2026

Anti-social behaviours including drinking on the street and riding e-bikes could be stopped under new ASBO-style powers to be introduced by the Government.

Respect Orders, implemented from today, allow agencies like police and councils to impose tougher restrictions on anti-social offenders for behaviours such as harassment, intimidation, street drinking, littering and drug use.

The orders - which only apply to adult offenders - will see criminals hit with punishments like town centre bans and curfews for engaging in disorderly conduct, as well as handed unlimited fines and community sanctions like volunteering.

And, for the first time, courts will also be given the power to enroll serial offenders into rehab programmes if their crimes are linked to drug and alcohol use, or order anger management classes.

The breaching of a Respect Order, which will be imposed across England and Wales, will be considered a criminal offence and be punishable by up to two years' jail time.

Those with the power to enforce the orders include the Environment Agency, social landlords, Transport for London, West Midlands Combined Authority, Transport for Greater Manchester, Natural Resources Wales and NHS Counter Fraud Authority.

Anti-social behaviours in offenders could be quashed under new Respect Orders imposed by the Government. Policing Minister Sarah Jones is pictured in Staffordshire, where she discussed the new orders with police and community members

Ms Jones said the public should not 'tolerate' anti-social behaviour, with the new orders intended to help communities 'feel safe'

Policing Minister Sarah Jones MP, speaking in Staffordshire, said the Respect Orders are intended to help neighbourhoods across the country 'feel safe'.

Ms Jones said: 'We want our neighbourhoods to be places where people can enjoy, they can feel safe, they can go to the pub, they can go to the shop, they can get their hair done, without feeling like they are going to be abused by somebody, threatened by somebody, or see something horrific going on in their neighbourhoods.

'That is what we want, and that is what should be normal. That's what we should all be aiming for. The Respect Orders will help with that. Antisocial behaviour is pernicious. We shouldn't tolerate it, we shouldn't accept it.'

Chief Inspector Cliff Goodwin, the local policing commander for Newcastle-under-Lyme and Staffordshire Moorlands, said the issues the town faces are the same issues many communities across the country face - including people riding e-bikes and e-scooters, drug-taking, fouling and people congregating in certain areas.

He said the Respect Orders would enable officers to stop antisocial behaviour 'in its tracks' by putting persistent offenders before the courts when they have not engaged with diversionary work beforehand.

The legislation has been passed 'in tandem' with the Ministry of Justice, Ms Jones said, and despite the court system being 'very challenged' at the moment, she insisted the orders were 'really important'.

She said: 'If the public are saying to us, antisocial behaviour is actually something that makes me feel less safe in my town, that really affects my quality of living, then we need to be responding, and the courts need to be responding.

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said anti-social behaviour is 'not inevitable' and she will not 'accept it'

Will tougher Respect Orders actually make our streets safer or just target everyday behaviour unfairly?

'We just need that concerted effort so that everybody is saying antisocial behaviour is not low level, it's not something that comes last on the list of priorities, it should be upfront as a really big push from all the different agencies to tackle it.'

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said she will 'not accept' anti-social behaviour which 'blights' Britain's communities.

She said: 'Unchecked, anti-social behaviour blights our communities. But it is not inevitable and I won't accept it.

'For too long, we have lacked the powers we need to deal with this problem properly. From today, that changes: authorities will be able to take tough action against these offenders.

'This delivers on a commitment we made to the British people in our manifesto. And, most importantly, those who make life a misery for others will now face the consequences of their actions.'

Председатель Еврокомиссии (ЕК) Урсула фон дер Ляйен и глава Европейского совета (ЕС) Антониу Кошта поздравили сербский «Студенческий список» с победой на досрочных парламентских выборах. Оба политика опубликовали одинаковые заявления в соцсети X.

Is 'ferritin face' a real thing - and can you really spot low iron from looks?

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Lucy says she noticed changes in her skin and hair alongside symptoms including fatigue and hair loss when her ferritin levels were low

"Do you have ferritin face?" a young woman asks on TikTok, pointing to dark circles, washed-out skin and brittle hair, alongside before-and-after style photographs. The content creator puts these features down to her iron levels.

It is one of a growing number of videos and posts on TikTok and Instagram claiming that low ferritin - a protein which stores iron in the body - can leave visible clues on your face.

Users list pale or grey-looking skin, dark under-eye circles, dry or cracked lips, hair shedding and weak nails, while others share portraits claiming stark visual differences.

The phrase "ferritin face" has spread widely across social media, with videos attracting millions of views.

But doctors say appearance alone cannot diagnose iron deficiency.

"Ferritin face is not a medical diagnosis," said GP Dr Aziza Sesay, who is based in the West Midlands. "However, it may be a clue to tell us that something is going on."

Pale skin, hair shedding or brittle nails can accompany low iron, but can also be signs of other conditions, she told BBC News.

Your ferritin levels show how good your body's iron stores are, while haemoglobin is the protein in red blood cells which carries oxygen. When haemoglobin is low, the blood cannot carry as much oxygen - a condition known as anaemia.

But iron stores can become depleted before that happens, meaning someone can be iron-deficient and have symptoms without being anaemic.

Can you tell your ferritin levels from your face?

"You've got no stored iron but you're maintaining your haemoglobin. That's the big disconnect," said consultant haematologist Dr Drew Provan.

Dr Sue Pavord, also a consultant haematologist, said iron deficiency is common and often under-diagnosed in women.

UK guidelines from the British Society of Gastroenterology say ferritin below 15 micrograms per litre indicates absent iron stores, while below 30 generally indicates low iron stores.

Social media users have shared posts and videos claiming that low ferritin can cause visible changes to the face

Lucy M, 32, who posts about hair loss as @marvluceskin on Instagram and TikTok, said "ferritin face" seemed to describe changes she had noticed in herself.

In 2021 she developed heart palpitations, fatigue and hair loss. After being told her blood tests were normal and being diagnosed with generalised anxiety disorder, a later test showed her ferritin was 12 micrograms per litre.

Lucy said she looked "grey" and her hair had become dull and brittle.

She shared photographs with the BBC from when her ferritin was low, compared to when it had recovered, and said the difference between how she felt and looked was "night and day".

Videos about "ferritin face" often encourage viewers to look for a combination of physical signs - from dark circles and pale skin to dry lips, thinning hair and brittle nails - and compare them with their own appearance.

"You're not ugly, your ferritin is low", one creator says, pointing to the camera.

Provan said it was "impossible" to determine someone's ferritin levels from how their face looks.

People who are significantly anaemic may look pale, he explained, while fatigue can contribute to dark circles, but these visual signs are "subjective and unreliable".

In people with darker skin, changes in skin colour may be less obvious, Sesay added, meaning someone looking specifically for the signs described online could miss iron deficiency.

Doctors say appearance alone cannot tell you whether your ferritin is low, and that iron deficiency should be confirmed with a blood test

For doctors, symptoms and how someone feels are more useful than appearance.

Blood tests matter not only to confirm iron deficiency, Provan and Sesay said, but to establish the cause and guide treatment.

Both said treatment should also address the cause. Heavy periods, low dietary iron, gastrointestinal blood loss and problems absorbing iron through the gut can all play a role.

Iron deficiency is commonly treated with oral iron. Pavord said iron tablets are best taken on an empty stomach, ideally first thing in the morning, and advised avoiding tea or coffee around the time they are taken because they can inhibit iron absorption.

Iron can also be obtained naturally from foods including meat, beans, lentils, leafy green vegetables and fortified cereals.

Taking iron supplements without a diagnosis can be harmful, while self-treating could delay the investigation of an underlying problem.

Lucy said social media has nevertheless helped women take symptoms more seriously, and has given some the confidence to ask questions when something feels wrong.

"There are so many women saying, 'Oh my gosh, this is me," she said, adding that she is not a doctor and presents her content as her own experience.

For her, doing so has been empowering - giving women information with which to advocate for themselves.

Sesay called social media a "double-edged sword" - it can encourage people to seek help, but can also lead them to incorrectly diagnose themselves from a short video.

For Lucy, the useful part is not diagnosing yourself from your appearance, but knowing when something doesn't feel right, and when to ask questions.
