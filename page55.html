Todd Blanche challenges Dana Bash for only citing part of Constitution during heated election clash

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Attorney General Todd Blanche confronted CNN's Dana Bash for highlighting only part of the Constitution's Elections Clause during a heated exchange over the DOJ's lawsuit involving Alaska's Senate race.

Attorney General Todd Blanche confronted CNN host Dana Bash on Sunday after the network highlighted only the first portion of the Constitution's Elections Clause during a heated exchange on "State of the Union" over the Justice Department's lawsuit concerning two candidates with similar names in Alaska's closely watched Senate race.

Blanche pushed back as Bash referenced the Alaska Supreme Court's earlier ruling allowing Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., who shares a name with incumbent Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan, to remain on the ballot. The attorney general demanded that CNN display the remainder of the constitutional provision, which grants Congress authority to make or alter regulations governing congressional elections.

"Conveniently, you put up the Constitution on your screen, but you highlighted only the first clause. Why don't you show the next clause to your viewers, which says that Congress is allowed to pass laws to make sure that that happens? Put that back up there," Blanche said.

BLANCHE WON'T RULE OUT DOJ BLOCKING ELECTION CERTIFICATION IN 'DOOMSDAY' SCENARIO

Attorney General Todd Blanche confronted CNN host Dana Bash after the network highlighted only part of the Constitution's Elections Clause during a heated discussion about Alaska's Senate election. (Getty Images)

The exchange centered on Article I, Section 4 of the Constitution, which gives state legislatures authority to establish procedures for congressional elections while permitting Congress to enact or modify those regulations.

Blanche argued that the second half of the provision supports the federal government's intervention under existing federal statutes.

"The second clause says that the Congress may at any time by law, and we have a law, and that's the law that we're relying on when we filed suit earlier this week," Blanche said.

The Justice Department’s lawsuit relies on the National Voter Registration Act, arguing that allowing a candidate who, according to the DOJ, intends to confuse voters to remain on the ballot undermines election integrity. The complaint asks the court to delay election certification if votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. could alter the final outcome.

The DOJ's complaint accused the challenger of deliberately attempting to mislead voters by using a name similar to the incumbent's.

DOJ OFFICIAL DEFENDS TRUMP'S NONCITIZEN VOTER PROBE ON '60 MINUTES,' ACCUSES SHOW OF EDITING OUT HER ANSWERS

Blanche defended the Justice Department's lawsuit over Alaska's Senate race, arguing that a second candidate named Daniel Sullivan could confuse voters and potentially influence the election's outcome. (Ken Cedeno/AFP via Getty Images)

Media Research Center President David Bozell criticized Bash's presentation of the constitutional language in a statement to Fox News Digital, accusing CNN of failing to provide viewers with the complete provision.

"An anchor who hands viewers half a provision on election integrity is running a magic act, and the trick fell apart on live television. Bash's job is to give the audience the full text up front. CNN owes its dozens of viewers the whole clause, and a better editor," Bozell said.

The DOJ lawsuit follows a June ruling by the Alaska Supreme Court allowing the challenger to appear on the ballot despite objections from state election officials. The court left the question of how his name should appear to the state's Division of Elections.

"Daniel J. Sullivan decided to run for Senator in 2026 as a Republican with the intention of misleading supporters of Senator Sullivan into voting for him," the complaint states.

Senate candidate Dan Sullivan, left, pictured alongside Sen. Dan Sullivan, R-Alaska, right. (Sullivan for U.S. Senate; Brandon Bell-Pool/Getty Images)

Republicans have accused the challenger, a retired teacher from Petersburg, of attempting to siphon votes from the incumbent. Daniel J. Sullivan has denied those accusations and maintained that his candidacy is legitimate.

President Donald Trump also addressed the controversy Friday while speaking to reporters in Florida, acknowledging that he personally encouraged the Justice Department to pursue legal action.

"I'm the one that said, do it," Trump said.

The federal challenge faces opposition from voting rights groups and the campaign of Democratic candidate Mary Peltola, both of which filed motions to intervene in the case. The American Civil Liberties Union argued that the National Voter Registration Act establishes voter-registration procedures and does not authorize blocking election certification over candidate placement.

Putin, Rus devlet kanalı Vesti'ye Avrupa'daki protestolar, göç sorunları ve enerji piyasalarındaki gelişmelere ilişkin açıklamalarda bulundu.

Rusya'nın "Avrupa'daki protestoları kışkırttığı" yönündeki suçlamalara tepki gösteren Putin, "Avrupa'nın büyük şehirlerindeki kitlesel gösterileri bizim kışkırttığımızı söylemek gerçekten aşırıya kaçmak. Bunun Avrupa ülkelerindeki sıradan vatandaşlar için de açık olduğunu düşünüyorum." değerlendirmesinde bulundu.

Putin, Avrupa ülkelerindeki yöneticilerin yaşanan sorunların sorumluluğunu üstlenmek yerine başka ülkeleri suçladıklarını belirterek, "Bu, bazı Avrupalı liderlerin zayıflığını ve yaşananlardan sorumluluk alamadıklarını gösteriyor." diye konuştu.

Protestoların Avrupa ülkelerinin kendi politikalarındaki hatalardan kaynaklandığını savunan Putin, bazı ülkelerde kamu borcunun Gayri Safi Yurt İçi Hasıla'ya oranının yüzde 100'ü aştığını, İtalya'da ise yüzde 139'a ulaştığını kaydetti.

Putin, Avrupa'ya geçmiş yıllarda gelen göçmenlerin ve çocukların topluma uyum sağlayamadıklarını dikkati çekerek, "Bu ülkelerden gelen göçmenlerin sayısı öyle bir seviyeye ulaştı ki artık topluma uyum sağlayamıyorlar. Devletler de onların uyumunu sağlayacak durumda değil. Kaynaklar yetersiz. Eğitimleriyle ilgilenmek, yerel gelenek ve görenekleri onlara benimsetmek gerekiyor." ifadelerini kullandı.

Avrupa'nın göç politikalarını da eleştiren Putin, bazı göçmenlerin uzun yıllardır yaşadıkları ülkelerin dilini konuşamadıklarını, Avrupa ülkelerinin Orta Doğu'ya yönelik politikalarının da bölgeden gelen göçmenlerin tepkilerini etkilediğini ifade etti.

Putin, Rusya'nın da çok uluslu bir ülke olduğuna dikkati çekerek, Avrupa'daki sorunlarla alay edilmemesi gerektiğini ve ülkesinin ulusal topluluklar arası ilişkiler politikalarına daha fazla önem vereceğini vurguladı.

Avrupa ülkelerinin Rusya'ya yönelik enerji politikalarını da değerlendiren Putin, ülkesinin Avrupa'ya enerji kaynakları tedarik etmeyi hiçbir zaman reddetmediğini söyledi.

Putin, petrol, doğal gaz, kömür ve uranyum gibi Rus enerji kaynaklarını satın almama kararının Avrupa ülkeleri tarafından alındığını anımsatarak, "Biz kimseye tedarik sağlamayı reddetmedik. Onlar vazgeçti ve halen Rusya'dan almaya devam ettikleri ürünlerden de vazgeçme planları yapıyorlar." dedi.

Avrupa'nın Rus enerji kaynaklarına yönelik kısıtlamalarına rağmen tedarikin sürdüğüne işaret eden Putin, "Rus sıvılaştırılmış doğal gazı (LNG) Avrupa pazarının yüzde 20'sini oluşturuyor. Bu, pazarın önemli bir bölümü." diye konuştu.

Putin, Avrupa ülkelerinin Rusya'dan gelen her şeyi yasaklama politikasını eleştirerek, "Hayat bunun işe yaramadığını gösterdi. Rusya'dan gelen her şeyi yasaklama politikası aptalca, tamamen aptalca." değerlendirmesinde bulundu.

Devlet Başkanı Putin, bazı yabancı şirketlerin de Avrupa hükümetlerinin baskısıyla Rusya pazarından ayrılmak zorunda kaldıklarını sözlerine ekledi.

Proteste in Deutschland: Union und SPD warnen vor möglichen Ausschreitungen

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Brennende Mülltonnen bei Schülerprotest im französischen Lille (am 1. Oktober)

Frankreich, Spanien, Italien, Portugal, Griechenland, Belgien, Albanien: In zahlreichen EU-Ländern geht die Jugend auf die Straße. Die Gründe sind unterschiedlich, doch vielerorts eskalierte die Lage (mehr dazu hier). Nun warnen Innenpolitiker von Union und SPD vor Ausschreitungen in Deutschland – und einer möglichen Unterwanderung durch extremistische Kräfte.

»Wir müssen die Gefahr sehr ernst nehmen, dass die angekündigten friedlichen Jugendproteste auch in Deutschland durch linksextreme Kräfte, Teile des migrantischen Milieus und Kriminelle vereinnahmt werden können«, sagte der innenpolitische Sprecher der Unionsfraktion, Alexander Throm, der »Rheinischen Post«. Entsprechende Aufrufe in den sozialen Medien, wenngleich derzeit in der Minderheit, seien »besorgniserregend«.

Die Jugend müsse friedlich für ihre Anliegen wie bessere Bildung und Generationengerechtigkeit demonstrieren können, sagte der CDU-Politiker. »Aber Gewalt, Angriffe auf Polizisten oder öffentliche Einrichtungen überschreiten eine Grenze und sind nicht zu tolerieren.«

Der innenpolitische Sprecher der SPD-Fraktion, Sebastian Fiedler, sagte derselben Zeitung: »Für gewalttätige Proteste gibt es zu keinem Zeitpunkt und an keinem Ort irgendeine Legitimation.« Er habe den Eindruck, »dass unsere Sicherheitsbehörden sich frühzeitig und außerordentlich professionell auf mögliche Proteste einstellen und die Lage analysieren«. Dazu gehöre, dass man sich sehr genau anschauen müsse, wer diejenigen seien, die »im digitalen Raum die Lage anheizen«, sagte der frühere Vorsitzende des Bundes deutscher Kriminalbeamter weiter.

Im Internet ruft eine Initiative, die sich »GenZ regelt« beziehungsweise »GenZ steht auf« nennt, für den 31. Oktober zu Protesten in 23 Städten auf. Sie setzt sich unter anderem für mehr Klimaschutz ein, für Maßnahmen gegen steigende Lebenshaltungskosten und gegen die Wehrpflicht. Dabei lehnt sie Gewalt ausdrücklich ab.

Die Bundesschülerkonferenz hatte sich am Wochenende von den Protesten am 31. Oktober distanziert. »Wir sehen aufgrund undurchsichtiger Organisationsstrukturen die Gefahr, dass die ernst zu nehmenden Anliegen junger Menschen zum Spielball politischer Instrumentalisierung und Polarisierung werden können«, hieß es einer Erklärung. »Das wird diesen Sorgen und Forderungen nach einer guten Bildung und sicheren Zukunft nicht gerecht.«

Politiker von Grünen und Linken warnten davor, junge Menschen unter Generalverdacht zu stellen und deren Anliegen zu diskreditieren. »Junge Menschen haben ein Recht darauf, gehört zu werden und friedlich für ihre Zukunft auf die Straße zu gehen«, sagte Grünen-Fraktionschefin Britta Haßelmann der »Rheinischen Post«.

Grünen-Parteichef Felix Banaszak sagte in der ZDF-Sendung »Berlin direkt«, bevor man über mögliche Gewalt spekuliere, sollte man ernst nehmen, was die Menschen in ihrem Aufruf zu den Demonstrationen schreiben. Gewalt sei inakzeptabel, sagte er. Und kritisierte zugleich: »Der Generationenvertrag geht nicht mehr auf.« Schulden würden nicht für Zukunftsinvestitionen gemacht. Die Sozialversicherungssysteme funktionierten nicht mehr, Mieten seien unbezahlbar geworden. Das einzige, womit der Staat auf die jungen Menschen zukomme, seien die Briefe an 18-Jährige für den neuen Wehrdienst.

Die Linke-Innenexpertin Clara Bünger sagte der »Rheinischen Post«, konkrete Gefahren bei den Protesten müssten »selbstverständlich ernst genommen werden«. Sie betonte zugleich: »Aber wer vor extremistischer Unterwanderung oder ausländischer Einflussnahme warnt, sollte dafür belastbare Anhaltspunkte vorlegen.« Statt junge Menschen zu kriminalisieren, solle die Bundesregierung ihre Anliegen ernst nehmen.

Mourinho keeps Clasico midas touch 13 years on as La Liga heats up

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Real Madrid have now won five consecutive games in all competitions

The Real Madrid manager celebrated Kylian Mbappe's late winner at Barcelona on Sunday with a trademark knee slide, as he maintained his midas touch over their bitter rivals.

Mourinho has become just the third Real boss to go six El Clasicos in a row without defeat - 13 years after he last led Los Blancos in this fixture.

Four wins and two draws in his past six matches in charge of Real Madrid against Barcelona put him up there with Miguel Munoz (1962 to 1965) and Vicente del Bosque (2001 to 2003) as Real managers who have gone six in a row without defeat against the old enemy.

It was also just a second victory in eight games against Barcelona for Real, with the return of Mourinho to the fixture haunting the reigning champions

And more importantly for Mourinho, Mbappe's double in Real's 2-1 win ended Barcelona's nine-game unbeaten domestic run with a crucial Clasico victory at the Camp Nou.

A Real defeat would have allowed Barcelona to establish a commanding nine-point advantage but the result has transformed the top of La Liga, bringing Mourinho's side to within three points of their rivals.

"In my view, Real Madrid do not play to win at the Camp Nou, Real Madrid play to win La Liga," he said.

"We could win with luck, but we won with calmness and with all merit," he said. "It's a very strong group. Everyone works very hard. Everyone was ready to win.

"The credit goes to the players. When you win, the credit goes to the players. But when you lose, the blame falls on you. And that's why we see coaches being sacked from their positions every week."

The Portuguese, who returned to Real 13 years after his first stint in charge this summer, has now won six and lost six El Clasicos.

He said: "I've learned that I have an extraordinary group, with the quality and mindset to play.

"In midfield, having a mix of talents gives us more than just having two players with similar characteristics. This mix of talents gives us balance when defending and quality when we go on the attack.

Repeated torrential rain and storms could flood more roads, small streams, narrow canyons and burn scars, according to CBS News meteorologist Andrew Kozak. The rain will expand across the central and northern Plains Sunday night before moving toward the Upper Midwest Monday and Tuesday.

The Colorado Department of Transportation said water flow levels in the San Juan River increased significantly overnight, and the San Juan River Bridge remains closed Sunday. Video shared with CBS Colorado shows a house in Pagosa Springs inundated with floodwaters. All that can be seen is a small piece of the roof and the chimney.

"I'm sorry, buddy. I tried. I really tried. But this one was bigger than the last one," Pagosa Springs resident Chris Mott said in another video of water rushing through a neighborhood. "It moved sandbags and everything."

About 4 feet of water flooded the home of San Juan County, New Mexico, Sheriff Shane Ferrari. He said the area's situation was stable Sunday, but he and other residents were trying to clean up and deal with damage.

San Juan County spokesperson Devin Neeley said the county did not have official numbers on damage to homes or the number of people evacuated, but he was not aware of any loss of life.

Neeley said the floodwaters were receding but left mud and silt, much of which has been contaminated with sewage because of failures at waste plants. There were still power outages affecting multiple areas, and it would likely be a few days until residents got their power back on.

"It's a pretty active disaster zone," he said. "We're not on the downswing by any means."

A tornado touched down in Santa Fe, New Mexico, around 7 p.m. Saturday, and forecasters were trying to confirm whether twisters also struck elsewhere in the state, said meteorologist Scott Overpeck with the weather service in Albuquerque. He did not have damage estimates.

Michelle Vialpando spotted a tornado from her patio in the Aldea de Santa Fe neighborhood, facing north towards Los Alamos. She had just gotten home from work and stepped outside to look at what she called "crazy" storm clouds when she noticed the twister.

In the Farmington area of New Mexico, local officials said swollen rivers were receding but that many people remained evacuated and hundreds were without power.

The flooding is just the latest from two months of on-and-off heavy rain caused by tropical moisture coming ashore from the Pacific. After months of drought, other recent deadly flooding struck the Grand Canyon and areas of New Mexico.

Forecasters said El Niño, the natural and temporary warming of parts of the Pacific, was likely to blame for the latest storm's unusually heavy rainfall.

"It's been more or less three or four days," said Anthony Brown, lead meteorologist at the weather service office in Santa Teresa, New Mexico. "It's pretty unusual."

See the early universe like NEVER before: Incredible simulation reveals exactly what happened in the first 2 billion years of our history

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

By WILIAM HUNTER, SENIOR SCIENCE & TECHNOLOGY REPORTER

Published: 00:01 GMT, 26 October 2026 | Updated: 00:01 GMT, 26 October 2026

An incredible simulation has revealed the earliest moments of our universe as they have never been seen before.

This groundbreaking model, dubbed 'Lumina', captures exactly what happened in the first two billion years of our history following the Big Bang.

Lumina traces the bafflingly complex interactions between matter, dark matter and radiation, showing how the very first galaxies formed and shaped the universe around them.

Now, stunning images and videos have revealed what that would have looked like at a level of detail never before possible.

Renderings depicting the earliest moments of the universe capture how fine filaments of dark matter wove together with fast-moving gas to sculpt the first structures in the cosmos.

Scientists can even use Lumina to chart the processes by which gases like helium transformed into the states we witness today.

Dr Aaron Smith, a physicist from the University of Texas at Dallas and co-author of a paper on Lumina, says: 'We feel incredibly fortunate to be building simulations like Lumina.

'For the first time, we can follow along as both hydrogen and helium changed into their modern form across such an enormous volume of the universe, while still resolving the galaxies and other luminous objects driving that transformation.

An incredible simulation has revealed the earliest moments of our universe as they have never been seen before. This image shows a rendering of the universe about 650 million light-years across, with fine filaments representing dark matter while orange and yellow tones show the areas of fast-moving gases

This is the first simulation to show the process of helium reionisation, shown here as the gas transforms into its modern form in the universe (cyan regions)

Created by scientists at the Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics, Lumina is what scientists call a 'cosmological radiation-hydrodynamics simulation'.

That means it takes all of the best theories we have about how the universe's basic building blocks interact, and predicts how they would play out over time.

What makes Lumina special, however, is that it is the largest simulation of this kind ever created.

Lumina models a cube of space spanning some 1.5 billion light-years on each side, roughly equivalent to the scale astronomers can observe with today's most powerful telescopes.

That is also a 100 times greater volume than had been simulated by the previous best simulations of a similar kind.

Importantly, this is also the first simulation that allows scientists to study a phenomenon called helium reionisation.

The early universe was a hot soup of particles and radiation left over from the Big Bang.

As it cooled, electrons combined with atomic nuclei to make atoms that were electrically neutral.

The simulation is the largest of its kind ever created, modelling a cube of space spanning some 1.5 billion light-years on each side. Pictured: The cosmic web as modelled by Lumina, showing where hydrogen has transformed into its current form in the modern universe (gold areas)

Simulations like these allow scientists to check whether our theories about the universe match the observations now being gathered by space telescopes

However, after hundreds of millions of years, the first stars and galaxies began to form, producing enormous volumes of ultraviolet light that was strong enough to knock electrons off atoms and produce charged helium and hydrogen ions.

As radiation from stars and the black holes growing in galactic centres spread through space, regions of ionised helium grew and merged like bubbles clumping together.

Using Lumina, scientists can finally study this process as it happens and immediately see how it influences the evolving universe within the model.

Creating Lumina required more than 15,000 graphics processing units running on Frontier, one of the world’s fastest supercomputers, located at Oak Ridge National Laboratory in Tennessee.

That vast amount of computing power was required in order to model such a massive volume of the universe while maintaining a level of detail needed to study the formation of stars, galaxies and black holes.

This detail is critical because models like Lumina are now a core part of how scientists test their theories about the universe.

The simulation shows how the universe's basic ingredients of matter, dark matter, and radiation combine to create stars, galaxies, black holes, and even the vast structure of the cosmic web (pictured)

Lead author Dr Oliver Zier, of the Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics, says: 'With the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), the early universe has become an exciting new frontier in astrophysics.

'To understand what we are seeing with this telescope, we need to translate our theories into detailed predictions.'

Models allow scientists to see what the universe should look like according to our current best theories.

If observations from the JWST or the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope match what the simulation predicts, it gives scientists confidence that they are on the right track.

Likewise, if the observations don't match the model, it could be a sign that scientists need to rethink some of their assumptions about the universe.

The Big Bang Theory is a cosmological model, a theory used to describe the beginning and the evolution of our universe.

It says that the universe was in a very hot and dense state before it started to expand 13,7 billion years ago.

This theory is based on fundamental observations.

In 1920, Hubble observed that the distance between galaxies was increasing everywhere in the universe.

The Big Bang Theory is a cosmological model, a theory used to describe the beginning and the evolution of our universe, based on observations - including the cosmic background radiation (pictured), which is a like a fossil of radiation emitted during the beginning of the universe, when it was hot and dense

This means that galaxies had to be closer to each other in the past.

In 1964, Wilson and Penzias discovered the cosmic background radiation, which is a like a fossil of radiation emitted during the beginning of the universe, when it was hot and dense.

The cosmic background radiation is observable everywhere in the universe.

The composition of the universe - that is, the the number of atoms of different elements -  is consistent with the Big Bang Theory.

So far, this theory is the only one that can explain why we observe an abundance of primordial elements in the universe.
