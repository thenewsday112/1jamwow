Russia’s Krasnodar Region Declares High Alert as Landfills Reach Capacity

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Authorities in Russia’s southern Krasnodar region have declared a high alert over nearly full landfills, years after a Kremlin-backed waste management overhaul was supposed to deliver new processing facilities.

Governor Veniamin Kondratyev introduced the measures on Oct. 22 in 10 districts, including the Black Sea resort city of Sochi and the port of Novorossiysk, the regional emergency task force said. It cited the “exhaustion of capacity at waste management facilities.”

The governor’s decree warned of a potential regional emergency, with disruptions to garbage collection threatening to leave waste piling up in towns and villages. It also warned of illegal dumping and the spread of infectious diseases carried by rodents and insects, the local news outlet Gorod Krasnodar reported.

The decree was published five days after Kondratyev signed it.

The regional task force said some landfills had begun operating as temporary waste storage sites, while others were undergoing reconstruction expected to take a “lengthy period of time.”

Authorities said the high alert would remain in place until landfill reconstruction is completed, the necessary permits are secured and new municipal waste processing facilities are built. Ten such facilities are currently under construction, according to the task force.

Officials pledged that garbage would continue to be collected regularly along existing routes.

Kondratyev put his deputy, Yevgeny Pergun, in charge of addressing the threat. Pergun is also overseeing efforts to resolve the region’s fuel crisis and contain gasoline prices.

He was instructed to draw up a plan for building and upgrading waste treatment and disposal facilities and using existing sites. Kondratyev also urged regional waste operators to keep their landfills running until the threat of an emergency has passed.

The Krasnodar region, which consistently ranks among Russia’s 10 largest regional economies, was supposed to receive at least eight municipal waste processing complexes under the nationwide waste management reform launched by President Vladimir Putin in 2019.

Regional prosecutors had already flagged serious problems in 2024, Gorod Krasnodar reported. Nine of the region’s 15 municipal waste disposal facilities were full, while coastal districts were left without legally operating landfills, prosecutors said.

They also found that violations in the selection of construction sites made some planned facilities unusable.

None of the nine facilities envisaged under the reform, including eight waste processing complexes, had opened by 2026. Authorities have since pushed back construction deadlines to 2027–2028.

The region has allocated 36.8 billion rubles ($456.3 million) to its waste management program through 2030, according to the Kommersant newspaper.

European nations will not be invited to the next Russia-US-Ukraine peace talks, Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said on Wednesday, a day after the leaders of Finland and Norway called Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Moscow agreed in principle to participate in a fourth round of trilateral talks following last week’s meeting between Putin and US special envoy Steve Witkoff in St. Petersburg. Mid-November has been floated as a possible timeframe, with Abu Dhabi suggested as the venue.

“We have agreed that trilateral talks will resume, involving representatives from Russia, Ukraine, and the US,” Ushakov told Vesti. He stressed, however, that European representatives will not be invited.

“They can get there if they buy tickets. But no one intends to invite them to the negotiating table.”

Ushakov’s comments came a day after Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store called Putin for a conversation that reportedly lasted over an hour. The call sent a shockwave through Kiev’s staunchest backers, prompting Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen to condemn the Nordic leaders’ initiative, insisting it had not been coordinated with “European partners.”

However, neither Stubb nor Store brought any new proposals for settling the Ukraine conflict, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told reporters on Tuesday when asked about the call.

Ukraine’s European backers have been excluded from all three rounds of trilateral negotiations so far and have increasingly sought direct participation in the peace process. They have, however, not tempered their demands, insisting on an unconditional ceasefire – something Moscow has repeatedly rejected, arguing that a temporary freeze to the fighting would only allow Kiev to rest and rearm with Western weapons.

Russia has also accused the EU of acting as the principal side arming Ukraine and prolonging the conflict. In April, Brussels approved €90 billion ($100 billion) to Ukraine for 2026–2027, financed via capital markets. Earlier this month, Vladimir Zelensky asked for a further €70 billion to keep Ukraine’s budget and military afloat through next year.

Moscow has maintained that Europe’s financial and military support for Ukraine makes it a party to the conflict, which Russia has long described as a NATO-led proxy war.

Putin has also described Europe’s claims of a Russian threat to justify its military buildup as “political schizophrenia.”

The Ontario government is slashing the tax rate for gambling operators in Niagara as it issues a call for new investors, part of Premier Doug Ford’s plan to attract big-name casinos to the region.

The government, along with the Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corp., which manages gambling in the province, issued a “call for investment” on Wednesday, with the goal of raising $3-billion in direct investment from the sector, as well as an additional $600-million from the private sector over the next 20 years.

The announcement is part of Mr. Ford’s multibillion-dollar Destination Niagara strategy, a plan to boost the region’s profile as a prime tourist attraction, which the government says will lead to billions of dollars in growth for the provincial economy, as well as tens of thousands of jobs.

But it has also raised questions about the government’s priorities and added to more general concerns around the proliferation of online gambling, as well as the need for community consultation.

Ontario is asking “world-class gaming operators” to express interest in the region, which includes Niagara Falls and borders New York State, by Dec. 3. The government said it will then shortlist proponents who will be able to enter a competitive process to submit detailed proposals for new casino developments.

As part of the pitch, the province said it will introduce a standardized gambling tax rate of 9.75 per cent for Niagara operators, which is considered highly competitive with other jurisdictions in North America. The current tax rates, which vary by individual casinos, are not public, but two industry sources put them upward of 40 per cent. The Globe and Mail is not naming the sources as they are not authorized to speak publicly about internal matters.

In addition, each new casino operator will have to pay a $100-million entrance fee. The fee is intended to offset provincial revenue losses after the government ended MGE Niagara Entertainment Inc.’s exclusivity rights to operate casinos in the area earlier this summer.

MGE is a division of Mohegan Tribal Gaming Authority, a company based in Uncasville, Conn., that operates two Ontario properties, Casino Niagara and the Fallsview Casino. In the nine months ending June 30, the two casinos reported revenues of US$199-million.

Mohegan has operated the two Niagara Falls properties since 2019 under a 21-year contract with OLG.

Russia Issues Permits for First Grain Vessels to Leave Sea of Azov Since July

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Russian authorities have issued permits for vessels carrying grain for export to leave the Sea of Azov for the first time since July 10, when they closed maritime traffic in the area due to Ukrainian drone attacks, seven industry sources told Reuters.

The sources said permits to pass through the Kerch Strait, which links the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea, would be issued for a period through Nov. 15. Two of the sources said the vessels would be accompanied through the Black Sea by a Russian naval convoy.

"The Harbor Master has instructed to start outward clearance procedures for vessels that are fully ready," one industry source said.

The issuance of permits suggests that Russian officials consider the passage through the Kerch Strait into the Black Sea is currently relatively safe.

The development coincided with the start of a localized ceasefire to allow safe passage for humanitarian supplies to the Russian-controlled town of Oleshky in the Kherson region.

U.S. first lady Melania Trump, who mediated the ceasefire, said it had expanded into the whole of the Kherson region, an area of fierce fighting, which has access to the Sea of Azov. Both Russia and Ukraine said that the ceasefire is holding.

Russia and Ukraine have been attacking each other's ports and vessels in the Black Sea area since the new grain marketing season started in July, bringing the once booming Black Sea agriculture trade to a standstill.

Turkey, an importer of grain from Russia, led the effort to introduce a ceasefire and resume shipments in the Black Sea, but Russian officials have said they are not interested in a ceasefire deal that does not cover targets in other sectors.

University Health Network gets $35-million donation for transplant institute

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The exterior of Toronto General Hospital. The $35-million gift will lead to the development of the Ajmera Transplant Institute, which UHN said will be a global hub for organ transplants, research and education.Tijana Martin/The Globe and Mail

The University Health Network says a landmark $35-million donation from a prominent Toronto family will help advance organ transplantation in Canada through the creation of a specialized institute.

With this latest donation, the Ajmera family – which founded FGF Brands, one of North America’s largest industrial baking and food-processing companies – has cumulatively given $62-million to UHN. The total amount is the largest known donation in Canada to support transplant care by a single family.

The gift will lead to the development of the Ajmera Transplant Institute, which UHN said will be a global hub for organ transplants, research and education.

Sam Ajmera, who was born in India and co-founded FGF Brands, said in a statement that he wanted to build a better life for his family when he immigrated to Canada at age 26, and now he wants to continue to give back to the country that allowed him to do just that.

“My hope is that the Ajmera Transplant Institute will turn the discoveries of today into the treatments that save lives tomorrow – improving the lives of patients and families for generations to come,” Mr. Ajmera said.

University Health Network CEO Kevin Smith has a plan to fix hospitals – even with a dangerous shortage of money

The institute will build off the work already done at the Ajmera Transplant Centre, the country’s largest and most comprehensive transplant program. Located at Toronto General Hospital within the UHN, it is also named after the Ajmera family in recognition of a previous donation.

The team at the centre have been at the forefront of organ donation. Their successes include creating the world’s first centre for living organ donation, performing a rare five-organ transplant and using living-donor liver transplantation to treat people with otherwise incurable metastatic tumours.

Deepali Kumar, medical director of the Ajmera Transplant Centre, said in an interview that there are not enough organs available for everyone who needs one.

Each year, more than 250 Canadians die waiting for a life-saving organ, according to Canadian Blood Services. Hundreds more face rejection or other complications related to transplantation.

The new institute, Dr. Kumar said, will help the team identify new ways to either generate organs or make better use of the organs available.

“The institute will be all things transplant, so it will include the clinical care, including all the organs we transplant. It will include research. It will include education. We will be able to recruit the best and brightest people from around the planet.”

She said investments will be made in artificial intelligence to improve organ transplantation, and in ex vivo technologies, which manipulate living cells, tissues or organs outside of a body.

A section of the institute will focus on diabetes, specifically pancreas and islet transplantation, which are therapies to restore insulin production, she added.

“The gift marks a defining moment for UHN and for the future of transplantation,” Dr. Kumar said in a subsequent statement. “With it, we will have the opportunity to reimagine what is possible for those facing organ failure, reduce wait times and improve outcomes, and advance the next generation of transplant therapies.”

The UHN said in a statement that challenges in transplant care require a new collaborative model between experts in clinical care, engineering, immunology, regenerative medicine, cell therapy and data science. The Ajmera Transplant Institute, it said, will meet that need.
