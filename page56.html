It's hard to believe it's been 15 years since fairy tale fantasy drama Once Upon A Time first hit screens back in October 2011.

Spanning across seven seasons, the ABC series centered around two alternate worlds, featuring characters who lived in the real-world town of Storybrooke, Maine and their fairy tale counterparts who resided in the Enchanted Forest.

The main premise saw The Evil Queen aka Mayor Regina Mills (Lara Parrilla) cast a dark curse which results in the fairy tale characters losing their memories of who they really are and becoming trapped as their alter-egos in Storybrooke.

Their saviour comes in the form of bail bonds collector Emma Swan (Jennifer Morrison), who is actually the long-lost daughter of Snow White (Ginnifer Goodwin) and Prince Charming (Josh Dallas), who was sent away as a baby before the curse took hold so that she could one day return and break it.

Blending a mix of fantasy, drama, adventure and romance, the age-old conflict between good and evil and a huge helping of nostalgia thanks to the plethora of classic Disney, fairy tale and folklore characters; Once Upon A Time was a major hit when it first premiered, attracting a whopping 12.93 million viewers for its pilot episode.

A nurse was killed and a health worker severely injured after being attacked while responding to an Ebola alert in Congo, according to the World Health Organization.

It was the latest in a series of attacks on health workers and responders that authorities say is hampering efforts to slow the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak in the world.

The attack took place Saturday in the town of Bafwasende in the country's eastern Tshopo province, WHO Director General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said in a post on X on Sunday.

“Everyone working to protect communities from this deadly disease must be able to work in dignity and safety. Healthcare must always be protected,” he said.

WHO regional director for Africa, Dr. Mohamed Yakub Janabi, also condemned the attack on X. “Misinformation and fear must never fuel violence,” he said.

Local authorities have not yet commented on the attack or the suspects.

The country’s health ministry’s most recent data indicates more than 9,800 cases including more than 4,700 deaths have been recorded since the start of the outbreak in May, making it the deadliest Ebola outbreak in Congo’s history.

Last month, a Congolese politician was beaten to death in North Kivu after publicly defending the government’s response to Ebola. He had been calling on the public to obey response measures, particularly social distancing and handwashing, on a radio show.

It came after authorities launched a village-centered approach to fight the outbreak which faced resistance from some residents who dismiss Ebola as a hoax. There is widespread distrust of the country’s government because of how it has managed past Ebola outbreaks as well as chronic corruption.

Responders say skepticism about the disease among communities has complicated efforts to work with residents, including in remote areas and emerging hot spots that already face shortages of medical supplies and healthcare workers.

Back in May at the start of the outbreak, two tents at health centers were set alight in two separate attacks in just one week by angry residents.

Those on the front line of the response to the outbreak have previously said some members of various response teams have been held captive in some health zones, and some teams responsible for safe and dignified burials have been threatened.

The Bayeux Tapestry attracted a bigger rush of spending than Hollywood blockbuster opening mornings or a typical Saturday night out, as the 1,000-year-old embroidery draws in younger culture enthusiasts, data shows.

Monzo’s customer data showed a surge in spending around the second tranche of public tickets to the British Museum being released.

Thousands joined a queue online to secure tickets when they went on sale at 10am last Wednesday, leading fans to draw comparisons to major ticketed events like Glastonbury Festival and the Oasis reunion tour.

The launch triggered payments to the museum of 48 times that of a normal Wednesday morning, according to Monzo’s spending data across its 16 million customers.

In the first hour, cardholders spent £14,100 on tickets to the exhibition – double the opening mornings for this year’s blockbuster films The Odyssey, at £7,000, and Spider-Man: Brand New Day, at £6,300.

Buyers spent five times more on average at the checkout than Spider-Man’s opening morning, at £97 versus £19.

In the two hours after tickets went on sale, Monzo customers spent £30,500 on tickets.

This was 60% more than the combined average Saturday night spend, between 8pm and 4am, across six major UK nightlife venues – Koko, Fabric, Roundhouse, Lakota, Thekla and Sub Club.

The exhibition at the London museum has been met with a huge surge of interest from people wanting to see the woven record of the Norman Conquest, which returned to the UK for the first time in almost 1,000 years.

The British Museum said the response to the Tapestry going on display last month had been “extraordinary”, leading it to make changes to address lengthy queues among ticket-holders, including banning visitors from taking photos.

Monzo’s data suggested that the buzz has been felt among younger as well as older generations, drawing in crowds of Generation Z and millennial viewers.

Some 55% of buyers at the latest sale were under the age of 35, it found.

Furthermore, almost half of all buyers were outside of London, with the furthest purchases coming hundreds of miles away from the Glasgow postcode area, the bank said.

The number of new homes started in the third quarter of this year fell by 13% compared with a year earlier, according to figures from a warranty and insurance provider.

The National House Building Council (NHBC) said 26,231 new homes were registered in the third quarter of the year, down by 13% compared with 30,279 in the third quarter of 2025.

With affordability pressures continuing to weigh on demand and supply-side barriers constraining delivery, private sector registrations fell by 8% to reach 18,359, compared with 20,054 a year earlier, the NHBC said.

Registrations in the rental and affordable sector fell by 23% to reach 7,872, down from 10,225 in the third quarter of 2025.

The NHBC has a 70%-plus share of the UK warranty and insurance market for new build homes, and its figures indicate the stock of new properties in the pipeline as homes are registered with it before being built.

The Government recently unveiled the Your First Home initiative, which will give first-time buyers the chance to buy a new build property with a 2.5% deposit, with the Government stepping in to lend them an extra 20% towards the cost.

The full details will be outlined in the Budget, including a household income cap and local property price caps, with the aim of ensuring the new support is targeted at those who need it.

Demand for tribute acts has grown as fans priced out of seeing big artists perform live are opting for cheaper alternatives, venue operators have said.

Some venues say they are putting on more events to cater to a growing cohort of people who want a more affordable night out.

Dan Ickowitz-Seidler, the director of Propaganda Venues, which includes XOYO and Barfly in London, Tramshed in Cardiff and The Warehouse in Leeds, said the group is booking more tribute shows in response to a “real increase” in demand.

This includes acts like Arctic Numpties, Oas-Is, Fleetwood Bac and Daft Funk, as well a tribute to Pitbull following the popularity of the singer’s live tour.

Live performances also include bands performing renditions of popular musicians’ back catalogues.

Mr Ickowitz-Seidler told the Press Association: “People want an affordable night out where they can see a quality band, hear songs they love and have a great evening with friends.

“As gig tickets become more expensive, tribute shows allow people to see live music regularly, with tickets typically costing £20–£30.”

Around a fifth of UK adults have attended a tribute act show in the past 12 months, a survey by the Night Time Industries Association (NTIA) found.

Some 30% of respondents said they think they will attend more events in the next year than they did in the previous year.

Among those who said so, the main reason given was that it is a cheaper alternative to seeing the original artist, while more than two-thirds agreed that seeing major acts live has become too expensive for them.

The soaring prices of tickets to attend live concerts has come under scrutiny following world tours for big artists like Harry Styles, Taylor Swift and Beyonce in recent years.

Ticket marketplace Ticketmaster made new commitments last year to ensure fans are told the range of prices for concert tickets when they join a queue, and informed when cheaper seats sell out.

The move followed the UK competition watchdog’s investigation into the way Ticketmaster sold Oasis tickets in 2024.

Meanwhile, live music operators are finding new ways to attract visitors amid a shift in consumers’ going-out habits since the Covid pandemic and an increase in the cost of keeping venues open.

Recent data by NTIA, produced with market analysts NielsenIQ, found nearly 29% of Britain’s late-night economy has disappeared since March 2020, led by a drop of more than a third for nightclubs.

Mr Ickowitz-Seidler said venue operators often find consumers spend more at the bar when they are attending tribute shows.

“People tend to be more relaxed about going to the bar and missing part of a song than they might be when watching the original artist or discovering an emerging band,” he told PA.

“That makes a real difference because people buying drinks in independent venues are crucial to keeping the doors open.”

Repeated antisocial behaviour offenders may be restricted from town centres under new, tougher police powers being brought into force.

The Home Office said the new Respect Orders filled a gap in existing anti-social behaviour powers, and would give police and councils the ability to take stronger enforcement action.

As well as police and local councils, housing associations and transport networks will also be able to apply for a Respect Order under the new rules.

The Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 classifies antisocial behaviour as “behaviour by a person which causes, or is likely to cause, harassment, alarm or distress to persons not of the same household as the person”.

The Metropolitan Police distinguish between 15 types of antisocial behaviour, including disruptive unlicensed drinking in public spaces, littering and “general nuisance behaviour”.

Frequent offenders could be hit with curfew bans, or be ordered to cease drinking in public centres, including high streets and local parks.

From Monday, breaching a Respect Order will be a criminal offence, punishable by up to two years’ jail, the Home Office said.

The orders will only apply to adults, but under-18s can still receive an existing Youth Injunction which includes both prohibitions and requirements.

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said: “Unchecked, anti-social behaviour blights our communities. But it is not inevitable and I won’t accept it.

Belgrade - The dominant force in Serbian politics for over 14 years, Aleksandar Vucic, who conceded defeat in a snap parliamentary poll on Oct 25, rose from the wreckage of strongman Slobodan Milosevic’s cabinet to the centre of power using his brand of populist nationalism.

For supporters, the 56-year-old represented stability, development and patriotism in a country scarred by the bloody wars of the 1990s and ensuing international isolation.

But opponents saw him as an increasingly authoritarian figure, twisting between the Kremlin and Brussels and leading a government accused of endemic corruption.

Vucic conceded his party had lost after voters cast ballots in record numbers in the vote on Oct 25, defeated by a new political force that emerged from a years-long protest movement.

“Based on a stratified sample, it is clear that we lost these elections,” Vucic told supporters at his party headquarters in Belgrade.

Vucic rose to prominence in the 1990s as an ultra-nationalist ally of Milosevic, serving as his information minister in 1998.

After protests ousted the Yugoslav strongman in 2000, Vucic became a senior figure in the far-right Serbian Radical Party.

He was then a vocal supporter of Bosnian Serb political and military leaders Radovan Karadzic and Ratko Mladic, both later sentenced to life for genocide and other crimes committed during the Bosnian war.

But Vucic underwent a dramatic rebrand in 2008, co-founding the conservative, pro-European Serbian Progressive Party (SNS), which won parliamentary elections in 2012 and has dominated Serbian politics since.

He served as deputy prime minister, defence minister and prime minister before becoming president in 2017.

Even after polishing his image, Vucic has been reluctant to distance himself from nationalist leanings and far-right associates.

He has stopped short of condemning Serbs convicted of atrocities during the 1990s conflicts and looked the other way as pro-government media and political allies openly praised war criminals.

Despite trying to deny state involvement in a large funeral gathering for Mladic in the Serbian capital in September, several of his ministers attended, drawing sharp criticism from Brussels.

A relentless campaigner, he maintained a near-constant presence on pro-government television channels, embracing locals in tiny rural villages and chastising critics during lengthy news conferences.

He has cultivated the image of a tireless leader criss-crossing Serbia, attracting investment and creating jobs.

At the same time, he has juggled the competing interests of European Union aspirations and Serbia’s close ties to Russia.

Supporters praised him as a brave defender of Serbian sovereignty, spending billions of euros on defence – as he warned of foreign influence and pressure from NATO-member neighbours.

He often cited Serbia’s infrastructure and improved living standards as proof of his success, once pledging to make the country a “land of cranes”.

From the gleaming waterfront district to the numerous projects unfolding in preparation for Expo 2027, his party’s slogan “United Serbia” is plastered on billboards across the capital.

But when a concrete canopy collapsed at a newly renovated train station in 2024 killing 16 people, the accusations of corruption which had dogged many of his signature projects caused an outpouring of anger.

Student-led demonstrations calling for transparent investigations drew hundreds of thousands to the streets in the largest protests since the Milosevic era.

As demands grew into calls for snap elections, Vucic and his supporters pushed back.

Students manning university blockades were labelled tools of foreign powers.

In September, nearly two years into the protests, Vucic finally called snap elections for October, citing “major turbulence on the domestic political scene”, thus meeting a key student demand.

Skończyła dwa uniwersytety w Wielkiej Brytanii, zna biegle cztery języki. W urzędzie pracy w Polsce usłyszała: mamy dla pani tylko pracę kelnerki. Wielu Polaków po studiach za granicą chce wrócić do Polski. I zderzają się z rynkiem pracy.

Zaczynam panikować — przyznaje 25-letnia Joanna z Lublina, absolwentka stosunków międzynarodowych i politologii na University of Portsmouth w Anglii. — Kasy wystarczy mi jeszcze na dwa miesiące życia w Polsce. A miało być tak pięknie.

Uczelnia, dzięki swojej stronie z ogłoszeniami, oferowała na początek pomoc w znalezieniu pracy w Wielkiej Brytanii. Związani z nią doradcy zawodowi zapewniali wsparcie w pisaniu CV, znalezieniu stażu i trening przed rozmowami kwalifikacyjnymi. — Po studiach zostałam na krótko w Anglii — mówi Joanna. — Dzięki stażowi poznałam wszystkie szczeble pracy w angielskim odpowiedniku polskiego NFZ. Etat w fundacji zajmującej się wsparciem dla osób starszych pozwolił mi wyćwiczyć się w przekładaniu skomplikowanego tekstu na prostą infografikę. Ale ciągnęło mnie do Polski — dodaje. — Myślałam: mam doskonały angielski, umiejętność researchu i weryfikowania informacji. Kto zrobi karierę w kraju jak nie ja?

Lądowanie w Lublinie — jak mówi — miała miękkie. Jedna przyjaciółka wynajęła jej mieszkanie za opłaty. Druga pomogła znaleźć pracę w biurze. Za najniższą krajową, bez szans na pracę zdalną i z jedną wolną niedzielą w miesiącu. Potraktowała to jako zajęcie na przeczekanie, do momentu znalezienia etatu z godną pensją.
