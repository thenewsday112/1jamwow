Jordan arrests three over Karak church attack, state TV says

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Nine people were killed and 40 others wounded on Oct 23 in an attack on a church in the village of Hamoud in the Karak governorate.

Jordanian security forces have arrested three armed suspects after a shootout on Oct 26 in connection with an attack on a church in the US-allied country that killed nine people, state television reported.

The Oct 23 attack during a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in the village of Hamoud in the Karak governorate, about 120km south-west of the capital Amman, also wounded 40 others. Witnesses said the shooting lasted several minutes.

Jordan’s Public Security Directorate said the suspects were located while hiding in an abandoned house in Karak.

They refused to surrender and attacked security forces, who returned fire and arrested all three after wounding them, the directorate said in a statement published on state media.

No security personnel were injured, and a number of firearms and ammunition were found in the suspects’ possession after their arrest.

“Terrorism has no place among us,” the directorate said, adding that the suspects had met their “just fate”.

King Abdullah, a US partner in the fight against Islamist militant groups, has ordered security forces to respond with an “iron fist” to the rare attack on Jordan’s Christian minority.

Such violence is highly unusual in Jordan, a relatively stable state in the Middle East, though the authorities say they have foiled several Islamist militant plots over the years.

The Public Security Directorate did not name the suspects or publicly identify possible affiliations with any Islamist militant groups.

Interior Minister Mazen al-Faraya said authorities were reviewing the circumstances that led young people towards extremism and would hold accountable anyone found to have participated in the attack.REUTERS

Singapore’s estimated geothermal resource potential more than 60 times annual electricity consumption: EMA

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A non-invasive geophysical survey found high-temperature geothermal resources several kilometres underground that may be suitable for electricity generation.

Visitors having a foot bath at the new Sembawang Hot Spring Park on Jan 4, 2020. (Photo: CNA/Jeremy Long)

SINGAPORE: Singapore's geothermal resource potential is estimated to be more than 60 times its annual electricity consumption, according to preliminary findings from a survey, the Energy Market Authority (EMA) said on Monday (Oct 26).

The non-invasive geophysical survey, conducted from 2024 to 2026, found high-temperature geothermal resources several kilometres underground that may be suitable for electricity generation. Singapore's current annual electricity consumption is around 60 terawatt-hours.

Further studies will be needed to validate the estimates of Singapore's geothermal technical potential and determine how much geothermal energy can be feasibly extracted.

Speaking at the Singapore International Energy Week 2026 on Monday, Minister for Energy, Trade and Industry (Energy and Industry) Tan See Leng described the findings as "a very good start", adding that if realised, geothermal energy could power Singapore for the next 60 or so years.

The nationwide survey was conducted by a consortium of leading global consultants, including Chevron, Metatek Group, and Surbana Jurong.

It used two non-invasive techniques to develop three-dimensional geological and heat resource models of Singapore.

Metatek Group undertook the data acquisition and processing, while Chevron used the processed data to develop a 3D geological and heat resource model of Singapore.

The survey assessed geothermal resources at depths of up to 10km for the purpose of power generation.

The consortium also assessed that there are lower-temperature geothermal resources at shallower depths, which could potentially support industrial heating and cooling using heat pumps and absorption chillers.

Following the preliminary findings, EMA said it would proceed with the next phase of studies to further assess the feasibility of harnessing geothermal energy for electricity generation, cooling and heating.

It has appointed a consortium formed by Surbana Jurong and GLJ Ltd to study the potential deployment of next-generation geothermal plants in Singapore.

The study will cover safety considerations, technical viability and the cost of geothermal energy production. It is scheduled to start by the end of this year and is estimated to take around 15 months.

Dr Tan noted that the process would not be straightforward, referring to how geothermal resources are located several kilometres underground.

The amount of electricity that can eventually be generated will depend on the cost and technical feasibility of extracting these resources, as well as the pace of advances in deep drilling technology, Dr Tan said.

"Our dense urban environment will also affect how many geothermal power plants we can eventually or feasibly deploy, and most importantly, we must keep safety as a top-of-mind consideration," he added.

EMA chief executive Puah Kok Keong said geothermal energy was an "indigenous source of clean energy that can provide constant power, and can potentially play an important role in our energy mix".

"If proven to be viable for electricity generation, Singapore’s geothermal energy resources will strengthen our energy resilience and support the decarbonisation of the power sector,” he added.

Dr Tan said that Singapore has not made a decision on whether to deploy geothermal power.

"It could take many years before we get to that decision. But nevertheless, as I've said many times before, no option is off the table for us here in Singapore. We will therefore explore this option seriously," he added.

The winner of the 2026 Ballon d’Or will be crowned at Monday’s gala in London, the first time it’s been held in Britain.

The race for the 2026 Ballon d’Or is heating up with less than 24 hours to go before the winner is announced at a glitzy ceremony in London.

A three-way contest between Lamine Yamal, Kylian Mbappe and Harry Kane leads the debate in the run-up to football’s most prestigious individual honour.

Yamal, Spain’s 19-year-old prodigy, and France’s 2018 World Cup winner Mbappe have both publicly endorsed themselves for the honour, while the unstoppable Kane of England has been relatively shy in campaigning for himself.

Predictions are pouring in thick and fast as we inch closer to the award ceremony at the London Palladium.

According to data released by Avisia, a French data analysis and artificial intelligence firm, Yamal is the favourite to win the 2026 Ballon d’Or.

The Barcelona winger is tipped to have a 23 percent probability of lifting the trophy, awarded to the best footballer of the 2025-26 season.

The data and AI consulting firm chose Kane as a close second, with a 21 per cent probability, while Mbappe ranked third at 19 percent, tied with Rodri, the 2024 Ballon d’Or winner.

The top five list is rounded out by Lionel Messi given an 18 percent chance to win for a record-extending ninth time.

Avisia claims it correctly identified the top five for the 2025 Ballon d’Or, won by Ousmane Dembele.

Even though Yamal is Avisia’s top pick, it is Kane who the bookmakers see as the outright favourite, with about 70 percent probability.

Atletico Madrid manager Diego Simeone thinks his compatriot Messi, who recently retired from international duty, deserves to lift the golden ball.

“After the World Cup, he played at his age… No doubt about it,” Simeone said.

Former Italy international Marco Materazzi echoed those thoughts.

“With all due respect to Yamal, I think Messi showed, at 38 years old, he’s in a different league,” he added.

Jude Bellingham, also shortlisted for the Ballon d’Or, backed England captain Kane and his attacking partner at the 2026 World Cup.

“He works so hard for it, and he’s a very honest and hard-working person,” Bellingham said. When asked if Kane could win, he added, “It’s definitely possible”.

Fellow England international Anthony Gordon sided with his Barcelona teammate Yamal.

“What Lamine has achieved at such a young age… the kind of player he is, is ridiculous. He’s on a whole other level,” said Gordon.

Mbappe, on the other hand, has the backing of his Real Madrid teammate Aurelien Tchouameni, who said the Frenchman had “an incredible season”.

Looking purely at individual statistics, Mbappe leads the race as La Liga’s top scorer with 25 goals and the Champions League’s leading marksman with 15.

Mbappe, furthermore, also won the World Cup’s Golden Boot and set the record for most World Cup goals (12).

Those staggering statistics make him a strong contender alongside Kane, who scored an incredible 73 goals last season – the most by any player since Messi netted 82 in 2011-12.

But Mbappe failed to win a trophy with either club or country, while Kane won the Bundesliga with Bayern but guided England only to a third-place finish. The lack of major titles weakens their case.

The youngster won the World Cup with Spain and La Liga with Barca, even though on the personal front, his statistics weren’t the greatest, with injury marring his campaign.

Recent history suggests that winning a title in one of the major tournaments has become almost a prerequisite.

Only four of the previous 19 Ballon d’Or winners failed to lift any of the Champions League, World Cup, European Championship or Copa America during the year they were honoured.

That bodes well for Yamal, who is bidding to become the youngest winner, hoping to surpass Brazilian Ronaldo, who was 21 when he won in 1997.

Bank of Japan's hawkish pause to leave scope for December hike

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

FILE PHOTO: Traffic signs in front of the Bank of Japan building in Tokyo, Japan, June 15, 2026. REUTERS/Kim Kyung-Hoon/File Photo

TOKYO, Oct 26 : The Bank of Japan will likely declare victory in a nearly 14-year battle to hit its 2 per cent inflation target at its policy meeting on Friday and emphasise the risks prices could overshoot, signaling the chance of a rate hike as soon as December.

Such hawkish language may reinforce expectations the BOJ, US Federal Reserve and European Central Bank will all raise rates by year-end, as brisk AI demand and the Iran war-induced energy shock worsen global inflationary pressures.

Still, the BOJ is likely to avoid dropping explicit hints on the timing and pace of future rate rises as it balances the need to forestall risks of an inflation overshoot without disrupting jittery markets with a premature hike, analysts say.

"With rises in prices and wages moderate, the BOJ will keep raising rates gradually without rushing," said a person familiar with its thinking, a view echoed by two more people.

At the two-day policy meeting ending on Friday, the BOJ is set to maintain its policy rate at 1.25 per cent by a majority vote, forgoing a back-to-back hike to spend more time scrutinising the effect of past rate hikes on lending conditions.

In a quarterly report due after the meeting, the BOJ is likely to slightly revise up next fiscal year's price forecast and signal that underlying inflation has roughly achieved its 2 per cent target, people familiar with the matter have told Reuters.

With wage gains becoming embedded and inflation expectations hovering around 2 per cent, the central bank will likely stress that it will now focus on anchoring inflation around its target by raising rates toward levels deemed neutral to the economy.

While Governor Kazuo Ueda has already signaled such a shift in policy focus upon raising rates in September, the pivot would mark another symbolic departure from the BOJ's prolonged efforts to fire up inflation through ultra-loose monetary policy.

"It's a historical moment for the BOJ, though it faces a more challenging task now that it needs to step not just on the accelerator but the brakes," said Naomi Muguruma, chief bond strategist at Mitsubishi UFJ Morgan Stanley Securities.

"The BOJ will probably scrutinise upcoming data and market moves in deciding whether to raise rates in December, or hold until January," she said.

Analysts polled by Reuters expect the BOJ to raise rates to 1.5 per cent in December and stick to a quarterly pace of rate hikes.

Under former governor Haruhiko Kuroda, the BOJ deployed a massive stimulus in 2013 to pull Japan out of deflation and fire up inflation to its 2 per cent inflation target.

Even after ending its mix of unorthodox easing steps in 2024, the central bank has trodden cautiously in raising rates on the view underlying inflation - or price moves excluding one-off factors and driven by domestic demand - remained below target.

With stubbornly high energy costs and rising import prices from a weak yen adding to inflationary pressures, the BOJ raised rates in September after delivering a hike in June - a shorter interval than the roughly twice-a-year hikes in 2024 and 2025.

The BOJ's policy rate, at 1.25 per cent, is now within the range of 1.1 per cent to 2.5 per cent its staff estimated as Japan's neutral rate, or the level that neither cools nor overheats the economy - drawing market attention to how far it could eventually hike rates.

With growth in bank lending still strong, the central bank is likely to stress on Friday that financial conditions remain loose - signalling scope for further rate hikes before its policy rate reaches levels close to neutral, the people said.

The BOJ's meeting follows that of the Fed, which is seen skipping a hike at its two-day meeting concluding on Wednesday. The ECB is also set to keep rates unchanged at 2.5 per cent on Thursday.

Reisen in die Boom-Region: Merz und Steinmeier gleichzeitig in Asien

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Kanzler Merz besucht ab heute Singapur, Japan und Südkorea - und dürfte versuchen, Partnerschaften auszubauen, um die Abhängigkeit von China zu reduzieren. Dort wiederum ist Bundespräsident Steinmeier zu Gast

"Außenkanzler" Friedrich Merz stört es nicht sonderlich, wenn er so bezeichnet wird - zumeist von Kritikern. Eher im Gegenteil. Ein deutscher Bundeskanzler müsse sich um die Außenpolitik kümmern, betont Merz immer wieder und schiebt nach: "Außenpolitik, Außenwirtschaftspolitik und Wirtschaftspolitik gehen Hand in Hand." Deshalb spreche er auf seinen Auslandsreisen immer auch über die strategischen wirtschaftlichen Interessen Deutschlands.

Das gilt für seine fünftägige Reise in den Indopazifikraum ganz besonders: Singapur, Japan und Südkorea - alle drei Länder seien "für uns zentral, um kritische, einseitige Abhängigkeiten abzubauen", sagte der Kanzler, als er die Asien-Reise Mitte Oktober in seiner Regierungserklärung im Bundestag ankündigte. "Das ist gegenwärtig eine Schlüsselaufgabe unserer interessengeleiteten Außenpolitik und unserer Wirtschaftspolitik", unterstrich der CDU-Chef.

Chinas Exportoffensive trifft Europas Industrie. Warum höhere Zölle allein nicht ausreichen. mehr

Abhängigkeiten abbauen: Gemeint ist damit vor allem die Volksrepublik China. Der Gigant, der wirtschaftlich, technologisch, geopolitisch und militärisch alles in der Indopazifik-Region dominiert - mit Ambitionen weit darüber hinaus. Umso interessanter, dass Bundespräsident Frank-Walter Steinmeier parallel zur Reise des Kanzlers zum Staatsbesuch nach China fährt. Aus Regierungskreisen heißt es, der Besuch des Bundespräsidenten werde mutmaßlich stark dadurch geprägt sein, dass er zu einem Moment stattfinde, "zu dem Deutschland seine Chinapolitik neu austariert". Dazu sei in den vergangenen Wochen einiges entschieden worden.

In Deutschland und Europa gibt es große Sorgen wegen unfairer Wettbewerbsbedingungen im Handel mit dem Riesenreich. Hohe staatliche staatlichen Subventionen Pekings, Überkapazitäten in der Produktion und aggressive Preise schaden der deutschen Wirtschaft massiv - ebenso wie die von China künstlich niedrig gehaltene Landeswährung.

"Wir sehen nicht länger tatenlos zu", kündigte Friedrich Merz in seiner Regierungserklärung an, ohne die Volksrepublik namentlich zu nennen. "Wer mit uns den Handelskonflikt sucht, der wird spüren, dass wir uns jetzt zur Wehr setzen - und zwar mit angemessenen Reaktionen", betonte der Kanzler und verwies auf neue Handelsschutzinstrumente, die die EU-Kommission plant.

China akzeptiert EU-Handelsbeschränkungen für Hybridfahrzeuge. Doch wie nachhaltig ist die Einigung? mehr

Aus dem Bundespräsidialamt heißt es, Steinmeier wolle bei seinem Besuch über die problematischen Wirtschaftsbeziehungen sprechen, über Fairness und wie sich Handelskonflikte vermeiden lassen. Ein Treffen mit Staats- und Parteichef Xi Jinping ist für Freitag geplant. Dann dürfte es auch um den Krieg in der Ukraine gehen. Der Bundespräsident wolle darum werben, dass China seinen Einfluss auf Russland nutzt, um die Angriffe zu beenden und ernsthafte Verhandlungen über ein Ende des Krieges zu ermöglichen.

Der Ukraine-Krieg wird auch am Rande der Kanzler-Reise eine Rolle spielen, wenn Merz politische Gespräche mit den jeweiligen Regierungschefs von Singapur, Japan und Südkorea führt. Bei den diversen Terminen des Kanzlers geht es aber vor allem darum, die Partnerschaft mit diesen drei wichtigen Industrieländern auszubauen. "De-risking", Steigerung der Wettbewerbsfähigkeit und Innovation - darum solle es unter anderem gehen, heißt es in Regierungskreisen.

Die Bundesregierung wirbt auf einem Gipfel um internationale Geldgeber - und das ist auch dringend notwendig. mehr

"Wenn der Kanzler jetzt nach Singapur, Japan und Südkorea fährt, dann trifft er in das Herz unserer Ambitionen, mit diesen Industriepartnern mehr zu tun", sagt Wolfgang Niedermark, Mitglied der Hauptgeschäftsführung des Bundesverbandes der Deutschen Industrie (BDI). Japan ist nach China der zweitwichtigste Handelspartner Deutschlands in Asien, Südkorea liegt auf Platz drei. Der Insel- und Stadtstaat Singapur gilt als der zentrale Wirtschaftsknotenpunkt für Südostasien und den Indopazifik.

"Wir haben in den vergangenen Jahren vielleicht zu sehr auf den natürlich maßgeblichen Industriepartner China gesetzt", sagt der ausgewiesene Asien-Experte Niedermark. "Wir sehen jetzt aus verschiedensten Gründen, die hauptsächlich geopolitisch sind, aber auch im industriellen Wettbewerb mit China, dass wir das Risiko anders verteilen müssen."

Niedermark und andere BDI-Spitzenvertreter reisen in dieser Woche zunächst nach China und von dort weiter nach Seoul. In der südkoreanischen Hauptstadt findet vom 29. bis 31. Oktober die "Asien-Pazifik-Konferenz der Deutschen Wirtschaft" statt. Das hochrangige Treffen mit rund 1.000 Teilnehmern aus Politik, Wirtschaft und Wissenschaft wird am Donnerstag von Merz eröffnet.

Wirtschaftsministerin Katherina Reiche (CDU) übernimmt zusammen mit Siemens-Vorstandschef Roland Busch die Leitung der Konferenz. "Es ist gut und wichtig, diese Beziehungen zu pflegen", betont der BDI-Repräsentant Niedermark und verweist auf die hochrangige Wirtschaftsdelegation, die den Bundeskanzler in der Regierungsmaschine nach Asien begleitet. An Bord sind unter anderem Vertreter aus den Bereichen der Industrie- und Technologieunternehmen, der Verteidigung und der Energie. Die Namen von Teilnehmern gibt die Bundesregierung aber gewöhnlich bis zum Abflug nicht bekannt.

Niedermark findet es "klasse", dass Merz und Steinmeier gleichzeitig in die boomende Region Asien-Indopazifik reisen. Nötig sei ein Engagement auf verschiedenen Spuren: "Wir müssen mit China weiter im Gespräch bleiben. Wir müssen aber gleichzeitig neue Allianzen mit etablierten und neuen Partnern in der Region Asien suchen."
