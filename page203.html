Brasilien-Wahl: Lula da Silva und Flávio Bolsonaro Kopf-an-Kopf

📅 Oct 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Knapp eine Woche vor der entscheidenden Stichwahl sehen Umfragen den linken Präsidenten Lula da Silva und den rechten Herausforderer Bolsonaro nahezu gleichauf. Die Entscheidung dürfte in der letzten TV-Debatte fallen.

Auf der Zielgeraden der Präsidentenwahl wird es in Brasilien immens spannend: Nur wenige Tage dauert es noch bis zur Stichwahl am 25. Oktober zwischen dem linken Amtsinhaber Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva und dem Rechtspopulisten Flávio Bolsonaro. Und beide Kandidaten liegen in der Wählergunst nahezu gleichauf. Während drei Umfragen aus der vergangenen Woche einen hauchdünnen Vorsprung für Bolsonaro sehen, kommt das Institut PoderData auf jeweils 50 Prozent für beide Kandidaten. Damit droht eine ähnlich knappe Entscheidung wie vor vier Jahren, als Lula da Silva mit 50,9 Prozent gegen Flávio Bolsonaros Vater, den mittlerweile wegen eines Putschversuchs inhaftierten Jair Messias Bolsonaro, gewann.

Flávio Bolsonaro hatte überraschend den ersten Wahlgang am 4. Oktober mit 47 Prozent zu 45 Prozent gewonnen. Zudem gewann sein rechtes Lager im Kongress viele neue Sitze, auf Kosten vor allem von Parteien des gemäßigten Zentrums. In der Woche nach dem ersten Wahlgang veröffentlichte Umfragen sahen Bolsonaro dann gar mit bis zu sechs Prozentpunkten vorn. Doch in der vergangenen Woche konnte Lula mit landesweiten Massenkundgebungen und einem frischeren Auftreten in den sozialen Medien an Momentum gewinnen. Bolsonaro hatte angesichts der guten Umfragen dagegen seinen Straßenwahlkampf zurückgefahren. Das erwies sich als Fehler. Nun mobilisiert auch er seine Anhänger für landesweite Kundgebungen kurz vor der Stichwahl.

Der Antagonismus zwischen dem linken Lager um Lula da Silva und dem ultrarechten Bolsonaro-Lager bestimmt Brasiliens Politik seit nunmehr einem Jahrzehnt. Landesweit bekannt geworden war Jair Messias Bolsonaro 2016 während des Impeachments gegen Lulas politische Ziehtochter, die damalige Präsidentin Dilma Rousseff. Die Präsidentschaftswahl 2018 konnte Bolsonaro Senior dann in Abwesenheit des wegen Korruptionsvorwürfen inhaftierten Lula gewinnen. Doch als die Urteile gegen Lula aufgehoben wurden, gelang dem Ex-Gewerkschaftsführer 2022 ein Comeback. Jair Bolsonaro hingegen wurde wegen eines Komplotts gegen Wahlsieger Lula zu 27 Jahren Haft verurteilt.

Diese Polarisierung Brasiliens sei für das Verschwinden von Parteien des Zentrums und das Fehlen einer Alternative, dem sogenannten dritten Weg, verantwortlich, sagt der Politologe Marco Aurelio Nogueira im Gespräch mit der Deutschen Welle. "Die Aufspaltung zwischen Lula und dem Bolsonarismus hat die brasilianische Politik eingefroren, und alles, was nicht dem einen oder dem anderen Pol angehörte, ist verschwunden." Angefeuert würde dies durch die sozialen Medien. "Die Algorithmen der Plattformen leben vom Konflikt und belohnen Aufregung." Platz für einen dritten Weg bleibe inmitten dieser Gegensätzlichkeiten nicht.

Dass Flávio Bolsonaro, anders als sein Vater 2022, den fast 81 Jahre alten Lula da Silva am 25. Oktober tatsächlich besiegen könnte, führt Nogueira auf das zivilisiertere Auftreten des 45-jährigen Senators zurück. "Jair Messias Bolsonaro war eine grobe, rhetorisch aggressive Figur. Flávio Bolsonaro gibt sich hingegen moderater und verjüngt das ästhetische Image des Bolsonarismus. Er kommuniziert geschickt, postet gut gemachte Videos und spricht auch Arbeiterfragen an, wie die Einführung der Fünf-Tage-Woche."

Mit der versprochenen Verkürzung der gesetzlichen Arbeitszeit konnte Lula da Silva zuletzt im Wahlkampf punkten. Alarmiert von Lulas steigenden Umfragewerten gab Flávio Bolsonaro seinen Widerstand auf und stimmte in der vergangenen Woche für die Fünf-Tage-Woche. Auch in fiskalpolitischen Fragen gibt er sich nun weniger radikal. Hatte er vor dem ersten Wahlgang noch drastische Kürzungen im Haushalt angekündigt, die an die "Kettensägen-Politik" seines argentinischen Verbündeten Javier Milei angelehnt waren, will er nun Lulas Sozialprogramme weiterführen. Einen Plan, wie er die hohe Verschuldung Brasiliens trotzdem zurückfahren will, hat Bolsonaro bisher nicht vorgelegt.

Überschattet wird der Wahlkampf von dem gigantischen Korruptionsskandal um das insolvente Finanzinstitut Banco Master. Gegen den ehemaligen Eigentümer der Bank, Daniel Vorcaro, wird wegen Betrug, Geldwäsche und Korruption ermittelt. Vorcaro soll Persönlichkeiten aus Politik, der Finanzwelt sowie der Justiz geschmiert haben. Auch mehrere Oberste Richter sollen involviert sein, es geht um Millionenzahlungen und dubiose Sexorgien. Und es trifft sowohl Richter, die dem Lula-Lager zugerechnet werden, als auch solche, die von Bolsonaro Senior ernannt wurden.

Auch Flávio Bolsonaro soll Sach- und Dienstleistungen von dem Banker angenommen haben, darunter zehn Millionen Euro für einen Film über das Leben seines Vaters. Allerdings konnte Lula da Silva mit den zweifelhaften Verbindungen des Bolsonaro-Clans zu dem Bankier nicht groß punkten. Denn auch Lula da Silvas eigener Sohn ist in einen Korruptionsskandal verwickelt. Zudem bringt Bolsonaro immer wieder in Erinnerung, dass Lula da Silva 2017 wegen Korruption verurteilt wurde - auch wenn diese Urteile samt und sonders vom Obersten Gerichtshof aufgehoben wurden.

Die Schlammschlacht um die Frage, wer denn nun korrupter sei, habe die Wähler längst abgestumpft, glaubt der Politologe Nogueira. "Es gibt von allen Seiten ununterbrochen diese Berichte über Skandale und gegenseitige Beschuldigungen. Das führt dazu, dass sich der Effekt im Wahlkampf quasi aufhebt. Der Durchschnittswähler verschließt die Ohren und misst diesen Anschuldigungen bei der Wahlentscheidung keine große Bedeutung mehr bei."

Am Sonntag fand im TV-Sender Band das erste direkte TV-Duell der beiden Kandidaten statt. Am Freitag, nur zwei Tage vor der Stichwahl, treffen sich Lula und Bolsonaro dann bei der traditionellen Debatte von Brasiliens größtem Sender Globo. Die Entscheidung, wer am 25. Oktober die Nase vorne hat, dürfte vom Auftreten der Kandidaten bei diesen TV-Duellen vor einem Millionenpublikum abhängen. Es werde darum gehen, sich von Provokationen der Gegenseite nicht aus der Ruhe bringen zu lassen, glauben Experten. Inhalte spielten nur eine untergeordnete Rolle.

Witkoff heads to Oslo, plans Russia trip for peace talks

📅 Oct 20, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

White House envoy Steve Witkoff is traveling to Oslo for talks Tuesday with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and European leaders, a U.S. official said.

The big picture: The talks will bring together Ukraine, European allies and the U.S. as they seek to develop a peace proposal to present to Russia.

  • The Oslo talks are expected to include Zelensky, Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, representatives of the E4 nations (the U.K., France, Germany and Poland) and officials from the EU and NATO.

Driving the news: The U.S. official said that following Oslo, Witkoff is also planning to travel to Russia, where he hopes to meet with President Vladimir Putin and present ideas developed in close consultation with Ukraine and European partners.

  • Trump adviser Jared Kushner has the flu and will not attend the Oslo meeting in person, but will participate virtually, the U.S. official said.
  • The planned meetings in Oslo build on the discussions held at Witkoff's Miami-area Shell Bay club, where Zelensky committed to reviewing and advancing a series of proposals aimed at creating a pathway toward a comprehensive and durable peace agreement, the U.S. official added.

Zoom in: The U.S. official said American, Ukrainian and European representatives worked together over the past week to develop the proposals.

  • "Steve and Jared also spoke with President Zelensky on Sunday to review progress and discuss next steps," the U.S. official said.
  • The trip is also expected to include efforts to secure the release of Stephen Hubbard, a retired American teacher whom the U.S. says has been wrongfully detained in Russia.

What's next: The U.S. official said the discussions are focused on two parallel tracks:

  1. Securing Ukraine's future: Establishing a sovereign, secure, and prosperous future for Ukraine, including credible security guarantees, European integration, and a comprehensive postwar economic framework that addresses Ukraine's financial needs and creates opportunities for long-term growth and investment.
  2. Creating the conditions for a durable peace: Developing a comprehensive proposal to Russia that addresses the issues necessary to end the war, establishes a new and enduring security architecture between Russia and Europe, and explores opportunities for postwar economic cooperation.
  • "President Trump has directed an intensive process to develop a comprehensive peace proposal with Ukraine and Europe, and the next step is to test whether that work can form the basis for serious negotiations with Russia," the U.S. official said.
  • "These discussions remain fluid, and substantial work remains. President Trump's objective is clear: to end the killing, bring the war to a close, and establish the conditions for a lasting peace that benefits Ukraine, Russia, Europe, and the United States."

Cooking homemade pizza on the stove in a cast-iron pan and then finishing it in a very hot oven yields a perfectly crispy crust in very little time. The crust is a super-quick food processor dough that requires little work and we top this pie with creamy ricotta and part-skim mozzarella, plus blanched broccoli and juicy roasted peppers. Feel free to substitute your own favorite vegetables — torn kale leaves, sautéed mushrooms and roasted cauliflower are all delicious on this “pizza bianca.”

RD Tip: If you decide to save some time and use jarred roasted peppers instead of fresh, remember to drain and pat them dry so they don’t add unwanted moisture or sogginess to your dough.

Active time: 15 minutes | Total time: 2 hours

Pulse the dough ingredients together in a food processor with 3/4 cup warm water (110°F/43°C) for 1 minute, until the dough comes together. Let rest for 1 minute, and then process the dough for 1 minute more. Alternatively, mix the flour, yeast and salt in a large bowl and stir to combine. Add the water and 1 tbsp of the oil and stir until a shaggy dough forms. Knead the dough on a clean surface, adding flour as necessary if the dough sticks, until the dough is smooth and elastic, about 5 minutes.

Lightly coat a large bowl with cooking spray and place the dough in the bowl. Cover with plastic wrap and set aside in a warm place until the dough has doubled, 1–1 1/2 hours, depending on the ambient temperature.

Put the broccoli in a small bowl with 2 tbsp water. Cover and microwave on high for 2 minutes. Drain and set aside. Alternatively, steam the broccoli for 2 minutes. Set aside.

Preheat the oven to 500°F (260°C). Divide the dough in half. Roll the dough out on a lightly floured surface into two 12-inch rounds. Cover 1 round and set aside.

Brush a large (12–14-inch) cast-iron skillet with half of the oil. Place 1 dough round in the skillet, pushing the dough into the edges of the pan with your fingertips. Spread half of the ricotta over the dough. Sprinkle with half the pepper and crushed red pepper. Spread half the broccoli, red bell peppers and mozzarella evenly over the ricotta. Set the skillet on the stove over medium-high heat and cook until the crust is beginning to brown on the bottom, about 4 minutes.

Transfer the skillet to the oven and bake until the edges of the crust are browned and the broccoli is charred in places, 10 minutes. Remove from the oven, transfer to a cutting board, and cover with foil. Repeat with the remaining ingredients to make a second pizza. Cut each pizza into 6 wedges and serve.

Serves: 6 | Serving Size: 2 slices/1/3 pizza

Hummus is one of the smarter options for snacks and lunches, because it’s high in protein and low in calories (1). This riff on the classic adds more vegetables to lighten it up and makes it even healthier. Adding carrots gives it a pop of color and a little natural sweetness — and serving it in cucumber boats cuts out the carbs in the standard pita bread.

RD Tip: This recipe will leave you with some extra hummus, which would work well as a sandwich spread or a light snack with cut up fresh vegetables.

Active time: 15 minutes | Total time: 20 minutes

Set up a steamer and steam the carrots and whole garlic cloves over simmering water for 5 minutes. Transfer to the bowl of a food processor. Add the navy beans and process, scraping down the sides as needed, until completely smooth. Add the tahini, and process until smooth and well-mixed. Drizzle the lemon juice through the feed tube while the machine is running, then add the salt and paprika and mix. Scrape into a medium bowl. It will be thick; if desired, stir in a little water.

To serve, peel the cucumbers (if desired), then halve lengthwise. Using a small spoon, scrape out the seeds and pulp. Spoon 3 rounded tbsp of hummus into the cavity of each cucumber, spreading evenly with the spoon. Place on a plate, and sprinkle with green onions. Serve immediately.

Toronto’s mayoral race has more than two candidates — why haven’t we heard from them?

📅 Oct 20, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Denise Balkissoon joined The Narwhal in 2021 as a founding member of its Ontario bureau. Her first (unpaid) job, as child to a municipal politician, saw her deliver many flyers and knock on many doors in north Scarborough.

This article was produced in collaboration with the Toronto Star.

Every election, I consider whether I’m voting for a candidate — or against one. My dream is to hopefully fill in the bubble beside the name of someone exciting. Instead, I often find myself drearily choosing someone okay (I guess) to block someone I can’t stand. The upcoming Toronto mayoral election is a case in point.

The two front-runners each have a caricaturish portrait of the city. The incumbent, Olivia Chow, lives in a shimmery mirage where Care Bear stares transform traffic jams into affordable homes. Her challenger, Brad Bradford, lives in an apocalyptic hellhole where rats keep trying to gnaw his leg off. The rest of us watch as this once-shiny city dulls, made sticky by a spiderweb of problems. We need sophisticated, strategic ideas, and have been given a choice between cops and school lunches.

No, actually. There will be 53 mayoral candidates’ names on the ballot on Oct. 26. But Torontonians tend to ignore the so-called fringe, perhaps burned by eras when kooky or frightening candidates got too much airtime. We rarely meet, never mind evaluate, let alone choose, a promising unknown.

Hoping to find a bubble worth filling, I went in search of options — and actually found a little hope. Before I name names, though, here’s how I did it.

First, I used the Toronto Star’s Vote Compass tool, which showed how my takes on the city’s challenges, costs and priorities compared to candidates’ positions. I used that to narrow down which platforms to read in The Local’s candidate tracker, clicking through to websites of those who seemed serious and promising.

It wasn’t quick, but given the threats to democracy from misinformation, shrinking attention spans, dark money, competing crises and plain old apathy, the effort seemed worth it. Then again, I get to count this as work. Not everyone has the time or ability to do it. And Toronto has a type of electoral system that is “really challenging for voters,” according to Jack Lucas, a political science professor at the University of Calgary.

Lucas is co-director of the Canadian Municipal Barometer, an ongoing study of local government. One of his interests is the role and value of political parties at the municipal level. In some provinces, municipal candidates can run with party affiliation, as they do federally or provincially. In Ontario, including Toronto, each candidate — and winner — is a lone wolf.

Theoretically, this discourages hard divides between “conservatives” and “progressives,” since councillors vote independently on every issue, allowing them to disagree or join forces from one cause to the next. Lucas says polarization is happening anyway, though, which the cops vs. school lunches showdown suggests is true.

Meanwhile, voters are left adrift in a sea of unknown candidates. All alone, they need to seek out and analyze information on not just policy, but integrity, experience and the ability to actually get the job done. Lucas believes a party system helps voters discern who’s worth their time. Without them, he says, it’s not surprising people gravitate towards known quantities.

I’m not quite sold — to me, independence and coalition-building are unique, important aspects of municipal governance we should try to strengthen, not abandon. Also, parties can be risk-averse and boring, stoking stereotypes and prejudice. But Lucas did sway me when he said the muscle of party machinery can help new candidates find their feet. “It’s a lonely enterprise,” he said, “and if you don’t already have a network of support behind you, it can be really challenging to break through.” That, plus parties have money.

Which brings me to mayoral candidate Laura Dean, who has raised $25,000 for her campaign. It’s more than she expected, she told me. It’s also oodles less than the nearly $1.6 million Chow spent in 2023. It’s not enough to buy even one ad between Blue Jays’ innings, Dean said, let alone the back-to-back spots Bradford is running.

Dean has spent more than a decade advising Ontario municipalities on land use (including housing starts!) as a partner at a Bay Street law firm. It’s a career she chose after time spent at the Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing and the now-defunct provincial climate secretariat. That last bit is what made this environmental journalist give her a call.

“When this election cycle came up, I was really shocked by the tone and just the quality of the discourse — it’s so poor. And, more, extremely polarizing,” Dean said about deciding to run. “The Canadian public so desperately needs more options and good quality conversation.”

Dean isn’t starry-eyed about her prospects and that’s fine. Plenty of worthwhile candidates need to try and try again. The point is, not every unknown is kooky or scary. Many are worthwhile, and with dozens of options, it’s bad practice to let polls decide front-runners (and debate invitees) before the race even starts. If a wider choice sounds appealing, Dean and other non-celebrity candidates will headline an “alternative” mayoral debate at City Hall on Oct. 20.

Dean said the enthusiasm she’s received has her already considering her options for 2030. While many people say they’re glad she’s running, she said, they aren’t ready to fill her bubble just yet. Torontonians are practiced in voting for the person they consider okay (I guess) as they try to block the candidate they can’t stand. Maybe next time, we’ll believe we deserve better.

Denise Balkissoon is based in her hometown of Toronto. Prior to becoming executive editor, Denise helped launch The Narwhal's Ontario bureau, steering the team to Canada's highest journalism honour, the Michener Award. (More from Denise Balkissoon)

The affordability question Republicans can't escape

📅 Oct 19, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

One issue is breaking through even among the most loyal Republican supporters: gas prices.

Why it matters: Republicans promised to slash gas prices. But the Iran war sent them soaring, up 37% since President Trump took office.

  • Republicans call it temporary. But there are limits to what voters will accept.
  • On recent campaign trips with House Majority Whip Tom Emmer (R-Minn.) and Majority Leader Steve Scalise (R-La.), gas prices were the most frequent concern raised at events attended by Axios.
  • These weren't rooms full of undecided voters. They were GOP donors, activists and supporters who had turned out to help Republicans win in November.

Zoom in: The first question Scalise fielded at a campaign event for Republican Marty O'Donnell in Nevada was about high gas prices.

  • "Everyone's always worried about gas prices," the attendee said, asking whether Republicans could do more to promote Trump's efforts to expand domestic energy production.
  • Another attendee pointed to the gas-price difference between California and Oregon on a recent trip, and asked whether Congress could intervene to ease state restrictions.
  • Scalise acknowledged the impact of the Iran war on gas prices — and said they'd fall once it's resolved — but blamed Democratic energy policies for making the U.S. more vulnerable to rising costs.

Zoom out: Emmer faced similar concerns during a recent campaign trip through North Carolina and Virginia.

  • "Four-dollar gas, five-dollar diesel, six-dollar diesel, totally unacceptable," Emmer told supporters at a North Carolina fundraiser. "But it ain't enough to make me vote for communists."
  • "[Democrats] are talking about affordability. That's the problem. We need to be sensitive to the fact that gas and diesel are way up. Just sensitive to it because people are struggling," he told Axios in an interview.

The big picture: Republican candidates are feeling the pressure too.

10 Animal Moves for Kids

📅 Oct 19, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Animal moves are great way to "trick" your child into exercising, building strength, improving mobility, and getting a great workout. The key is that rather than force exercise kids can have fun walking like a duck or instead of doing box jumps they can do frog jumps.

However, we are not quadrupeds or amphibians or water fowl—we are humans with completely different anatomies than the animals we mimic. Therefore our animal moves aren't exactly the same as the animals who actually perform them. They might look even different if you're an adult practicing animal movements for mobility and strength. For kids though, here are my ten favorite animal moves.

To perform the duck walk, sit in a full squat with heels down and feet angling out 8 to 15%. While staying in the squat position, walk. One cue to maintain proper technique is to ensure your head doesn’t bob up and down. Aim to keep your head level as you duck walk. This is an excellent exercise for quad strength and hip mobility.

A bear crawl is straightforward. Start on all fours and move on your hands and feet, keeping your back straight and parallel with the ground. Then, crawl contralaterally, which means moving your right arm with your left foot and your left arm with your right foot—opposite arm and leg moving together. As you become comfortable, try crawling forward, backward, to the right, and to the left. Challenge yourself further by crawling upstairs and downstairs or uphill both backward and forward.

This movement is an ideal general warm-up for an exercise day as it gets your shoulders firing and engages your core. One thing to consider is doing five minutes of bear crawls a day around your house. Just accumulate five minutes daily, and you will feel more limber than you ever thought possible.

Squat down on your heels and lightly place your hands on the floor in front of you for balance. Hop forward a foot or two without fully standing up, always maintaining that squatting position. Allow your hands to touch the floor, more as a reference point than as a base of support. Use this exercise to build strength in the squat's bottom position.

Begin in a position similar to the bunny hop, but with feet slightly wider and more weight on the hands. To move in the gorilla walk, place most of the weight on your arms and catapult your lower body forward. Imagine being a silverback gorilla vaulting through the jungle on your massive knuckles.

This is similar to the bunny hop, but there's no arm involvement. It's all about the quads and legs. Keep your torso more upright and hop along. You can also achieve a bit more hip extension on the jump, making it feel like more of a leap.

Lay down on your back and cross your arms. Move your hips and push off with your feet to crawl backward, pulling with your shoulder blades. This movement closely mimics a snake crawl. The key is to minimize limb use and rely mainly on your torso's gyration.

For the alligator crawl, start in a low plank position, as if you're at the bottom of a push-up, with weight on your hands and toes. Keep your belly and chest just above the ground and crawl. Start slowly, then try speeding up as you get the hang of it. This crawl might be one of the most challenging ones and is excellent for building isometric arm, tricep strength, and core tension.

Enjoying this post? Share it so others can find it!

The frog jump starts in a full squat. From there, jump as high and far as you can, landing back in a squat, then repeat. Ensure you maintain technical integrity; it's not a race. Each jump should represent maximal output with perfect technique. If you begin to feel tired and notice sloppy technique, stop the exercise. This jump is excellent for peak power output. If you continue frog jumps into your 40s, 50s, or even older, you'll be in a good position for long-term fitness.

There are a couple ways to do the monkey bars.

Bent arms: You can muscle your way across, keeping your arms flexed/bent as you pull yourself across from bar to bar. This is the fastest way to traverse the bars, but it's also very hard on your arms. A great bicep workout.

Straight arms: You can also swing with straight arms, using the momentum of your swinging legs and torso to carry you from bar to bar. This isn't about arm strength, rather it's predicated on being in rhythm and having a strong enough grip and stable shoulder girdle to support you going the distance.

Sit on the ground with your legs stretched out in front of you. From that position, place your hands and feet flat on the ground and lift yourself up so your body is no longer touching. Keeping your hips elevated, walk around forward, backward, and sideways. Always move contra laterally. Right hand, left foot. Left hand, right foot.

Kids can use animal moves at any point in the day, but if you're wondering when best to incorporate them take a look at the below suggestions:

A morning routine: Every morning, do 30 seconds to a minute of each of the animal moves. It's a great way to start the day and get some movement going and blood flowing.

Microworkouts: Every couple hours or so, do a minute or two of animal moves. Use them to break up sitting on the couch for extended amounts of time or better encourage them while kids are watching TV.

Warmups: Animal moves are great for warming up before a more intense activity. If your kid plays soccer duck walks, alligator walks, and frog jumps. If they play a racket sport try bear crawls or straight arm monkey bars. The point of a warmup is to increase body temperature, elevate the heart rate, and mobilize the targeted muscles and joints. Animal moves are great for all that.

Animal moves are a great way to have fun, build strength and mobility, and increase athleticism. Plus, they're not just for kids, they're great exercises for adults too.

What Happened: A New Mexico Democrat in the House introduced a bill last week that would make it easier for U.S. citizens to sue immigration agents and the federal government over wrongful detentions and arrests.

Rep. Teresa Leger Fernández credited ProPublica’s reporting as the catalyst for the bill, the Justice for Citizens Wrongfully Detained by ICE Act. Earlier this month, we revealed that more than 500 U.S. citizens have been detained in warrantless arrests during the second Trump administration.

Leger Fernández said the legislation would roll back the protections from lawsuits that agents and the federal government currently have. It would also establish a minimum level of compensation for people who win their suits, and extend the statute of limitations for such cases.

Without legislation, Leger Fernández said, agents “will continue to violate the law, to violate their own policy, and to violate the Constitution without any consequences.”

Asked about the legislation, the Department of Homeland Security, ICE’s parent agency, repeated claims that it is not arresting U.S. citizens and defended its immigration operations as “highly targeted.”

Background: The federal government denies that there is any pattern of immigration agents detaining U.S. citizens, but according to ProPublica’s count, more than 100 citizens were held after agents questioned their citizenship. At least 70 children and teens were also detained.
