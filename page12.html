Up to 100,000 people take part in protest against war economy in Rome — organizers

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

ROME, October 30. /TASS/. Some 100,000 people took to downtown Rome to march in protest against the war economy and rising prices, a TASS correspondent learned from the action organizers in the Unione Sindacale di Base (USB) trade union, who were supported by other unions and associations, including youth and student groups.

Faculty and students are protesting against the reallocation of funds from social programs to military spending. "I came to the demonstration because I support its goals. And students have the same right to protest," a faculty member holding a banner reading "Money for schools, not for war" told TASS. Short speeches broadcast over loudspeakers criticized the government of Giorgia Meloni, "a Christian woman who, like a coward, sends young people into the maelstrom of war."

Despite the fact that most of the demonstrators were young people, the protest was held without incident. The entire central part of the city was deserted, with no cars, taxis, or public transport moving around. Only a few shops and restaurants closed while the rest were happy to welcome the hungry demonstrators. Some tourists emerging with their luggage from Termini Central Station, where the march began, looked quite surprised. The demonstrators then marched through the neighboring streets, including those near the Russian Embassy, and headed toward the Colosseum. Off the march’s route, particularly near the Colosseum, the atmosphere was completely the same as any other day: crowds of tourists and street vendors. The Colosseum museum is operating as usual. Despite the general strike called by the same trade union, the subway is operating as usual. Security measures have been stepped up, but police are keeping a good distance away from the protesters, blocking off only certain sections and guarding ministry buildings, in particular, the transport ministry, which the march passed by. According to s experts, such distance is maintained to avoid potential clashes. "But everyone has been placed on high alert," they told TASS.

Similar marches were held simultaneously in dozens of Italian cities. Thus, according to the organizers, demonstrations drew 50,000 participants in Turin, up to 20,000 - in Naples, and 10,000 - in Milan. Partial blockings of ports were reported from Naples, Livorno, and Genoa.

Russia-Africa Summit by the numbers: What was achieved despite Western pressure

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Delegations from 48 African countries, including 21 leaders, gathered in Moscow for the third Russia-Africa Summit under the motto “For Peace, Security and Development.” The gathering concluded on Thursday with a final declaration and a new cooperation plan. Organizers reported 146 documents signed across the Summit and its accompanying economic, humanitarian and media forums, comprising 92 agreements and 54 memorandums.

More than 3,500 participants from 74 countries attended, including over 1,200 business representatives from around 630 companies.The forum hosted more than 40 sessions.

Russian President Vladimir Putin has already held at least nine bilateral meetings with African leaders, including the presidents of Egypt, Equatorial Guinea, Mozambique, Namibia, the Republic of the Congo, Burundi, and Kenya, as well as Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed and Libya’s Presidential Council Chairman Mohamed Al-Menfi.

Bilateral talks continued on Friday, with the day’s lineup including the leaders of Sudan, Madagascar, Ghana, Mali, South Sudan, the Central African Republic, DR Congo, and Eritrea, as well as Togo’s prime minister.

The October 28-29 summit capped four days of events, beginning with Monday’s media forum and including Tuesday’s separate gas exporters’ summit. Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov told RT that Western governments had tried to discourage African leaders from attending.

An overnight Ukrainian drone attack also prompted temporary restrictions at three Moscow airports before Thursday’s proceedings. Mayor Sergey Sobyanin reported 231 drones destroyed on the approaches to the capital. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov questioned the timing during an international gathering and praised air defenses.

Here are the main outcomes and key moments.

Putin and Burundian President Evariste Ndayishimiye, the African Union chair, co-chaired Wednesday's and Thursday’s proceedings. Leaders addressed a plenary session in Kremlin before continuing over a working breakfast.

They adopted the Moscow Declaration, a Russia-Africa Partnership Forum action plan for 2027-2029 and a declaration on civilian applications of artificial intelligence.

The political declaration backed greater African representation in international institutions, opposed unlawful unilateral sanctions, and supported efforts to address colonial-era damage. It also called for South Africa’s full G20 participation to be restored, following its exclusion from meetings under the US presidency. Pretoria remains a member.

At their closing press conference, Putin emphasized the potential for further cooperation, while Ndayishimiye pressed for measurable benefits. Another summit will follow, with its date and location to be agreed.

Russia and Africa are “natural allies in building a multipolar, genuinely democratic world order,” Putin told the plenary. He backed stronger African representation in global decision-making and described Russian businesses as partners investing in host countries rather than pursuing colonial exploitation.

African leaders also stressed that cooperation should strengthen their ability to make independent decisions and develop their economies.

“Contrary to what malevolent forces might think, Africa has not come to ask for aid,” Ndayishimiye told the Kremlin plenary. He called for commitments to become concrete investment programs and for international cooperation to follow African priorities.

Democratic Republic of Congo President Felix Tshisekedi made a similar distinction. “Diversifying our partnerships is not about choosing sides: it is about strengthening our freedom of decision-making in the service of our populations,” he said.

Zimbabwean President Emmerson Mnangagwa highlighted the need to turn agreements into action while processing more minerals domestically. Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi presented his country’s cooperation with Russia, including the El Dabaa nuclear plant and planned Suez Canal industrial zone, as a model for wider partnerships.

Russia also signed several documents to deepen cooperation with African regional organizations. These included memorandums with the East African Community, the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa, and the African Continental Free Trade Area Secretariat, alongside an action plan with the African Union Commission.

Sergey Lavrov met counterparts from 41 African states.

Ghanaian Foreign Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa said Lavrov pledged support for Accra’s application for BRICS partner status and would seek consensus among founding members.

Peskov said Putin presented country-specific cooperation roadmaps during bilateral meetings. Putin also highlighted expanding diplomatic representation: eight Russian embassies have opened in Africa since 2023, bringing the total to 48. Six trade missions cover 16 countries, while Botswana, Seychelles, and Togo plan embassies in Moscow.

Signings included a Wednesday memorandum between Russia’s development institution VEB.RF and BCID-AES, the joint investment bank serving Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger, to identify and assess development and integration projects.

Rosatom also signed a peaceful nuclear cooperation memorandum with Togo, establishing working groups to examine potential projects and training. Floating nuclear power units are among the possibilities.

Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed sought priority for nuclear cooperation, including financing, engineering training and medical isotopes. A roadmap for Ethiopia’s first nuclear plant was signed in March, Rosatom chief Aleksey Likhachev said in an interview published Friday. Site-selection preparations are underway. Burundi expressed interest in small modular reactors. Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi highlighted El Dabaa and the planned Russian industrial zone near the Suez Canal as examples of bilateral cooperation.

Wednesday’s Economic and Humanitarian Forum at Gostiny Dvor in the Russian capital set much of the practical agenda. Putin proposed at least doubling Russia-Africa trade to more than $50 billion. On Thursday, he reported that the ruble accounted for up to 85% of commercial transactions between the two sides.

Putin and African leaders toured the “Platform for Sovereign Development” exhibition, showcasing energy, healthcare, agricultural, and digital technologies.

Elsewhere, Kenyan President William Ruto visited Sberbank’s smart city development and highlighted potential AI cooperation in healthcare and education.

Tuesday’s eighth Gas Exporting Countries Forum summit adopted its own Moscow Declaration, separate from the Russia-Africa documents. It reaffirmed sovereign rights over gas resources and supported supply security, investment, and a balanced transition toward lower-carbon energy.

At Monday’s ministerial meeting, Russian Energy Minister Sergey Tsivilev promoted a proposed gas bank for long-term infrastructure financing, including in Africa. The initiative remained under discussion.

The Russia-Africa Media Forum ran from Monday to Thursday, organized by RT alongside Sputnik and TASS. Organizers welcomed more than 200 participants from 46 African countries for discussions on reporting, professional cooperation, and media sovereignty.

The forum also produced several media partnerships. These included Ethiopia’s Fana renewing its agreement with RT and Libya’s national news agency signing with RT Arabic. Rossiya Segodnya concluded partnerships with outlets in Algeria, Chad, Morocco, Ghana, and Zimbabwe, as well as Ethiopia’s Debre Markos University and Niger’s Higher School of Journalism and Communication.

RT’s Thursday session, “Forging Independence: Lessons from the Founders of African Nations,” featured descendants of African liberation leaders. Samia Nkrumah, daughter of Ghana’s first president Kwame Nkrumah, credited Russian support during Africa’s independence struggles, saying, “We are all here in Russia for a reason.” Ndileka Mandela, granddaughter of South Africa’s Nelson Mandela, recalled first meeting him through a prison window on Robben Island in 1981, aged 16: “I touched the window on one side and he touched on the other.”

Putin extended Russia’s epidemic-control assistance program for Africa through 2029.

Eight countries have received 15 mobile laboratories, and around 450 African specialists have been trained under the program, he said.

Among the healthcare agreements was a five-year memorandum with DR Congo covering training, research, maternal and child health, nuclear medicine, and digital healthcare.

Nearly 40,000 Africans study at Russian universities, up 50% since 2020, according to Putin. Government-funded scholarship places have nearly tripled over that period, reaching 5,500 for 2026-2027. Announced initiatives include ten centers helping African applicants enter Russian universities and vocational training programs.

Andrey Maslov, director of HSE University’s Center for African Studies, told RT that extended visits by African leaders and their separate business programs showed political ties with Moscow are gaining practical substance.

Russia-Africa ties can help continent overcome economic dependence — Ugandan educator

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Education  |  Read Source →

MOSCOW, October 30. /TASS/. Closer cooperation between Russia and African countries could bring economic opportunities to Uganda and the whole continent, Ugandan educator and founder of the Chunga-Changa interactive Russian-language school Daniel Omara told TASS.

"Russian people and Africans have been tied in this sort of collaboration before. The Russian people, together with other peoples of the Soviet Union, helped Africans fight for and gain their independence," said Omara, who won the Grand Prize in the Art of Teaching category of the International Eurasia Award.

He said that Africa had continued to face economic dependence even after gaining independence, with some Western and European partners maintaining relationships that, in his view, benefited them at the expense of African countries.

"Unfortunately, after getting independence, we almost found ourselves in a new season called neocolonialism. These colonial powers did not actually let go of African peoples. They kept taking advantage of them, with deals that are almost parasitic in nature," he said.

Omara believes closer ties with Russia could help African countries build partnerships on different terms, with greater respect for their independence and sovereignty.

Speaking about the outcome of the Russia-Africa summit, which ended on Thursday, he said: "This forum is bringing us back to a new reality, where we respect ourselves, respect each country's sovereignty and are able to meet on equal terms. We are not taking advantage of anyone."

He also welcomed the growing trade between Russia and African countries and the expansion of Russian businesses on the continent.

Omara singled out plans for local manufacturing and pharmaceutical production, saying that producing vaccines and medicines in Africa could open up new possibilities for the continent.

"These kinds of economic collaborations that we are seeing announced at this forum this year are just the beginning of the future that Africa is contributing to create together with Russia and countries of BRICS," he said.

The third Russia-Africa Summit was held in Moscow on October 28-29, bringing together delegations from 48 African countries, including 22 represented by heads of state. Participants discussed expanding cooperation in trade, investment, education, healthcare, technology and other areas.

Rosatom assists African countries with non-nuclear technologies — CEO

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

MOSCOW, October 30. /TASS/. Rosatom helps African countries that are not yet ready to join nuclear projects with non-nuclear energy technologies, Chief Executive Officer of Russia’s state corporation Alexey Likhachev told reporters as he commented on results of the Russia-Africa forum in Moscow.

"There are countries that are not yet ready to participate in nuclear projects. For them, we provide help with non-nuclear energy technologies. We are working with Mali on the development of a solar power plant equipped with our energy storage systems. Negotiations with the Congo on the supply of small-scale hydroelectric power plants have also resumed," he said.

In building constructive relations with countries on the African continent, Rosatom strictly adheres to the principles articulated by Russian President Vladimir Putin at last year’s Global Atomic Forum: partnership, equal access to advanced technologies, and a firm rejection of technological neocolonialism, Likhachev stressed.

From Lenin to Star Trek captain – screen legend dies at 86

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

British screen legend Patrick Stewart, who portrayed Soviet leader Vladimir Lenin and a powerful KGB chief before finding global fame as Captain Picard in Star Trek and Professor Charles Xavier in X-Men, has died aged 86.

Stewart passed away at his Los Angeles home on Wednesday after battling Alzheimer’s disease and Lewy body dementia, his representatives confirmed on Thursday. His family said he spent his final days surrounded by loved ones following “a brief and poignant journey” with the illnesses.

Born in Yorkshire in 1940, Stewart built his reputation on the British stage, spending decades performing Shakespeare and joining the prestigious Royal Shakespeare Company in the 1960s.

Long before commanding the USS Enterprise, Stewart had already brought some of the most prominent figures in Russian and Soviet history and literature to the screen. In the 1974 BBC historical drama Fall of Eagles, he portrayed Soviet leader Vladimir Lenin in a series chronicling the collapse of Europe’s great empires, including Russia’s Romanov dynasty.

Five years later, Stewart took on one of his most intriguing Cold War roles. He played Karla, the formidable Soviet intelligence chief and principal rival of British spy George Smiley, portrayed by Alec Guinness, in the BBC adaptation of John le Carré’s Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy (1979).

Stewart returned as Karla in Smiley’s People (1982), another adaptation of le Carré’s espionage novels. Despite his character’s central role in the intelligence struggle between Moscow and London, Stewart delivered the entire performance without uttering a single word.

His ties to Russian literature went beyond Cold War espionage. In 1973, Stewart appeared as Gurvich in the BBC adaptation of The Love-Girl and the Innocent, a play by Alexander Solzhenitsyn set in a Soviet labor camp. The production aired months before the Nobel Prize-winning Russian writer was expelled from the USSR in 1974.

The actor’s career took a dramatic turn in 1987 when he landed the role of Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation. The series propelled Stewart to international stardom, spawning several films and a later spin-off centered on his character.

He went on to portray the wheelchair-bound telepath Professor Charles Xavier in the blockbuster X-Men franchise, sharing the screen with longtime friend Ian McKellen.

Stewart was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II in 2010 for his services to drama.

His death prompted tributes from across the entertainment industry, with fellow Star Trek actor William Shatner remembering him as “a friend, a colleague, and a magnificent actor.”

Chad eyes deeper security, energy cooperation with Russia

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Education  |  Read Source →

HARARE, October 30. /TASS/. Chad seeks to deepen cooperation with Russia in security, energy, education and advanced technology, the Tchadinfos media outlet reported following the Russian-Africa Summit in Moscow.

The Chadian delegation to the summit was led by Minister of Foreign Affairs, African Integration and Chadians Abroad Ahmat Kogri.

According to the outlet, Chad aims to achieve concrete results in cooperation with Russia. Priorities include joint efforts to combat terrorism and extremism, assistance with personnel trading and strengthening the capabilities of Chadian law enforcement agencies.

N’Djamena also intends to attract Russian investment in railroads, energy, agriculture and mining. In addition, Chad is interested in increasing the number of scholarships available to its young citizens wishing to study in Russia.
