A high-profile separatist leader has been kicked out of the United Conservative Party.

Alberta Premier Danielle Smith told reporters in Calgary on Friday that the party’s executive director made the decision to revoke Jeffrey Rath’s membership over code of conduct issues.

It’s a decision she supports, Smith said, calling Rath “somebody whose objective and mission is to turn us into something we are not.”

Smith says her United Conservatives must support the party’s decision, adding anyone in party ranks who still wants to stump for separation needs to find a new political home.

“He has said publicly things like he wants to come to the (annual general meeting) to turn the UCP into an independence party — and we are not an independence party,” Smith said.

“The objective of the party is to get re-elected.”

Rath, a vocal critic of Smith and the UCP, said Friday the decision made him laugh and proves points he’s been making for some time now.

“It’s probably the worst thing that they could do,” Rath told Global News.

“I mean you know, we’ve been criticizing Danielle Smith for behaving like a Stalinist dictator and her response is to behave like a Stalinist dictator and throw me out of the party. So it’s pretty funny to be honest, right?”

Rath said it was another “questionable political choice” by the premier.

Rath has been a key figure in the province’s independence movement and a harsh critic of the premier.

He has been calling for Smith’s resignation over her support of Alberta staying in Canada and has been working to try to force a vote on her leadership at the party’s AGM at the end of November in Calgary.

“I’m basically, as far as I’m concerned, being thrown out of the party because they don’t want me coming to the UCP AGM in Calgary this year, which is my hometown, and embarrassing Danielle,” he said.

Rath said his ejection doesn’t change things.

“If they think that it’s gonna stop people within the party from campaigning for a leadership review and removing her as leader, they just probably poured a whole bunch of gasoline on the fire,” he said.

On Friday afternoon, Rath posted to his social media screenshots of what appeared to be an email from the party’s executive director informing him of the decision.

The issue surrounds the decision by three-quarters of Alberta voters in last week’s referendum to stay in Canada rather than hold a second referendum on leaving Confederation.

Smith called the referendum. She said it was necessary to quell a growing debate in Alberta over its role in Confederation. Critics said there was never such a groundswell of separatist sentiment and that Smith called the vote to appease UCP separatists and keep her job.

After the referendum, she promised there wouldn’t be another separation vote while she’s premier. She said the referendum had settled the debate.

She also urged in-house separatists to find a new home.

The United Conservative Party, in confirming the news, told Global News while it doesn’t normally speak to internal membership matters, the facts of Rath’s ejection were already made public.

“Jeff Rath’s membership in the United Conservative Party was revoked for cause on October 29, effective immediately,” said a statement Friday from UCP director of communications Dave Prisco.

“His actions and words made clear that he did not support the UCP or its objectives. The decision was made under Article 7 of the party’s Membership Rules and Procedures because of his public conduct toward the party and its leader.”

Rath said he has sent a letter telling the party he intends to appeal the decision, although he suspects it is unlikely to succeed.

“They say I’m being removed from membership for cause. So, I’ve ask them to particularize the cause and then we’ll take it forward from there,” Rath said.

Toronto police have charged the driver of a truck that struck and killed a traffic agent at the edge of Thorncliffe Park on Thursday.

Global News has learned the person man was employed as a third-party driver for Metrolinx.

On Friday afternoon, the Toronto police said in a new release that a 33-year-old man from Brampton had been arrested and charged with careless driving causing death.

The collision happened Thursday around 9:02 a.m. at the intersection of Millwood Rd. and Overlea Blvd. after a Kenworth dump truck struck the 44-year-old traffic agent. Life-saving measures were performed, but the man died at the scene.

As a memorial continues to grow, including messages from residents thanking the traffic agent for keeping them safe, questions are also on the rise. Several sources have confirmed the driver of the dump truck was a sub-contractor with Metrolinx, which is working on the Ontario Line in the area.

Councillor Rachel Chernos Lin who represents the ward, said in a statement that there have been ongoing traffic concerns in the area, particularly related to truck movement and construction-related traffic around the Ontario Line project. Chernos Lin said her office has repeatedly raised concerns and pushed for safety measures to be made by Metrolinx and city staff.

“It is imperative that we understand the circumstances surrounding this tragic incident,” she said. “We will be pushing for an urgent review of what additional measures can be implemented to improve safety at this and other nearby intersections.”

Ontario’s Transportation Minister Prabmeet Sarkaria told Global News that his ministry is cooperating with police during the investigation.

“This is an absolutely unfortunate incident, but we would absolutely review this and implement any further policies and procedures that would mitigate against any future incidents such as this,” he said.

This isn’t the first time a truck with ties to the Ontario Line has been involved in a pedestrian related collision.

In February 2025, another dump truck driven by another Metrolinx sub-contractor seriously injured a woman near the Pape Avenue and Mortimer Avenue prompting calls for the transit agency to review its safety plans.

Sarkaria said Friday that several measures were implemented following the 2025 incident.

Councillor Paula Fletcher, who represents the ward where the 2025 incident occurred, said not enough has been done to address the problem.

“I think those safety standards have been relaxed and they need to be tightened up so this never happens again,” Fletcher said.

In a statement, a Metrolinx spokesperson said their thoughts are with all those impacted by the incident.

“Further updates will be provided as information becomes available and in coordination with Toronto Police Services,” they said.

B.C. Premier-designate Lorne Doerkson is about to become the new “captain” of the province and one of the issues he wants to dive into is BC Ferries.

Specifically a decision that was made last year to award a contract for new ferries to a Chinese shipyard.

“I don’t know what the deal looks like, I mean we would obviously have an interest in seeing that,” Doerkson said.

“There’s so much that we haven’t seen, but there’s a reason to look at it for the betterment of British Columbia and I’ll look at it for sure.”

BC Ferries chose Weihai Shipyards to build four new Summit-class hybrid-electric ferries, with the first delivery expected in 2029.

It prompted a wave of criticism, but BC Ferries said no Canadian shipyard bid for the project.

“The contracts have been signed and moving out of these contracts would very likely come with a contractual penalty,” Werner Antweiler, a professor at UBC’s Sauder School of Business, said.

“That is a cost that would have to be considered in addition to finding a replacement.”

A study conducted last year found that building the four ferries in B.C. would have generated an additional $1.5 billion in economic activity, most of it in B.C.

During the campaign, the Conservatives vowed to fix BC Ferries, but it will take much more to reshore shipbuilding in the province.

“They will have to be ready with concrete measures including a change in the mandate for BC Ferries to ensure economic development issues are part of what BC Ferries does,” Jim Stanford, an economist and director of the Centre for Future Work, said.

It is also a time of upheaval at BC Ferries.

CEO Nicholas Jimenez was dismissed just two days after the election as part of what it’s calling a “revitalization review.”

In a statement BC ferries said: “We recognize the interest in what the change in government and recent leadership transition could mean for BC Ferries, including our fleet renewal program.”

“I think the ship has probably sailed, pardon the pun, on the Summit class coming out of the Weihai Shipyards, but that’s not to say there aren’t other vessels in the BC Ferries fleet that are going to require renewal,” president of the BC Ferries and Marine Workers Union, Eric McNeely, said.

Dru Brown to start for Winnipeg Blue Bombers in East semifinal versus Argos

Dru Brown will start at quarterback Saturday when the Winnipeg Blue Bombers visit the Toronto Argonauts in the East semifinal.

Brown’s status was confirmed when Winnipeg released its depth chart Friday.

The move wasn’t a surprise as incumbent Zach Collaros (ribs) was listed as questionable for the playoff game.

Brown replaced an injured Collaros in last week’s 34-10 win over the B.C. Lions, completing all 11 passes he attempted for 185 yards and two TDs.

Chad Kelly will start Saturday’s game for Toronto.

Kelly didn’t play in Toronto’s regular-season finale versus Montreal last week due to a knee injury but was a full participant in practice this week.

Kelly understands how important game management will be in the East Division semifinal but a message from home emphasized that point.

The two teams split their regular-season series 1-1 but both contests were played in Manitoba due to World Cup games being staged in southern Ontario.

This week, Kelly, of Buffalo, N.Y., signed a five-year extension with Toronto that will pay him over $1 million annually. The deal comes after the ’23 CFL outstanding player established career highs in passing yards (5,067) and TDs (33).

But Kelly, 32, also threw a CFL-high 24 interceptions, some coming when he pressed to make the big throw downfield. Kelly has continuously said this season it’s important he manage games, sustain drives and not force matters.

“I got a text from my uncle Jim this morning,” Kelly said Friday. “He said, ‘Take what the defence gives you.’

“I’ve got to make sure I come out managing the situation and knowing when I can take my shots but just trying to feel the flow of the game and get comfortable out there.”

Kelly’s uncle is none other than Jim Kelly, the Hall of Fame quarterback who guided the Buffalo Bills to four straight Super Bowl appearances in the 1990s.

Toronto averaged a CFL-best 342.5 passing yards per game and was tied with Montreal for most TD passes (39). Winnipeg’s defence allowed the fewest passing yards (272.1 per game) and completions (389) during the regular season.

But a challenge Toronto’s offence presents is a receiving corps that had four 1,000-yard performers, Damonte Coxie, Tyler Kahmann and Canadians David Ungerer III and Kevin Mital – a franchise first.

“That’s huge,” Kelly said. “You just know where they’re going to be at all times.

“Everyone has understood to run fast every play (because) you might get the ball. We just try to make sure we’re on the same page and paint the picture the way it’s supposed to be painted.”

Kahmann was named the CFL’s top rookie Friday after registering 77 catches for 1,092 yards and eight TDs.

There could be opportunities for Toronto to establish its ground game as Winnipeg was ranked eighth (117.4 yards per game) against the run. But the Argos only rushed for 72.2 yards per game, second worst in the CFL.

“Well, you’d like to think so (there be chance for Toronto to run versus Winnipeg),” said Argos head coach Mike Miller. “But it’s another thing when you hit the field and you’ve got to go out and start moving guys around and make things happen.”

The Bombers have the CFL’s fifth-ranked ground game (101.6 yards per game) but it’s spearheaded by Canadian Brady Oliveira. The Winnipeg native led the league in rushing attempts (237), yards (1,493), 100-yard games (eight) and runs of 10-plus yards (44).

В Польше отстранили председателя Конституционного трибунала. Здание оцепила полиция, а оппозиция заявила о «перевороте»

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

В Польше вечером 30 октября полиция оцепила здание Конституционного трибунала, сообщает Associated Press.

Это произошло после того, как на сайте суда была опубликована информация об отстранении от работы председателя трибунала Богдана Свенчковского и его заместителя и у здания начала собираться толпа протестующих.

Премьер-министр Дональд Туск, комментируя сообщение об отстранении Свенчковского, заявил, что «восстановление закона — процесс, который требует решительных действий». Отстраненный заместитель Свенчковского и представители оппозиции, в свою очередь, назвали происходящее «переворотом».

На фоне событий вокруг Конституционного трибунала президент Польши Кароль Навроцкий прервал визит в Сенегал и объявил, что возвращается в страну.

Конфликт вокруг Конституционного трибунала между правительством Туска и оппозиционной партией «Право и справедливость», которая была у власти до 2023 года, длится уже несколько месяцев. Он начался, когда Навроцкий (он опирается на поддержку «Права и справедливости») отказался принимать присягу у трех новых судей, избранных новым составом парламента.

Туск и его правительство, в свою очередь, отказывают трибуналу в легитимности, поскольку в его составе остаются судьи, назначенные при правлении «Права и справедливости».
