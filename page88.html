Cinquante personnes ont été tuées dans un village rural du nord-est du Nigeria, selon des survivants. Les assaillants ont ouvert le feu, vendredi 23 octobre, juste après la prière, selon des habitants. "Les gens couraient dans toutes les directions pour échapper à l'attaque. Personne ne savait où aller pour être en sécurité", a déclaré un habitant de Binnari.

Malam Abubakar Saifullahi, un responsable communautaire, a déclaré que les assaillants étaient arrivés à bord d'une cinquantaine de motos, avant de lancer leur attaque contre le village. "Ils ont pillé des boutiques et emporté de l'argent liquide, des denrées alimentaires et d'autres provisions. Ils ont également pris des motos abandonnées par les personnes qui fuyaient l'attaque", a-t-il ajouté.

Le phénomène du banditisme prend de l'ampleur dans le nord-ouest et le centre du Nigeria. De puissantes bandes criminelles, motivées par l'appât du gain plutôt que par une idéologie, affrontent les forces de police et l'armée, déjà débordées, dans des zones rurales difficiles d'accès. Le pays lutte aussi contre une insurrection jihadiste depuis le soulèvement de Boko Haram en 2009.

4 rescued on third day after Kenya building collapse as families lose hope

The number of people rescued from a collapsed building in Kenya's capital has risen to four, while several others remain missing and are reported to have communicated with loved ones from beneath the rubble.

Rescue operations entered a third night Sunday as relatives said they were losing hope of finding those trapped alive.

The 18-story building collapsed Friday in a busy commercial street of the capital Nairobi, trapping construction workers and people reportedly inside shops on the ground and basement floors that had been illegally occupied.

Rescuers searched through the concrete and steel debris throughout Sunday and pulled two more people alive from the rubble, bringing the number of survivors rescued to four.

One of those rescued Saturday was tailor Victor Onyango, who said he remained hopeful and spoke with Zaynaba Mohammed, a shop owner who was also trapped overnight Friday.

“On Saturday morning, I saw some light coming through the rubble, and I knew those were rescuers,” Onyango told journalists Sunday. “When they pulled me out, I told them there was a woman behind the next rubble, and she was rescued after me.”

Both Onyango and Mohammed were treated at a hospital and discharged.

Families waited throughout Sunday for news about their loved ones. The interior minister had on Friday said seven people were reported to be inside the rubble but the Kenya Red Cross on Sunday said the missing persons reports had risen to 47.

Peter Barasa, a relative of one of those trapped, told journalists he was losing hope of finding his loved one alive.

“This would be the third day without food and water for him,” Barasa said.

The Nairobi County Gov. Johnson Sakaja called for stricter enforcement against building regulations’ flouters, calling the current fines a “slap in the wrist” for developers who are risking lives.

The owner of the collapsed building is deceased, and his family was in a joint ownership agreement with another party. It remains unclear if anyone has been arrested despite authorities saying the building had not been approved for construction and did not have a certificate of occupancy to allow for the renting of shops in the basement and ground floors.

Days before Israeli election, military conducts massive West Bank operation

The Israeli military carried out a massive military operation in the occupied West Bank over the weekend, displacing hundreds of Palestinians and placing major restrictions on Palestinian movement, in response to a gun attack that killed two Israeli soldiers and wounded three others in Hebron on Friday.

The operation comes as Israel enters the final days before a tense election, which is largely seen as a referendum on current prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s leadership and security policies during the past three years of war.

Most Palestinian workers with permits will not be allowed to enter Israeli communities for work in the occupied West Bank on Sunday and Monday, and crossings into Israel will remain closed until after Israel’s elections on Tuesday, its military said.

The Israel Defence Forces said it operated in more than 400 different locations over the weekend and arrested 40 Palestinians it claimed were affiliated with Hamas or Palestinian Islamic Jihad.

The operation displaced hundreds of families, forcing some to navigate crossings on foot after the roads were closed to traffic. At some military gates, people passed sacks of flour and gas canisters through the gates, while an elderly man pushed his wife in a wheelchair through an opening beside one of them. Another family attempted to navigate the gate to access medical appointments.

After Friday’s attack, Israel’s military said it found weapons in the gunmen’s car and that they were likely planning to attack Israeli civilians before being stopped at the checkpoint.

The military said that troops were “conducting precise, targeted and thorough searches” in the area where the gunmen operated, and warned residents to evacuate Deir Bahha, the suspected gunmen’s neighbourhood.

Hebron municipal officials said between 200 and 300 families had been displaced from Deir Bahha and four surrounding communities overnight, most going to other parts of the city.

Six Israelis have been killed this year in attacks in the West Bank, which is experiencing a surge of violence. During the same period, 112 Palestinians have been killed, including the suspected gunmen, according to the Ramallah-based Palestinian Health Ministry.

The attack sparked outrage in Israel, with Mr Netanyahu and three members of his right-wing government giving statements from the site of the attack on Friday afternoon, vowing to respond forcefully. “We warned a few weeks ago about attempts by our enemies to harm us, our citizens and our soldiers ahead of the election,” he said. “We will act to destroy this place.”

Mr Netanyahu is under pressure to demonstrate a strong show of force against Palestinian attackers to bolster his image to Israelis concerned about security.

Roughly three million Palestinians and 540,000 Israeli settlers live in the West Bank, which Israel occupied in the 1967 Six-Day War. Hebron is one of its most tense cities and the only place where Jewish settlers live in the heart of a densely populated Palestinian area under Israeli military protection.

Congrès du RN à Orléans : pour son premier meeting sous tension, Marine Le Pen promet une « renaissance » sans « rupture brutale »

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Toute l'actualité de votre région en continu sur notre application

Devant 8 500 militants et sous haute surveillance policière, la candidate à l’Élysée a esquissé son projet présidentiel tandis que quelques incidents ont éclaté dans la salle

Pas d’attaques, ni d’annonces : pour son premier grand discours de campagne dimanche 25 octobre à Orléans, sous haute surveillance policière, Marine Le Pen a développé son projet de « renaissance », et esquissé les contours d’une présidence que tous les sondages lui promettent, à six mois du scrutin.

Malgré les manifestations de la veille et l’attentat raciste de Trappes survenu vendredi, « la France a de beaux jours devant elle », a affirmé d’entrée la figure de proue du Rassemblement national. Dans une arène remplie de 8 500 militants chauffés à blanc, scandant régulièrement « on va gagner », la quadruple candidate à l’Élysée a voulu délivrer un message optimiste, autour du thème central de sa campagne : la « renaissance ».

Parce qu’à ses yeux « la bataille du constat est gagnée » et que « tout le monde en France est d’accord avec nous », la cheffe de file de l’extrême droite française s’est efforcée de rassurer ceux qui redoutent son accession au pouvoir – quasiment acquise selon les sondages qui la donnent largement en tête au premier tour et gagnante dans tous les scénarios au second. Sans se soucier de l’épée de Damoclès judiciaire qui pèse sur sa campagne, puisque la Cour de cassation pourrait rejeter son pourvoi et rendre définitive sa condamnation pour détournement de fonds publics (dans l’affaire des assistants parlementaires européens) à quelques semaines du premier tour le 18 avril.

Cette « renaissance » qu’elle promet ne serait ni une « rupture brutale », ni une « révolution destructrice », a-t-elle lancé durant son heure d’allocution à la tribune. Au contraire, elle y voit une manière de « s’élever vers le beau ». Pour y parvenir, elle mise cependant sur « la puissance » et la fermeté radicale d’un programme déjà connu, notamment en matière de sécurité et d’immigration. Mais elle a laissé les déclarations martiales à son dauphin Jordan Bardella, qui s’est chargé de souligner qu’une fois en place, il « utilisera tous les moyens légaux pour rétablir l’ordre et protéger les honnêtes gens ».

Marine Le Pen, elle, a réaffirmé sa volonté d’en faire son Premier ministre en cas de victoire, à la tête d’un gouvernement qu’elle souhaite « d’union nationale ». Justement, leur allié Éric Ciotti venait de vanter cette « union pour la France » et d’appeler ses « anciens amis » des Républicains à « revenir à la maison ».

Les deux lieutenants ayant copieusement fait huer tous ses adversaires, Marine Le Pen s’est abstenue de « donner des noms » en pâture. Ce qui ne l’a pas empêché de mettre dans un même sac les ennemis de sa « renaissance » : les « obscurantismes » de l’islamisme, des antifascistes, des Insoumis et des écologistes.

Des opposants soigneusement tenus à distance du meeting, par les 3 000 policiers et gendarmes déployés tout le week-end dans une ville qui s’était barricadée par crainte des débordements. Un dispositif qui avait permis la veille de tenir à l’écart les 13 000 à 26 000 manifestants venus dénoncer la tenue du congrès du parti à la flamme. Et qui a encore empêché une centaine de militants antifascistes d’approcher du lieu du meeting.

Une poignée d’activistes ont néanmoins réussi à se glisser dans la salle, déployant une banderole ciblant Jordan Bardella, réélu la veille à la tête du parti : « Le racisme et l’antisémitisme ça ne dérange pas le RN, au contraire ». Allusion aux révélations de Mediapart sur des écrits de jeunesse antisémites attribués au dirigeant nationaliste, qui a porté plainte pour diffamation.

L’action, revendiquée par l’Union des étudiants juifs de France (UEJF), a immédiatement donné lieu à une altercation avec le public, puis à une intervention musclée du service d’ordre du RN, qui a également fait sortir de la salle un journaliste de Mediapart présent à proximité. Déjà pris à partie la veille lors du congrès, les journalistes du site d’investigation ont choisi de quitter les lieux, estimant que leur sécurité n’était « pas assurée dans cet endroit ». L’UEJF a, pour sa part, annoncé une plainte pour violences en réunion.

En réponse, Jordan Bardella a accusé « des militants d’ultragauche, qui se revendiquent de l’UEJF » d’être « venus accompagnés de Mediapart pour agresser et attaquer des militants ». L’incident n’a pas fait dévier Marine Le Pen du message qu’elle était venue délivrer. Pas même quand elle a fait l’éloge de la « civilité », autre pilier de sa « renaissance ». Un remède à « l’ensauvagement », a-t-elle affirmé. Mieux, « le chemin le plus sûr pour faire reculer la violence dans notre société ».

ASX rebounds to end on a high; CSL names new chair

The Australian sharemarket has rebounded after an early fall to close in the green, with materials and energy stocks posting gains thanks to a rise in oil prices over the weekend.

The S&P/ASX 200 gained 14.7 points or 0.17 per cent, to 8809 on Monday. The Australian dollar was trading at US70.33¢.

The gains were largely thanks to advancing energy and materials stocks off the back of a rise in oil prices. Earlier in the day, Brent crude advanced above $US116 a barrel while West Texas Intermediate rose above $US102 before retreating slightly in the afternoon.

The gains were linked to fears the US would restart its war with Iran, with President Donald Trump telling Fox News it was “getting to be decision time” on whether he would make a deal with Tehran or “finish it off”. Meanwhile, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said if rationality existed, the US would not attempt an attack.

Woodside Energy rose 1 per cent and Santos added 1.3 per cent. Among the refiners, Ampol advanced 0.3 per cent and Viva Energy surged 3.2 per cent. Meanwhile, miners BHP and Fortescue added 1.3 per cent and 2.4 per cent respectively.

Gold miners also reported gains, with Northern Star gaining 2.1 per cent and Evolution rising 1.6 per cent as the gold price steadied at about $US4000 ($5690) per ounce.

Technology stocks fell early, following their Wall Street peers, but rebounded as the day progressed. WiseTech grew 1.7 per cent, Xero rose 1.4 per cent and TechnologyOne added 1.7 per cent. Embattled AI company NextDC slumped 2.5 per cent as it announced its chairman, Douglas Flynn, would stand for re-election at its annual meeting with investors next month but retire once a replacement is in place, and chief executive Craig Scroggie will forfeit millions in bonuses.

Pharmaceutical giant CSL has named its interim chief executive, Gordon Naylor, as its next chair, replacing Brian McNamee, who will end his decades-long association with the Australian pharmaceutical giant.

Naylor will take over as chair on March 31. He will hand over chief executive duties to Deborah Waterhouse in February.

McNamee, as CEO, helped steer the company’s transformation from a government agency into one of Australia’s largest listed companies, but it has struggled with a string of downgrades over the past year which sent its shares sharply lower. CSL shares ended the day down 1.6 per cent.

On Wall Street on Friday, the S&P 500 fell 0.5 per cent as the index posted its second-straight weekly loss. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.2 per cent, and the Nasdaq composite slipped 0.2 per cent after giving up a modest gain.

Several AI-related companies rallied, helping ease some of the impact from the broader market slide. Micron Technology rose 2.5 per cent, Intel gained 1.7 per cent, Microsoft added 0.7 per cent, and Advanced Micro Devices closed 1.7 per cent higher.

The tech sector’s influence is also capable of quickly switching over to a heavy weight. Stocks that have benefited from the frenzy over AI have been swinging over rising worries about their path ahead. Investors are worried about whether their high prices are justified. Many of those companies have been borrowing to fund investments in AI technology, and high interest rates are making those loans more expensive.

“Investors appear increasingly focused on the economics of AI, particularly the substantial upfront costs required to build AI infrastructure and the uncertainty around the timing of returns on those investments,” said Angelo Kourkafas, senior global strategist at Edward Jones, in a research note.

The latest round of corporate earnings is being closely watched to help get a better sense of whether those investments are paying off, or might pay off. Several influential AI-related companies report their latest financial results next week, including Google’s parent, Alphabet, along with Microsoft and Amazon.

Industrial companies had some of the biggest losses. Caterpillar fell 2.4 per cent.

American Express slumped 3.7 per cent, one of several financial stocks that lost ground, despite reporting profits for its most recent quarter that beat analysts’ forecasts. The company did announce its first major job cuts in roughly a decade.

JPMorganChase fell 1.5 per cent, Citigroup dropped 1.5 per cent and Capital One Financial slid 1 per cent.

While higher oil prices weighed down much of the market, they helped boost energy companies. Chevron rose 2.9 per cent and ConocoPhillips gained 3.6 per cent.

European markets closed mostly lower. France’s main index fell 0.4 per cent, though yields on French government bonds eased a bit.

Treasury yields in the US edged higher following sharp drops on Thursday. The yield on the 10-year Treasury edged up to 4.99 per cent from 4.96 per cent late on Thursday.

Yields have been rising globally, partly over worries about governments taking on too much debt. Inflation also remains a concern and is stubbornly well above 3 per cent in the US. That prompted the Federal Reserve to raise its benchmark interest rate at its last meeting in an effort to cool inflation.

Wall Street expects the US central bank to hold rates steady at its meeting next week, but is leaning heavily towards another rate hike in December.

Wall Street will also get several big economic updates next week, including the Fed’s preferred measure of inflation. The personal consumption expenditures index is expected to show that inflation rose at a 3.7 per cent rate in September, sharply above the Fed’s 2 per cent target.

For Michael Carrick, it is now a question of when, not if. When the axe falls, when he reverts from being head coach of Manchester United to a season-ticket holder, presumably despairing of the next manager, when he pays the price for a calamity of a start to this season.

“It wasn't a farewell from me,” he said. But it certainly looked that way as he walked down the tunnel, patting the badge on his club blazer, the Stretford End singing the song about him they coined when he was in the midfield, not the firing line. They showed sympathy, based on his 12 years and 464 games as a player, on the dignity he has brought to what looks the impossible job. They had chorused “Jason Wilcox is a w***er”. They had booed the United players at half-time and seconds after Carrick disappeared from view after the end.

It was an indication that responsibility stretches from the boardroom to the dressing room. But, as Bournemouth eviscerated United with embarrassing ease, their buoyant supporters in a corner of Old Trafford targeted Carrick. “Sacked in the morning,” came the predictable chant. Whether or not they have got the timing right, the outcome appears inevitable.

United are in meltdown. Their worst start to a campaign for 40 years has just got worse. Humiliated by Como, hammered by Bournemouth, they have produced successive shocking displays. On the day Carrick, the nice guy of the dugout, tried to show his tough side by dropping captain Bruno Fernandes, the players he did select were a hapless rabble.

That should not detract from the excellence of Bournemouth. If United’s is a chastening fall from grace, theirs is a wonderful rise. Remarkably, ridiculously, Bournemouth have three 3-0 wins at Old Trafford against three different United managers in their last four visits. For good measure, they have a 3-0 victory against Ruben Amorim this week, since his move to AC Milan.

In 2008, when a United team with Carrick in the midfield were champions of Europe, Bournemouth had a 17-point deduction in League Two. Now they have beaten clubs with a combined 10 European Cups in the last four days. For the second time in four days, their fans sang: “It’s happening again.”

It happened again to Carrick, too. This was another shambolic performance, United’s second in five days and fifth in a season that is only 12 matches old. Their winless run has extended to eight games. Their clueless run arguably stretches back for 13 years.

After Carrick came out fighting in his press conference on Friday, his team did not. They subsided meekly. Carrick had delivered a five-and-a-half minute monologue two days earlier. In five-and-a-half first-half minutes United conceded twice to condemn themselves to a defeat that left them in 16th.

The supporters backed Carrick. “That meant an awful lot,” he said. They chanted against the Glazers and against Wilcox, the sporting director who has put together this increasingly wretched team. As is his wont, Carrick defended his players. “There's a lot of the boys quite hurt,” he said. “It's not like they don't care. The ones I’ve spoken to, it’s affecting them a lot.”

But even in that, it was notable he had not talked to them all after the final whistle. And probably not to one in particular after the biggest call of his reign backfired. After Fernandes turned on Carrick in his post-match interview in Italy, the manager dispensed with his captain. Carrick called it a “pure football” decision but it felt more personal when Mason Mount and Youri Tielemans started instead of him and Matheus Cunha, Joshua Zirkzee and Shea Lacey were brought on ahead of him.

Fernandes was snubbed. “He’s fit and available,” confirmed Carrick. Without the reigning Footballer of the Year, United were terrible at football.

Take the one man out of the one-man team and United were even more abject. A team without goals, creativity or pace also had clear defensive deficiencies.

This was all too simple for Bournemouth. Carrick had told United’s players to learn from the club’s most famous Ryan – Giggs – on Friday. Instead, they conceded to Rayan, the Brazilian volleying in a free kick taken by Justin Kluivert and conceded by Ayden Heaven, with a ludicrous lunge at Evanilson.

Bryan Mbeumo almost equalised straight away, Djordje Petrovic making a fine save. Instead, United were soon breached again. Marcus Tavernier was left with a tap in when Adrien Truffert centred after Leny Yoro first dallied and then slipped. That was United, failing at the basics, making their own problems. Carrick argued Yoro was unlucky, Heaven too. That was being generous.

Evanilson scored the third, brilliantly, from an acute angle after Senne Lammens charged out of his box and missed the ball, which in turn came because Harry Maguire lacked the speed to chase Lewis Cook’s long ball. United lacked a forward of the dynamism of Evanilson. They could not halt Rayan, precisely the sort of player they ought to have signed in recent years.

But Bournemouth are far better at recruitment than United these days. Most clubs are. And Marco Rose, who had made a stuttering start after replacing Andoni Iraola, could instead take on the role of the magnanimous victor. “I feel with Michael, yes,” he said. The United fanbase do, too. But that might not be enough to save him.

Ukraine disputes Elon Musk’s claim that its military violated Starlink terms during drone operations

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A Ukrainian official disputed SpaceX CEO Elon Musk's claim that Ukraine's military violated Starlink's terms of service, prompting the company to restrict Ukraine's access to the high-speed satellite technology.

Serhii Beskrestnov, a defense technology advisor to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, wrote an online post on Sunday contesting Musk's reason for temporarily throttling Ukraine's access to high-speed service from SpaceX's Starlink satellite communication systems.

Ukrainian officials previously said the restrictions disrupted the country's military drone services and left the country vulnerable to Russian attacks.

Musk on Saturday claimed in a post on X that the "Ukraine military violated Starlink terms of service and used it for offensive drone strikes to kill people."

Beskrestnov pushed back on Musk's allegation, arguing that pre-existing restrictions on the systems would have made it "physically impossible" for Ukraine to use Starlink in the types of strikes the SpaceX CEO described.

"It is very strange that Elon Musk is unaware of the operational limitations of Starlink in Ukraine," Beskrestnov wrote in a post on Telegram, noting that Starlink's civilian system in Ukraine "does not function over the territory of Russia" and that Ukrainian drones operating with Starlink have had a speed restriction of 70 to 90 km/h (roughly 43 to 56 miles per hour) for months.

"Consequently, it is PHYSICALLY IMPOSSIBLE for us to use commercial Starlink for deep-strike or medium-range strike operations," Beskrestnov wrote.

Ukraine officials acknowledged on Saturday that high-speed service for SpaceX's Starlink and Starshield satellite communications systems had been restored. The country has blamed that restricted service for disrupting its drone air defense capabilities during frequent Russian air attacks.

Starshield is SpaceX's military-focused satellite network for government and national security use, built on technology developed for Starlink, the company's broader commercial satellite internet network.

Before Musk acknowledged that he had restricted the systems, confusion and speculation abounded over who ordered the high-speed service changes.

At one point, Musk wrote in a social media post that the U.S. government was behind the decision, though President Donald Trump claimed he had no knowledge of the decision. Ukrainian officials have also said Starlink or the U.S. had imposed the restrictions without warning, leaving the country blindsided.

Musk has not explained the reported lack of notification to Ukraine before his company implemented the restrictions.

Musk and SpaceX did not respond to CNBC's requests for comment.

The restrictions on Starlink and Starshield in war-torn Ukraine have driven Ukraine and its allies to seek or build alternative, "sovereign" satellite communication systems.

Poland's Deputy Minister of National Defense, Cezary Tomczyk, discussed his country's plans on a podcast called Influence Zone (Strefa Influenceów) earlier this month. Ukraine also announced partnerships with Norway and Finland's mobile giant Nokia, as well as satellite maker ICEYE, to develop alternatives.

Beyond Ukraine, several countries are also exploring sovereign systems. The European Union is building IRIS², a constellation of hundreds of satellites, but it doesn't expect its first launches until 2029.

A wide array of customers, including non-government entities, the governments of Poland and France, and the Pentagon, have paid SpaceX to provide its Starlink and Starshield hardware and wireless internet services to Ukraine.
