I saw a ghost on the baby monitor. For a year after my home was haunted by sudden chills, drawers torn open and a dark presence that focused on my sons. It was so terrifying our babysitter refused to come back. Here's how I banished it: CAPRICE BOURRET

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

As an American, I’ve always enjoyed Halloween. And, having spent half my life in the UK, I’ve watched it grow in popularity here too.

It falls very close to my birthday so I’ve often combined the celebrations. But it’s the dressing up and having fun aspect I like, not the spookiness! There’s a good reason for that – a very unwelcome spook that invaded my home and terrified me.

I’d recently given birth to twin sons back in 2013 and, like any new mum, my overwhelming instinct was to protect my precious babies.

So when, one evening, my husband Ty turned to me on the sofa and said: ‘Someone’s in the house… there’s an intruder, they’re in the boys’ room,’ my blood ran cold.

Ty and I were in the living room of our six-bedroom west London home watching TV while our sons Jax and Jett, then six months old, were upstairs in their cribs.

We had a video baby monitor and on it Ty and I could clearly see a female figure. This person was dressed in black and bending over and staring down at the children as they slept. In that instant I felt sheer panic, thinking my babies were about to be harmed or kidnapped.

We both leapt to our feet and sprinted to the nursery, but there was nobody there and the boys were fine. There was no sign of a break-in either and nothing was missing, and yet we both knew what we had seen.

Little did I know then that this was the beginning of a nightmare that was to take over my life for the best part of a year.

We’d just moved into our house following a huge renovation project. It’s a large Victorian building that used to be a very well-known nightclub, somewhere The Rolling Stones, David Bowie and a host of other rock stars had played.

I love knowing that, at one stage, some of the biggest names in music had performed in what became our lounge.

Before that it was a working men’s club, so it has a fascinating history.

We were able to buy the house partly thanks to success I’d had in the UK. I hit the headlines for wearing a daring dress to the 1996 National Television Awards. And over the next decade I appeared on more than 350 magazine covers as well as being voted the World’s Sexiest Woman.

The building is Grade II-listed and it took a long time to get all the necessary permissions to turn it into a residential home.

But property renovation and interior design is a passion of mine, so it was worth the wait.

By the time we moved in we had created this amazing space over four floors with beautiful light and we were so excited to make it our family home. However, within months I became so terrified by the ghostly presence that I didn’t think I’d be able to stay. Even though I never saw the figure in black again, I knew she was still around as I constantly sensed her.

On the baby monitor she was wearing a long, black dress and had a slim, feminine figure but I never saw a face, because it was covered by a veil.

Then other things started to happen, always at night and only ever in two areas – the boys’ bedroom and a nearby guest room, both on the top floor.

In these rooms I’d suddenly experience an intense chill, the kind that goes to your bones and makes you shiver, yet just a few feet away the room would feel warm.

We had underfloor heating and I called out three different engineers in case it had gone wrong, but they all said it was working just fine. The odd thing was that the babies’ cribs were always warm and toasty.

At night I’d hear movement upstairs, such as doors slamming, and sometimes I’d go up there to find drawers open that I knew had been left shut.

When I went into the two affected rooms everything felt ‘off’. There was a strange and heavy atmosphere.

Not long after the baby monitor incident, Ty and I went out to an event and got a babysitter in.

We weren’t gone for very long but arrived home to find the babysitter, a young woman, in the downstairs playroom crying and in real distress.

She looked terrified and told me there was ‘something’ in the house.

She said she could feel it on her, watching her.

She told me I needed to go and check the boys’ bedroom because she was too scared to go up there. Of course I raced up the stairs and they were fine and sound asleep but suffice to say that she refused to babysit for us again.

After that, I moved a bed into my sons’ room and slept there because I was so frightened for their safety.

I was imagining all these awful scenarios. What if this ghost started to flip things over and chuck objects around and injured the kids?

People will ask why I didn’t move the children into our room but by then I had a strong sense that the babies were her focus and if I moved them into our bedroom, where we never experienced anything strange, then trouble would follow.

Because I was scared and in this permanent state of heightened alert I wasn’t sleeping well and was constantly exhausted and on edge.

My husband, like a lot of men, is a logical thinker and can be a bit unemotional. He’d been the first one to see the ghost on the monitor but once he knew it wasn’t a real intruder he wasn’t concerned whereas I was quite the hysterical drama queen about it all.

He’d tell me: ‘Cap, whatever it is doesn’t seem to be hurting anyone, so just ignore it.’

I’d say: ‘Are you joking? OK, she hasn’t hurt us so far, but that could change!’

I’m a very spiritual person. I believe in fate and destiny. I’ve had psychic readings before and have been given messages from loved ones that have passed away, which I absolutely believe to be real.

I do believe in ghosts too – even though they terrify me – because I’ve encountered one before.

A friend of mine has a huge, old house in the country and one weekend 20 of us were staying there. We did a seance, where we tried to communicate with spirits – so I guess you could say we were asking for trouble.

The lights went out and the whole house was plunged into blackness and the room went cold. That was enough for me.

I said, ‘see you guys later, I’m out of here,’ and checked myself into a hotel. If it wasn’t for my children, I think I’d have moved into a hotel in London too but it would have been too unsettling for them.

The thing was, even though the ghost seemed obsessed with them, they were totally oblivious to what was happening – they slept well and were always happy and content.

After months of this – the cold rooms, slamming doors and this constant sense of something watching us – I decided to call in the ghostbusters.

Where do you start with something like that? In the end I just Googled it and took it from there. The first one came and she had this pendulum instrument that she walked around the house with. I didn’t tell her which rooms were affected because I wanted to see how good she was.

She walked around downstairs and all was calm and then she went into the guest room and I swear the pendulum started swinging manically.

It was alarming but I didn’t expect the ghostbuster to be scared. She freaked out and said ‘I’m leaving,’ and literally fled. I remember calling after her ‘hang on a minute! I paid you up front, you can’t just walk out!’

The second one came and wasn’t much better. She said she could feel a very aggressive presence. A rabbi also came and agreed there was a spirit living with us. Both claimed to have healed the house. But they hadn’t, our ghost was still there watching us and causing mischief. I imagined her laughing at them and me too, probably, for wasting my money.

Then, one night, after nearly a year of this, I had had enough.

I was sleep-deprived and at my wits’ end. I was in my sons’ room on my makeshift bed when that icy chill came over my part of the room again. I just sat up and pleaded out loud: ‘I cannot do this any more, you have to leave. You’re scaring me. I can’t sleep, I can’t cope, please leave us alone.

‘This isn’t your house, it’s ours and I don’t want you here. Just go.’

And yes, I know it sounds mad and people reading this will think I’m a crackpot, but it worked. There was a shift in energy almost immediately and the temperature went back to normal. The room suddenly felt as tranquil as the rest of the house. I was on tenterhooks for weeks after that, wondering if she’d reappear, but she never bothered us again.

I have no idea who she was or what she really wanted. Maybe she wanted to protect the boys and watch over them, but that was my job.

In the end, for all my messing around with ghostbusters, all it took was for her to be asked nicely to leave. My sons know all about it but, at 13 years old, they just think I’m their crazy mum telling stories and laugh at me.

Our London house is for sale now, nothing to do with the ghost – although I’m so relieved we got rid of her – but these days we mostly live in Ibiza and it’s where the boys go to school. I love England but I don’t see myself living in the UK full time again. London feels less safe, whereas in Ibiza we leave the doors open, the kids walk into town by themselves and scooter everywhere, it’s a wonderful carefree life for them.

Also, although I love Halloween, my professional career is now based around a jollier seasonal event: Christmas.

At 54, I’m focused on my movies. I’ve made six of them via my own production company JJLove Productions and we have a new film, An English Christmas Wish, which I star in and which will be on Channel 5 next month.

All my films are Christmas- and romance-themed and, yes, they stick to a formula and everyone knows the ending, but people love them.

They’re comforting, unlike scary ghost stories – real or otherwise.

I look back and think about my ‘haunting’ experience a lot. I’ve never doubted myself though, never wondered if I imagined it all because I was an exhausted new mother.

It was very real, very frightening and I hope nothing like that ever happens again.

My Ring Doorbell is sabotaging my relationship. The video description feature reported his infuriating habit - and my secrets have been caught out too. But our Ring keeps making false accusations, and I'm worried what neighbours will say

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The other night, while I was out on the razzle, a neighbourhood spy caught my partner – a reformed smoker – brazenly having a ciggie in front of our house.

The cheek of the man, waiting until I left to secretly indulge his vice.

Who was this co-conspirator? My Ring doorbell. Thanks to its new AI-enhanced ‘Video Descriptions’ service, which summarises the action on your doorstep as a line of text, my partner got stitched up like a kipper when Ring told me: ‘There is a man on the driveway smoking a cigarette.’

It pinged in alongside a thumbnail of the incriminating video featuring his easily identifiable hulk with the offending item in his mouth.

Enraged, and perhaps fuelled by one too many glasses of lady petrol, I fired off several accusatory texts. His defence? ‘I was just doing the recycling.’ Pah.

Then I actually bothered to watch the video, rather than just lazily depending on the description. He was right. One hundred per cent, humiliatingly right.

His arms juggling a green box overflowing with drained wine bottles and empty jars of overpriced chickpeas, he’d had no choice but to hold the front door key in his mouth.

A key that Ring’s AI, in all its wisdom, had decided was a ciggie.

Helen's Ring doorbell thought someone was smoking outside the house, when in reality it was her partner, sorting the recycling, with his front door key in his mouth

An embarrassed apology ensued, followed by cursing at Ring’s new feature.

It’s not the first time Video Descriptions has caused domestic drama chez nous.

My 17-year-old, all six-foot three-inches of him, was distinctly miffed when Ring described him as a child (‘A child is walking on the path’).

And I can no longer do a secret Co Op snack dash without being busted (‘A woman is approaching the front door carrying a snack’).

What’s galling is, I never even signed up for Video Descriptions. Like many Ring users, I had a month’s free trial foisted on me in the hope that I’d cough up for another hefty subscription. I didn’t.

Ring, which is owned by Amazon, often sells the doorbell itself for next to nothing.

On Prime Day you can grab one for as little as £39.99, a 75 per cent discount. Bargain! Until you realise the real spend is in the add-ons.

I bought my first Ring four years ago, after my urchin of a teen tried turning the house into a nightclub while I was on holiday. What I didn’t know was that, without a subscription, Ring is just a good old-fashioned doorbell with a camera stuck on it.

You can snoop in real time, but if you want to check retrospectively on whether that vital parcel has arrived, or if your teen was lying about getting home at midnight, you need, for £49.99 a year, a Ring Protect subscription.

In the last four years, I’ve handed over nearly £200 for the privilege of watching my own doorstep. I’m not alone. At least one in five Brits now own a smart doorbell, with Ring the dominant brand.

And in 2024, when Ring hiked its Protect subscription prices by 43 per cent, increasing the yearly fee from £34.99 to £49.99, most of us sucked it up.

At least one in five Brits now own a smart doorbell, with Ring the dominant brand

Not just because it’s handy for tracking deliveries but also because they create a sense of security. Albeit a false one given that Ring doorbells, as a sign of affluence, can make houses more attractive to burglars.

The biggest problem, however, is that some people – you know who you are – use smart doorbells for digital curtain twitching.

There’s a fine line between monitoring your parcels and snooping on the folks next door. If I wanted to, I could set my camera zone wide enough to spy on my neighbours’ doorstep as my own.

This potential for invading privacy is about to get more problematic. Last week Ring launched a feature called ‘Neighbours’ in the UK.

Billed as a ‘free, community-powered space’, Neighbours will be part of the Ring app that acts like a public forum and message board.

And since it will be open to every busybody in town, not just Ring owners as anyone can download the app for free, it will pose a threat to social media platforms such as Next Door and street-based WhatsApp groups. Which would be a shame because I love my local WhatsApp thread.

It’s a gem that’s helped find new homes for battered sofas and supplied no end of plumber recommendations.

And in the past month alone, we’ve had an update on a local fire, a plea for toiletries for a refugee charity, requests to check sheds for an AWOL moggie, a ‘Thank you’ for the glorious sunflower display outside Number 12 and, most bafflingly, a request to borrow a garter.

But it really comes into its own when public spats begin. Watching from the sidelines as fights break out over the Low Traffic Neighbourhood scheme, peskily parked Lime bikes and the controversial building development two streets away; it’s as juicy as an episode of The Traitors.

The neighbourhood WhatsApp group is a wondrous microcosm of British politeness, passive-aggression and eccentricity. Let Big Tech take over and we’ll lose something precious.

Worse still, Neighbours will allow anonymous posting. And this is where things could go horribly wrong.

Our WhatsApp group already goes into overdrive when unidentified ‘yoofs’ in hoodies walk the street after 9pm, despite the fact that, more often than not, it’s only a couple of innocent teenagers heading home after a ‘motive’ (meet-up) in the park.

On WhatsApp, the poster is a known and named neighbour who has to look you in the eye as you nod ‘Hello’ the next morning.

Strip away that accountability, add someone who’s got nothing else to do, some blurry camera footage and AI that can’t distinguish a key from a ciggie – and you have a recipe for all hell breaking loose. So no, Ring. We don’t need another platform for local trivia, and we don’t like crisp snitches either.

Yesterday, the father-in-law of the King's son was said by investigators to be a 'possible sex offender of the grossest kind'.

This astonishing claim from the interior minister of the Philippines came after authorities there began analysing digital images of child sexual abuse seized from the former home of the Duchess of Sussex's estranged father Thomas Markle.

The repercussions of this repulsive discovery – said to include an image of Markle engaging in sex with a girl who appears to be underage – were being felt 7,000 miles away as Buckingham Palace raced to find a coherent position on what is shaping up to be yet another highly damaging crisis for the monarchy.

On the face of it, this sordid episode should have no impact on the working life of the Royal Family, which will this week see King Charles and Queen Camilla embark on a sensitive tour of the Caribbean, which will take in the biennial Commonwealth Heads of Government meeting in Antigua and Barbuda.

As if that gathering was not already delicate enough, with the King facing demands for both an apology and reparations for the slave trade, the revelations from South-East Asia have added another level of complexity.

There has, understandably, been a surge of public sympathy for the King's daughter-in-law, who has disclosed that she finally broke off all contact with her father a year ago after reportedly telling him that she was aware of allegations he had abused young girls.

In a letter sent to Markle while he was in hospital in the Philippines having had a leg amputated, Meghan apparently made clear he would never see his grandchildren – Prince Archie, seven, and Princess Lilibet, five – and she would never contact him again.

The revelations concerning her father have prompted an outpouring of affection for her, particularly from mothers. By speaking out about her distress over the Met police's failure to act on the abuse claims when they were first raised, that swell of popular sympathy has only increased.

But for the monarchy this newfound goodwill represents both a danger and an opportunity.

Ever since their return to Britain in August, public interest in Prince Harry and Meghan has remained high, despite them making only a handful of public appearances. It is their mere presence on UK soil that is intriguing. Why did they come back? How long will they stay? Will they be reconciled with William and Kate?

The questions are as endless as they are fascinating. And the few quasi-royal engagements they have carried out have proved emphatically how much they can distract attention from the activities of the working Windsors.

In many ways it has meant that the Royal Family, whether they like it or not, are being judged by the activities of the two former working royals. With some notable exceptions, many of their headline-grabbing stunts during their 'Megxit' years in California could be ignored because of their physical distance from the UK and the time difference. But now they are based in the Cotswolds, it is a different story.

And for the royals it can feel too close for comfort.

So how then should the King and the rest of the family react to what is, after all, a clearly troubling but deeply personal issue?

If the public's perception of Meghan has shifted then, many will argue, why can't the royals' attitude change, too?

One reason, of course, relates to the issue of trust. From the Oprah Winfrey interview and the wildly over-hyped Netflix docuseries to Harry's tawdry memoir, Spare, the couple have demonstrated scant regard for family ties and royal obligations. Scores were settled, vitriol dished out and grievances aired on an unprecedented scale.

Was William, in short, upping his game because of his brother's presence?

Some in William's circle insist that nothing has changed. As disturbing as the claims swirling around Thomas Markle are, they do not turn the clock back. 'The Prince of Wales remains deeply hurt by the betrayal of him and Catherine in the book,' says one.

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting tells us we must be ready for war in 1,000 days. He’s thinking of a Russian attack. Scary stuff.

His warning comes days after he launched a ‘national resilience conversation,’ when he asked us to prepare for disruption caused by natural disasters or cyber attacks by hostile states. He said we should all have three days of emergency supplies.

The Government is trying to make the hairs stand up on the back of our necks. War with Russia in less than three years! That isn’t just serious. It is apocalyptic.

Why, then, doesn’t the Government prepare for the life-or-death struggles it claims lie ahead? Why does it concentrate on the trivial or the ridiculous – or both – instead of rearming our depleted Armed Forces so that we can defend ourselves?

The “fifth plenum” began on Monday and is set to focus on “major issues on advancing the party’s full and rigorous self-governance”, according to a Chinese state media. In party-speak, “governance” is a byword for discipline, loyalty and anti-corruption.

As Xi continues to crack down on corruption, the fact that investigations nearly always result in a conviction begs the question over whether targets are truly guilty of corruption, or are they just political rivals to the people in charge.

Kerry Brown, director of the Lau China Institute at King’s College London, explained that in Xi’s worldview, which is obsessed with discipline and loyalty, there may not be much difference between the two. “If you’re corrupt, you’re not politically loyal. He added that in the CCP system, “corruption is not just material things”, but also “spiritual corruption”, which includes political disloyalty.

Some analysts argue that the reason that the anti-graft drive is so enduring is because corruption is a feature rather than a bug of an authoritarian, top-down political system with a sprawling bureaucracy that governs 1.4 billion people.

Neil Thomas, a senior fellow at the Asia Society thinktank, said: “Xi has made the party less corrupt and strengthened his control, but he knows that tougher punishment cannot fully resolve the problem of officials putting their own interests ahead of the party’s.

The fifth plenum is the fifth of seven gatherings that normally happen in every five-year cycle between CCP congresses. The plenums bring together more than 300 members of the CCP’s Central Committee, the most senior officials in the party hierarchy.

The meeting is particularly significant because it is likely to be the last substantive meeting before next year’s 21st Party Congress, where a new Central Committee will be chosen, and where Xi is expected to be anointed for a fourth term as the head of the CCP.

Eliminating anyone who poses a risk to his authority or to the party’s credibility is a key priority for Xi. And potential threats are everywhere. Nearly 15 years after Xi took office, according to CCP proclamations and a host of recent investigations, the problem is still not stamped out. “It is a deeply ingrained part of [Xi’s] worldview,” said Brown. “Everyone else is corrupt”.

In the past, Xi has warned that the party could “lose its ruling status and inevitably be eliminated by history” if it could not get its house in order. “This is by no means an exaggeration,” he said.

The current focus on party governance comes amid a host of high-profile investigations against senior party and military leaders.

Geleceğin Türk hukukçuları uluslararası mahkemelerde tarihi duruşmaları izledi

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sanat, edebiyat ve iş dünyasının buluşma noktası olan, kamuoyunda "Kahve" adıyla bilinen Rindan ekibinin hamiliğinde hayata geçirilen projede, yazılı sınav ve mülakatlarla seçilen öğrenciler Strazburg, Lüksemburg, Paris ve Lahey'de, 1-10 Eylül'de saha çalışması yürüttü.

Toplam 40 kişilik öğrenci ve akademisyen heyeti, 12 haftalık teorik eğitimin ardından Avrupa'nın en önemli uluslararası yargı mercilerini ziyaret ederek tarihi nitelikteki duruşmaları yerinde izledi.

Avrupa İnsan Hakları Mahkemesi (AİHM), Avrupa Birliği (AB) Adalet Divanı, Uluslararası Adalet Divanı (UAD), Uluslararası Ceza Mahkemesi (UCM) ve Milletlerarası Ticaret Odası (ICC) gibi kurumları ziyaret eden öğrenciler çalışmaları yakından inceledi.

"Uluslararası Hukuk Yolculuğu Projesi"nin danışmanı avukat Kamil Uğur Yaralı, AA muhabirine yaptığı açıklamada, projenin temellerinin bürosunda gençlerle yürüttükleri hukuk ve edebiyat sohbetleri sırasında atıldığını söyledi.

Yaralı, öğrencileri, öğrendikleri teorik dersleri sahada uygulama imkanı bulmaları için AİHM, UAD, AB Adalet Divanı, UCM ile Uluslararası Tahkim gibi önemli yargı kurumlarına götürdüklerini belirterek, "Öğrenciler duruşmalara girdiler, yetkililerden brifingler aldılar. Özellikle teorik derslerin verimli geçtiğini, duruşmalarda ve temaslarda yönelttikleri sorulardan gayet iyi anlamış olduk." diye konuştu.

Öğrencilere seçim sürecinde İngilizce sınav ve mülakatların yapıldığını dile getiren Yaralı, interaktif derslerin yanında felsefe, mantık ve hukuki argümantasyon becerilerini artırmak adına kitap tahlilleri ve film kritikleri gibi disiplinler arası etkinliklere de yer verdiklerini anlattı.

Türk hukukçularının uluslararası alandaki görünürlüğünü artırmayı temel hedef olarak belirlediklerine dikkati çeken Yaralı, "Ana amacımız ve vizyonumuz, Türk hukukçuların uluslararası görünürlüğünü ve temsilini artırmak, nitelikli temsile katkıda bulunmak." dedi.

Yaralı, projenin edebiyat kahveleri geleneğinden gelen "Rindan" ekibi, Gençlik ve Spor Bakanlığı, Türk İşbirliği ve Koordinasyon Ajansı (TİKA) ile Diyanet İşleri Başkanlığının desteğiyle hayata geçirildiğini, çalışmayı kurumsal bir yapıya kavuşturarak diğer üniversitelerin katılımıyla sürdürmek istediklerini belirtti.

Projenin genel koordinatörü Boğaziçi Üniversitesi Hukuk Fakültesi Dr. Öğr. Üyesi Hasan Basri Bülbül, teorik eğitimin ardından öğrencilerin yargı mekanizmalarını bire bir tecrübe etmelerini hedeflediklerini söyledi.

Ziyaret takvimini özellikle UAD'de görülen kritik bir davaya göre ayarladıklarını belirten Bülbül, şöyle devam etti:

"Uluslararası Adalet Divanı bizim için oldukça önemliydi. Almanya’nın Filistin’de yaşanan soykırıma destek verdiği gerekçesiyle Nikaragua’nın açmış olduğu davanın ilk itirazlarının incelendiği duruşmaya öğrencilerimiz katılma imkanı buldu. Ancak mahkeme, 'Dinleyici locasına ilk gelen girer' kuralıyla dünyadan yalnızca 15 kişi kabul ediyordu. Rezervasyon imkanı yoktu. Öğrencilerimiz saat 10.00'daki duruşmaya girebilmek için sabah 05.00'te kalkıp mahkeme kapısında sıra bekledi. 3-4 gün boyunca peyderpey bu şekilde içeri girerek davayı takip ettiler."

Bülbül, Hollanda'nın Lahey kentinde bulunan UCM'de Sudan İç Savaşı'nda işlendiği öne sürülen suçlara ilişkin yargılamayı da yerinde takip ettiklerini aktararak, "Sadece mahkemelerin binalarını ziyaret edip kuru bilgiler almayı amaçlamadık. Aynı zamanda bu mahkemelerde yaşanan süreçlerin öğrenciler tarafından gerçekten tecrübe edilmesini istedik." dedi.

Kosova'nın eski Cumhurbaşkanı Haşim Taçi'nin yargılandığı mühim davanın son duruşmalarını da takip ettiklerini anlatan Bülbül, "Öğrenciler de bu noktada uluslararası yargı mekanizmalarının, uluslararası adalet sisteminin nasıl işlediğine dair bir fikir edinme imkanı buldu." diye konuştu.

İstanbul Üniversitesi Hukuk Fakültesi öğrencisi Ahmet Doruk Akbaş, projenin Türkiye'nin uluslararası kurumlardaki temsilini artırma hedefine hizmet ettiğini söyledi.

Akbaş, "Projeyle Türkiye'nin uluslararası yargı organlarında, uluslararası savunma mekanizmalarında ve uluslararası örgütlerdeki varlığına hizmet edebilecek, buralarda bir kariyer inşa etmemizi sağlayacak çok iyi fırsatlar, eğitimler ve deneyimler elde etmiş olduk." ifadelerini kullandı.

Boğaziçi Üniversitesi Hukuk Fakültesi öğrencisi Yuşa Özölçer, 12 haftalık eğitimde ders aldıkları Jose Argüello Gomez'i Uluslararası Adalet Divanı'nda Nikaragua adına savunma yaparken görmenin son derece etkileyici olduğunu dile getirdi.

Özölçer, yurt dışında Türkiye'den mezun olan hukukçuların temsil oranlarının çok düşük olduğunu belirterek, "Bunları yerinde görerek kendimize ilham almak, ileride bizim de bu konumlara gelebileceğimize dair inancımızı artırdı." dedi.

Marmara Üniversitesi Hukuk Fakültesi öğrencisi Mehmet Eren Kozalı, eğitimin interaktif yapısına dikkati çekerek, "Uzak görülen o uluslararası kurumların hiç de öyle olmadığının farkına vardık. Süreç bizim için ulaşılabilir oldu. Divanda tarafların yürüttükleri savunma biçimleri çok öğreticiydi." diye konuştu.

İstanbul Üniversitesi Hukuk Fakültesi mezunu olan, Londra Üniversitesi'nde uluslararası hukuk alanında yüksek lisans eğitimini sürdüren Nurefşan Akbaş ise projenin temellerinin atıldığı 2021 yılındaki ilk katılımcılardan biri olduğunu belirtti.

Akbaş, "Bu gezi bizim için bir hayal gibiydi, yıllar sonra tamamlanması beni çok mutlu etti. Derslerde gördüğümüz mahkemeleri bizzat ziyaret etmek, özellikle Nikaragua/Almanya duruşmasına katılmak uluslararası hukuk alanında ilerlemek isteyen biri olarak çok değerliydi." ifadelerini kullandı.

İstanbul Üniversitesi Hukuk Fakültesi'nde yüksek lisans eğitimini sürdüren Muhammed Yusuf Kaba da "Sadece hukuki bir gezi değil, kültür ve sanat anlamında da dolu dolu bir süreçti. Şehirler ve kurumlar üzerine otobüs yolculuklarında birbirimize sunumlar yaptık. Son derece doyurucu bir etkinlik oldu." değerlendirmesini yaptı.
