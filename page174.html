Golimbeo gana el Campeonato de España de Repostería en Badajoz

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

El Campeonato de España de Repostería ha cerrado su edición con Golimbeo como primer clasificado. El obrador artesano de Los Santos de Maimona, en Badajoz, se ha llevado el primer premio en una competición integrada en el XII Campeonato de España de Cocina y Repostería. La cita se ha celebrado en IFEBA, dentro de la III Feria Agroalimentaria Espiga, y ha estado organizada por FACYRE y ATUGABA. Hasta allí han viajado equipos de distintas comunidades autónomas para medirse en las modalidades de cocina y repostería.

El triunfo ha correspondido a los hermanos Abel y Juan Manuel Castaño, responsables de Golimbeo, que han presentado una propuesta dulce construida sobre tres ingredientes obligatorios de la tierra: higo, uva y miel. La elaboración ha convencido a un jurado que no solo ha mirado el resultado final, sino también el respeto por el producto, la originalidad y el aprovechamiento de los ingredientes. La victoria deja el máximo galardón de repostería en Extremadura y sitúa a Los Santos de Maimona en el primer puesto del certamen nacional.

La propuesta ganadora ha sido una de las más atendidas de la jornada. Los hermanos Castaño han integrado en su postre higo, uva y miel, tres productos muy vinculados a Extremadura. El jurado ha valorado el sabor, la presentación y la originalidad, además de la sostenibilidad, el aprovechamiento de los ingredientes, la limpieza y la profesionalidad durante el trabajo en cocina. Esos criterios han sido decisivos para que el primer premio se quedara en la región anfitriona.

El podio de repostería se ha completado con Madrid en segunda posición y La Rioja en tercera. En la categoría de cocina, el primer puesto ha viajado hasta La Rioja de la mano de Cristian Solana e Igor González. Los anfitriones de ATUGABA, con Antonio Falcón y Ezequiel Euliarte, se han quedado con la plata, mientras que Castilla y León, representada por Sara Cámara y Javier Fuentes, ha logrado el bronce. Extremadura ha firmado una actuación notable en el conjunto del campeonato, aunque el título dulce se ha quedado en casa.

La organización ha puesto el foco en la diversidad gastronómica española y en el papel del producto autóctono. En las pruebas de cocina, los participantes tuvieron que trabajar con ingredientes como la tenca y el jabalí, mientras que en repostería el higo, la uva y la miel marcaron el camino. Esa elección de productos obligatorios buscaba que los profesionales demostraran su capacidad de adaptación y su creatividad con materias primas muy concretas, algunas de ellas poco habituales fuera de su territorio.

La competición se ha desarrollado los días 19 y 20 de octubre en las instalaciones de IFEBA, en Badajoz, en el marco de la III Feria Agroalimentaria Espiga. La coincidencia con esta feria ha permitido que el campeonato tenga una dimensión más amplia y que el público y los profesionales del sector puedan conocer de cerca el nivel de la cocina y la repostería que se hace en España.

Desde la organización se ha insistido en que el certamen no se limita a repartir premios. También pretende impulsar el talento culinario, poner en valor el producto de cada territorio y abrir nuevas oportunidades de visibilidad para los profesionales. En ese sentido, el resultado de Golimbeo resulta significativo: un obrador de repostería artesana de una localidad pequeña ha logrado imponerse en una competición en la que participaban representantes de distintas comunidades autónomas y en la que el nivel técnico ha sido exigente.

La entrega de galardones estuvo marcada por la emoción de los vencedores. Abel Castaño ha explicado que este reconocimiento supone cumplir un sueño y ha recordado el trabajo diario que hay detrás de un premio así. Juan Manuel Castaño ha subrayado el orgullo de haber ganado en su propia tierra, delante de un público que ha seguido con atención el desarrollo de las pruebas. Sus palabras han dejado claro que el triunfo va más allá de lo deportivo y que también tiene una lectura emocional para la repostería extremeña.

El campeonato ha reunido a cocineros y reposteros con trayectorias muy distintas, pero con un objetivo común: mostrar su mejor versión ante un jurado que ha valorado tanto el sabor como la presentación, la originalidad y la limpieza durante el trabajo. Esa exigencia se ha traducido en platos muy cuidados, en los que la técnica y el producto han ido de la mano. La competición, por tanto, ha vuelto a demostrar que la gastronomía española mantiene un nivel alto y que la repostería está ganando peso dentro de los certámenes nacionales.

La edición se cierra con Golimbeo como primer nombre de la repostería, Madrid y La Rioja en el podio dulce y una Extremadura que ha tenido presencia destacada en el conjunto del campeonato. Badajoz ha ejercido de anfitriona en una cita que ha combinado competición, producto y promoción del talento local. El resultado deja una foto clara: la pastelería artesana de una localidad pacense ha logrado el primer puesto nacional y lo ha hecho con una propuesta que habla de su tierra, de sus ingredientes y del trabajo que hay detrás de cada elaboración.

Nach 150 Jahren – Aus für Pferdekutschen im Central Park in New York

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Der New Yorker Stadtrat stimmte für ein Gesetz, das die Attraktion schrittweise bis zum 1. Juni 2028 abschaffen wird.

Warum sollen die Kutschfahrten verboten werden? Vor allem Tierschützer fordern schon lange ein Ende. Sie kritisieren, dass die Pferde überarbeitet und schlecht untergebracht seien, und dass sie leicht in Panik gerieten. Die Betreiber weisen die Vorwürfe zurück – und forderten mehr Regulierungen anstelle eines Verbots. Viele von ihnen argumentieren, die Immobilienbranche stecke hinter den Verbotsinitiativen, weil sie darauf hoffe, die hauptsächlich an der Westseite Manhattans gelegenen Ställe lukrativ umbauen zu können.

Wie kam das Gesetz zustande? Der New Yorker Stadtrat hat das Gesetz verabschiedet, das die Kutschfahrten abschaffen wird. Auf den Gesetzentwurf hatten sich Bürgermeister Zohran Mamdani und die Vorsitzende des New Yorker Stadtrats, Julie Menin, geeinigt. Ein Gesundheitsausschuss des Stadtrats hatte dem Papier zuvor ebenfalls zugestimmt.

Was sagt der US-Präsident dazu? Sogar US-Präsident Donald Trump mischt sich in die Diskussion ein. Das Ende der «fundamentalen New-York-Erfahrung» sei «so traurig», schreibt der in der Millionenmetropole geborene Trump bei seinem Online-Sprachrohr Truth Social. «Es war eine grossartige Tradition. Ein weiteres Stück Geschichte, das verschwindet. Es wird vermisst werden.»

Warum kommt die Verbotsinitiative gerade jetzt? Über das Thema wird in New York schon seit vielen Jahren gestritten. Immer wieder wurden Verbotsinitiativen angekündigt – beispielsweise von Mamdanis Vorvorgänger Bill de Blasio. Aber die Initiativen versandeten – und kochten immer dann wieder hoch, wenn es zu Unfällen mit den Pferden kam. Im Juni gab es dann einen besonders dramatischen Unfall: Ein 18-jähriger Tourist aus Indien, der mit seiner Familie in New York zu Besuch war, kam ums Leben. Er war seiner Mutter aus einer Kutsche hinterhergesprungen, nachdem sie herausgeschleudert worden war. Nach dem Unfall nahm der Druck deutlich zu, die Fahrten zu verbieten.

Wie soll es für die Kutscher weitergehen? Seit rund 150 Jahren rollen Kutschen durch den berühmten Central Park. Seine Regierung wolle nun dafür sorgen, dass die Rechte der Kutscher, Betreiber und Tiere geschützt werden, versprach Bürgermeister Mamdani – liess Details aber offen. Möglicherweise könnten einige der Kutscher künftig Gäste in speziellen Rikschas durch den Park fahren, schlug Stadtratsvorsitzende Menin vor.

Powerful earthquake hits Tibet killing 16 people, reports say

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A magnitude 7.0 earthquake has hit a mountainous region in Tibet, killing at least 16 people and injuring dozens, according to Chinese state media.

The centre of the quake hit BaqÃªn County in the Nagqu region on Thursday night, Xinhua reported, citing the local emergency management bureau.

The county - located in what China calls the Tibet Autonomous Region after annexing it in the 1950s - is 400km (250 miles) north-east of the region's capital, Lhasa.

Authorities have declared the incident an emergency and deployed rescue teams to the affected area, where 65 others have been injured, including 11 people with severe injuries.

The electricity supply has been cut off and 339 homes have collapsed in the affected region, though by Friday morning there were reports of power restored in some areas.

Local media said more than 12,400 people had been relocated following the earthquake.

Rockfall has forced roads in the region to close, making it more difficult for crews to reach impacted high-altitude areas.

The US Geological Survey (USGS) has recorded seven tremors close to the epicentre, all of which are located in the mountainous region. The largest tremor was recorded at 5.2 magnitude at a depth of 10km.

Shallow earthquakes and tremors closer to the Earth's surface are more damaging than deep quakes.

Rescuers search through rubble after Tibet earthquake

Several survivors of the earthquake have told local media about their harrowing escape.

Nagqu resident Yixi Cuomu told Tibet Daily that she was asleep in her bed when her house started shaking violently. Realising it was an earthquake, she headed outside barefoot, her "legs trembling uncontrollably" as she descended the staircase in her home.

Another eyewitness said the shaking was so violent "that in that moment I felt as if several hundred yaks were running past us".

Residents chose to spend the night outdoors in subzero temperatures, seeking shelter in tents or their cars, as they feared aftershocks could trap them in their homes, according to the news outlet.

Chinese state media reported on Friday morning that more than 400 aftershocks had been registered since the quake occurred.

China's Premier Li Qiang has called for "urgent efforts" to organise rescue operations and medical treatment for the injured, and relocate and resettle affected residents appropriately, according to state media.

Li noted that the earthquake-hit area is at a high altitude, around 4,800m (15,750ft), and prone to aftershocks.

The Ministry of Emergency Management has dispatched a working group to the affected area to guide rescue and disaster relief operations.

Separately, the Chinese government announced it was allocating 50 million yuan (Â£5.6m; $7.5m) for disaster relief efforts in Tibet.

Videos on social media of the moment the quake struck show villagers running into the streets as buildings shake violently in the dark. These have not been independently verified.

The region is prone to devastating seismic activity.

In 2023, a 6.2 magnitude quake killed more than 120 people in the nearby mountainous Gansu province of China.

Tibet and Nepal were also recently devastated by massive floods and landslides less than two months ago, unleashed by a glacier collapse, which killed at least 1,400 people. Hundreds of others are still reported missing.

Despite the region presenting some of the most challenging rescue conditions, rescue teams on the Tibetan plateau are highly experienced and have shown an ability to mobilise quickly.

In January 2025, military personnel, firefighters, police and specialist rescue teams were in the area within hours after a powerful eartquake struck the Shigatse region of Tibet.

The Chinese military also have drones to help survey the area.

Tibet, which has long demanded greater autonomy, is one of the most tightly controlled regions in China.

With foreign media restricted from the region and information heavily censored by authorities, Chinese state media is the main source for updates and details when such disasters happen.

Blancpain recupera uno de sus relojes más asombrosos

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

La manufactura suiza recupera un reloj femenino de los años cincuenta con una caja similar a una esfera. Dentro late un movimiento automático de pequeño diámetro, fabricado por la propia firma.

Siempre hablamos de la forma de los relojes en un plano bidimensional. Los hay redondos, cuadrados y en tonel, por solo nombrar algunas de las más conocidas. Lo que no es para nada habitual es encontrarnos con un reloj esférico. Y eso es esta curiosa maravilla que responde al nombre de Lady B: una pequeña bola capaz de indicar el tiempo.

Tan increíble como descubrir que este modelo tiene la friolera de 70 años. Conocido en su primera época como Bullet, esta creación fue la primera referencia de Ladybird, la colección femenina más icónica de Blancpain. La historia de este reloj es tan fascinante como su forma.

En la época de su lanzamiento, Blancpain era la única firma suiza dirigida por una mujer. Betty Fiechter fue todo un personaje desde luego. Nacida en 1896, entró a trabajar en Blancpain con apenas 16 años y con 20 más tuvo la oportunidad de adquirirla después del fallecimiento del último descendiente de la familia fundadora. Fiechter no solo logró superar la gran crisis mundial de los años 30, sino que fue capaz de devolver a Blancpain su prestigio pasado durante la década de los 50 con creaciones como el legendario Fifty Fathoms. También demostró tener un ojo especial a la hora de crear Ladybird, una colección exclusiva para la mujer, en una época en la que pocas casas se atrevían a apostar de un modo tan fuerte por el mercado femenino.

Bullet, su primera pieza, se inspiró en las corrientes racionalistas del momento que querían romper con la estética costumbrista anterior a la II Guerra Mundial. Un reloj así no hubiese sido posible desarrollarlo de no contar con la capacidad técnica de su firma para fabricar el R55, el movimiento mecánico redondo más pequeño del mundo, con unos asombrosos 11,85 milímetros de diámetro, título que aún conserva.

El nuevo Lady B es una reinterpretación de aquel Ladybird Bullet. El reloj tiene un diámetro de 19 milímetros y un grosor de 13,2. Su volumen apenas ocupa 2,9 centímetros cúbicos. La caja está dividida en dos partes, con la superior diseñada para incorporar el movimiento en sus escuetas dimensiones. Se ha escogido en esta ocasión el 615, también manufacturado por Blancpain, que es ligeramente más grande que el R55 (19 milímetros de diámetro), pero con la ventaja de ser automático.

La parte inferior del reloj es plana, para que sea más cómodo de llevar, e incorpora un hueco por el que cruzar la correa de doble vuelta en piel de becerro, disponible en siete colores: blanco, beige claro y oscuro, burdeos, amarillo, fucsia y azul clásico. El modelo está disponible tanto en versión acero, desde 10.950 euros, como en oro rosa, 16.350 euros.

Habitación con las mejores vistas de Barcelona y a estrenar

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

El rascacielos de la Barceloneta se halla inmerso en una reforma total. Sus 400 habitaciones y 67 suites ya están renovadas, convertidas en espacios de calma para el huésped. Ahora le toca el turno a las zonas comunes.

Cuando en 2009 se inauguró este icónico rascacielos fue algo realmente rompedor. Un edificio ideado por el arquitecto Ricardo Bofill con forma de vela de 99 metros de altura en plena Barceloneta casi inmerso en el mar. De hecho las vistas de algunas de las habitaciones dan la impresión al huésped de estar realmente metidos en el Mediterráneo.

Pero los años pasan, las modas también, y la competencia entre los cinco estrellas en la ciudad catalana no da tregua. En los últimos años, y meses, han llegado a Barcelona marcas como Bless, METT, SLS, The Hoxton... Meliá ha abierto Torre Melina Gran Meliá y ME Meliá; el Hotel Sofía se convirtió en Grand Hyatt, a lo que sumar la renovación integral del Hotel Arts en el Puerto Olímpico, el Majestic en Paseo de Gracia...

Tocaba ponerse manos a la obra para renovar materiales y concepto, y eso es lo que ha hecho este cinco estrellas propiedad del fondo soberano Qatari Diar, que gestiona Marriott International bajo la marca W. La reforma empezó hace dos años en el espacio panorámico de la planta 26, que ahora cuenta con zona de barra, restaurante y club nocturno. Después le llegó el turno a las 400 habitaciones y 67 suites, que se reparten entre las plantas 7 y 25 del edificio.

El proyecto del nuevo interiorismo lo firma el estudio Rockwell Group, con experiencia en otros establecimientos de la marca. Lo que más llama la atención es que aquellos vanguardistas tonos azules, turquesas, rojos, metalizados, han dado paso a otros mucho más sosegados blancos, arenas, ocres, amarillos y melocotón que buscan una "experiencia serena" para el huésped, un espacio de calma que invita a desconectar y a disfrutar de las vistas, que a través de los ventanales de suelo a techo siguen siendo igual de espectaculares. Los materiales (piedra natural, madera clara...) ayudan igualmente a crear una atmósfera acogedora en la que el mobiliario también acompaña con piezas de formas orgánicas en sofás, mesas, butacas, lámparas, alfombras...

Las obras de renovación van a continuar este otoño en las zonas comunes del hotel, y está previsto que concluyan en la primavera de 2027.

GTA cities are missing climate targets. Could the election change things?

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Rajpreet Sahota has been reporting on provincial environmental policy in Ontario with a focus on how it impacts racialized communities. She is based in Toronto.

In 2019, municipalities across Ontario declared a climate emergency. The backdrop was a groundswell of attention to global warming, including tens of thousands of people filling the streets around Ontario’s legislature in September, demanding urgent action on climate change.

The demonstration was part of the Global Week for Future, inspired by Swedish climate activist Greta Thunberg’s Fridays for Future movement. Young people marched alongside families, seniors and environmental advocates. The crowd outside Queen’s Park in Toronto was so large that some demonstrators couldn’t hear the speakers addressing the rally.

Lyn Adamson, co-chair of the environmental advocacy group ClimateFast, spent months helping organize the demonstration. “It was something I’d never seen,” she said. “I’d never seen that kind of mobilization.”

Mississauga and Brampton had declared an emergency in June, while Toronto City Council joined the list the week after the march. In the country’s largest city, council called for accelerated action to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and asked city staff to examine how Toronto could reach net-zero emissions by 2040.

Seven years later, municipalities across the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area have introduced new climate policies, invested in energy-efficient buildings and expanded programs aimed at reducing emissions. But despite that progress, emissions are not falling quickly enough to meet many of their targets.

With Ontario’s municipal election approaching on Oct. 26, environmental advocates say the next four years will be critical in determining whether cities can turn their commitments into measurable results. And the term will take municipalities to 2030, a deadline attached to several major greenhouse gas reduction targets.

In Toronto, decisions about building emissions standards and retrofits, and transportation investments remain outstanding. Mississauga’s next council is expected to consider an updated emissions reduction plan in early 2027 and Brampton faces questions about how much progress it is actually making on its climate commitments.

And progress across the province has been slowed by the Ontario government stepping in to prevent cities and towns from instituting ambitious strategies it said could impede development.

Here’s a look at some of the commitments and progress made by municipalities, as they welcome the next municipal council to take the helm.

Greenhouse gas emissions across the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area represent 46 per cent of Ontario’s emissions. They reached approximately 54.7 million tonnes in 2024, a modest increase of 1.1 per cent from the previous year, according to the latest carbon emissions inventory from the Toronto Atmospheric Fund.

Buildings accounted for 45 per cent of regional emissions, largely from natural gas used for heating and electricity generation. Transportation made up another 37 per cent.

Overall emissions remain far from where they need to be to reach net-zero targets that were developed in line with the United Nations and global scientists to stave off some of the worst effects of climate change.

The Atmospheric Fund, an agency of the City of Toronto, estimates the region would need reductions of approximately 11 per cent every year to meet its collective 2030 climate targets.

Gabriella Kalapos, executive director of Climate Action Partnership, said municipalities have made progress since 2019, particularly in understanding where emissions come from and identifying opportunities to reduce them. “It’s nowhere near the level of action that’s actually needed to respond to this climate emergency,” she said.

For Adamson, Toronto’s climate commitments aren’t just about reducing greenhouse gas emissions. They’re about making everyday life easier for residents, whether that’s because cycling is safe, public transit is reliable or utility bills are lower because homes use less energy.

Under its TransformTO climate strategy, the city has set a target of reducing community-wide emissions by 65 per cent below 1990 levels by 2030, and ultimately reach net-zero emissions by 2040.

Toronto acknowledges it is not on track to meet that goal. In 2023, the city released its most recent inventory of greenhouse gas emissions, finding community-wide emissions were just 35 per cent below 1990 levels. Buildings accounted for 55 per cent of emissions, followed by transportation at 36 per cent.

In an email to The Narwhal, James Nowlan, executive director of City of Toronto’s environment, climate and forestry division, wrote that buildings and transportation remain among the most challenging sources of emissions to address because many of the necessary changes extend beyond the city’s direct control.

Take the city’s multi-year battle with the provincial government to maintain some existing bike lanes and build more on main arteries of the city. Right now, the province is winning, having dispatched crews on a $48-million job to rip lanes out of major streets in the middle of the election campaign.

Premier Doug Ford’s government has also clamped down on how municipalities can implement green building standards. It restricted municipalities from mandating any green building features that go beyond provincial requirements. This put the Toronto Green Standard, a framework that has shaped development in Canada’s largest city for more than 15 years, in the crosshairs.

The standard required developers to incorporate features — such as bicycle parking, electric-vehicle charging stations and green roofs — to reduce emissions and improve resilience to climate change. The province has argued that standardized development requirements can help speed up construction during a housing crisis.

Environmental advocates, however, say the changes undermine municipal authority, including the ability to address local climate priorities. Kalapos said municipal progress on energy-efficient development policies has been complicated by provincial intervention.

“If you don’t have a supportive framework for climate action at the provincial and federal level, municipalities are really hamstrung to be able to move forward on their targets,” she said. “We are not going to meet this climate change imperative off the property tax base.”

The city is still developing emissions standards that would see building owners gradually reduce emissions through upgrades and retrofits. But provincial legislation will prevent Toronto from making the changes mandatory.

“It’s all voluntary. There is no deadline for anything,” Adamson said, arguing that relying on voluntary measures makes it difficult to achieve the scale of change needed.

Still, cities are trying. In Toronto, a new TransformTO action plan outlines further measures to reduce emissions between now and 2030, including support for community-led initiatives.

The city budgeted more than $2 billion in 2026 for emissions-reduction initiatives, including ongoing support for a bike share program and household composting.

In neighbouring Mississauga, the city reports progress on 59 of the 89 actions outlined in its 2019 climate change action plan, a 10-year municipal roadmap aimed at reducing emissions by 40 per cent by 2030 and 80 per cent by 2050. The plan was adopted months after council declared a climate emergency in June 2019.

Only 13 of the actions are considered fully complete, standalone actions, such as developing a program that diverts dog waste from landfills and converts it to energy and a strategy on increasing urban agriculture. Another 46 items, like gradually replacing municipal vehicles with electric alternatives, are mostly complete, with work ongoing and possibly evolving.

Carrah Bullock, Mississauga’s supervisor of climate policy, said the original plan was designed to lay the groundwork for future climate action, but did not include ways to measure its direct impact on emissions. The city is now updating its climate plan to better track the reductions associated with individual initiatives.

Despite this progress, Mississauga acknowledges, like its neighbouring cities, that it is not on track to meet its 2030 emissions targets. In fact, preliminary 2025 data suggest that emissions may have been higher than 2024, due largely to issues that, once again, are in the provincial government’s hands.

Ontario’s power grid has become less clean since 2021, when it was 94 per cent emissions-free. That number is now below 90 per cent, due largely to nuclear plants being taken offline for refurbishment. Having cancelled hundreds of renewable energy projects upon taking office in 2018, the Ford government has relied mainly on natural gas to replace them. The Atmospheric Fund reported that electricity-related emissions increased 28 per cent across the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area in 2024 because of greater reliance on natural gas-fired generation.

The city has made progress on emissions reductions within its own buildings and vehicle fleet. Dianne Zimmerman, Mississauga’s environment manager, pointed to the introduction of a green standard for city-owned buildings, including a new fire station built to a net-zero standard. The city has also electrified municipal vehicles and equipment.

But reducing emissions from privately owned homes and commercial buildings remains a significant challenge. Bullock said a proposed community building retrofit program has been progressing more slowly than the city would like.

The city’s updated emissions reduction plan is expected to go before council in early 2027, followed by a separate climate resilience strategy focused on coping with extreme heat, storms and other effects of a warming planet.

David Laing, president and co-founder of the Brampton Environmental Alliance, regularly uses a bicycle for transportation. When Brampton began introducing cycling infrastructure in the years after declaring a climate emergency in 2019, he noticed the difference.

The improvements, he said, “made my life an awful lot easier and safer and more comfortable.”

But now, the city is reversing course, removing some of those bike lanes to make way for cars — such as on Howden Boulevard in the city’s Westgate neighbourhood — leaving him frustrated with what he sees as inconsistent decisions about transportation and climate action.

According to The Atmospheric Fund, transportation produced approximately 1.81 million tonnes of greenhouse gas emissions in Brampton in 2024, accounting for nearly half the city’s total.

It’s one example of why the city’s progress on community-wide emissions has been in question.

In 2019, Brampton released its community energy and emissions reduction plan, aiming to cut community-wide emissions by 50 per cent below 2016 levels by 2041, and at least 80 per cent by 2050.

Since then, it has taken steps to electrify public transit, including a $4-billion partnership in 2025 with U.K.-based company Zenobē to transition to as many as 1,000 electric buses over the next decade. And the city has developed an updated environmental master plan, subsidized building retrofits and launched a program to help local businesses reduce their energy use.

But the results of those efforts haven’t been published. Laing said the city needs to make it easier for residents to understand which climate commitments have been completed, how much money has been spent and whether emissions are actually declining.

Without that information, there’s little to keep city administration — and incoming mayors and councillors — accountable.

Will bystander economies be able to finance their governments?

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Will AI poison the public finances of many modern states? There is a real risk that it will.

Yesterday, I circulated a new paper, “Saving for the Machines,” which makes a simple point. Building the AI transformation will require enormous investment. The only way to induce agents to save for that investment is through a large increase in the real interest rate. For instance, in my benchmark scenario, the ten-year real yield jumps from 5.1% to 13.6% on impact.

But that means government borrowing will become much more expensive. Here is a back-of-the-envelope calculation. Take a government that owes 100% of GDP in 2025 (a typical case in Western Europe) and runs a balanced primary budget from then on (not so typical but never mind; I want to build an optimistic scenario). That means the debt ratio changes only with the gap between the real interest rate and growth.

I input the interest rate and the growth rate for my benchmark transition to full automation, and I roll over the debt at the short rate. I consider two economies. The first economy is an AI adopter, for example, the U.S., which experiences an AI boom at home. The government pays the new interest rate but also enjoys faster growth.

The second economy is a bystander, for example, Spain. This economy lives in an integrated financial market but does not adopt AI (this is an extreme case of zero adoption, just to make the exercise transparent): it pays the same world interest rate and continues growing at its old 1.6% trend.

Let’s first consider the adopter. With an intertemporal elasticity of substitution of one-half, the Euler equation implies that consumption growth is about half the real interest rate. Hence, the gap between the interest rate and growth does not close during the AI-driven growth boom. Instead, it widens. For example, with a unit elasticity of substitution among tasks, the real rate rises from 5.1% to 42% by 2040, while growth increases only from 1.6% to 22%. Debt doubles by 2038, reaches 296% of GDP in 2040, and 907% in 2045. To hold the ratio at 100%, the government needs a primary surplus of 10% of GDP in 2035 and 24% from 2045 onward. With a low elasticity of substitution of 0.2, the numbers are milder, but the pattern is the same: 204% in 2040, 397% in 2045, and a stabilizing surplus of 13% of GDP.

But the adopter has a way out. The capital share goes to one, so net capital income is about 88% of GDP, and a flat tax on it can cover the surplus. A 36% tax on net capital income holds the debt at 100% of GDP in the limit with unit elasticity, and a 20% tax does so with strong complementarity across tasks. The tax is above the gap because it slows growth, from 21.9% to 13.6% with unit elasticity and from 11.7% to 9.1% with an elasticity of 0.2, while it does nothing to the rate the Treasury pays, which technology pins at the machines’ net return. Painful, but the boom survives and the adopter can pay its debt.

The grim news is that the bystander economy has no way out. Its capital income is still a third of GDP, which grows at 1.6%, and the surplus it needs is 11% of GDP in 2030, 26% in 2035, and 44% forever with unit elasticity (18% and 24% with an elasticity of 0.2). On a balanced primary budget, its debt doubles by 2033 and triples by 2035. No tax system raises that.

Two more bad pieces of news for the bystander. First, long yields jump on impact, so a Treasury that issues ten-year debt pays more, and sooner, than rolling over at the short rate assumes: it is the same bill but front-loaded. Second, the bystander’s own capital flows to the machines abroad, so its GDP and tax base fall below trend. One relief is that households can save for other people’s machines and get rich on the returns, which the bystander’s government can tax to some extent. This back-of-the-envelope calculation is a benchmark, not a forecast of what is to come. But the arithmetic is simple, and it runs counter to the hope that an AI-fueled growth boom will take care of public debt, because growth comes with an interest rate that is twice as high. Not a pretty future for most of Western Europe. Due to AI, the choice is between default, inflation, or, most likely, financial repression: capital controls that keep domestic savers from investing in machines abroad.

I am somewhat more optimistic than this.  The assumption of zero Spanish adoption already is noted, but also ideas are public goods, and Spain can benefit from American (and Chinese) AI, and the resulting scientific advances.  The long-run supply of capital also might be quite elastic, we will see.

Heat killed ‘staggering’ 50,000 people in Europe’s cities this summer, scientists warn

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

People succumbed to heat at highest rates in Malta, Italy and Hungary, with health issues, loneliness and poverty exacerbating toll

Heat killed a record 50,000 people in European cities this summer, new research suggests, with scientists blaming climate breakdown for two in every three of the deaths.

Morgues and funeral homes filled with bodies from June to August as the heat added by planet-baking pollution cut short an extra 32,000 lives in urban areas, the researchers found. Hot days pushed vulnerable bodies into overdrive and warm nights prevented them from recovering.

The “staggering” death toll, modelled using historical relationships between heat and health in 854 cities, was the worst on record, the study found. Hot weather marked by relentless heatwaves killed nearly twice as many people as it did in each of the two next most dangerous years.

“In some sense, the absolute numbers of the heat-related deaths estimated across the 854 urban areas are indeed staggering,” said Malcolm Mistry, a climate and health scientist at the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine, who co-authored the study. “But perhaps what is even more alarming is the proportion of these deaths that we attribute to anthropogenic climate change.”

The share of deaths due to climate breakdown has risen from about 40% at the start of the century to 65% this summer, the study found.

People succumbed to heat at the highest rates in Malta, Italy and Hungary, while absolute death tolls were greatest in Italy, France, Spain, Germany and the UK. Because death certificates rarely record contributing factors – such as dangerous temperatures that push a stroke victim or lung cancer patient past their limits – families rarely know that heat and human activity hastened their relatives’ death.

“These shocking figures represent thousands of families who have lost a loved one this summer due to the climate crisis,” said Katie White, the climate transition minister for the UK, where scientists estimated 4,354 heat deaths this summer.”

In Naples, the hardest-hit city on the continent, 815 people per million died from the heat, according to the study’s age-standardised estimates. Mario Guarino, an emergency doctor at Ospedale CTO in the north of the city, said he witnessed a 15% increase in emergency room visits during the hottest weeks.

The “defining characteristic” of the period was the complexity of health problems exacerbated by heat, he added, with loneliness and poverty worsening the situation further.

“We took in a terminal cancer patient who lived in a hot attic simply because his family couldn’t find a company to install air conditioning during the summer,” said Guarino. “An elderly man with dementia was abruptly abandoned by his Ukrainian caregiver who returned home to say goodbye to her son, who had been called to war. Emergency services found [the man] lying on the floor one night with a fractured pelvis, desperately trying to call the tenant next door.”

The study found people over the age of 85 made up almost half of the death toll, though younger people were not immune, with 833 people between the age of 20 and 44 also thought to have died due to the heat. The scientists said the death toll from the cities studied, which account for about 40% of the European population, could not be easily extrapolated to estimate the full death toll including those in towns and villages.

The most pressing stories and debates for Europeans – from identity to economics to the environment

The scorching heat that persistently baked western Europe this summer was the worst on record, the EU’s climate monitoring service announced this month, with more than half the continent seared by “very strong” heat stress that felt hotter than 38C. Slightly less punishing temperatures in eastern Europe brought the continental average to its third-hottest level on record.

Greenhouse gases have warmed Europe about twice as fast as the global average, exposing more people to intolerably hot summers and fewer people to dangerously cold winters. While cold is a bigger killer than heat, scientists expect rising temperatures to claim more lives than they save, as heat deaths are projected to rise faster than cold deaths fall.

The study combined established relationships between heat and mortality with temperature data for 2026 to rapidly estimate the fallout from this summer’s heat. The death tolls for individual countries differ from what national public health agencies have calculated using their own methods.

David García-García, a researcher at the mortality monitoring system at the Carlos III health institute in Madrid, who was not involved in the study, said it was a “valuable contribution” that allowed for comparisons across the continent. “However, it is important to bear in mind that these are estimates based on observed temperatures and epidemiological models, which do not incorporate actual data on deaths occurring in the summer of 2026,” he said.

Doctors have urged governments to burn fewer fossil fuels – the core driver of rising temperatures – while bolstering health systems, greening cities, rolling out air conditioning and encouraging people to drink water and seek shade. They have also stressed the benefits of people checking in on vulnerable neighbours and family members so they can get help before tragedy strikes.

“The real epidemic these days is loneliness,” said Guarino. “Many elderly people are left alone during the summer, abandoned by their families, and due to their low income, they can’t afford air conditioning or, if they do have it, avoid turning it on because of the cost of electricity.”

Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 review – a thrilling airport novel of a game

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Activision; PC, PS5, Switch 2, XboxFrom parkour over Manhattan rooftops and car chases in Paris to the climactic battle at a North Korean air base, the developers have thrown absolutely everything at the game

And so we return perhaps one final time to Call of Duty’s Modern Warfare strand, with stories, “ripped from the newspaper headlines”, featuring shady terror organisations, nefarious rogue states and the brutally capable, monosyllabic war fighters who stand between us and Armageddon. Last year’s Black Ops 7 instalment was a sales disappointment for Activision, and that has no doubt put extra pressure on Infinity Ward, the studio leading Modern Warfare 4 development. Fortunately, this developer – like series heroes Price, Gaz and Ghost – seems to thrive on pressure.

As you may have read, this year’s campaign is based around a fictitious North Korean ruling family that has seized an opportunity to invade its southern neighbour. Amid the chaos, a small group of Korean and American marines must help lead the fightback, while in the shadows, SAS hard man John Price makes an unlikely new comrade in his own morally ambiguous quest to put the world to rights. Mads Mikkelsen pops up as evil mastermind The Archer, but like Kiernan Shipka in Black Ops 7 he’s rather wasted, with very little screen time.

Nevertheless, it’s a bombastic, thrilling airport novel of a game, crammed with classic CoD set pieces, including but not limited to parkour over Manhattan rooftops, a car chase in Paris and a shootout in a train yard that somehow feels like a cross between Frogger and Time Crisis. A climactic battle at a North Korean airbase throws absolutely everything at you from sniper firefights to helicopter gunship attacks.

There are also moments where you’re forced into stark unethical actions, faintly recalling the notorious “No Russian” mission from 2009’s Modern Warfare 2. Elsewhere, a whole open-world section lets you explore a snowy landscape, carrying out story missions and side quests to improve your armoury. It’s a nice break from corridor shooting, and somehow it doesn’t stall the game’s momentum. Throughout the six-hour campaign, bullets fly and buildings collapse, friends become enemies, enemies become friends, and a breathless story of geopolitical skullduggery emerges. It’s a shame, though, that the Capt Price arc gets a little overlooked toward the end and his denouement lacks the intended emotional clout, but it’s fun and at least a little bit emotional while it lasts.

The multiplayer mode is a refinement of the turbo-paced, ultra-lethal experience of Black Ops 7. Players are still extremely agile, able to vault over obstacles and skid around corners, blasting their guns like John Wick on a recently polished vinyl floor. But movement is a touch more naturalistic here, and weapons have a heft to them that makes you feel deadly, even if you’re getting shot in the face every five seconds.

Alongside all the classic modes there are decent newcomers, such as the epic, up-to-64-player, Combat Outpost, and Collateral, a round-based shootout where you buy new weapons and items at the start of each bout (the better you do, the more cash you get to spend on goodies). The highlight though is Kill Block, which takes place in a modular training centre where three landscape types are randomly selected from dozens of possibilities, then slotted in to a play space at the start of the round so you’re never sure how everything will fit together. You might have to move from a train yard to a storage depot to a trench network, and weapons are randomised too, so you could all be fighting with pistols or sniper rifles, adding to the unpredictable challenge.

The last part of the package is a reconstructed take on Call of Duty’s extraction shooter mode, DMZ. Taking place in and around Hajin City, abandoned during the events of the campaign, you parachute in with up to two teammates, then carry out various PvE missions to earn cash and gear, before calling in for air rescue – all while avoiding or attacking other players on the same map. New to this version is the Forward Operating Base, a safe house you return to after each expedition where you can upgrade your weapons, 3D print new gear and get info on the current roster of boss characters. There’s also a three-branch skill tree for your character, which you populate by earning in-game XP. This gives you a range of new abilities and improvements including taking more loot from downed enemies, faster revives and reducing damage from explosives.

Cómo congelar el pan correctamente para que quede como recién hecho

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

¿Quién no ha sentido la frustración de comprar una barra de pan deliciosa y que, al segundo día, esté tan dura que parezca una piedra? Es un clásico de cualquier hogar, pero la realidad es que no hace falta ir a la panadería cada mañana para disfrutar de un buen desayuno. Congelar el pan es la solución más inteligente para optimizar el tiempo y, sobre todo, para evitar que terminemos tirando comida a la basura, algo que hoy en día es fundamental.

Aunque parezca un proceso sencillo, hay un montón de matices que marcan la diferencia entre un pan que sabe a plástico y uno que parece recién salido del horno. Desde la temperatura ideal hasta el truco del papel vegetal, existen técnicas específicas según el tipo de masa que permiten mantener intactas las propiedades nutricionales y la textura. Si quieres dejar de pelearte con el pan duro, aquí te contamos cómo dominar el arte del congelador.

La respuesta es un rotundo sí. De hecho, es uno de los métodos más eficaces para prolongar la frescura sin que el producto pierda su calidad original. No obstante, hay que tener en cuenta que no todos los panes reaccionan igual al frío extremo. Por lo general, aquellos panes artesanales con una corteza más resistente y una miga densa, como las hogazas rústicas o el pan de masa madre, soportan mucho mejor el proceso que los panes industriales muy ligeros o el pan de molde comercial, que tienen un contenido de agua distinto.

Para obtener un resultado óptimo, lo ideal es congelarlo el mismo día de la compra. Si esperamos a que el pan ya esté empezando a secarse o reblandecerse, el resultado tras la descongelación será mediocre, ya que el frío no devuelve la frescura, solo la mantiene.

En cambio, los panes de fermentación rápida o aquellos excesivamente secos tienden a volverse quebradizos o demasiado duros al sacarlos del hielo.

Para que el pan no se queme por el frío ni absorba los olores del congelador (que nadie quiere que su tostada sepa a pescado), sigue estas recomendaciones:

Primero, la regla de oro: esperar a que el pan se enfríe por completo si es casero. Si lo guardas caliente, la humedad residual creará cristales de hielo que arruinarán la miga y la dejarán gomosa. Una vez frío, la forma más práctica es cortarlo en rebanadas. Esto permite sacar solo la cantidad necesaria y evita tener que descongelar una pieza entera para comerse solo un trozo.

En cuanto al envoltorio, lo mejor son las bolsas herméticas de congelación, extrayendo todo el aire posible. Si quieres subir de nivel y evitar que las rebanadas se peguen entre sí, puedes usar el truco del papel vegetal: coloca pequeños trozos de papel sulfurizado entre cada loncha. Como este papel es impermeable, no se pegará al pan y facilitará muchísimo la tarea de sacar una sola tostada por la mañana.

Respecto a la temperatura, asegúrate de que tu congelador esté a -18 ºC. En estas condiciones, el pan puede durar entre uno y tres meses. A partir del tercer mes, aunque sea seguro comerlo, empezará a perder aroma y sabor, por lo que conviene consumirlo antes.

Sobre el uso del microondas, hay que tener cuidado. Aunque es la opción más veloz, tiende a evaporar la humedad de la miga, dejando el pan con una textura gomosa o excesivamente seca. Si no tienes otra opción, usa una potencia baja (unos 300W) en intervalos cortos de 10 a 15 segundos y coloca un vaso con agua al lado para generar vapor y mantener la humedad.

Un punto crítico es la cadena del frío. No se debe volver a congelar el pan que ya ha sido descongelado, especialmente si ha pasado tiempo a temperatura ambiente, ya que esto favorece la proliferación de bacterias y degrada totalmente la estructura del alimento.

Si a pesar de todo algún pan no ha quedado perfecto al descongelarlo, no lo tires. El pan duro tiene un valor gastronómico increíble y puede transformarse en picatostes, pan rallado, torrijas, sopa de ajo o canapés, evitando así cualquier desperdicio en la cocina.

Dominar la congelación del pan permite ahorrar dinero, tiempo y reducir la basura orgánica, asegurando que siempre haya una opción crujiente y sabrosa disponible en casa, ya sea utilizando el método de las rebanadas separadas con papel vegetal o recuperando una hogaza artesana en el horno.
