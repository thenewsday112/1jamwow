Indonesia cuts gas exports to Singapore amid row over haze

The cut follows a week of warnings from Jakarta

Indonesia has cut natural gas exports to Singapore, escalating a row over the haze from its wildfires.

The cut began on Saturday and totals 31 million standard cubic feet a day, with 15 million from the Natuna field in the Riau Islands and 16 million from Sumatra, the head of the state agency that supervises the oil and gas sector said in a statement.

Djoko Siswanto of SKK Migas said the move was made "to maintain the reliability of domestic gas supply for electricity”, according to AFP.

"We will continue to monitor developments on a daily basis," he said. Only Singapore is affected for now.

Singapore's Energy Market Authority said the reduction is less than two per cent of the city-state's total daily gas demand and that it has contingency plans in place.

"EMA understands that our importers will pursue all relevant recourse in their contracts for the gas supply disruption," it said in a statement to AFP.

The cut follows a week of warnings from Jakarta.

Indonesia's energy minister, Bahlil Lahadalia, said on Thursday that Indonesia could stop supplying countries that do not respect it, at the opening of gas facilities off East Kalimantan, according to Eco-Business.

"I will carry out an evaluation, and I will not hesitate to consider halting exports to countries that disrespect Indonesia," he said.

"The direction is clear: our natural resources will be used for domestic needs first. This is survival mode." He did not name any country.

The day before, president Prabowo Subianto ordered police, prosecutors, and the forestry ministry to investigate the companies behind the fires, and rebuked critics abroad.

"For certain countries that like to insult the Indonesian nation, in time we will reveal the companies," Mr Prabowo told local media.

Singapore has served legal notices under its haze law on 10 Indonesian companies it suspects of contributing to the fires, and has promised legal action against those responsible. Its stock exchange has also questioned seven listed agricultural companies about possible links to the fires.

Singapore relies on gas for about 95 per cent of its electricity and imports Indonesian gas mainly by pipeline under contracts that expire at the end of 2028, according to Singapore's trade ministry.

Liquefied natural gas made up more than half of its gas imports in 2024, and a second terminal for it is being built. Indonesia supplied 145.3 billion cubic feet to Singapore in 2024, according to US Energy Information Administration data.

The fires have consumed more than 1.9 million hectares in the nine months to September, according to the deforestation monitor Nusantara Atlas. More than 333,470 people have suffered acute respiratory illnesses across seven Indonesian provinces since mid-August, according to the health ministry.

The haze has also reached Malaysia and East Timor, and the ASEAN regional bloc has called a meeting over the crisis.

Malaysia's environment minister Arthur Joseph Kurup said his country reserved the right to demand effective action from Indonesia and would raise the haze at a meeting of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) in Manila.

Anger has spread on both sides of the border. Thousands of students have protested across Indonesia over the government's response, including more than 500 outside the central Kalimantan governor's office on Friday, demanding a national disaster declaration.

In Malaysia, some consumers have begun boycotting Indonesian goods after Indonesia's government communications chief Syahganda Nainggolan said the oxygen in Singapore and Malaysia comes from Indonesia's forests, the Straits Times reported.

First international flight takes off from new Sydney airport

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Western Sydney International Airport offers the first alternative to the city's main airport, which has been operating for more than a century.

An Air New Zealand plane takes off as the first international flight to depart the newly-opened Western Sydney International Airport in Sydney on Oct 26, 2026. (Photo: AFP/Saeed Khan)

SYDNEY: The first international flight took off on Monday (Oct 26) from a new, multi-billion-dollar airport in the fast-growing western suburbs of Sydney.

After more than four decades of planning, Western Sydney International Airport offers the first alternative to the city's main airport, which has been operating for more than a century.

An Air New Zealand plane from Auckland landed and soon afterwards flew out of the airport in the morning, launching a three-flights-a-week service to Sydney's largely working-class western suburbs.

"It's not every day you get to be part of opening a brand-new international airport," said Air New Zealand chief operations officer Kate Boyer.

"Western Sydney is home to more than 2.5 million people and it's a further gateway for Australian visitors to discover everything our country has to offer."

The new single-runway airport, Australia's first new major aviation hub in more than five decades, began domestic flights on Sunday, and freight operations in July.

Sydney's main airport, Kingsford Smith, is Australia's busiest, its three runways handling more than 42 million passengers last year.

Lying in Botany Bay just 9km from the city centre, planes using the airport roar over many city homes, and a curfew bars passenger aircraft from operating during the night.

The new airport in western Sydney has no such restrictions.

"Through our 24-hour operations, Western Sydney International Airport gives airlines greater flexibility to connect into global hubs and travellers more choice in how they reach the world," said airport chief executive Simon Hickey.

Qantas' budget carrier Jetstar is now the main domestic carrier, flying to the Gold Coast, Melbourne and Brisbane.

The airport says other international carriers are coming, including Qatar Airways from December 2027, Fiji Airways from March 2027, and Vietjet Air from January 2027.

Built at a cost of A$5.3 billion (US$3.7 billion), excluding surrounding transport works, the airport is reachable by road but is not expected to have a direct rail link until at least December 2027.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said the new airport marked a "wonderful new chapter" for Australia.

It would also drive economic and jobs into western Sydney, an area often overshadowed by the city's famed harbour and central business district (CBD), he said at the weekend.

"For too long, Sydney has looked towards the harbour and the CBD. What this does is turn it around to where people live, to where jobs occur, to where investment occurs in western Sydney."

Attackers kill nurse, injure health worker responding to DRC Ebola case

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

The attack in eastern DR Congo is the latest on health workers and responders to the deadly Ebola outbreak.

A nurse has been killed and another health worker severely injured after they were attacked while responding to an Ebola alert in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), according to the World Health Organization.

It was the latest in a series of attacks on health workers and responders that authorities say is hampering efforts to slow the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak in the world.

The attack took place on Saturday in the town of Bafwasende in the DRC’s eastern Tshopo province, WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said in a post on X on Sunday.

“Everyone working to protect communities from this deadly disease must be able to work in dignity and safety. Healthcare must always be protected,” he said.

The WHO’s regional director for Africa, Dr Mohamed Yakub Janabi, also condemned the attack on X.

“Health is NOT a target,” he said. “Those responding to the Ebola outbreak must be protected. Misinformation and fear must never fuel violence.”

Local authorities have not yet commented on the attack or the suspects.

Data from the DRC’s Ministry of Health indicate that more than 9,800 cases, including more than 4,700 deaths, have been recorded since the start of the outbreak in May, making it the deadliest Ebola outbreak in the country’s history.

The government’s handling of the outbreak, combined with chronic corruption, has sown widespread distrust, with some even dismissing Ebola as a hoax.

Responders say scepticism about the disease among communities has complicated their efforts, including in remote areas and emerging hotspots already facing shortages of medical supplies and healthcare workers.

Last month, a DRC politician was beaten to death in North Kivu after publicly defending the government’s response to Ebola. He had called on the public to obey response measures, particularly social distancing and hand-washing, on a radio show.

In May, at the start of the outbreak, angry residents set alight two tents at health centres in two separate attacks in just one week.

Responders say some residents have held captive or threatened team members in designated health zones.

The current outbreak has now been reported in four countries across eastern, central ⁠⁠and western Africa, giving it the widest geographic spread of any Ebola outbreak on the continent to date.

On Sunday, Uganda’s Minister of Health Chris Baryomunsi announced that all citizens returning to the country with a history of travel to the DRC will be quarantined for 21 days.

Uganda says it is ⁠Ebola-free after declaring an end to its latest outbreak in July. But health experts remain on alert after two people who had recently passed through the country later tested positive in Kenya and Nigeria.

Ebola spreads through direct contact with the bodily fluids of infected people.

The strain responsible for the current outbreak, known as Bundibugyo, has no approved vaccine or specific treatment.

Symptoms can take up to 21 days to appear, according to the WHO. In an October 23 update, it urged stronger monitoring and cooperation across borders, while advising against restrictions on travel or trade with affected countries.

Referendum w Częstochowie. Mieszkańcy zdecydowali o losach prezydenta i rady miasta

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

To już pewne – Częstochowa żegna się z dotychczasowym prezydentem. Krzysztof Matyjaszczyk z Lewicy został odwołany w referendum. Próg frekwencyjny został przekroczony, a większość głosujących opowiedziała się za zmianą władzy. Co dalej z miastem?

Noc z niedzieli na poniedziałek przyniosła przełomowe informacje dla mieszkańców Częstochowy. Jak poinformowała delegatura Krajowego Biura Wyborczego, w referendum dotyczącym odwołania prezydenta Krzysztofa Matyjaszczyka oraz rady miasta frekwencja przekroczyła wymagany próg.

Spośród 135 obwodów dane z 134 potwierdzają, że do urn poszło więcej osób niż wymagane minimum – 46 625 głosów w przypadku prezydenta i 48 754 w sprawie rady miasta.

W referendum wzięło udział 51 863 osób. 97 proc. głosów oddano za pozbawieniem prezydenta urzędu. Odwołano również radę miasta. W tej sprawie wypowiedziało się w niedzielę 51 844 osób, a 96,2 proc. z nich było za odwołaniem rady.

Większość głosujących opowiedziała się za odwołaniem zarówno prezydenta, jak i rady miasta. To efekt narastającego od miesięcy kryzysu politycznego w Częstochowie.

Przypomnijmy, że Krzysztof Matyjaszczyk od ośmiu miesięcy nie mógł pełnić swoich obowiązków z powodu prokuratorskich zarzutów korupcyjnych. Na mocy sankcji nie miał prawa wstępu do urzędu miasta ani kontaktu z pracownikami. Obowiązki prezydenta przejął jego pierwszy zastępca.

Centralne Biuro Antykorupcyjne zatrzymało prezydenta Częstochowy 25 lutego br. Prokuratura przedstawiła mu zarzut przyjęcia co najmniej 40 tys. zł w zamian za zmiany w budżecie miasta pozwalające na realizację zamówienia publicznego z korzyścią dla jednego z przedsiębiorców oraz przyjęcia od wielu osób korzyści majątkowych w łącznej kwocie nie mniejszej niż 129 tys. zł.

W tym samym śledztwie zarzuty usłyszało ponad 20 osób. Jako pierwszego zatrzymano i aresztowano w październiku 2024 r. wicemarszałka woj. śląskiego Bartłomieja S. z KO - także w związku z jego wcześniejszą funkcją wiceprezydenta Częstochowy. Zarzuty ma m.in. kilku przedsiębiorców i dwaj radni (z Lewicy i KO).

Inicjatorzy referendum stawiali też prezydentowi zarzut nepotyzmu w Urzędzie Miasta i spółkach komunalnych. Wskazywali m.in., że partnerka Krzysztofa Matyjaszczyka (obecnie żona) stanęła na czele utworzonego Biura Ładu Korporacyjnego (kilka tygodni temu p.o. prezydenta je zlikwidował). Mówili też o zadłużeniu Częstochowy, braku kluczowych inwestycji, np. stadionu Rakowa.

Sam Krzysztof Matyjaszczyk nie przyznaje się do winy i twierdzi, że padł ofiarą pomówień. Zgodził się na podawanie swojego nazwiska w mediach. Przypomnijmy, że Matyjaszczyk był prezydentem Częstochowy od 2010 roku, a wcześniej zasiadał w Sejmie jako poseł. Miastem rządziła koalicja Nowej Lewicy i Koalicji Obywatelskiej.

Udane referendum oznacza, że w Częstochowie zapanują rządy komisaryczne. Do czasu przeprowadzenia przedterminowych wyborów prezydenta i rady miasta, miastem będzie zarządzać komisarz wyznaczony przez premiera.

Jeszcze przed referendum wysłaliśmy list do Donalda Tuska z apelem, by nie był to nikt z tutejszego układu władzy, bo rada miasta, w której większość ma koalicja Lewicy z KO, legitymizowała prezydenta, dając mu wotum zaufania mimo zarzutów korupcyjnych - powiedział Marcin Nocoń.

Krzysztof Matyjaszczyk prezydentem Częstochowy był 16 lat. Został nim w 2010 r. w przedterminowych wyborach po odwołaniu w referendum prawicowego prezydenta Tadeusza Wrony. Reelekcję w kolejnych wyborach na prezydenta Częstochowy Matyjaszczyk uzyskał w 2014 r. (w II turze), 2018 (w I turze) i 2024 r. (w II turze).

Stolen car slams into South Carolina home as teens bolt, authorities say

A stolen car barreled through neighborhood yards and smashed into a South Carolina home before two teenagers ran, triggering a search involving deputies, K9 teams and a drone, authorities said.

Video released by the Greenville County Sheriff’s Office shows the car slamming into the house before deputies rush toward the wreck and chase a fleeing suspect.

“Is everybody alright?” a deputy can be heard asking as authorities check the home for anyone who might be hurt.

The sheriff’s office said deputies attempted to stop the reported stolen vehicle around 2:30 p.m. Oct. 15. The driver fled, but deputies said they did not pursue the car before it crashed into the home on Renwood Road in Simpsonville.

Two teenagers, ages 15 and 16, were eventually arrested and charged with possession of a stolen vehicle, authorities said. No injuries were reported.

The video shows one deputy running after a fleeing suspect while another heads toward the damaged home. The passenger was quickly taken into custody, while the search for the driver brought help from above and on the ground.

K9 teams used multiple dogs to track the driver’s path, and drone operators provided an aerial view of the neighborhood, according to the sheriff’s office. The video also shows a dog picking up a scent collected from the vehicle before tracking the driver.

“When someone decides to run from law enforcement in Greenville County, they quickly learn that our deputies have more than one way to find them,” the sheriff’s office wrote in a post accompanying the video.

“You can run, but our deputies have a pretty impressive toolbox. And they know how to use it,” the agency added.

For neighbors, the crash brought a frightening interruption to an otherwise routine afternoon.

“I was just putting my baby down for a nap, and I heard a big crash outside,” next-door neighbor Rachel Bush told WYFF.

Just minutes earlier, Bush said, she had been walking outside with her baby and dog.

“We were grateful we were back inside before everything happened,” she told the outlet.

One of the home’s residents told WYFF that no one was home when the car crashed through the front door. He and his fiancé returned from an errand minutes later to find the damage.

The resident said the car missed another vehicle in the garage by inches, leaving behind an extensive cleanup and repair job.

Another neighbor, Robert Belcher, told WYFF he offered support after seeing what happened.

“I don’t know what to say to you but thank God no one was at home,” Belcher recalled telling his neighbor.

The resident also offered a pointed message to the teenagers accused in the incident.

“Think about what you are doing; you never know if it’s going to impact lives,” he told WYFF.

Caribbean seeks reparations and questions role of monarchy before King Charles III visits

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The royals are coming, and the Caribbean wants to be heard.

Islanders are seeking slavery reparations from Britain, a formal apology for its role in the slave trade and greater independence from the former colonial power.

The Bahamas National Reparations Committee said it already knows what awaits the Caribbean as King Charles III and Queen Camilla prepare for this week's trip: “Schools emptied of children who will stand in the sun and wave for the Crown that has taken everything from their ancestors and comes back smiling.”

Charles is expected to visit the Bahamas on Tuesday to begin the nine-day trip to the Caribbean. His last official visit to the archipelago was in July 1973 as a prince who granted the Bahamas independence, ending 300 years of British rule. But the Bahamas remains a Commonwealth realm, along with six other Caribbean nations that recognize Charles as their king and head of state.

While in the Bahamas, Charles is scheduled to visit Clifford Park, where the archipelago’s black, turquoise and yellow flag was raised for the first time more than half a century ago.

His visit will raise important questions, said Niambi Hall Campbell-Dean, chair of the Bahamas National Reparations Committee.

“Why do we still have a king? What does this person really represent to us? Why is he coming and how will that benefit us?” she said.

A psychology professor at the University of the Bahamas, Campbell-Dean said she recently mentioned Charles’ upcoming visit to her students, and they were not excited. She responded: “I know y’all might not care about this man who is coming, but here’s why this is of importance.”

Leaders across the Caribbean have long been pushing for an apology and reparations from Britain.

In early September, Jamaica took its case for reparations to Charles, and the case has since been referred to the United Kingdom’s highest court of appeal for the Caribbean island. It was an unprecedented legal maneuver, even though the British government has consistently rejected demands for either an apology or reparations.

A visit to Guyana more than 200 years after a slave revolt

After three days in the Bahamas, Charles is scheduled to fly to the South American country of Guyana. The former British colony was the site of one of the biggest slave revolts in the region during the colonial era, with thousands of slaves uprising during the 1823 Demerara rebellion on the plantation owned by the father of former British Prime Minister William Gladstone.

More than 200 slaves were killed as a result; several were decapitated and their heads placed on poles.

In a recent interview with ITV News, Guyanese President Irfaan Ali said Charles has “rightfully recognized slavery as the darkest days in human history.”

“Moving to the apology is the next step,” he added. “You exploited the region. You destroyed lives. You created underdevelopment.”

While in Guyana, Charles is expected to meet with Ali and elected leaders of Amerindian villages known as “toshaos.”

Nikita Miller, toshao of Chinese Landing, a rural Amerindian community in northern Guyana, said she was not invited.

She noted that her community has been mired in a decades-long land fight with miners seeking to extract gold from the area, and she would have liked the opportunity to talk about an issue that affects other toshaos as well.

“We have been suffering,” she said. “We want the government to respect Indigenous rights. … We have long-standing issues with our land.”

Miller said she believes the king’s visit might help improve things for Guyana overall, but not for Amerindian villages.

After two days in Guyana, Charles is scheduled to fly to Antigua and Barbuda for the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting and meet with leaders of 56 member states. Talks are expected to focus on climate, trade and development, but some Caribbean leaders have said slavery reparations should dominate conversations.

The royals to skip Jamaica as it seeks more independence

The British royals have long visited Jamaica, but it’s not on Charles' itinerary this time.

It’s just as well, said 51-year-old Rastafarian Steven Bennett.

“We don’t need any royal wave or photo-taking while the wounds of our ancestors are still being ignored. He still refuses to formally apologize for chattel slavery,” Bennett said. “Until the British monarchy is ready to stand on Jamaican soil, look us in the eye, apologize, and talk about actual repair and restitution, he has no business here. We want justice, not a parade.”

Jamaica in recent years has been pushing to oust Charles as head of state and become a republic like Barbados did in 2021.

Antonio Williams, a 25-year-old university student, said young Jamaicans have zero connection to the Crown.

“I think Buckingham Palace has read the room after the absolute PR disaster of William and Kate’s Caribbean tour a few years back,” he said, referring to the 2022 visit of the Prince and Princess of Wales to the region, where they faced protests and calls for reparations.

“We are actively moving toward becoming a republic and breaking these colonial ties,” Williams said. “Having the king visit now would just be incredibly awkward. Skipping us is a sign that they know their influence here is expiring.”

But 68-year-old retired civil servant Hermine Reid recalled with fondness how Charles’ mother, Queen Elizabeth II, used to visit Jamaica.

“It was always such a grand, respectful occasion, and people would line the streets in their Sunday best,” she said. “Things are very different now. It’s a little sad for my generation to see how much the relationship has fractured, but I suppose this is simply the modern era. Jamaica is growing up and charting its own course, and the king clearly recognizes that.”

Myers Jr. reported from Kingston, Jamaica.

Częstochowa odwołała prezydenta i radę miasta. Będą wybory

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Aktualizacja: 26.10.2026 06:29 Publikacja: 26.10.2026 05:10

Prezydent Krzysztof Matyjaszczyk i rada miasta zostali odwołani w referendum

PAP, oprac. arb

Dane ze 134 spośród 135 obwodów głosowania w referendum wskazują, że próg frekwencyjny został przekroczony.

Referendum jest ważne, jeśli zagłosuje w nim minimum 3/5 liczby osób biorących udział w wyborze prezydenta (II tura) i rady miasta. Próg frekwencyjny wynosił 46 625 głosów w przypadku pytania o odwołanie prezydenta i 48 754, gdy chodzi o radę. Większość głosujących była za odwołaniem prezydenta i rady miasta. W tej ostatniej większość miała koalicja Nowej Lewicy i Koalicji Obywatelskiej.  – W Częstochowie zadziałał efekt Łukasza Gibały – ocenił wyniki pełnomocnik inicjatywy referendalnej Marcin Nocoń.

Mieszkańcy Częstochowy zakończyli o godz. 21 głosowanie w sprawie odwołania prezydenta Krzysztofa Matyjaszczyka i rady miasta. O ważności referendu...

Odwołanie prezydenta i rady miasta oznacza, że w Częstochowie wprowadzone zostaną rządy komisaryczne do czasu wyłonienia prezydenta i rady w przedterminowych wyborach.

Komisarza w Częstochowie wyznaczy premier.  – Jeszcze przed referendum wysłaliśmy list do Donalda Tuska z apelem, by nie był to nikt z tutejszego układu władzy, bo rada miasta, w której większość ma koalicja Lewicy z KO, legitymizowała prezydenta, dając mu wotum zaufania mimo zarzutów korupcyjnych – mówił w rozmowie z PAP Nocoń.

Komitet referendalny stworzyła grupa aktywistów miejskich oraz osób, które w ostatnich wyborach samorządowych kandydowały do rady miasta z listy Trzeciej Drogi (ugrupowanie nie uzyskało mandatów). W kampanii referendalnej najbardziej aktywny z polityków był poseł Piotr Strach, Częstochowianin wybrany z listy Trzeciej Drogi z okręgu gliwickiego. Inicjatywę wsparło Prawo i Sprawiedliwość – w tej sprawie wypowiedział się prezes partii Jarosław Kaczyński – oraz inne ugrupowania prawicowe.

Prezydent Krzysztof Matyjaszczyk od ośmiu miesięcy nie mógł pełnić swojej funkcji, wchodzić do urzędu miasta ani kontaktować się z pracownikami, na mocy prokuratorskich sankcji w związku z zarzutami korupcyjnymi. Obowiązki prezydenta sprawuje obecnie I zastępca prezydenta.

Centralne Biuro Antykorupcyjne zatrzymało prezydenta Częstochowy 25 lutego. Prokuratura przedstawiła mu zarzut przyjęcia co najmniej 40 tys. zł w zamian za zmiany w budżecie miasta pozwalające na realizację zamówienia publicznego z korzyścią dla jednego z przedsiębiorców oraz przyjęcie od wielu osób korzyści majątkowych w łącznej kwocie nie mniejszej niż 129 tys. zł.

W śledztwie zarzuty usłyszało ponad 20 osób. Jako pierwszego zatrzymano i aresztowano w październiku 2024 r. wicemarszałka woj. śląskiego Bartłomieja S. z KO – także w związku z jego wcześniejszą funkcją wiceprezydenta Częstochowy. Zarzuty ma też m.in. kilku przedsiębiorców i dwaj radni (z Lewicy i KO).

Odwołany ze stanowiska prezydent Częstochowy ma 52 lata, z wykształcenia jest inżynierem budownictwa po Politechnice Częstochowskiej, skończył podyplomowe studia z zarządzania. Działał w organizacjach studenckich i Federacji Młodych Socjaldemokratów, w 1999 r. wstąpił do Sojuszu Lewicy Demokratycznej (SLD). W 2002 i 2006 r. był wybierany na radnego. W 2007 r. w przedterminowych wyborach dostał się do Sejmu z listy lewicy. W 2008 r. został członkiem komisji śledczej ds. nacisków na służby specjalne.

Krzysztof Matyjaszczyk nie przyznaje się do stawianych mu zarzutów. Twierdzi, że jest niewinny i padł ofiarą pomówień. Matyjaszczyk zgodził się na podawanie jego pełnego nazwiska. Od czerwca nie pokazuje się publicznie. Prezydentem Częstochowy został w 2010 r., wcześniej był posłem. Do czasu wyjaśnienia sprawy decyzją władz partii został zawieszony w prawach członka Nowej Lewicy.

Inicjatorzy referendum stawiali też prezydentowi zarzut nepotyzmu w Urzędzie Miasta i spółkach komunalnych. Wskazywali m.in., że partnerka Krzysztofa Matyjaszczyka (obecnie żona) stanęła na czele utworzonego Biura Ładu Korporacyjnego (kilka tygodni temu p.o. prezydenta je zlikwidował). Mówili też o zadłużeniu Częstochowy i braku kluczowych inwestycji, np. budowy stadionu Rakowa.

W kampanii przedreferendalnej władze podkreślały, że zadłużenie Częstochowy w stosunku do wysokości budżetu jest mniejsze niż w wielu dużych miastach. Skupiły się na pokazywaniu, jak rozwinęła się Częstochowa nie tylko w tej, ale i poprzednich kadencjach.

US military says it helped Gulf states export 1.5 billion barrels of oil

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

US Central Command says more than 3,000 commercial vessels have transited the Strait of Hormuz with its support.

US military forces have helped Gulf countries export more than 1.5 billion barrels of oil since June, despite Iran’s efforts to control the critical waterway, according to US Central Command (COMMAND).

The US military command said on Sunday that more than 3,000 commercial vessels had transited the Strait of Hormuz with its support over the past six months.

It added that more than 95 percent of ships navigating the channel had sailed through safely in recent months despite Iranian threats, “clearly demonstrating that the United States and regional partners control the international waterway”.

CENTCOM announced the figures as Commander Admiral Brad Cooper made a visit to the region, during which he observed the “current operational environment” in the strait while flying in a US Army helicopter.

“We continue to stand shoulder to shoulder with our Gulf partners and the shipping industry,” Cooper said in a statement.

“This visit underscores our strong commitment to continued freedom of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz.”

Cooper said the primary transit routes for vessels in the strait were free of Iranian mines due to the “incredible efforts” of personnel who conduct mine clearance operations.

“We remain intensely focused on keeping commercial traffic flowing as we work with our teammates in the US interagency, Gulf Cooperation Council, and shipping industry,” he said.

Crude oil flows have largely returned to levels seen before the US and Israel launched their war on Iran in late February, despite the ongoing threat of attacks on commercial vessels by Iranian forces.

At least 16.5 million barrels of oil per day left the region in September, equal to the pre-war average, excluding Iran, according to market intelligence firm Kpler.

Analysts have attributed the rebound to a combination of offshore ship-to-ship transfers, US military escorts, and increased use of oil pipelines.

However, oil prices remain far higher than before the start of the war.

Brent crude futures stood at about $114.50 a barrel on Sunday, after easing about 2 percent from last week’s nearly six-month high.

Brent settled at $72.48 per barrel on February 27, the day before the US and Israel began strikes on Iran.
