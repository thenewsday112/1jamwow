Republican gubernatorial candidate Bruce Blakeman called Gov. Kathy Hochul a "puppet" of NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani at a Sunday rally, accusing her of embracing his agenda while neglecting immigration and crime.

Republican New York gubernatorial candidate Bruce Blakeman blasted Democratic Gov. Kathy Hochul as a "puppet" of New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani during a campaign rally Sunday.

Speaking at the event in Yorktown Heights, New York, Blakeman, the current Nassau County executive, accused his opponent of embracing the democratic socialist mayor's agenda while failing to crack down on illegal immigration and crime.

"Kathy Hochul, she was out campaigning with Mamdani this week," Blakeman said, according to the New York Post. "And I say that’s great."

"Please take him everywhere you go, Kathy, because I want to remind people that this un-American, antisemite, anti-Christian mayor is running the Democratic Party and Kathy Hochul is nothing more than his puppet," he continued.

Hochul has been campaigning alongside Mamdani, including at a recent rally in Brooklyn.

The political alliance between the mayor and the governor, who is seeking reelection to a second full term, comes as democratic socialists have gained greater influence in New York politics.

Along with Mamdani's victory in last year's mayoral election, candidates aligned with Mamdani and New York's democratic socialist movement scored major victories in congressional primaries this year, including the defeats of two Democratic incumbents.

Blakeman has previously targeted Hochul and Mamdani over public safety, accusing both Democrats of failing to address crime in New York City.

"I think the governor did a good job," Mamdani told reporters when asked about Hochul's response to Blakeman's criticism. "And I'll never fault someone for not being able to catch all of it because that is a fool's errand when it comes to Bruce Blakeman."

Россиянка отдохнула зимой на курорте Европы и назвала разочаровавшие ее вещи

Российская туристка отдохнула зимой на популярном курорте Европы — испанском острове Тенерифе — и назвала разочаровавшие ее вещи. Своими впечатлениями она поделилась с порталом «Тонкости туризма», ее отзыв опубликован на платформе «Дзен».

В частности, читательница издания отметила, что обещанное вечное лето оказалось «бутафорским» — вода в бассейнах была ледяная, а солнце то и дело уходило за массивные тучи. Кроме того, вместо белого песка на пляже лежал серый вулканический песок, от которого чернели ноги.

«Если вы хотите сфотографироваться у воды, вам придется потом обрезать снимок или заранее приближать экран, чтобы ступни не попали в кадр. Мокрые ноги, сплошь покрытые стекающей с них черной грязью, вряд ли сумеет исправить даже последняя версия фотошопа. Получается фото "Трубочист на отдыхе"», — поиронизировала автор отзыва.

Кроме того, россиянка пожаловалась, что в январе на острове темнеет в шесть вечера, и нужно искать другие развлечения, помимо пляжного отдыха.

«Лучше уж лучше подкопить денег и летом провести не одну, а две недели на настоящем море с правильным пляжем и теплыми бассейнами», — заключила туристка.

Ранее российский тревел-блогер побывал в испанском Магалуфе и описал его фразой «рай для холостяков». По его словам, улицы популярного пляжного курорта Европы переполнены одинокими девушками в приподнятом настроении.

Врач назвал связанные с онемением пальцев болезни

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Онемение — это сигнал о том, что нерв или сосуд где-то страдают, и этот сигнал нельзя игнорировать, потому что за ним могут стоять серьезные заболевания, предупредил старший преподаватель кафедры хирургии медицинского факультета Государственного университета просвещения Александр Умнов. Комментарий он дал «Ленте.ру».

Так, по его словам, может проявляться синдром запястного канала — это когда срединный нерв сдавливается в узком костно-связочном туннеле запястья. При этом врач описал типичную картину — немеют большой, указательный и средний пальцы, сильнее ночью и по утрам, из-за чего люди просыпаются и встряхивают руку. Умнов предупредил что без лечения развивается атрофия мышц кисти и стойкая потеря чувствительности, а на запущенной стадии потребуется операция. Онемение мизинца и безымянного пальца может быть следствием сдавления локтевого нерва.

«Это может быть болезнь Рейно и серьезные проблемы с сосудами. В этом случае пальцы белеют, потом синеют на холоде или от стресса, а при согревании «отпускает» с болью — это спазм мелких артерий. Сама по себе болезнь Рейно довольно неприятна и опасна при выраженном атеросклерозе или воспалении сосудов. Как сопутствующие симптомы могут проявляться резкая смена температуры, смена цвета кистей, исчезающий пульс, мраморность кожи», — предупредил хирург.

Промедление в таких случаях грозит трофическими нарушениями вплоть до некроза

Также Хирург указал что диабетическая полинейропатия начинается именно с кончиков пальцев, при ней идет симметричное онемение, которое постепенно поднимается выше. Опасность, по его словам, в том, что человек перестает чувствовать боль, ожоги и раны — а это прямой путь к незаживающим язвам и, в худшем случае, к ампутации.

«Онемение также может говорить о проблемах в шее и позвоночнике. Грыжи и протрузии шейного отдела сдавливают нервные корешки, и тогда онемение идёт по всей руке — от шеи до пальцев, часто со стреляющей болью и слабостью определенных групп мышц. Запущенная компрессия корешка приводит к стойкому парезу нерва — не всегда рука может заработать снова», — сказал Умнов.

При этом самым опасным является внезапное онемение, особенно вместе со слабостью одной половины тела, перекосом лица, нарушением речи или сильной головной болью. Умнов объяснил, что это признаки инсульта или транзиторной ишемической атаки, когда речь идет на минуты. То же самое, по его словам, касается онемения после травмы — перелом или вывих мог повредить нерв, а разорванный нерв восстанавливается только при своевременной операции.

Ранее хирург Александр Умнов рассказал о приводящих к простуде привычках. Одной из них он назвал сон с открытыми окнами и форточками.

From affordability to AI, the Issues driving the US Midterms

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sign up now: Get ST's newsletters delivered to your inbox

With US President Donald Trump in the White House, his party is likely to bear the brunt of voter anger.

Ahead of the US midterm elections on Nov 3, national polls show that most Americans are deeply dissatisfied with the direction of the country.

Economic anxiety is at the fore of voters’ frustration. Many Americans say they are struggling to afford basic living expenses.

Also weighing on voters is an unpopular war with Iran that has disrupted global oil supplies and driven up the cost of petrol, home heating and diesel.

Other top concerns include healthcare costs, data centres, immigration and government corruption.

The midterms, held halfway through a president’s four-year term, determine control of Congress.

All 435 seats in the House of Representatives and about a third of Senate seats are up for election.

Republicans currently hold narrow majorities in both chambers.

With President Donald Trump in the White House, his party has unified control of the federal government, which means it is likely to bear the brunt of voter anger.

This election cycle, spending on political advertising is projected to reach a record US$11.6 billion (S$14.8 billion), surpassing even the 2024 presidential election.

Republicans are outspending Democrats. Still, polls suggest Democrats are favoured to win control of the House and have a meaningful shot at the Senate.

Here’s a look at some of the top issues that are animating the elections.

The economy is almost always the dominant issue in US elections.

About 84% of registered voters say the economy is “very important” to their midterm election vote, according to a Pew Research Center survey conducted from Sept 28 to Oct 4.

By a 51%-45% margin, voters say they trust Democrats more than Republicans to do a good job handling the economy, according to a New York Times/Siena poll conducted in mid-September.

“Affordability” is a major buzzword of the 2026 election, and voters overwhelmingly view the national economy through the prism of household expenses.

Average petrol prices were US$4.29 a gallon on Oct 24, up from about US$3.06 in 2025; mortgage rates in September crossed 7% for the first time in almost two years and have largely continued to climb higher. Those costs are increasingly straining people’s budgets.

Democratic candidates blame Trump’s tariffs and the Iran war for rising prices and are campaigning to end both.

Republican candidates tout Trump’s 2025 tax-cut law, which granted tax breaks for tipped employees, overtime work and Social Security retirement income.

About 65% of Americans disapprove of Trump’s handling of US military action in Iran, compared with 33% who approve, according to Pew, and Democratic candidates have blasted the war as unpopular, costly and open-ended.

The war has also created fissures within the Republican Party. Some Republican candidates have privately or publicly broken with Trump, who disappointed some supporters after campaigning for president on an “America First” platform and promising to avoid new military conflicts.

Texas Attorney-General Ken Paxton, the Republican nominee in one of the most consequential Senate races, said in a leaked audio recording that the war and high prices were a drag on his campaign.

Other Republican candidates have called for the war to be brought to an immediate end.

“I have been one of the leading and deciding votes against the continuation of the war in Iran because I take that seriously,” Representative Tom Barrett of Michigan, who is in one of the most competitive House elections in the nation, said in a campaign debate on Oct 6.

A deep distrust of government courses through the electorate.

A record-high 89% of US adults view government corruption as widespread, according to a Gallup poll released in early September.

The surge was particularly pronounced among Democratic voters: 91% said government corruption was widespread in 2026, up from 57% in 2024, when Trump’s predecessor Joe Biden, a Democrat, was in the White House.

Democratic candidates and some Republicans have linked government corruption to high costs, arguing that federal lawmakers and officials are elites enriching themselves at the expense of ordinary Americans struggling to make ends meet.

There are growing bipartisan calls to ban stock trading by members of Congress.

Other government accountability proposals offered by candidates include term limits for members of Congress and Supreme Court justices and limitations on members of Congress becoming lobbyists when they leave office.

Democrats have pointed to Trump’s stock trading and earnings from cryptocurrency-related ventures while in office as examples of the president using his position for personal financial gain.

Democrats and some Republicans condemned his Justice Department’s now-abandoned plan to create a US$1.8 billion “anti-weaponisation” fund to compensate victims who claim they were targeted by the Biden administration for political or personal reasons.

The Justice Department’s handling of files related to the late sex offender and financier Jeffrey Epstein angered victim advocates and sparked allegations that the Trump administration is protecting powerful individuals.

In a Pew survey conducted from Sept 28 to Oct 4, 85% of Democratic voters and 69% of Republican voters said the future of democracy was very important to their vote in the midterms.

Democratic leaders have repeatedly characterised Trump as an existential threat to democracy.

They have pointed to his repeated false claims that his 2020 presidential election loss to Biden was “rigged” and that voting by mail is riddled with fraud.

Party leaders accuse Trump of employing authoritarian-like tactics in intimidating political opponents and minority groups with hostile language on social media and at political events.

Individuals who leak government information to the media “should be shot”, Trump said on Oct 14 at a campaign rally in Arizona.

“Our President is shredding our democracy” and “Republicans in Congress simply sit idly by, and don’t stop the chaos”, California Democratic state Senator Mike McGuire, who is favoured to win a House seat on Nov 3, said at a campaign debate on Oct 6.

However, Democratic candidates in the most competitive elections have publicly downplayed a potential third impeachment of Trump and centred their campaign platforms on lowering costs.

“Any sort of corruption has to be rooted out,” Representative Chris Pappas, a New Hampshire Democrat running for the Senate, said on CBS’ “Face the Nation” programme on Oct 11. “But we have to focus like a laser on the economic challenges that people are living right now.”

About six in ten voters are worried about healthcare costs, and about half say it is “extremely important” for candidates in the midterm elections to talk about that issue, according to a KFF Health Tracking Poll released on Oct 6.

That survey also found that voters, by a 42% to 22% margin, trust the Democratic Party to do a better job handling healthcare costs than the Republican Party.

That 20-point advantage for Democrats is a wider gap for the party than in a survey from KFF released in April.

Many progressive Democratic candidates have campaigned for a government-run, universal health insurance system that would replace private health insurance.

Some Democratic centrists seeking politically competitive seats, such as Representative Marie Gluesenkamp Perez of Washington state and former Representative Elaine Luria of Virginia, say that sweeping proposal is unrealistic.

They say the party should focus instead on incremental improvements to the Affordable Care Act, Democrats’ healthcare overhaul signed into law by then-President Barack Obama in 2010.

Democrats have campaigned against the Republican-led 2025 tax law’s reductions in spending on Medicaid, a joint federal and state programme that serves low-income and disabled Americans.

Republican candidates have focused more on targeting healthcare fraud. They describe a universal government-run system as tantamount to a government takeover of healthcare that would hike taxes.

A surge in migrant crossings into the US from Mexico during Biden’s presidency helped power Trump’s 2024 election victory over Biden’s vice-president, Kamala Harris.

But a sweeping federal immigration crackdown, including ramped-up deportations, in Trump’s second term triggered a public backlash and calls to abolish or scale back the US Immigration and Customs Enforcement agency – including in states and districts that favoured Trump in 2024.

In some of those districts, Hispanic voters, who shifted sharply towards Trump in 2024 but have since moved back toward Democrats, can swing election outcomes.

They overwhelmingly disapprove of Trump’s handling of immigration (by 69% to 30%) and border security (by 58% to 39%), according to an NBC News/Telemundo poll conducted from Sept 21 to 28.

Most Republican candidates have defended Trump’s immigration policies and warned voters that Democrats would return to Biden-era “open borders”.

Some Democratic candidates running in Republican-leaning districts have charted a moderate path on the issue, mixing support for strict limits on illegal entry with proposals to expand legal pathways for undocumented immigrants to achieve citizenship.

Adam Hamilton, a Kansas Democratic pastor vying to unseat Republican Senator Roger Marshall in an agricultural state that relies heavily on immigrant labour, called Biden’s border-security policies a “disaster”.

Representative Don Davis, a North Carolina Democrat seeking re-election in a district that favoured Trump by 12 percentage points in 2024, praised the President’s border enforcement policy but said the government needs to do more to increase screenings at ports of entry to detect fentanyl and other contraband.

AI data centres, the warehouse-like computing facilities that support artificial intelligence systems, have aroused intense opposition and become a bipartisan flashpoint in the 2026 election.

Many Americans express concerns about data centres’ energy, water and land consumption impacts.

Additionally, the broader public unease and pushback against AI often manifests as opposition to data centres.

Democrats are more likely than Republicans to be concerned about the role of AI in daily life and its potential to take jobs, according to Pew.

Trump supports the expansion of data centres and AI, arguing that the US needs to outpace China in an emerging technology that is reshaping society.

Many candidates for Congress from both major parties have emphasised their opposition to the construction of new data centres in campaign advertising.

They include former senator Sherrod Brown of Ohio, the Democratic nominee in a Senate election that could sway control of the chamber.

Brown attacked incumbent Republican Jon Husted for helping lure data centres to the state with tax incentives when he was Ohio’s lieutenant governor.

Transgender rights is a prominent wedge issue in the 2026 campaign, just as it was in 2024, when Trump’s presidential campaign benefited from targeting Harris’ support for transgender inmates having access to gender reassignment surgery.

“Kamala is for they/them. President Trump is for you,” was the tagline in a Trump TV ad that Democratic and Republican campaign officials said was effective.

Republican ads in the midterm elections emphasise opposition to transgender athletes’ participation in women and girls’ sports.

According to a Sept 18-24 survey from The 19th/YouGov, 45% of US adults are more likely to vote for a candidate who supports banning transgender athletes from women’s and girls’ sports teams, compared with 20% who favour candidates opposing bans.

Some Democratic candidates in culturally conservative areas have aired campaign ads stating their opposition to transgender athletes competing in women’s sports.

Older Americans vote in disproportionate numbers in midterm elections, and many of them are concerned about the stability of the Social Security social insurance programme, which provides monthly income to retired workers, surviving family members of deceased workers, and disabled individuals.

The main fund that pays for retirement and survivor benefits is projected to exhaust its accumulated reserves in 2032, and incoming tax revenue will only be able to pay 78% of benefits if Congress does not intervene.

Social Security is funded primarily by a compulsory payroll tax that applies to the first US$184,500 of workers’ earnings in 2026, and surveys show widespread public support for raising or eliminating that cap to shore up the programme’s finances. BLOOMBERG

Venezuela opposition leader María Corina Machado returns to country

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Updated on: October 25, 2026 / 10:27 PM EDT / CBS News

Venezuelan opposition leader and Nobel laureate María Corina Machado is back in her home country after nearly a year in exile, her team confirmed to CBS News on Sunday.

"I am here. With you," Machado posted in Spanish on X.

Later Sunday night, she posted in Spanish, "Finally, Caracas, I'm here!!" along with video of supporters enthusiastically greeting her arrival.

Machado's senior adviser David Smoalnsky said in a statement to CBS that her return is "a major step toward a democratic transition and free, fair, and verifiable presidential elections in the near future."

"Machado returns to stand with Venezuelans in their pursuit of freedom, democracy, and justice, and to help advance a peaceful, inclusive, and sustainable transition leading to free, fair, and verifiable presidential elections, with guarantees for all," he said, thanking President Trump, the U.S. government and others for "their continued leadership and support."

Machado covertly snuck out of the South American country in December with a private U.S. rescue team to accept the 2025 Nobel Peace Prize in Norway, her first public appearance in 11 months. The event was a month before the U.S. military deposed the country's then-President Nicolás Maduro.

Machado, who was barred by Maduro's government from exiting Venezuela, is considered a fugitive by the Venezuelan government, which accuses her of calling for military intervention against the country.

Edmundo Gonzalez, a former presidential candidate for Machado's movement who has been living in Spain, confirmed her return in his own social media post and said he would be next to her "very soon."

"Today is a very important day for Venezuelans. Our sovereign voice returns with her to lead and guide this process!" he wrote. "You know it, this is until the end."

After the news of her arrival, Machado was seen at a church in the state of Lara, west of Caracas. Dozens of supporters shook her hand and hugged her, with cellphones aloft to record her return, the Associated Press reported.

"María! María!" someone cried out, as another shouted, "Welcome to Venezuela!"

"Long live freedom, damn it!" a man yelled.

One woman wiped away tears and called it "a miracle" while another woman's hands flew to her face as she saw Machado enter the church.

Asked by reporters Sunday about Machado's return to Venezuela, President Trump said, "I don't know anything about it, but I think she's a very nice woman. She gave me her Nobel Prize. So how can I say anything else? And I hope she's safe. She's a very nice woman. But we're doing really well in Venezuela."

Machado made several failed attempts to return to her home country, including during the aftermath of the devastating June 24 double earthquakes.

In September, the opposition leader was denied entry into Venezuela three times over 24 hours, Smoalnsky said in a statement shared with CBS News' Margaret Brennan and other journalists.

"She has attempted to enter the country by air and sea but has been turned away each time," he said at the time.

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Sunday the conditions that allowed for Machado's return to Venezuelan soil are "the direct result of the U.S. support reconciliation talks."

"The United States expects the freedoms of all sectors of Venezuelan politics to continue to be respected," he wrote on X. "Likewise, we expect all political actors to support these talks which represent the best chance Venezuela has had in thirty years to peacefully achieve not just a free and fair election but an enduring democracy."

Machado's homecoming comes as doubts over Venezuela's democratic future — and the U.S.'s role in it — deepen.

Since Maduro's ouster, Venezuela has been run by acting President Delcy Rodríguez, a longtime Maduro ally who received the Trump administration's blessing to succeed him.

Rodríguez has exceeded the time she was authorized to serve as acting president following Maduro's capture and no election is scheduled.

Machado and her allies have been demanding a date for a presidential election in Venezuela, but U.S. officials have emphasized economic stabilization first.

Olivia Gazis and The Associated Press contributed to this report.

Britons have been warned to brace for more rain on Monday after Storm Boelo battered the nation.

Winds of up to 85mph and heavy downpours wreaked havoc over the weekend, with travel disrupted and an estimated 160,000 homes across the country left without power.

But unfortunately the extreme conditions are not set to subside just yet, with the third storm of the season potentially on the horizon.

The Met Office predicts that rain will once again 'sweep across the north-east' of Britain on Monday, while Tuesday will remain 'windy with rain'.

A forecast for Monday reads: 'On Monday rain will sweep northeast across the UK, with strengthening winds bringing gales in the west later. After a cold start for some, it will feel increasingly mild in the west.'

Storm Chloe is the next available name on the 2026-27 storm list and, should the weather intensify enough to trigger an amber or red warning, the Met Office will officially declare that the third storm of the season has hit.

The forecaster's amber warning which signalled Storm Boelo came to an end at 9pm on Sunday evening.

Met Office forecaster Aidan McGivern said: 'It's a very similar situation to Boelo on Monday and Tuesday.

'In other words, the strongest winds expected to affect northern parts of the UK and expected to arrive on Tuesday.

'Risks of 70mph wind gusts, yellow warning in force, but of course we'll be watching this one very closely.'

Only days after the first storm of the autumn hit the UK, Storm Boelo caused chaos over the weekend.

Gusts of 60-70mph battered northern parts of Britain early on Sunday with the most extreme conditions recorded in coastal areas. In Machrihanish, a small village on Scotland's west coast, wind speeds as quick as 85mph were recorded.

A 60-year-old man was killed when he was hit by a tree blown over in gale-force winds in Dumfries before, only hours later, it was confirmed that a man in his 20s had also died on the same day after his car was crushed by a falling tree in Mill Hill, north London.

Footage from Dalmarnock, County Durham, showed a stream of water flooding through the town early this morning, forcing dozens of shops into closure. Meanwhile the town of Largs, in Ayrshire, was left submerged following flooding overnight.

A store owner in the Scottish town was brought to tears after her property was ruined by the rain, forcing her to throw away her stock.

Toni Dawson, who runs Geraldo's ice cream and gift shop, told Sky News: 'Literally within half-an-hour hour the water is coming up so fast and the main street is flooded.

'We're trying to clear out as much of the debris as we can and board up the front of the shop and put some sandbags there.'

The Met Office said the succession of weather systems hitting the UK is being driven by the jet stream which is currently 'particularly active'.

Statistics released tonight by the Energy Networks Association (ENA) revealed that some 161,400 homes across the country have been left without power, while more than 800,000 have suffered power cuts since Storm Austen hit.

The ENA said: 'The networks have been focussed on reconnecting customers this morning, though the continuing severe weather in some regions is limiting network engineers' opportunities to safely start the work of restoring customers' power today and access to certain areas remains difficult.'

Earlier, Northern Powergrid warned customers to 'err on the side of caution and consider seeking alternative accommodation' due to the risk of power outages.

In Aberfeldy, Northern Ireland, two women were injured and rushed to hospital after being hit by a fallen tree.

Meanwhile, hundreds of miles away from the eye of Storm Boelo, two people were taken to hospital in Streatham, south London, yesterday after a large section of scaffolding was brought down by strong winds.

Sylvester Stallone hat die Besetzung seiner Tochter Scarlet Rose Stallone in der Serie "Tulsa King" verteidigt. Im Interview mit "USA Today" räumte der 80-Jährige ein, dass sein berühmter Name der 24-Jährigen Vorteile verschaffe. Daran störe er sich allerdings nicht.

"Zuerst heißt es: 'Nepotismus ist schrecklich'", sagte der "Rocky"-Star. Er sehe das anders. "Ach bitte, man ist kein guter Elternteil, wenn man nicht ein bisschen Nepotismus betrieben hat. Dann sind einem die eigenen Kinder offensichtlich egal", erklärte Stallone. "Natürlich ist das Nepotismus, du Idiot. Dafür sind wir doch Eltern. Wir wollen das Beste für unsere Kinder. Also verschaffen wir ihnen natürlich jeden Vorteil."

Ganz allein könne er den Erfolg seiner Tochter allerdings nicht sicherstellen, betont Stallone. Wie weit sie es bringe, liege letztlich nicht in seiner Hand. "Aber wenn man die Tür öffnen kann, werden wir sehen, ob sie das Zeug dazu haben, hindurchzugehen", so Stallone. Am Ende stelle sich womöglich sogar die Frage: "Wie komme ich hier wieder raus?" Man solle vorsichtig mit dem sein, was man sich wünscht, warnte er.

Dass seine Tochter durch die familiären Verbindungen leichter an die Rolle gekommen sein könnte, bestreitet Stallone nicht. "Die Leute werden sagen: 'Sie hat den Job bekommen, das ist zu einfach.' 📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Фото: Victor Lisitsyn / Global Look Press

В первые дни после удаления зуба, когда идет заживление, важно соблюдать запреты и ограничения. Об этом рассказала стоматолог Динара Аль-Абед в беседе с «Газетой.Ru».

По ее словам, в этот период важно не повредить образовавшийся в лунке кровяной сгусток, который защищает рану и помогает заживлению. Из-за этого необходимо отказаться от активного полоскания и курения на срок, который посоветовал стоматолог, а также соблюдать другие ограничения.

«После операции лучше сделать паузу в занятиях спортом, посещении бани и сауны, отказаться от горячей ванны и очень горячего душа. Физическая нагрузка и перегрев усиливают кровообращение и могут спровоцировать повторное кровотечение из лунки», — подчеркнула Аль-Абед.

Она напомнила, что небольшой дискомфорт и умеренный отек являются нормальной реакцией организма. При этом если боль усиливается, следует обратиться к врачу, отметила Аль-Абед.
