Scientists overcome a hidden obstacle to more powerful quantum computers

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Silicon spin qubits could play a major role in the future of quantum computing because they can be produced using the same semiconductor manufacturing methods that make today's computer chips. However, researchers have faced a significant challenge. When multiple silicon qubits are controlled at the same time, their operations tend to become less accurate. Building practical, large quantum computers will require scientists to control many qubits simultaneously while keeping errors to a minimum.

Researchers at the RIKEN Center for Emergent Matter Science and their collaborators have now made important progress toward solving this problem. In a study published in Nature Physics, the team demonstrated highly accurate control of several silicon spin qubits operating simultaneously. Their experiments used an array of five qubits connected to a shared microwave control line. By identifying a major cause of errors during simultaneous operation, the scientists developed a technique to reduce those errors and maintain precision.

Leon Camenzind, a RIKEN research scientist and co-first author who supervised the experiments, explains the significance of the results: "This shows that high-fidelity control can be maintained even when several qubits are operated at the same time and represents an important step toward scaling up silicon-based quantum processors."

Scientists Identify a Hidden Source of Quantum Computing Errors

Unlike conventional computers, which store information as bits representing either 0 or 1, quantum computers use quantum bits, or qubits, that can exist in combinations of both states. Silicon spin qubits store this information using the spin of individual electrons confined within silicon. Performing reliable quantum calculations requires extremely precise control over these electron spins.

Microwave signals provide that control, but operating multiple qubits simultaneously can create unwanted effects that interfere with their accuracy. The researchers traced much of this problem to a phenomenon called the AC Stark shift.

This effect occurs when microwave control signals alter a qubit's effective energy levels, producing changes in the phase of its quantum state. Those phase shifts can disrupt the intended operation of individual qubits, particularly when several are being controlled at once.

To counteract the problem, the team created a calibration method that compensates for these unwanted shifts. Rather than separately calibrating every possible combination of qubits, the technique relies on calibrations performed between pairs of qubits. This makes the approach more practical for systems containing larger numbers of quantum bits.

"Rather than requiring an entirely separate calibration procedure for every possible combination of qubits, our method uses calibrations between pairs of qubits to compensate for the unwanted phase shifts," explains Yi-Hsien Wu, the first author who carried out the experiments as part of his PhD research.

Quantum Qubits Achieve Accuracy Above 99.99%

The researchers combined their calibration technique with an improved device design and carefully adjusted microwave control pulses. Their results showed that exceptionally high accuracy could be maintained even when multiple qubits were operating simultaneously.

When the five qubits were tested individually, each achieved single-qubit gate fidelity well above 99.99%, with the highest approaching 99.999%. Fidelity describes how closely a quantum operation matches its intended result.

The team then tested how well the system performed when several qubits were active together. With as many as three qubits operating simultaneously, fidelity remained above 99.99%. Even when all five qubits were controlled at once, accuracy stayed at approximately 99.9%, approaching the levels needed for practical fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Although the calibration method greatly reduced errors, it did not eliminate them entirely. The researchers found that the remaining loss of accuracy was linked to drive-induced decoherence, an effect caused by the greater microwave power required to operate multiple qubits simultaneously.

Unlike the unwanted phase shifts corrected by the new technique, decoherence involves the loss of the delicate quantum coherence needed for reliable operations.

According to Camenzind, "This is a limitation, but further improvements to device design could help mitigate this source of error."

A New Path Toward Larger Quantum Processors

The findings show that controlling several silicon spin qubits at the same time does not necessarily require a major sacrifice in accuracy. By correcting a key source of errors, the researchers have demonstrated a promising approach for operating more qubits through shared microwave control lines.

This is especially important for the development of large quantum processors. Giving every qubit its own independent control system would make increasingly complex devices difficult to manage. Shared control lines could offer a more practical way to expand these systems while maintaining the precision needed for quantum calculations.

"By addressing a key source of control errors through pairwise calibration," says Camenzind. "Our study supports the use of shared control lines as a practical approach to scaling up quantum processors. Combined with silicon's compatibility with established semiconductor manufacturing, we believe that this advance could help pave the way toward larger quantum processors capable of performing increasingly complex computations."

The broader objective is to develop quantum computers that can reliably perform calculations despite the errors and disturbances that affect quantum systems. Such machines, known as fault-tolerant quantum computers, will require precise, dependable control over large numbers of qubits.

"Our long-term goal is to build a fault-tolerant quantum computer based on silicon spin qubits, and reliable parallel control is an essential step toward that goal," adds Seigo Tarucha, project manager of a silicon quantum computing project under JST's Moonshot Goal 6.

Lula logra una remontada histórica en Brasil y da la victoria a la izquierda frente al avance ultra de los Bolsonaro

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

El actual mandatario será presidente por cuarta vez tras vencer con cuatro millones de votos más que el candidato del clan de ultraderecha y se consolida como resistencia ante Trump

Lula escribe un nuevo capítulo épico en una trayectoria legendaria. El izquierdista Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, de 80 años, presidente de Brasil y uno de los políticos más influyentes de América Latina en este siglo, ha conseguido la ansiada remontada este domingo. Lula ha derrotado al senador Flávio Bolsonaro, de 45 años, heredero político del clan ultraderechista, con el 51,7% de los votos frente a los 48,2%, es decir, cuatro millones de votos más. Es el primer mandatario que vence tras perder la primera vuelta en una elección en el gigante latinoamericano. Solo el emperador Pedro II y Getúlio Vargas han gobernado por más tiempo. El líder del Partido de los Trabajadores (PT) conquista un cuarto mandato, para los pies a la extrema derecha y confirma su fortaleza, pese al odio que genera en buena parte de sus compatriotas. Lula, que el martes cumple 81 años, gobernará un Brasil profundamente dividido y se consolida como resistencia del progresismo ante Donald Trump.

“Quiero agradecer al pueblo brasileño esta generosidad que tiene conmigo hace tanto tiempo, agradecer a la juventud que salió a la calle a defender la democracia, a los mayores, que fueron a votar”, dijo en su discurso tras la victoria, en un hotel de São Paulo. “Esta elección es la derrota negacionismo y de la mentira” y “demuestra que no somos el patio trasero de nadie”. El mandatario, que esta semana estrenó el primer móvil de su vida, reconoció el papel de la movilización en redes sociales en el resultado. “Vencimos la intromisión de fuera, de las plataformas digitales”, afirmó, en referencia al favoritismo de Instagram y WhatsApp hacia Bolsonaro.

Aún estaba Lula hablando al país cuando Flávio Bolsonaro pidió la anulación de los comicios, en una comparecencia en Brasilia. “Es imposible reconocer el resultado”, afirmó.

La victoria llegó tras un conteo de infarto después de que la votación se extendiese dos horas extra por orden de un juez. Lula basó su demanda a la justicia en los problemas con el transporte público, en restricciones impuestas en algunas ciudades. “No vamos a permitir robos, ni alcaldes sin escrúpulos”, dijo el mandatario. “Esa gente tiene que aprender a respetar la democracia”. La ampliación del horario de votación, ordenada por un juez al filo del horario de cierre oficial de las urnas, generó caos y confusión. A las siete de la tarde (hora de Brasilia) cerraron los colegios y comenzó el recuento con el país en vilo ante el incierto desenlace.

Ese caos alimentará las dudas sobre la votación. “Si no nos dan un golpe, ganamos”, decía en un colegio electoral de São Paulo Rafael Nunes, de 40 años, ingeniero electricista, vestido con una camiseta amarilla de la canarinha con el 22 de Bolsonaro en la espalda. En esta campaña Flávio Bolsonaro ha sembrado algunas dudas sobre la seguridad del sistema de votación, pero con menos intensidad que su padre.

El antiguo obrero y sindicalista que sacó a millones de brasileños de la pobreza peleó por este triunfo hasta el último aliento, pero gobernar será un infierno. Afronta un desafío colosal. Pero aprendió de su madre, doña Lindu, a no rendirse jamás. Tampoco es rencoroso. Gobernará un país partido en dos mitades y con un Congreso en el que el Partido Liberal (PL) de los Bolsonaro tiene una mayoría récord y la capacidad de moldear el Tribunal Supremo a base de nombramientos. Además, Brasil está rodeado casi por completo de adversarios ideológicos (con la excepción de la progresista Uruguay) y Donald Trump, empeñado en ampliar la influencia de Estados Unidos en América Latina.

Bajo el lema “Brasil está listo para más”, Lula consigue un nuevo mandato. Esta era la última elección de su vida, de ahí el aliciente de ganarla, porque la ley le impide presentarse a la reelección en 2030. En ese contexto y porque tendrá 85 años cuando termine el mandato, cobra especial relevancia su vicepresidente, Geraldo Alkcmin, un histórico del centro derecha, un católico conservador de 73 años.

El líder del PT promete proseguir la tarea de reconstrucción nacional que emprendió, con Alckcmin de número dos, tras derrotar en 2022 a Bolsonaro padre, condenado por intentar dar un golpe de Estado. El mandatario planteó esta campaña como una batalla en defensa de la democracia y contra los Bolsonaro, contra el oscurantismo, las políticas excluyentes y los peligros que para la mitad de los brasileños representa el clan.

En campaña Bolsonaro prometió respetar los resultados de las urnas, queda por ver si cumple o emula a su padre, que sin un reconocimiento oficial de la derrota dio, sin embargo, inicio a la transición. Pero cuando Lula llevaba una semana en el poder, una turba bolsonarista protagonizó el ataque más grave contra la democracia desde el fin de la dictadura.

Leandra Sena, arquitecta de 45 años, viene de votar “por la democracia, porque es todo o nada”. Fatima Aparecida da Silva, 69 años, llegó a votar en São Paulo con una camiseta roja y muy angustiada: “Si gana Flávio, volverá la represión, que ya conocemos de la dictadura”, opina. Inmediatamente, apunta que Lula no es perfecto, pero “mis hijos son lo que son gracias a sus políticas públicas”. La hija que le acompaña es médica.

Al otro lado de la avenida Consolação, vota Kelly, 47 años. Viste una camiseta que combina a Jair y Flávio Bolsonaro en un solo rostro. Y, encima, la palabra “derecha” con el lema nacional Orden y Progreso. “Voté por el padre y ahora por el hijo. Por la seguridad y por la familia”.

El triunfo de Lula también significa un fuerte revés al proyecto de Trump de aumentar la influencia de Estados Unidos al sur del río Bravo. La racha de victorias conservadoras, siete en siete elecciones, duró hasta este domingo. Los dos países más poblados de América Latina, Brasil y México, ejercerán de contrapeso progresista al republicano y a sus vecinos. Con Lula, Brasil seguirá a la cabeza de la defensa del multilateralismo frente a la ley del más fuerte que despliega Trump. Y mantendrá el ánimo de tener relaciones fructíferas tanto con Washington como con Pekín.

Esta rotunda victoria llega impulsada por los abstencionistas, 33 millones de electores que no votaron en primera vuelta. Lula instó a los suyos a luchar voto a voto para que los mayores de 70 años y los adolescentes de 16 y 17 años ejercieran un derecho que, en su caso, no es obligatorio.

En esta contienda electoral, disputada hasta el extremo, Lula denunció el favoritismo de Meta, dueña de Instagram, WhatsApp y Facebook, para favorecer a su rival ultra. E incluso la virgen de Aparecida protagonizó una polémica basada en una falsa acusación contra Bolsonaro.

Para conquistar la reelección, Lula anunció una larga lista de medidas de impacto. Iban dirigidas al electorado femenino, más numeroso e indeciso que el masculino. Lula aumentó un 15% la cuantía de Bolsa Familia —una paga mensual que reciben 20 millones de familias pobres y es el emblema histórico del PT—, prohibió por decreto las apuestas deportivas y los casinos en línea para combatir la epidemia de deudas y problemas mentales, prometió inyecciones de adelgazamiento gratuitas para los obesos y garantizó hasta seis meses de salario a las víctimas de violencia doméstica.

La campaña estuvo marcada por un empate pétreo entre el presidente izquierdista y el senador ultra en las encuestas casi desde el principio hasta la víspera de la votación. Los dos debates electorales no fueron decisivos. La campaña arrancó en ambiente de apatía total, pero alcanzó una formidable movilización en la recta final, especialmente notable en la izquierda tras la conmoción de que Bolsonaro fuera el más votado en primera vuelta, hace tres semanas.

Lula y su equipo eran conscientes de que ir a una segunda vuelta era un grave riesgo. Mermaba muy considerablemente las probabilidades de victoria. Como el líder del PT era el candidato único de casi toda la izquierda, las opciones de atraer nuevos votos eran reducidas, pero lo ha logrado al espolear una gigantesca movilización. Con ese músculo ha logrado superar a una derecha que, tras la primera vuelta, se apresuró a arropar a Bolsonaro para sacar del poder a Lula, al que medio país odia.

Sharon Osbourne: 'I visit Ozzy's grave every day'

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Sharon Osbourne spoke to BBC News as the family prepares to release a film about Ozzy's final concert with Black Sabbath

It's been 15 months since Ozzy Osbourne died, at the age of 76, at his home in Buckinghamshire.

Although he had suffered from health problems for years, including Parkinson's disease and a fractured vertebra, his death was a shock.

Less than three weeks beforehand, he had been on stage in Birmingham, playing a triumphant farewell concert.

"He was just so, so happy," recalls his wife Sharon.

"He was online, going through the reviews and saying, 'There's something terribly wrong', and I'm like, 'Why?'

"He goes, 'I didn't get slagged by anybody. Why are people being nice to me?'

On the morning he died, Ozzy had woken up early and gone downstairs for his usual morning workout. Shortly afterwards, he had a heart attack. An air ambulance was called and paramedics tried to save him. But it was too late.

"We'd spoken in the night. Ozzy had woken in the night and he asked me to kiss him twice, and I did.

"And then he said, 'Hold me, hold me tight,' and I did.

"And then the next thing I know, it's early in the morning, and someone comes rushing into the room saying, 'Come down now'.

Sharon and Ozzy Osbourne, pictured in 2017 after renewing their wedding vows

"You've lost the love of your life, so you don't just wake up and go, 'I'm fine now. I'm cured'.

"You never get over it. But you learn to live with it, and that's what I'm trying to do. So you either sit in a room and don't come out, or you accept it, befriend it, and that's it."

The affection among Ozzy's fans was apparent before the singer's funeral. Thousands of people lined the streets of Birmingham, laying floral tributes and singing Black Sabbath songs as Ozzy's body was transported through his home city in a hearse topped by a purple floral tribute in the shape of a cross.

Sharon says he would have been mystified by the outpouring of love.

"He'd have been like, 'What's the matter with you guys? Go home!'" she says.

But for the family, the public response was a huge source of comfort.

Daughter Kelly said last year: "I never could have imagined it to be as helpful as it has been to know that we're not alone in our grief and our sadness, and that the rest of the world loved him as much as we did."

Sharon says the fans' cards and messages were all collected, sorted and photographed; while the flowers were composted for the family garden, where Ozzy is buried.

"There was no way I could put Ozzy in a public graveyard," she says.

"I've seen what happens to other people in the public eye. There's no peace because people want to come and they want to pay their respects. That's an ongoing thing, and I wanted Ozzy to rest and just be."

"Of course, every day. I can see him from my window."

She still talks to him regularly, she says. Asked what she discusses, she puts a finger to her lips and gives a gentle "shh".

Ozzy's family - including children Jack and Kelly - stopped to read the tributes during his funeral procession last July

Ozzy and Sharon's relationship spanned more than 50 years.

They met in 1970 and married in 1982. Their time together could be turbulent - he was arrested for attempting to murder her in 1989 (she dropped the charges), and they split briefly in 2016 amid rumours of infidelity - but the couple maintained that they were soulmates.

"It was an unusual relationship because we had such highs and such terrible lows," she admits. But at the end of his life, "he knew" how much he was loved.

You can see proof of that love - from his family, his fans and his bandmates â in a new documentary about his final gig at Birmingham's Villa Park last year, when he reunited with heavy rock legends Black Sabbath.

Titled Back To The Beginning, it features more than 25 of the songs played at that star-studded concert, including Ozzy's solo set and Sabbath's entire performance.

In backstage footage, fellow A-list performers discuss the star's influence in awestruck tones.

"If there was no Sabbath, there's no Metallica," says the band's frontman James Hetfield. "It's as simple as that."

Yungblud, whose version of Changes was one of the concert's emotional high points, talks about how, as a kid who didn't fit in, Ozzy changed his life.

"The fact he was celebrated for his madness and his individuality gave me hope."

Watch: Black Sabbath blast out Iron Man at final gig

Sharon says the concert was Ozzy's final wish: the one thing that would make him happy after years of deteriorating health.

Six months before the show, Ozzy fell and fractured his vertebrae. In recovery, he caught pneumonia three times. After surgery on his back, he caught sepsis and nearly died.

Two weeks before the show, doctors urged him to call it off.

"They came to see us at the house and they said, 'You can't do this. We're advising you not to do this. Your heart's too weak'," Sharon recalls.

But the singer was determined to see it through. In the film, it's hard to believe he's so ill. Although he performs seated on a throne, his eyes are full of life and his voice is powerful. As he looks out at the crowd, he seems elated.

Backstage, Sharon admits, "the whole family was terrified". But something changed when Ozzy entered the stadium.

"He was sat in his dressing room, watching everybody on a screen performing, and he was like, 'I just can't believe they're all here'.

"It's amazing what support and love and determination can do for you, because [before the show] he was somebody that couldn't breathe. He had a lung condition. He had a heart condition. He had terrible, terrible agony from his spine. He had blood clots and he had Parkinson's.

"Some days just to even talk was an effort, let alone sing. And I don't know how he did it, but he did it.

"And you saw it in his eyes, in his face. It just made him feel like he was alive again."

Ozzy is survived by Sharon, their three children (left-right: Kelly, Aimee and Jack) and three children from his previous marriage

The effect was so galvanising that, when he came off stage, Ozzy declared he wanted to go on tour.

"He was raring to go," Sharon laughs. "He was like, 'I can do this, and the fans don't mind if I'm sat down'."

It's an incredible and rare thing, I suggest, to be able to grant your partner their last wish.

Ozzy may have suffered immense pain in his final years, but he was able to say goodbye in style.

"To quote one of his songs, he was flying high again," Sharon says.

"He was just floating for those last 17 days. It was great. It was joyous."

Brazil election: Lula, Brazil’s political titan, defies the passage of time

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Sign up now: Get ST's newsletters delivered to your inbox

Brazil's President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva celebrates after winning the presidential run-off in Sao Paulo.

RIO DE JANEIRO – Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, Brazil’s president granted an unprecedented fourth term in office, once likened the events of his life to a miracle.

The titan of the political left made history by becoming the first person to win a presidential run-off after losing the first round in Brazil.

His shock win follows a long and storied political career, beginning in the bowels of a metalworkers’ trade union and even including stints behind bars.

He charted a rocky road to his fourth election victory.

Questions about his ability to do the job at almost 81 encouraged Lula to do squats and jog along the campaign trail to prove that he was fitter than ever.

Initially startled by his second-place finish to Flavio Bolsonaro earlier in October, Lula dusted himself off and launched into an energetic run-off campaign.

He upped his social media game at the eleventh hour, posting selfie videos and buying a cellphone for the first time.

The shift highlighted a certain disconnect with his country’s extremely online, deeply polarised population, 14 years after he was first elected.

His victory preserves Latin America’s largest country as a leftist stronghold amid a regional lurch to the right championed by US President Donald Trump.

Lula, a defining figure in Brazilian politics over four decades, rose from poverty to the presidency, fell into disgrace and was jailed, before once again rising from the political ashes when he returned to power almost four years ago.

His first-round campaign focused heavily on his life story and legacy, which critics said was a misreading of what weary Brazilians wanted to hear.

The president grew up in deep poverty, the seventh of eight children in a family of illiterate farmers in the arid north-eastern state of Pernambuco.

He worked as a shoeshine boy and peanut vendor before becoming a metalworker and trade unionist. In the 1960s, he lost a finger in a workplace accident.

After losing three presidential bids, Lula finally entered the presidential palace in Brasilia in 2003 and was re-elected in 2006.

He left office in 2010 as a blue-collar hero who presided over a commodity-based economic boom that helped lift 30 million people out of poverty.

Massive corruption scandals haunted Lula and his party, fuelling a wave of disgust that propelled Jair Bolsonaro into the presidency in 2018.

Lula was jailed before the election until the charges were annulled on procedural grounds and the lead judge on the case was found to be biased.

However, Lula was not exonerated and the scandal contributed to enduring polarisation in Brazil.

Bolsonaro’s presidency brought more division, and by the 2022 election, Lula pulled off a dramatic comeback by a razor-thin margin.

In his third term, Lula is credited with restoring social programmes eroded by his predecessor, curbing the destruction of the Amazon rainforest, and putting Brazil’s democracy back on a more secure footing.

But the father of five still triggers fierce rejection among voters frustrated by crime and corruption.

He has also faced criticism over Brazil’s mounting public debt, which, with high interest rates, costs the government around US$18 billion (S$23 billion) a month in interest alone.

Lula came under fire for an election-season spending push, including an increase to the Bolsa Familia social welfare benefits which, launched 20 years ago, remains his most significant legacy.

“He has that love for the poor. That’s all it is,” fisherman Antonio Carlos Lima Ferreira told AFP at his home in the interior of Maranhao state, recalling times when he had to send his son to bed hungry.

“Today, at my table, I can choose what I want to eat.”

Lula said recently he did not want to be remembered as “just the Bolsa Familia president”.

Lula, who has not cultivated a potential successor, campaigned on a pledge to stop the far right from returning to “destroy” the country.

His improbable victory on Oct 25 awed his supporters, many of whom feared an erosion of rights under a theoretical Flavio Bolsonaro presidency.

Lula “will always remain a symbol of the defence of democracy”, civil servant Gustavo Monteiro, 58, told AFP at a leftist rally.

“He represents the fight against political repression.”

The leftist veteran has already spent more time in office than any other democratically elected leader in Latin America’s biggest country.

“There is no one in this country with more experience as president than me,” Lula told foreign media, including AFP, in an interview. AFP

An Israeli strike on a university in Gaza City killed three people on Sunday (October 25, 2026), staff and a local hospital said, while the civil defence agency reported five others killed elsewhere in the territory.

Reached for comment on the strike at the Islamic University, the Israeli army said that it was currently under review.

Al Shifa hospital reported receiving three bodies and treating eight people who were wounded in the drone strike, with two sources at the university saying the dead were employees and the injured were students.

The university said the campus and its surroundings had been targeted twice on Sunday (October 25, 2026), condemning the attacks and mourning those killed in a statement.

Gaza's Education Ministry likewise condemned the strike and said, "The global silence regarding attacks on students and universities serves as encouragement for the continuation of these crimes".

"This strike occurs at a time when the higher education sector is facing total annihilation, with 95% of the buildings belonging to academic institutions having been damaged as a result of the war," it continued.

In a statement, the army said it had been "targeting a terrorist from Hamas' military wing".

"Following the identification of uninvolved individuals in the area, the munition was diverted in order to mitigate harm to them. The incident is under review," it said, without specifying whether those killed at the university were hit by accident.

A critically endangered orangutan mother and baby have been rescued after being ensnared in a trap that caused severe injuries.

Gadis, believed to be 25, and her 10-month-old son, Garis, were found by conservation workers in Indonesia enmeshed in a trap thought to be for wild boar.

The pair - who were rescued early on Friday morning - were discovered near a village located in the Central Kalimantan province on the Indonesian part of Borneo, with Gadis suffering painful lacerations caused by the trap tearing 'deep into her flesh'.

Both are now receiving medical treatment at the Orangutan Foundation International clinic near the village of Pangkalan Bun.

They said: 'Gadis was found with the ropes tied to her upper right arm and lower left arm, and the wounds were bad because the ropes had torn deep into her flesh.

Orangutan mother and baby, Gadis and Garis, were found by conservation workers in Indonesia enmeshed in a trap thought to be for wild boar

Wild boar hunting, which uses rope snares, is legal and common in parts of Indonesia as a pest control measure to protect crops, but illegal inside protected forests or national parks.

Officials have repeatedly cautioned that careless placement of snares near wildlife areas could instead trap endangered sun bears or orangutans.

Orangutans - native to the rainforests of Indonesia and Malaysia - are considered critically endangered, with declines in their population resulting human activities.

Only about 120,000 remain in the wild in Borneo and Sumatra - about half the number from a century ago, according to conservation group WWF.

But Gadis and Garis's rescue comes as dozens of orangutans have also been recovered in recent weeks from rampant wildfires burning across Indonesia.

Two more were released in Ketapang, West Kalimantan, on Saturday after being rescued from an oil palm plantation.

A federal appeals court on Sunday scrutinized the Trump administration's emergency effort to force California to set aside thousands of mail-in ballots tied to people the Justice Department suspects are noncitizens ahead of the midterm elections.

The three-judge panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit raised questions about both the government's legal arguments and whether the court has jurisdiction to intervene during a hearing in which attorneys representing the Trump administration and California argued for nearly two hours, Fox News' Matt Finn reported.

The federal government asked the panel to rule immediately, arguing that hundreds of thousands of California ballots could be tabulated each day while the dispute remains unresolved.

The hearing comes after the Trump administration filed an emergency request involving roughly 20,000 mail-in ballots sent to people it suspects are noncitizens.

CALIFORNIA MUST RESPOND TO DOJ'S EMERGENCY REQUEST OVER BALLOTS MAILED TO ALLEGED NONCITIZENS, COURT RULES

In this photo, an official vote by mail ballot, mailed to a Southern California resident ahead of the general election is seen on Oct. 17, 2026, in Los Angeles, California. (Jay L Clendenin/Getty Images)

The DOJ wants California election officials to set aside ballots associated with individuals on its list while their eligibility is reviewed, rather than allowing the ballots to be processed alongside other votes.

Circuit Judge Mark Bennett, a Trump appointee, questioned whether the administration's requested injunction could be reconciled with the Constitution's Elections Clause, which gives state legislatures authority over the times, places and manner of congressional elections, subject to congressional regulation.

"It seems to me that you want us to issue an injunction that is entirely inconsistent with the law that the California legislature has adopted pursuant to the Elections Clause," he told Justice Department attorney Matthew Donnelly.

Judge Patrick Bumatay, another Trump appointee, focused more heavily on the government's concerns about potential noncitizen voting and questioned California's handling of challenges involving suspected ineligible voters.

The Justice Department sought emergency relief from the appeals court after Carter did not rule by a deadline previously requested by the department.

Antonelli delivers 'the race of a champion who isn't the champion yet'

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Kimi Antonelli needs two points in Mexico to be certain of becoming world champion

The race that took Kimi Antonelli to the brink of a record-breaking world championship was, in the words of his Mercedes team boss Toto Wolff, "the best he has delivered so far" and "the race of a champion who isn't the champion yet".

The 20-year-old Italian's victory in the United States Grand Prix puts him in an impregnable position.

Antonelli is so far ahead in the points that the only way he could not win the title in Mexico City next weekend is if either his team-mate George Russell or Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton win the race. Even then, Antonelli will guarantee the title if he finishes ninth, or 10th if it's Hamilton.

He arrived here thanks to one of the most dominant victories of the season, in a car in which Russell could finish only eighth, albeit hampered by a five-second penalty.

While Russell was toiling and failing to pass McLaren's Oscar Piastri, Antonelli was operating on another level entirely, as he has so often this season.

"Exceptional," Wolff said, "and I think he's outperformed the car."

Antonelli wins in US but has to wait to secure title

Antonelli superb and Leclerc close to perfect - driver ratings

Antonelli came to Austin already in a strong position in the championship, knowing he would clinch the title if he won with Russell finishing lower than eighth and Hamilton lower than fourth.

He achieved his part of the bargain but Russell and Hamilton did just enough to keep things alive until this coming weekend. Russell was eighth and Hamilton fourth.

Some drivers tighten up when they get this close to achieving their lifetime's ambition. Not Antonelli. He said he was going to treat this like any other weekend, but it wasn't just talk. It's exactly what he did.

"He comes in," Wolff told BBC Sport after the race, in a conversation after finishing his official news conference, "sits on my chair, feet up, and keeps chatting away and saying: 'This is so funny. I'm not thinking about the championship at all.' And in a kind of youngish excitement, almost like it's surreal. And that's why it's not so important."

Did Wolff think that was because Antonelli's lead was so large that he had no need to feel any pressure, or was it innate?

"The advantage helps," he said. "But it's also innate in him and I don't think that it's something to be trained for. He's not over-thinking it."

Wolff's claim about this being Antonelli's best performance so far might surprise anyone who watched him win his home grand prix at Monza from 19th on the grid.

When this was pointed out, Wolff replied: "My problem is that I don't remember past races. So now you're saying Monza was actually also pretty good, obviously with the advantage of the home crowd and seeing all these tifosi that were a little bit confused, wearing red, and an Italian with the Mercedes outfit up on the podium. And I think we're going to see more Kimi caps, Mercedes caps next year.

"So both of them were pretty good. But the headline that this is the best we can keep."

Antonelli agreed. "Monza, we clearly had the fastest car," he said. "We were just super quick, so of course it was incredibly special coming from the back. But it was also a track where it was relatively easy to overtake, and we were so quick.

"Definitely, Monza was a super-special one, and one that I will remember forever. But this one probably, in terms of execution, was better, because the way we built up and the way we built the performance throughout the weekend was very good."

Mercedes went to Austin with both cars in the new upgraded specification that had proved unconvincing, to say the least, when it made its debut in Malaysia two races ago.

There, Wolff said he thought it had made the car 0.3secs per lap slower. The team split the cars in Singapore to try to understand the package better, and then used it on both cars in Austin. Antonelli stuck it on pole and dominated the race. Russell's performance looked more like the one the car produced in Malaysia.

"The update we put on the car is difficult to operate," Wolff said. "And Kimi landed it.

"Maybe it's a bit harsh to say but I think his driving style just glues with the car. We saw that in qualifying yesterday where on his last lap he could have been another 0.15-0.2 secs up. And on the other side George just struggled so much. And I believe this is because the operational window of the car is so difficult."

Alberto Ascari is the last Italian to win the F1 drivers' championship, in 1952 and 1953

When Antonelli clinches the title, he will become the first Italian to do so since Alberto Ascari in 1953. It's a remarkable statistic for a country which is so engrained in the sport, and it certainly resonates with his compatriots.

McLaren team principal Andrea Stella said: "As an Italian, wow, we need to go back to Ascari, the names that they created the inspiration for all of us. To see an Italian achieving this level of performance and results in Formula 1, that's particularly thrilling, as an Italian person in motorsport."

Stella's answer continued into an area that begins to explain the way in which he has proved so superior to Russell this year.

"Going more into the technical, and specifically this season, Kimi is a very interesting one to look at, because he pretty much attacks all the times," Stella said.

"You see how often he's just going off track, and you see his onboard (camera), he's pretty aggressive. But at the same time, this seems to be not only kind of an aggressive driving, or an attacking driving, but effective.

"And if we think how much we talk in these media sessions about the cars being difficult to drive consistently fast, it should be recognised how much he's been able to harness his driving style with what is required of the cars.

"But at the same time, almost contradicting what I'm saying, when is the time to bring home the result, he's been able to do it.

"So he's the deserving leader of this championship. He's writing an important piece of history for motorsport, because once he achieves the championship, he will be the youngest."

Antonelli will beat by more than three years the previous record for youngest winner of the championship, Sebastian Vettel in 2010.

As with the title itself, he is aware of the forthcoming achievement, but not letting it affect him.

"I'm going to approach it with the same mindset," he said. "Just trying to have as much fun as possible, trying to hopefully fight for another win.

"It would be best to finish on a high, and that's the goal. But I'm just going to try to enjoy the rest of the season from now on."

Длительное употребление сукралозы — распространенного заменителя сахара — может негативно влиять на кишечник, печень и головной мозг. К такому выводу пришли ученые из Национального университета Чхуннам в Южной Корее. Результаты исследования опубликованы в журнале Nutrients.

Специалисты провели эксперимент на молодых мышах, которым в течение 14 недель давали сукралозу в дозировке пять миллиграммов на килограмм массы тела. Часть животных дополнительно подвергали хроническому стрессу, чтобы оценить его влияние на организм.

Выяснилось, что длительное употребление подсластителя изменяло состав кишечной микрофлоры и снижало разнообразие бактерий. Кроме того, у животных нарушалась регуляция обмена холестерина в печени, хотя его уровень в крови существенно не увеличивался.

Исследователи также обнаружили изменения в активности генов, связанных с воспалением печени. При этом некоторые эффекты зависели от того, подвергались ли животные стрессу.

Особое внимание ученые обратили на изменения в головном мозге. У мышей, получавших сукралозу, усиливались признаки воспаления нервной ткани и чаще наблюдалось поведение, напоминающее симптомы депрессии.

Авторы предположили, что эти изменения могут быть связаны между собой через взаимодействие кишечной микрофлоры, печени и головного мозга. Однако пока исследование проводилось только на животных.

Ранее ученые выяснили, что употребление сладкой газировки может повышать риск жировой болезни печени.
