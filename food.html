Russian ombudswoman suggests humanitarian aid be delivered to Alyoshki every month

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

SIMFEROPOL, October 30. /TASS/. Russian human rights ombudswoman Yana Lantratova has suggested that humanitarian deliveries be organized to the city Alyoshki in the Kherson Region every month.

She said earlier that she and her Ukrainian counterpart are asking US First Lady Melania Trump to coordinate regular humanitarian missions for people in need in Alyoshki.

"We voiced a request for such humanitarian assistance to be organized on a regular basis, for instance, every month," Lantratova told reporters, adding that not all of the city’s residents are able to leave it due to various reasons.

Earlier, Russia and Ukraine agreed on a US First Lady Melania Trump-mediated ceasefire in Alyoshki to carry out a humanitarian operation.

The ceasefire in the area came into force at 4:00 a.m. Moscow time (1:00 a.m. GMT) on October 27 to prepare and conduct the humanitarian operation in the city.

A humanitarian mission was carried out on October 30. Several dozen tons of humanitarian aid - food, hygiene and sanitation products, fuels for power generators, and other daily essentials - were handed out to the city residents, Lantratova said. All those willing to leave the city were given the opportunity to evacuate either to Russia or to Ukraine.

Russian presidential press secretary Dmitry Peskov on September 21 described the humanitarian situation in Alyoshki as critical as food and medicines could not be delivered to the city amid Ukrainian shelling and drone attacks.

SIMFEROPOL, October 30. /TASS/. Evacuees from the city of Alyoshki in the Kherson Region are being examined by medics at a temporary accommodation center in Simferopol, a TASS correspondent reported.

In addition to the physical examination, people undergo diagnostic testing, including examinations using a mobile fluoroscope.

The humanitarian mission in Alyoshki was aimed at providing assistance to civilians. A ceasefire in the Kherson direction is in effect from 4:00 a.m. Moscow time (1:00 a.m. GMT) on October 27 until 10:59 p.m. Moscow time (7:59 p.m. GMT) on November 1. All those wishing to leave the city were given the opportunity to evacuate, while those remaining in Alyoshki received food, medicine and essential supplies. Preparations for the mission began in May 2026, with the coordination of Russian Human Rights Commissioner Yana Lantratova and Ukrainian Ombudsman Dmitry Lubinets, facilitated by US First Lady Melania Trump. The mission to Alyoshki also involved Russian and foreign media representatives.

Family with seven children evacuated from Alyoshki during humanitarian mission

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

ALYOSHKI /Kherson Region/, October 30. /TASS/. A family with seven children was evacuated from the town of Alyoshki in the Kherson Region as part of a humanitarian mission, a TASS correspondent reported.

The parents, Lyudmila and Kirill, left the town together with their children.

"Honestly, I was very worried about the family. I knew about them and their seven children, and it was important for me to make sure the children were safe," Russian Human Rights Commissioner Yana Lantratova said.

She stressed that the family would be guaranteed "full safety."

The Russian Armed Forces introduced a ceasefire in the Kherson direction at 4:00 a.m. Moscow time (1:00 a.m. GMT) on October 27 to prepare for a humanitarian operation in the town of Alyoshki. Earlier, Russia and Ukraine agreed to a ceasefire in Alyoshki, brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump, to facilitate humanitarian aid deliveries and the evacuation of civilians via a special safe corridor. On September 21, Russian Presidential Spokesman Dmitry Peskov described the situation in Alyoshki as critical, saying that food and medicine could not be delivered there amid ongoing Ukrainian shelling and drone attacks. Slightly over 1,000 residents currently remain in Alyoshki, down from a population of 20,000.

Frontline ceasefire EXCLUSIVE: Inside the aid mission to trapped residents

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

A humanitarian mission in the frontline town of Alyoshki in Kherson Region, Russia has delivered essential supplies and helped civilians evacuate under a temporary ceasefire between Moscow and Kiev.

More than 1,000 residents have chosen to stay despite years of shelling, drone attacks, and severe shortages, RT correspondent Murad Gazdiev reports from the scene.

Many of those remaining are elderly. Some wanted to evacuate but feared they would be turned away after losing their identity documents when their homes were destroyed. Others refuse to leave behind their pets and livestock, even as they struggle to find enough food.

“Despite what they’ve been through, the shortages, the violence, the bombing, most of them... a little over a thousand, are choosing to stay,” Gazdiev says.

The evacuation and delivery of humanitarian aid are taking place under a temporary ceasefire between Moscow and Kiev, coordinated with the involvement of US First Lady Melania Trump. Russian and Ukrainian human rights commissioners are overseeing the operation.

Russian human rights commissioner Yana Lantratova told RT that a family with seven children and their pets, who had remained in Alyoshki, had been evacuated to safety.

“We were deeply concerned for their lives and wellbeing. Ultimately, the decision was made to move them to a safe location. For now, that place is Skadovsk,” she said.

Everyone wishing to leave Alyoshki was given the opportunity to do so, including those who had not registered in advance, according to Lantratova. A separate vehicle was also arranged to evacuate pets.

More than 50 doctors and paramedics took part in the mission, which delivered food, medicine, hygiene supplies and fuel to residents. The humanitarian ceasefire was fully observed, the commissioner said.

The Russian and Ukrainian human rights commissioners also jointly appealed to Melania Trump to coordinate similar humanitarian missions on a regular basis, Lantratova said.

Melania Trump wrote on X that the humanitarian convoy had prepared to safely evacuate more than 750 residents who had voluntarily chosen to leave Alyoshki. “Our priority: saving lives,” she said, adding that the convoy and safe passage routes had been agreed upon by “Russian and Ukrainian leaders.”

‘No food, no medicine’: Alyoshki evacuees recount Ukrainian siege (VIDEO)

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

People who were evacuated from Alyoshki in Kherson Region, Russia told RT of hunger, relentless Ukrainian drone attacks, and homes being destroyed, describing how Russian soldiers brought supplies and helped them escape.

The frontline town, across the Dnieper River from Ukrainian-held Kherson, has been largely cut off from essential supplies for months. Evacuees described the targeting and destruction of vehicles carrying food and the threat of drones making even a few minutes outdoors dangerous.

“There’s nothing to eat. No medicine. Drones are constantly hovering above you,” one resident said, adding that people could barely step outside for a cigarette before another drone appeared.

He said three vehicles attempted to bring supplies in the spring. “They blew up the first one, then the second, then the third. So they wouldn’t let us get anything in.” People had to rely on vegetables from their gardens, he said, with no vehicles able to reach them.

Another evacuee, Sergey, described living without running water, electricity, and gas after flooding devastated the area. Russian soldiers and volunteers brought supplies. “That’s how we survived,” he said.

For some, escape followed the loss of family members. One woman said her husband died after encountering mines while cycling to collect potatoes. Russian troops guided her to safety using drones and a radio during a five-hour journey from her village to a hospital.

Another woman, whose husband was killed in a Ukrainian attack, described repeated bombardments that forced her to move out of her damaged home and shelter in an adjoining kitchen.

“Houses were just being blown away,” she said. Russian soldiers supplied food, drinks, and gas as residents struggled to survive.

One evacuee said Ukrainian forces were responsible for the attacks, while Russian soldiers evacuated them.

“Ukraine believes that we are traitors, that we are scum. We just want to live a normal life, a regular life,” he said, noting that soldiers shared cigarettes, canned meat, grains, and offered further help.

The humanitarian operation is underway under a temporary Russia-Ukraine ceasefire around Alyoshki. The ceasefire began on October 27 and is scheduled to continue through November 1, following mediation involving US First Lady Melania Trump.

A convoy carrying food, medicine, drinking water, hygiene supplies, and fuel for generators arrived in the town on Friday. The operation also provides the opportunity for those who wish to leave to evacuate.

Russian officials accused Ukrainian forces of obstructing deliveries and evacuations by targeting access roads and scattering mines from drones. Russian military engineers have been clearing the routes to allow the humanitarian convoy through.

Russian bomb disposal specialist killed clearing way for US-backed aid mission

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

A Russian bomb disposal specialist and another person have been killed while clearing the way for a US-backed Russian-Ukrainian humanitarian mission.

The fatalities in Russia’s Kherson Region were reported on Friday by Russian human rights ombudsman Yana Lantratova, who briefed journalists on preparations to deliver essential supplies to the frontline town of Alyoshki and evacuate residents wishing to leave.

The Defense Ministry later named the military service member who was killed as Sgt. Andrey Vedernikov of the 98th Airborne Troops Division. He was fatally wounded while trying to defuse an explosive device deployed from a Ukrainian drone that was equipped with a proximity sensor, the statement said.

Lantratova held the press conference jointly with her Ukrainian counterpart, Dmitry Lubinets, in the town of Armyansk, approximately 90 km from Alyoshki, which Kiev claims as Ukrainian territory. The humanitarian operation was mediated by US first lady Melania Trump.

Organizers have assembled 25 buses capable of evacuating at least 750 people, along with specialized vehicles for up to 30 people with limited mobility, nine ambulances and more than 50 medical personnel, Lantratova said.

Lubinets said the ceasefire around Alyoshki had held without reported violations for more than 75 hours, making it the longest lull in fighting since the escalation of the conflict in 2022. He expressed hope that the arrangement could serve as a model for similar operations elsewhere.

Friday’s mission aims to deliver essential supplies requested by local residents, including food, fuel, medicines, and hygiene products. Evacuees will receive assistance relocating to either Russia or Ukraine, depending on their preference.

The joint briefing, held against the backdrop of a convoy of vehicles, was attended by dozens of journalists from Russia, Ukraine and the US, including RT’s Murad Gadziev. Organizers said they wanted to ensure maximum transparency throughout the humanitarian mission.
