Regalarle a alguien un ramo de flores siempre suele ser recibido como un gesto bonito. Sin embargo, en Austria, si el número de flores del ramo es par, habitualmente es considerado un grave error social y un augurio directo de mala suerte. A diferencia de otras tradiciones, donde regalar docenas o pares resulta habitual, la cultura austríaca reserva las cantidades pares exclusivamente para ceremonias fúnebres, condolencias y visitas a los cementerios. Entregar una cantidad par de flores a una persona viva se interpreta como el deseo de cerrar su ciclo vital o un presagio de desgracia. Esta costumbre está tan arraigada que los floristas locales cuentan minuciosamente cada tallo para evitar malentendidos, permitiendo únicamente como excepción aceptada el ramo compuesto por doce flores.

El origen histórico de esta profunda superstición se remonta a antiguas creencias paganas y eslavas que atribuían propiedades místicas y espirituales a la naturaleza de los números. Para los antiguos pobladores de la región, las cifras impares representaban el movimiento, el crecimiento constante, lo incompleto y, en consecuencia, la continuidad de la vida. Por el contrario, los números pares simbolizaban la simetría perfecta, el equilibrio final, la quietud absoluta y la conclusión definitiva de una etapa, asociándose con la muerte. Por esta razón, colocar flores pares sobre una tumba busca desearle al difunto descanso eterno, mientras que obsequiarlas a los vivos resulta un gesto percibido como sumamente inadecuado.

Junto al simbolismo pagano, que es la razón más extendida que explica la mencionada curiosidad de las flores pares, existe una segunda hipótesis histórica basada en la antigua etiqueta de cortesía y en las tradiciones de galantería del cortejo caballeresco europeo. Según este relato tradicional, cuando un caballero compraba un ramo completo para su amada, retiraba cuidadosamente una sola flor del conjunto antes de hacer la entrega formal. El galán colocaba esa flor elegida en el ojal de su chaqueta, justo al lado del corazón, como un símbolo visible de su afecto sincero y de su devoción hacia la dama pretendida. Al restar esa única pieza al arreglo inicial, el ramo entregado se convertía de inmediato en una cifra impar, transformando el gesto floral en una prueba tangible de amor caballeresco.

Una tercera explicación proviene del ámbito comercial y de la diferenciación social surgida durante el siglo XIX en los grandes mercados callejeros de las ciudades centroeuropeas. En aquella época, los vendedores ambulantes comercializaban lotes cerrados de flores en pares, vendiendo habitualmente docenas o medias docenas a precios populares para el gran público. Para distinguirse de la venta ambulante, las floristerías de gran nombre comenzaron a confeccionar ramos con números impares, demostrando así que sus productos venían de un comercio fino. Comprar un ramo de cantidad impar se transformó en un símbolo de estatus y elegancia, consolidando una norma social de distinción que perdura en el protocolo austríaco actual.

Desde la perspectiva del diseño floral clásico, el uso de números impares responde también a criterios estéticos fundamentales orientados a lograr la máxima armonía visual. Hay floristas profesionales que consideran que una cantidad impar de tallos, especialmente en ramos pequeños de menos de doce unidades, facilita una distribución asimétrica y orgánica. Esta disposición asimétrica resulta mucho más atractiva, dinámica y natural para la vista, evitando la rigidez que suelen provocar los arreglos estructurados con números pares. Un conjunto par tiende a verse dividido o demasiado simétrico, perdiendo fluidez visual, por lo que la regla estética refuerza de manera perfecta la tradición cultural de la región.

A pesar de la rigidez de esta norma en la vida cotidiana, la cultura austríaca admite una única excepción ampliamente aceptada en las celebraciones modernas: el ramo de doce flores. Debido a la influencia de la cultura occidental, regalar doce flores en aniversarios, bodas o en San Valentín se considera un gesto romántico totalmente correcto y aceptable. Sin embargo, cualquier otro número par como dos, cuatro, seis u ocho sigue siendo tabú y debe evitarse estrictamente al momento de hacer un regalo a familiares o amigos. Incluso en las floristerías de Viena, los dependientes aconsejan a los compradores inexpertos modificar la cantidad si detectan que el ramo solicitado podría causar algún malentendido.

En conclusión, la costumbre de regalar flores en número impar en Austria (y otros países de Europa central) combina historia, misticismo, normas sociales de distinción y principios fundamentales de diseño estético. Lo que a ojos de un visitante extranjero podría parecer una simple o curiosa superstición constituye en realidad un complejo código cultural transmitido de generación en generación. Conocer el significado preciso de los números en la floristería austríaca permite evitar situaciones incómodas y demuestra una profunda sensibilidad hacia las tradiciones locales. Así, el simple acto de contar los tallos de un ramo antes de ofrecerlo se convierte en una apuesta segura de respeto, cortesía y buenos deseos.

What do you do when your love and your life are in two different places?

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

When news breaks, you need to understand what actually matters — and what to do about it. At Vox, our mission to help you make sense of the world has never been more vital. But we can’t do it on our own.

We rely on readers like you to fund our journalism. Will you support our work and become a Vox Member today?

How to make a huge decision when there’s no perfect choice.﻿

Hi readers! Shayla Love here, science journalist and longtime fan of Your Mileage May Vary. I’m honored to be subbing for Sigal Samuel while she’s out on parental leave. I’m diving into your questions as a way to help understand human nature and our choices through multiple lenses: philosophical, psychological, and beyond. Please send in any emotional, body/brain, sociological, perceptual, or other kind of life quandaries you might have.

I am someone who notoriously struggles with big decisions. Over a year ago, I found a wonderful partner, who very much feels like they could be my soulmate. We’ve been dating three time zones apart for a while. Being near our families, communities, and aging parents is a shared value — unfortunately one that makes it hard to compromise on location. Either way, I have to give up something I want and value — a wonderful partner who I can see a life with (the first time I’ve felt this way about someone), or the place I’ve always imagined settling down and that represents the person I am becoming and values I want to live. Two good things are pulling me toward opposite paths and there’s not much room for compromise. How do I make the “right” decision?

You’ve found a place in the world that you feel connected to and you met a person you envision spending the rest of your life with. Geographically restless and lonely people everywhere are jealous! But with your good fortunes seemingly at odds with one another, it feels that by choosing one, you’ll forsake the other — and doom the choice you did make to feel like a sacrifice.

When circumstances have forced me to make a tough decision, I’ve daydreamed about a thought experiment: the Many Worlds Interpretation.

In quantum physics, particles behave differently than us mortals. For example, an electron can be in more than one place at once until it’s measured. How can it be in multiple locations and then be observed to be in a single position? In the 1950s, the American physicist Hugh Everett proposed an explanation: The universe splits into alternate versions of itself, one for each place a particle could be measured. To apply this theory to your life, if you chose to move cities, don’t worry, another universe would be created in which you didn’t choose to do this, and you’ll stay right where you are!

Yes, I know you can’t transport yourself into such a quantum realm, but there is something helpful in thinking about alternative universes. Did you have an immediate reaction to which universe you would be upset not to inhabit? Take that information to heart, because it’s likely you’re not ultimately going to make your choice based on a tidy pro-con list.

The psychologists Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky won a Nobel Prize for pointing out that most of our decisions are not based on rationality. People use two systems for decision-making: System one is faster and emotionally based, and system two is more logical and slow. But even the slower, more deliberate decision-making can fall prey to any number of cognitive biases.

In your case, one called loss aversion may be especially relevant. People have the tendency to worry more about what they’ll lose, even if what they stand to gain is equal in value. For example, it will likely feel worse to lose a $10 bill from your pocket than it would feel good to find a $10 bill on the ground. This bias is relevant in people’s social lives too: They hold onto older, maybe fading, friendships because the thought of losing them is too painful, but don’t seek out new friends with equal effort. Or, they stay in a meh job because it feels scary to let it go, rather than applying that same energy to finding a new one. Once you accept that your decision-making process will be subject to messy feelings and biases, you can let go of trying to make the perfect choice.

You opened your question by sharing that you struggle with big decisions, and it makes me wonder if you simply feel anxiety about making a choice at all. This is called analysis paralysis. When outcomes are uncertain, anxiety wakes up, and a voice in your head tells you to avoid taking risks. People with analysis paralysis overthink their options, over-research, and second-guess their first instincts. These actions have more to do with stress over committing to any decision rather than the content of what you’re deciding on.

A surprising finding from psychology may ease your paralysis. Researchers told people to make a big life decision — on the order of ending a relationship or quitting their job — by flipping a coin. Heads, they made the change; tails, they didn’t. For those who followed the results of the toss, the people who made the change were overwhelmingly happier, even though their decision to do so was made by pure chance. These random-flip outcomes also show “that people may be excessively cautious when facing life-changing choices,” the authors wrote. The coin helped the participants resist the bias of tending to stick with the status quo, even if it doesn’t make them happier.

To begin to loosen your paralysis, something that might assist you, paradoxically, is to introduce more options into the mix. Jacques Derrida, the 20th-century French philosopher, is known for the concept of deconstruction, a way of analyzing texts or ideas more critically, and showing they’re not as stable as they seem. He pointed out that we often think in binaries and false opposites: good vs. evil, man vs. woman, nature vs. culture. This can set up an easy dichotomy in which one of the items is good, and the other, by being its opposite, is bad.

You’ve created a binary situation, and you don’t actually have to be in one. What if there’s a third option, or a fourth? What if you and your partner move to a brand-new city, and set out to create new community, together? What if you and your partner find a way to split your time between your respective hometowns? Now you don’t have only two good things pulling you in opposite directions, you would have different options you can be more creative with. You also don’t have to commit to the decision you make now for the rest of your life. You can always move cities again, or try to find another mate.

You are with a partner you love, and live in a place that you love. You might reframe your situation as a win-win, rather than a lose-lose. And remember that if you don’t make a choice, you’re not successfully avoiding a decision — it’s a choice of its own. Your partner may get tired of long-distance or end the relationship for another reason. Or, you could start to resent the place you live since you have to travel so much to spend time with your partner.

I also want to point out how you describe your partner. Don’t turn your nose up at finding someone who you can see making a life with, especially as you note, if you’ve never felt that way before. Billionaire investor Warren Buffett has said that there were two most important moments in his life: when he came out of the womb, and when he met his wife. “What happened with me would not have happened without her.” Buffett’s view is supported by research that shows that people with supportive spouses tend to take on more rewarding challenges throughout their lives, and end up having more personal growth and happiness.

Another quantum idea that might interest you is spooky action at a distance. This is when two particles, even far apart, become connected to one another. Measuring one affects the other, no matter how far apart they are. If your partner feels like your “soulmate,” perhaps you have become entangled already. Don’t fret about making the perfect choice about the next phase of life, but do make a choice (any choice!) that considers your futures as bound up together. For the other details, maybe it’s time to find a coin to flip.

Wallhack M-001 Review: When Fun Overrides Specs

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Batteries. They're not typically thought of as a significant source of innovation. Most companies have spent the last few years removing mice in favor of more lightweight designs.

That's what's made the return of the replaceable and rechargeable battery surprising—something I once thought to be a lost technology from a bygone era. A few companies have recently realized that the ability to pop out and replace your battery in seconds is a great way to extend mouse life without needing to increase battery size or efficiency.

From the handful of mice I’ve tested with hot-swappable batteries so far, Wallhack does it best. The mouse is designed around the function’s ease-of-use, and everything else comes together to make a mouse that feels not only competitive for gaming, but also well thought-out and satisfying to use in everyday life.

The M-001’s shape is a standard right-handed, symmetrical design. This means the mouse’s left and right sides are shaped identically, but only the left side has thumb buttons. There are two main buttons, two side buttons, a standard ratcheting scroll wheel, and a power switch on the underside.

With a length of 120 mm, the M-001 is slightly shorter than contemporaries like the Razer Viper V4 Pro (125 mm) and Logitech Pro Superstrike X2 (127 mm). The overall width and height are comparable, at 63.5-mm tall and a 39-mm wide. The largest difference is the profile of the mouse: The front end sits significantly lower than other mice, and the rear has a more significant hump, which creates a more angled shape overall. When using a palm grip, this puts your hand at a slightly more aggressive forwards angle, with your palm sitting higher and your fingers pointing more downwards. It’s a slight difference, but I found this angle to be better for quick movements compared to a more “flat” design. However, like most shape-related choices, this is ultimately down to individual preference. (To compare mouse sizes, I typically use a website like eloshapes)

The two main buttons are lightly sculpted, with subtle concavity for fingers to comfortably rest in. Similarly, there is a subtle taper at the bottom center of the mouse which makes lifting and moving easier. The side buttons use a simple, rounded shape placed just above the taper, making it easy to rock your thumb upwards to press them without getting in the way. They’re just elevated enough that the buttons don’t recess into the shell when pressed. The only shape-related issue I had was the scroll wheel, which is a good bit more elevated than other mice. This isn’t a huge issue, and I adapted to it after a few hours of use, but it’s something I could see being off-putting for users accustomed to a lower-profile scroll wheel.

The most important part of this mouse’s construction is the magnesium shell, which provides significantly more rigidity than the plastic shells used on most mice. Because of this, the mouse feels incredibly sturdy and solid. The metal shell also gives the mouse a cool, slick feeling underneath your fingertips, which I prefer to the matte plastic feeling that most mice have.

Despite using metal, it only weighs 53.6 grams, making the M-001 quite light by most standards, It still falls slightly behind the Viper V4 Pro’s 50.3 grams and lags way behind Corsair’s superlight, 36-gram Sabre V2 Pro Ultralight. But the M-001 is incredibly solid, with none of the flexibility or flimsiness that most plastic mice have. When compared to other magnesium mice like the AngryMiao Infinity .97 or Corsair’s magnesium-shelled Sabre, the M-001 has an entirely solid upper shell without any cutouts or holes, which feels much better for regular use with a multitude of grip styles (plus, it’s much less likely to have dust, dead skin, or anything else build up inside of it). While the full magnesium shell increases weight, it’s a worthwhile trade-off for a smooth and rigid shape.

It’s also worth noting that some of Wallhack’s promotional material depicts the mouse as being a solid black color, but this isn’t quite the case. The unit I received is much closer to a gunmetal color, with a significant amount of metallic flake across the finish. The color looks fantastic in person, but it's difficult to properly capture in images.

Flipping over this mouse reveals one of the more atypical design decisions being made: the lack of a bottom shell. Instead of an entire underside, there is a thin frame around the circumference of the mouse that holds six feet in place. Between them is a chasm that reveals the entire PCB and battery.

Of particular note is the PCB’s function inside the mouse: Instead of simply sitting inside of the shell, the PCB works as a primary structural component. Similar techniques have been used in designing supercars and sport bikes, with the Ferrari F50 using the engine block as a stressed member that provides rigidity to the entire car, a technology borrowed from Ferrari’s F1 team. This provides some obvious benefits to rigidity and weight-savings, but it’s also just undeniably cool to be able to flip over your mouse and see the entire circuit board.

Right behind the PCB is the star of the show, a hot-swappable battery. While I’ve tested quite a few mice with this technology, none of them have managed to make the battery-swapping so satisfying. Instead of being attached with magnets or a rotating locking mechanism, the batteries use a custom-made -spring-loaded ejector similar to an SD card or a Nintendo Switch cartridge. To insert the battery, simply press until you feel a click, and it will lock into place. To remove it, do the same: press until you feel a click, and it will pop out slightly. Of all the mice I’ve tested with removable batteries, this is by far my favorite mechanism to use: It’s intuitive, fun, and feels sturdy.

This battery system does come with a trade-off. Because hot-swappable batteries means an effectively-infinite battery life (as long as you remember to plug the old one into the dongle when you swap), Wallhack has decided to entirely remove the USB-C port on the mouse. This means it only functions as a 2.4G wireless mouse and cannot be plugged or charged in directly. This isn’t a huge issue (it never inconvenienced me, since there was always a fully charged battery ready to swap in), and it theoretically means the connection quality will be better, since there’s fewer components between the antenna and the receiver. But it is worth noting that, if a battery dies or your dongle craps out, there’s no other way to use this mouse.

In every high-polling mouse I’ve reviewed, the dongle has been a bit of a pain point. A high polling rate necessitates proximity, which in turn requires in a small thing sitting on your desk. In most cases, these things have only one or two functions: They connect to the mouse, and they maybe have a small number of LED indicators (between 1 and 3) that can display battery life, connection quality, or some other setting. Almost every time, they’re small and light enough to slide around on your desk and will inevitably end up falling behind it once or twice due to the weight of the cable pulling it back.

With the M-001, nearly all of these issues are resolved: The dongle is given purpose, function, and I’d even argue dignity. Instead of the small, existing-by-necessity designs that most manufacturers ship with their mice, Wallhack has managed to make a beautiful piece of industrial design. The dongle is a medium-sized metal wedge sporting a gunmetal gray finish identical to the mouse. It has two camera-like dials up front, a button, and a slot for a rechargeable battery. The DPI dial features numbering from 400 to 25,600, and the polling dial goes from 125 Hz to 8,000 Hz.

The dials both have LED indicators next to them, which illuminate alongside the mouse to indicate a setting being changed. When rotated, they both have a satisfying ratcheting mechanism that’s stiff enough to avoid accidental inputs, without being difficult to turn using a single finger.

While I loved these dials from the moment I saw them, I really didn’t expect to use them much. I’ve always been more of a set-and-forget type of person. But after testing this mouse for a while, I regularly found myself using both of the dials: On-the-fly DPI adjustments were great for games like Marvel Rivals. I tended to prefer, for example, a higher DPI for Thing and a lower for Thor. Polling adjustments, too, were useful in situations where older games (like Destiny 2) got grumpy when I was using a mouse above 1,000 Hz.

Like many mice today, the M-001 uses an in-browser customization client. Wallhack’s web client, Wallhack Terminal, takes a unique stylistic approach, relying on a black-and-white, text-based system that resembles Command Prompt more than a typical gaming software. This is both a breath of fresh air stylistically, and much more straightforward than some of the software I’ve used in the past.

The configurator has an extensive feature set, including adjustments for mouse rotation, acceleration, and lift-off distance, which is adjustable by millimeters instead of the vague Low/Medium/High that some softwares use. The sensor can also be pushed to scan faster than the mouse’s 8,000 Hz polling rate, which can potentially improve the accuracy of the sensor, but limits battery life.

Along with adjusting DPI and polling with the dials on the dongle, switching them to the red “DPI” And “Hz” text allows for more granular adjustments within the configurator.

The sensor inside the mouse is a Pixart PAW3950. For anyone who doesn’t have an exhaustive list of mouse sensors floating around their head, this is the same type of sensor used in the 2022 Razer Viper V2 Pro (branded as the Razer Focus Pro 30k). That's four years old at this point. It’s not quite a cutting-edge sensor anymore. But unless you're in the top-tier of competitive play, the sensor will feel consistent and reliable enough, being able to easily track and manage direction changes during fast-paced shootouts. My personal daily driver is a Viper V2 Pro with the exact same sensor (I prefer the shape to the V3 and V4), and it’s still perfectly serviceable after four years of use.

The main buttons inside this mouse use Omron Optical switches, while the side buttons opt for low-profile Omron blue-dot mechanical switches. The scroll wheel’s encoder is a mechanical unit as well, which means it doesn’t have quite the same longevity as the optical units some mice are beginning to use. However, they are easier to replace or fix if something does go wrong.

The internal design of this mouse is impressively simple: There are six feet on the underside, and the outer four all have screws underneath. When you remove these four screws, the bottom half of the shell comes off cleanly (no flimsy plastic clips!), and a few more screws separate the internals from the top shell.

I’ve said many times that, when it comes to peripherals, technical specs are only one part of the story. More than that, it’s important to have a device you enjoy using. Of all the mice I’ve tested, the M-001 is by far one of the most enjoyable. The shape is comfortable and natural for multiple grip styles, and the material ensures that the mouse feels rigid and stable despite its light weight. The dongle is a brilliant design that elevates itself from a sad desk tchotchke to a practical and functional device. And the battery mechanism is one of the most clever and fun designs I’ve seen in ages.

Everything about this mouse is deeply satisfying, and I found myself excited to use it every time I sat down. Of course, this mouse isn’t at the cutting edge of sensors or switches, and because of this, I can’t exactly recommend this mouse to someone looking for an ultra-cost-effective option with min-maxed specs. But in using the mouse on the daily, I didn't find that it mattered as much as you might think. 8,000 Hz polling and a 25,600 DPI sensor are enough for all but the pros. Instead, it uses tried-and-true internals that function perfectly well and, more importantly, have crisp and precise tactile responses.

Que sont les élections de mi-mandat aux États-Unis ? Un guide simple

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Il reste quatre semaines avant les élections de mi-mandat américaines, lors desquelles les électeurs participeront à un scrutin qui pourrait avoir un impact majeur sur le reste de la présidence de Donald Trump.

Le 3 novembre, les Américains choisiront les membres du Congrès, composé de deux chambres : la Chambre des représentants et le Sénat.

Les élections ont lieu tous les deux ans, et lorsqu'elles se déroulent au milieu du mandat présidentiel de quatre ans, on les appelle les élections de mi-mandat.

L'une des tâches essentielles du Congrès est de créer et d'adopter des lois fédérales, c'est-à-dire des lois qui s'appliquent à l'échelle nationale.

L'issue des élections de mi-mandat déterminera quel parti politique – les républicains de Trump ou les démocrates de l'opposition – contrôlera le Congrès et la facilité avec laquelle le président pourra mettre en œuvre ses projets.

Ces résultats sont également perçus comme une mesure du degré de satisfaction des Américains quant aux performances du président jusqu'à présent.

Les 435 membres de la Chambre des représentants (la chambre basse du Congrès) sont élus. Leur mandat est de deux ans. C'est généralement à la Chambre des représentants que les projets de loi sont présentés et débattus.

Par ailleurs, 35 des 100 sièges du Sénat, la chambre haute, seront également à renouveler. Les sénateurs sont élus pour un mandat de six ans. Ils ont notamment pour mission d'approuver ou de rejeter les projets de loi transmis par la Chambre des représentants.

Le même jour, les électeurs d'une grande partie des États-Unis voteront également pour leurs représentants au niveau de l'État. Les élections des gouverneurs en Californie, en Floride, en Géorgie et au Michigan seront parmi les plus suivies.

Des informations vérifiées à portée de main

L'économie américaine est au cœur des préoccupations de nombreux électeurs qui peinent à faire face à la hausse des prix du carburant, des produits alimentaires, du logement et des soins de santé.

L'inflation reste supérieure au niveau ciblé par la Réserve fédérale américaine, en partie à cause de la guerre que les États-Unis et Israël ont déclenchée contre l'Iran en février.

Les démocrates affirment que le programme de droits de douane de Trump a également fait grimper les prix pour les Américains.

L'immigration est un autre enjeu crucial. Les républicains affirment que Trump a tenu une promesse majeure de sa campagne de 2024 en renforçant les contrôles à la frontière et en lançant une répression généralisée – et souvent controversée – contre l'immigration clandestine.

Les inquiétudes concernant l'IA se sont accentuées ces dernières semaines. Des personnalités politiques de premier plan, issues des deux partis, se sont accordées sur la nécessité de la réglementer, s'opposant ainsi à Trump, qui a rejeté l'idée de lois visant à contrôler le développement de cette technologie.

Le Congrès est l'un des trois pouvoirs de l'État, avec la présidence et le pouvoir judiciaire. Son contrôle est essentiel pour l'élaboration des lois.

La Chambre des représentants et le Sénat sont actuellement contrôlés par le Parti républicain. Cependant, sa majorité est fragile. À la Chambre, il dispose de 218 sièges, soit le minimum requis pour la contrôler, auxquels s'ajoute un indépendant qui vote avec lui.

Au Sénat, ils détiennent 53 sièges. La chambre haute ne changera de camp que si les démocrates obtiennent 51 sièges, ce qui leur permettrait de départager les voix en cas d'égalité (vote du vice-président, actuellement républicain), si chaque camp obtient 50 sièges.

Si les démocrates de l'opposition reprennent le contrôle d'au moins une chambre, ils auront le pouvoir de soumettre leurs propres lois au vote.

Par le passé, les électeurs ont eu tendance à « punir » le parti du président en votant contre lui.

Les récents sondages indiquent une faible cote de popularité pour le président. Il a entamé son second mandat avec un taux d'approbation légèrement supérieur à 50 %, selon le site d'analyse de sondages Silver Bulletin, mais début octobre, ce taux était tombé à environ 37 %. Parallèlement, 61 % des personnes interrogées le désapprouvaient.

Mais tous les sièges de chaque chambre ne sont pas disputés. Certains sont des acquis pour les républicains ou les démocrates.

Le partenaire américain de la BBC, CBS News, a identifié dix courses sénatoriales à suivre de près, dans des États comme l'Iowa et le New Hampshire, et 42 courses à la Chambre des représentants, dans des États comme l'Arizona et l'Ohio.

Le vote est plus imprévisible cette année en raison d'une bataille entre les deux partis concernant le découpage des circonscriptions électorales à la Chambre des représentants.

Les républicains ont tenté de prendre l'avantage en créant des cartes électorales plus favorables dans certains États, et les démocrates ont fait de même.

Si les démocrates remportent la Chambre des représentants ou le Sénat, Trump aura du mal à faire adopter des lois par le Congrès.

Ses projets nationaux pourraient donc être contrariés, même s'il a trouvé des moyens d'exercer le pouvoir sans le soutien de son parti dans son ensemble.

Le contrôle démocrate de l'une ou l'autre chambre leur permettrait également de recourir à des commissions d'enquête pour examiner certains aspects de la présidence Trump. Les allégations de corruption, la nouvelle salle de bal de la Maison-Blanche et les tactiques de l'ICE pourraient faire l'objet d'un examen approfondi.

Il existe également un débat au sein du Parti démocrate sur l'opportunité de destituer Trump, ce qui implique de porter des accusations devant le Congrès qui constitueraient la base d'un procès politique, et non pénal.

Si le Parti républicain conserve le contrôle du Congrès, Trump pourra poursuivre ses projets jusqu'à la fin de son mandat.

Le nom du président ne figure pas sur les bulletins de vote lors des élections de mi-mandat, bien que Trump ait exhorté les républicains à faire comme s'il y était en votant pour lui, en guise d'approbation de ses actions jusqu'à présent.

Quel que soit le résultat, les commentateurs l'utiliseront probablement pour argumenter sur la façon dont les électeurs perçoivent la direction du pays sous la présidence de Trump.

Trump ne peut pas briguer un troisième mandat présidentiel. Après les élections de mi-mandat, on peut s'attendre à ce que les spéculations aillent bon train quant à son éventuel successeur comme candidat républicain à la présidentielle de 2028, et quant à ses potentiels adversaires chez les démocrates.

I felt like the insurance companies were the invisible third party in every exam room, deciding which tests I could order and which medications my patients deserved. I wasn't just a doctor anymore; I was a data-entry clerk for multibillion-dollar payers. The result was a total, soul-crushing burnout that followed me home every night.

On the other side of it, some of my patients were forgoing necessary care because the costs presented to them were simply too high. Did it make sense to spend $300 to check a thyroid test to ensure the $10 per month medication I prescribed was working as it was supposed to? Many patients, rightfully so, decided to spend their money elsewhere.

I knew I had to get out — not just for my own sanity, but to actually provide the type of care I believed in.

I eventually traded the treadmill for Direct Primary Care (DPC). In this model, my patients (or their employers or an insurance company) pay a flat monthly membership fee directly to me (around $100 per month). This type of care is affordable— my current patient panel includes attorneys, electricians, teachers, and retirees. There are no co-pays, no deductibles, and most importantly, no insurance companies breathing down my neck.

Once I saw how much better medicine functioned without traditional insurance interference, I took the ultimate leap. I took my wife and children, now 9 and 6, off our health insurance plan.

Instead, we joined a medical cost-sharing community. It isn't insurance; it's a community of members who share each other's large, unexpected medical expenses. When I combined a DPC membership for our routine care with a health share for the big stuff, I realized I had found a better way to protect my family — and was paying far, far less for it.

To understand why we left, look at the cost of childbirth. When our first child was born under a supposedly high-end traditional insurance plan, we still walked away owing about $10,000 after paying our insurance premiums. We were paying twice: once for the premium and again for the actual care. My wife spent hours of her precious maternity leave arguing with insurance companies about the services we received in the hospital. When our infant son was hospitalized a few months later, she was back on the phone again, having to advocate for his coverage during the time of a family crisis. We spent years paying off the cost of the birth and his illness.

With our second child, we looked at the numbers through the lens of a cost-share program. On a plan like ours, the Initial Unshareable Amount (IUA) — the part we pay before the community kicks in — was only $1,500.

Beyond that $1,500, the community shared the rest. We didn't have to spend my wife's recovery time fighting with a claims adjuster or waiting for pre-authorizations. We knew exactly what our responsibility was, and more importantly, we knew our family was covered by a community, not a corporation.

As a physician, I've seen the insurance trap from both sides. We've been conditioned to believe that without a blue or green plastic card in our wallets, we are unsafe.

The reality is that for some, traditional insurance has become a barrier to care rather than a bridge. By opting out and pairing Direct Primary Care with a health share, I've reclaimed my time as a doctor and my peace of mind as a father. I'm no longer waiting for an insurance company to tell me how to care for my kids or my patients. As a doctor and as a father, for the first time in my career, I feel truly secure.
