Cómo rescatar un plato que ha salido mal y trucos para salvar tus recetas

📅 Oct 09, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A quien no le ha pasado que, por un despiste de cinco minutos, la cocina se llena de ese aroma penetrante a chamuscado o que, al probar la salsa, te das cuenta de que te has pasado tres veces con el salero. Son esos pequeños dramas culinarios que nos pueden arruinar la cena, pero lo cierto es que la mayoría de los errores en la cocina tienen solución si sabemos cómo reaccionar rápido y con cabeza.

No hace falta tirar la comida a la basura ni rendirse ante un plato que parece perdido. Existen una serie de técnicas de rescate muy sencillas que permiten neutralizar sabores fuertes o eliminar rastros de quemado, devolviéndole al plato su equilibrio original. A continuación, te contamos detalladamente cómo gestionar estas crisis gastronómicas para que tus invitados ni siquiera noten que hubo un problema.

Cuando detectamos que algo va mal por el olor, el primer impulso suele ser remover todo para ver qué ha pasado. ¡Error garrafal! Lo primero que debes hacer es trasvasar la comida a un recipiente limpio con muchísimo cuidado. La clave aquí es volcar el contenido sin rascar el fondo de la olla, ya que si arrastras los restos quemados, distribuirás ese sabor amargo por todo el guiso y ya no habrá vuelta atrás.

Una vez que hayas rescatado la parte aprovechable, es vital que no remuevas la preparación bruscamente. Para limpiar la olla dañada, lo ideal es dejarla a remojo con agua tibia, jabón y un chorrito de desengrasante; así evitarás tener que frotar con fuerza, lo que podría dañar la capa protectora del recipiente y hacer que, en el futuro, la comida se pegue con más frecuencia.

Si el sabor a quemado persiste, existen varios trucos según el tipo de plato. En los cocidos, legumbres o guisos de carne, puedes añadir un poco más de caldo o agua y cocinar a fuego lento para aligerar la mezcla, ajustando la sal y añadiendo un hilo de aceite de oliva para recuperar la textura. Si el sabor es muy intenso, un truco infalible es introducir patatas crudas troceadas en trozos grandes; estas actúan como esponjas que absorben los sabores desagradables. Solo recuerda retirarlas antes de servir el plato.

Para terminar de limpiar el ambiente, recuerda que ventilar la estancia es básico, pero preparar un poco de café recién hecho es el mejor aliado para neutralizar el olor a humo que queda impregnado en las cortinas y paredes de la cocina.

La sal es fundamental, pero es muy fácil pasarse, especialmente cuando vamos probando y sentimos que el plato está soso. Si te has pasado de la raya, no entres en pánico, ya que existen diversas formas de equilibrar la sapidez de la receta dependiendo de los ingredientes que tengas a mano.

Lo ideal, por supuesto, es no tener que llegar al rescate. Para evitar que un guiso se queme, es fundamental no descuidar la olla hasta que rompa a hervir y, en ese momento, bajar la intensidad del fuego para mantener un burbujeo suave. Asegúrate siempre de que hay líquido suficiente y mantén la cazuela tapada para evitar que el agua se evapore demasiado rápido.

En cuanto a la sal, la mejor estrategia es añadirla al final de la cocción o hacerlo muy gradualmente. Un buen consejo es espolvorearla desde una altura de unos 25 centímetros para que se distribuya de forma homogénea por toda la superficie y no se concentre en un solo punto. Recuerda que, al reducirse los líquidos por evaporación, el sabor de la sal se intensifica, por lo que es mejor pecar de cauteloso al principio.

Dominar estas técnicas de emergencia permite cocinar con más tranquilidad, sabiendo que un descuido con el fuego o un exceso de condimento no significan el fin de la comida. Ya sea usando patatas para absorber sabores no deseados, equilibrando con azúcar o controlando mejor los tiempos de cocción y la salazón, siempre hay una manera de recuperar la calidad de cualquier elaboración culinaria.

Iran: Mehr Druck auf Frauen ohne Hidschab

📅 Oct 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Immer mehr Frauen in der Islamischen Republik widersetzen sich der Hidschab-Pflicht. Hardliner setzen zunehmend auf sozialen Druck - statt auf die "Sittenpolizei" auf der Straße.

Frauen, die in der Öffentlichkeit ohne Hidschab unterwegs sind, gehören inzwischen in vielen Teilen Irans zum alltäglichen Straßenbild. Es ist eine der sichtbarsten gesellschaftlichen Veränderungen seit den Protesten der Bewegung "Frau, Leben, Freiheit" im Jahr 2022. Ausgelöst wurden diese nach dem Tod der jungen Frau Jina Mahsa Amini in Polizeigewahrsam, die wegen eines angeblich nicht korrekt getragenen Kopftuchs festgenommen worden war.

Den Behörden ist es bislang nicht gelungen, das Ausmaß an Straßenkontrollen aus der Zeit vor den Protesten wiederherzustellen. Der teilweise Rückzug der berüchtigten "Sittenpolizei" bedeutet jedoch nicht, dass sie die Durchsetzung der Hidschab-Pflicht für Frauen aufgegeben haben.

Behörden und konservative Kreise suchen nach neuen Wegen, den gesellschaftlichen Wandel einzudämmen, ohne die offenen Konfrontationen auf der Straße wiederzubeleben, die 2022 maßgeblich zu den Protesten beigetragen hatten. Justizchef Gholamhossein Mohseni Ejei forderte kürzlich ein härteres Vorgehen gegen das, was er als gesellschaftliche "Abweichungen", "Unsittlichkeit" und "Nacktheit" bezeichnete. Staatsanwälte sollten dabei enger mit Polizei, Geheimdiensten und staatlichen Institutionen zusammenarbeiten.

Gleichzeitig werben Unterstützer der Hidschab-Pflicht in Online-Kampagnen dafür, Geschäfte von Frauen zu meiden, die sich nicht an die staatlichen Kleidervorschriften halten. Stattdessen sollen Kundinnen und Kunden gezielt bei Frauen einkaufen, die den Hidschab tragen. Die Folge ist eine stärker zersplitterte Form der Kontrolle, die sich zunehmend von Polizeipatrouillen hin zu Arbeitsplätzen, Unternehmen und alltäglichen Dienstleistungen verlagert.

Die Initiatoren der Online-Kampagnen sprechen von "bewusstem Konsum" und rufen dazu auf, gezielt Anbieterinnen zu unterstützen, die den Hidschab tragen. Eine Anwältin aus Teheran erklärt gegenüber der DW unter der Bedingung der Anonymität, solche Kampagnen könnten gesellschaftliche Spaltungen vertiefen, statt die Einhaltung gesellschaftlicher Normen zu fördern. "Dies ist ein gezielter Versuch, Menschen über ihre Identität zu kategorisieren und gegeneinander auszuspielen", sagt sie.

Wirtschaftlichen Druck allein aufgrund des äußeren Erscheinungsbildes auszuüben, gehe über das Recht des Einzelnen hinaus, frei zu entscheiden, wo er einkaufen wolle. "Wenn gegen es selbst (das Regime) Sanktionen verhängt werden, spricht das Regime von Druck, Ungerechtigkeit und Wirtschaftskrieg", sagte die Juristin. "Wenn dieselbe Logik jedoch im eigenen Land angewendet wird, um einen Teil der Gesellschaft zu einem bestimmten Verhalten zu zwingen, heißt es plötzlich: Unterstützung von Werten".

Zugleich stellt sie die Annahme infrage, dass Frauen, die sich an einen bestimmten Dresscode halten, automatisch die gesellschaftlichen Werte Irans verkörpern: "Die iranische Gesellschaft ist wesentlich komplexer als das."

Berichte iranischer Frauen in sozialen Medien deuten darauf hin, dass die Kontrolle von Kleidungsvorschriften mittlerweile auch bei Alltagsdienstleistungen eine Rolle spielt. Einige Frauen sagen, ihnen sei wegen fehlenden Kopftuchs der Zugang zu Kliniken oder Banken verweigert worden.

Shirin Shams, iranische Exilaktivistin und Leiterin der Organisation Women's Revolution Organization, sieht in den aktuellen Kampagnen den Versuch, gesellschaftliche Gräben wiederherzustellen, die während der Bewegung "Frau, Leben, Freiheit" überwunden worden seien.

"Die Islamische Republik versucht seit Jahren, Frauen anhand ihrer Kleidung gegeneinander auszuspielen", sagt die in Deutschland lebende Aktivistin der DW. Für sie stellt die Hidschab-Pflicht eine der wichtigsten ideologischen Grenzlinien des iranischen Staates dar. Die große Zahl von Frauen, die inzwischen ohne Kopftuch in der Öffentlichkeit erscheinen, zeige jedoch, wie weit sich die gesellschaftliche Realität von den offiziellen Vorstellungen entfernt habe.

Die aktuellen Kampagnen seien deshalb weit mehr als eine Debatte über Kleidung. Es gehe darum, die Trennlinie zwischen Frauen, die sich den Vorgaben fügen, und jenen, die dies nicht tun, erneut zu etablieren.

Auch der in Frankreich lehrende Soziologe Saeed Paivandi sieht darin einen grundlegenden Konflikt um gesellschaftliche Kontrolle. Der Professor der Universität Lothringen erklärt gegenüber der DW, die Behörden hätten über Jahre hinweg zwischen "korrekt verschleierten" und "schlecht verschleierten" beziehungsweise unverschleierten Frauen unterschieden. "Die Regierung macht den Körper von Frauen erneut zu einer sozialen, moralischen und sogar sicherheitspolitischen Frage", sagte Paivandi.

Eine Frau ohne Kopftuch werde nicht lediglich als jemand wahrgenommen, der eine individuelle Entscheidung trifft. Teile des politischen Establishments betrachteten sie vielmehr als sichtbare Herausforderung für die ideologische Ordnung des Staates.

Während Präsident Massud Peseschkian wiederholt die Bedeutung gesellschaftlichen Zusammenhalts und nationaler Einheit betont hat, fordern konservative Richter und Politiker weiterhin eine strengere Durchsetzung der Kleidervorschriften. Dadurch entsteht ein Spannungsfeld innerhalb des iranischen Machtapparats. Ein Teil des Establishments scheint sich bewusst zu sein, dass eine Rückkehr zu großflächigen Konfrontationen zwischen Polizei und Frauen erhebliche gesellschaftliche und politische Kosten verursachen könnte.

Andere wiederum wollen die zunehmende Normalisierung des Auftretens von Frauen ohne Hidschab nicht akzeptieren. Das Ergebnis ist weder eine Rückkehr zum früheren System flächendeckender Straßenkontrollen noch das Ende der verpflichtenden Kleidervorschriften. Stattdessen zeigt sich der Druck punktuell und auf unterschiedlichen Ebenen: vor Gerichten, am Arbeitsplatz, in Unternehmen, bei Behördengängen oder durch gesellschaftliche Kampagnen.

Nach Ansicht Paivandis hat die direkte staatliche Repression ihre Grenzen erreicht. Deshalb suchten Befürworter der islamischen Kleidervorschriften nach alternativen Möglichkeiten, gesellschaftliche Konformität durchzusetzen. Dadurch werde ein Teil der Verantwortung von staatlichen Institutionen auf die Gesellschaft selbst verlagert.

Bankangestellte, Klinikmitarbeiterinnen, Arbeitgeber, Geschäftsinhaber oder Kunden könnten so zu Akteuren der Kontrolle werden, selbst wenn kein Polizist anwesend sei. Die Grenze zwischen Gesetz, persönlichem Ermessen und ideologischem Druck verschwimme dadurch zunehmend.

Zugleich warnt Paivandi, dass die Verbindung von Religion mit Ausgrenzung, Erniedrigung und Zwang besonders junge Iranerinnen und Iraner weiter von religiösen Institutionen entfremden könnte. Auch Frauen, die sich freiwillig für das Tragen eines Hidschabs entscheiden, könnten in einen Konflikt hineingezogen werden, den sie selbst nicht gewählt haben, wenn ihre Kleidung zunehmend als Zeichen politischer Loyalität interpretiert werde.

Pourquoi le magnat zimbabwéen « Sir Wicknell » était-il à la fois aimé et détesté en Afrique ?

📅 Oct 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Wicknell Chivayo, le magnat zimbabwéen décédé la semaine dernière dans un accident d'hélicoptère avec son épouse Lucy Muteke, a divisé l'opinion de son vivant et continue de le faire après sa mort.

Il se faisait appeler « Sir Wicknell », un clin d'œil à l'embellissement qui caractérisait son style de vie – et un surnom que certains utilisaient pour cet homme de 45 ans qui a accumulé suffisamment de controverses dans sa courte vie pour durer plusieurs vies.

Il se documentait sans relâche, construisant une grande partie de son image publique sur les réseaux sociaux, où il posait avec des liasses de billets et annonçait les voitures et l'argent qu'il donnait aux personnes qu'il jugeait méritantes.

Chivayo prenait plaisir à afficher ses relations avec les chefs d'État africains et à exhiber ses possessions, en réalisant des visites vidéo de sa flotte de voitures de luxe, principalement des Rolls-Royce, avec des plaques d'immatriculation personnalisées, des montres et une vaste collection de chaussures de créateurs.

Ses admirateurs voyaient en un homme immensément riche un homme qui partageait sa fortune. Ses détracteurs, horrifiés par son faste, s'interrogeaient sur la provenance de sa fortune et ses relations politiques, et considéraient son mode de vie comme une illustration flagrante des inégalités.

Mais Chivayo est resté sourd à ces critiques. À mesure que sa fortune grandissait, il affichait sa richesse avec une impudence croissante, dans un pays où près de la moitié de la population vit sous le seuil de pauvreté international, selon la Banque mondiale.

« Il donnait à ces quelques privilégiés, isolés sur un îlot de richesse au milieu d'un océan de pauvreté », a déclaré à la BBC Pedzisai Ruhanya, directeur du think tank Zimbabwe Democracy Institute, expliquant que ses dons étaient principalement destinés à des personnes influentes.

Il a qualifié la générosité de Chivayo de simple manœuvre politique de la part du parti au pouvoir, la Zanu-PF, visant à s'assurer le soutien de personnalités politiques clés, voire de chefs religieux.

Néanmoins, les témoignages de chagrin ont été nombreux, notamment de la part des bénéficiaires de sa générosité, et des hommages ont été rendus par des personnalités politiques, religieuses et du monde des affaires.

Parmi les personnes venues rendre hommage aux Chivayos dans leur somptueuse demeure de Harare, la capitale, avant leurs funérailles jeudi, se trouvait Cleopas Mukungunugwa, un jeune et influent influenceur des médias sociaux du Zanu-PF, qui avait reçu deux voitures de luxe de Sir Wicknell.

Debout devant le manoir, où est affichée une immense affiche du couple, il a déclaré aux médias locaux qu'il était dévasté par la mort de son mécène, expliquant comment « Sir Wicknell » lui dictait souvent ce qu'il devait écrire dans ses articles.

« Il a été critiqué par de nombreuses personnes sur les réseaux sociaux et notre objectif était de renforcer sa réputation de philanthrope », a-t-il déclaré.

Chivayo a quitté le lycée à l'âge de 15 ans car sa famille ne pouvait plus payer les frais de scolarité après le décès de son père lorsqu'il avait 10 ans.

Son premier emploi fut celui de commis aux salaires dans une compagnie de bus. De là, il se lança dans sa première activité indépendante : la vente de téléphones portables et le change de devises, souvent au marché noir, au plus fort de la crise d'hyperinflation qui frappait le pays.

En 2005, il a été condamné à cinq ans de prison pour blanchiment d'argent portant sur 837 000 rands sud-africains (soit environ 137 500 dollars ou 72 000 livres sterling à l'époque). Il a purgé trois ans à la prison de haute sécurité de Chikurubi, dont deux avec sursis.

Les détails de sa carrière après sa libération restent flous, mais il s'est lancé dans le secteur de l'énergie, devenant ce qu'un journal d'État a décrit comme un « entrepreneur d'appels d'offres » - gagnant de l'argent en obtenant des contrats gouvernementaux.

Il dirigeait la société Intratrek Zimbabwe, partenaire du géant chinois de l'électricité CHiNT Electric. À cette époque, la législation exigeait que toutes les entreprises du pays cèdent le contrôle économique à des Zimbabwéens noirs, dans le cadre d'une politique d'indigénisation.

En 2014, Intratrek a été sélectionnée pour construire une centrale solaire de 100 MW dans la ville de Gwanda, au sud du pays. Un contrat de plus de 170 millions de dollars a été signé l'année suivante. Ce projet est devenu l'une des plus grandes controverses de la carrière de Chivayo.

Zimbabwe Power Company, la société d'État qui avait attribué le contrat, a cherché à le résilier lorsque Intratrek n'a pas respecté ses engagements, mais les tribunaux ont finalement statué que l'accord restait valide.

Une enquête parlementaire a par la suite décrit Intratrek comme une « société écran » utilisée pour attirer les investissements.

Chivayo a reconnu qu'Intratrek manquait d'expertise en matière de construction solaire, la décrivant comme le partenaire local tandis que CHiNT fournissait l'expertise technique.

Intratrek a reçu une avance de plus de 5 millions de dollars pour la centrale solaire de Gwanda, mais celle-ci n'a toujours pas été construite.

Quelques semaines avant la mort de Chivayo, Intratrek a reçu une notification du gouvernement l'autorisant à poursuivre le projet et disposait de 24 mois pour le mener à bien.

Alors que les accusations fusaient, l'homme d'affaires se contentait de sourire et de user de persuasion pour se frayer un chemin à travers la tourmente, allant même jusqu'à séduire le président Emmerson Mnangagwa, qui n'avait pourtant pas été impressionné par lui à son arrivée au pouvoir.

En 2018, Mnangagwa a cité Gwanda comme exemple des dangers liés aux relations d'affaires avec des « hommes d'affaires sans scrupules », ajoutant de manière inquiétante que « Chikurubi n'est pas plein ». Le journal d'État Herald a rapporté que cet avertissement visait Chivayo.

Pourtant, en quelques années, cette relation s'est transformée en une amitié virile. Sur l'une des nombreuses photos témoignant de leur remarquable familiarité, Chivayo, presque allongé, est allongé sur un canapé à côté de Mnangagwa, visiblement parfaitement à l'aise en compagnie du président.

Son accès ne se limitait pas à la Zanu-PF ; il publiait également des photos avec des figures de l’opposition, notamment feu Morgan Tsvangirai et Nelson Chamisa.

Mais il devint une présence remarquée autour de Mnangagwa, parlant avec enthousiasme du président et ne cachant pas son soutien à la Zanu-PF.

Il a été accusé d'avoir abusé de son pouvoir d'achat de matériel pour les élections de 2023, lors desquelles Mnangagwa a été réélu pour un second mandat.

L'enquête a révélé que le fournisseur sud-africain Ren-Form avait largement gonflé les prix et versé par la suite des centaines de millions de rands à une société qui lui était liée. Chivayo a nié toute malversation, et la commission anticorruption du Zimbabwe a déclaré par la suite n'avoir trouvé aucune preuve le reliant à une fraude aux marchés publics. Ren-Form a également nié toute malversation.

En avril, il a offert 10 000 dollars à chacun des 360 législateurs du pays, ce qui a suscité des accusations selon lesquelles il voulait influencer un vote imminent sur un amendement constitutionnel controversé visant à prolonger de deux ans, jusqu’en 2030, ce qui aurait dû être le dernier mandat de cinq ans de Mnangagwa.

Il a été contraint de retirer son offre suite à la vive réaction du public et a nié toute intention d'influencer le vote.

Lorsque la loi a été promulguée plusieurs mois plus tard, le Zanu-PF a publié un extrait vidéo de lui avec la légende suivante : « Le milliardaire Sir Wicknell Chivayo célèbre… en chantant '2030 Ndeya Emmerson Baba !' tout en naviguant à 51 000 pieds à bord de son Gulfstream G550 ».

L'accès au pouvoir est devenu un élément central de l'image que Chivayo projetait sur les réseaux sociaux. En juin, le roi Mswati III d'Eswatini lui a accordé la citoyenneté du royaume d'Afrique australe ainsi qu'un passeport diplomatique ; des photos et des vidéos le montraient agenouillé devant le monarque.

Il possédait un impressionnant album photo, avec des clichés de lui posant avec d'autres présidents africains tels que Bola Tinubu du Nigeria, Samia Suluhu Hassan de Tanzanie, Peter Mutharika du Malawi, Daniel Chapo du Mozambique, Hakainde Hichilema de Zambie, et il faisait partie de la délégation qui a rencontré Cyril Ramaphosa d'Afrique du Sud lors de sa visite à la ferme de Mnangagwa plus tôt cette année.

Certaines relations se sont prolongées au-delà des rencontres formelles. En août, Chivayo et son épouse ont assisté aux fiançailles de Charlene, la fille du président kényan William Ruto, au domicile familial de ce dernier.

Ensuite, Ruto a personnellement conduit le couple jusqu'à leur hélicoptère. Chivayo a filmé le trajet depuis le siège passager. Fidèle à lui-même, il s'est exclamé avec jubilation : « Être conduit par le président de la République ! Dieu est grand ! »

Après le décès, Ruto a écrit sur X : « Je me souviendrai de lui pour son amitié, son énergie et sa profonde conviction dans les possibilités de notre continent. »

Mais au Kenya, l'homme d'affaires zimbabwéen était perçu avec beaucoup de suspicion en raison de sa proximité avec Ruto – une suspicion largement exprimée sur les réseaux sociaux depuis sa mort.

Nombre de ces commentaires sont inqualifiables, mais comme l'a souligné le blogueur kenyan Cyprian Nyakundi, en énumérant certaines des controverses entourant Chivayo : « En tant que Kenyan, je me désolidarise totalement de l'hommage dithyrambique rendu par le président Ruto à Wicknell Chivayo. Ces condoléances et ce langage d'« ami cher » lui sont propres, et non à moi, en tant que Kenyan. »

Un commentateur de X a déclaré que sa mort avait « uni les Africains dans la joie ».

« Quand la disparition d'un homme suscite plus de soulagement que de chagrin, la réaction elle-même est très révélatrice. Il est temps que ceux qui détiennent le pouvoir réfléchissent et réévaluent leurs actions ! »

Au Zimbabwe, on observe également une réaction contre ceux qui affirment que médiser des morts est contraire aux valeurs africaines, car le décès du magnat est un sujet d'actualité brûlant ces jours-ci.

Ce week-end, dans un centre commercial d'Harare, Christabel Moyo a exprimé ce que beaucoup pensent tout bas.

« Qu’est-ce qui est africain ? S’enrichir grâce à des contrats d’État surévalués et utiliser cet argent pour soutenir la Zanu-PF ? » a demandé ce comptable de 42 ans.

« Il s'occupait à distribuer des voitures et de l'argent aux riches alors que le système de santé publique zimbabwéen s'effondrait. »

La frénésie de cadeaux extraordinaire pour laquelle il restera dans les mémoires a commencé vers 2023. Musiciens, personnalités du sport et des affaires, politiciens et chefs religieux figuraient parmi les bénéficiaires de voitures et d'argent liquide, dans ce que certains pourraient considérer comme une tentative flagrante d'acheter du soutien au parti au pouvoir.

Il a commencé avec des Toyota Aqua relativement modestes, mais les cadeaux sont rapidement devenus beaucoup plus chers.

Le célèbre musicien Jah Prayzah, proche du parti Zanu-PF, a reçu trois voitures de luxe et environ 400 000 dollars en trois ans.

Le musicien a reporté son mariage, qui devait avoir lieu le week-end dernier, à cause de l'accident d'hélicoptère, déclarant : « Wicknell était plus qu'un ami pour nous. »

Parmi les autres bénéficiaires importants figurait l'évêque Nehemiah Mutendi, chef de l'Église chrétienne de Sion, farouchement fidèle à la Zanu-PF. Le religieux a reçu des millions en espèces et quatre voitures de luxe.

Chivayo fréquentait régulièrement l'église apostolique africaine Johane Masowe weChishanu, l'une des plus importantes du Zimbabwe, dont les fidèles sont souvent vus en train de prier en plein air vêtus de robes blanches.

Elle a également reçu des dizaines de voitures, de bus et d'argent liquide de la part de l'homme d'affaires, qui a rapproché cette secte influente du parti au pouvoir, emmenant notamment Mnangagwa comme invité d'honneur à un rassemblement religieux massif en août.

The U.S. Public Is Losing Its Say on Environmental Policy

📅 Oct 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A hearing room in the headquarters of the Environmental Protection Agency in Washington, D.C. Carol M. Highsmith / Buyenlarge / Getty Images

Federal agencies have long been required to seek public comment when writing new regulations or approving large projects, from highways to data centers. The Trump administration is systematically undercutting that process, stifling public input while skirting federal laws.

Public participation is a hallmark of environmental regulation, embedded in every major environmental statute enacted over the past half-century. Yet the Trump administration has systematically sought to curtail public input into the government’s environmental decision-making by shirking decades of precedent and making strained interpretations of longstanding federal law. Looked at collectively, the pattern of public disenfranchisement is unmistakable.

There are good reasons to speed up the process of writing regulations and issuing permits, but truncating public input has serious costs. It can result in flawed policy that is harder to implement, full of avoidable error, and more vulnerable to being struck down by courts. Input from industry, public interest groups, academics, states, and other agencies gives regulators critical feedback on the potential impact of their proposed rules, helping them to anticipate and avoid unforeseen problems. Stakeholders possess information, experience, and expertise the government lacks, and they can suggest alternatives to make rules more effective or less costly. By testing agency assumptions and pushing officials to explain their rationale, public comments perform a crucial vetting function. The process also helps to make government more accountable by alerting Congress to potential pitfalls and building a record in the event of judicial review.

In 2025, the administration began stifling public input on the rigorous environmental reviews required by the 1969 National Environmental Policy Act. Under this law, federal agencies must evaluate and disclose the foreseeable environmental impacts of the major projects they build, fund, or approve, from highways to dams to data centers. The point of NEPA disclosure is to avoid unnecessary harm by having agencies look before they leap.

The process can delay projects, but it can also improve them substantially. Studies show that public-comment requirements and stakeholder engagement have repeatedly identified environmental risks that proposing agencies had missed. Often, significant harms can be reduced by taking sensible steps, like rerouting roads around sensitive areas, as was done with Utah’s Legacy Parkway, which was redesigned to largely avoid the Great Salt Lake wetlands. In another example, the Energy Department amended its permitting plan for the Los Alamos National Laboratory to include a comprehensive wildfire-mitigation program after commenters focused attention on the risks of wildfire. These steps significantly reduced impacts when a fire later burned the region.

For this process to work, however, the government must share its draft impact statements with the public, which past Republican and Democratic administrations have done. The Trump administration lifted that obligation, though, and agencies including Interior, Agriculture, Energy, and Transportation announced last year that they will no longer share drafts, making informed public comment practically impossible. Several agencies have also jettisoned the requirement to analyze proposed projects’ indirect and cumulative impacts, like climate change. The agencies made these changes without taking comment first, thus narrowing public involvement without public involvement.

A 2011 fire near the Los Alamos National Laboratory in New Mexico. Public commenters drew attention to the risk of wildfires when the lab was undergoing environmental review. Jae C. Hong / Associated Press

The Trump administration’s efforts to suppress public input are part of a larger backlash in which critics on both the right and left claim that procedural red tape strangles worthy projects. Some of this criticism is fair, but it can be overdrawn. Infrastructure projects falter for many reasons, including high costs, supply chain problems, and opposition from rival developers. Environmental reviews may be part of the problem, but project timelines would be shorter if agencies followed NEPA rules more carefully from a project’s inception. Congress already limited the scope and length of environmental reviews in 2023, and while more steps are needed to streamline federal permitting, that can be done without jettisoning valuable public consultation.

The permitting reform legislation proposed in the Senate in September would, among other things, drastically limit legal remedies when agencies violate NEPA, barring courts from delaying projects even when the agencies make blatant errors in environmental reviews. If projects proceed no matter what, there is little incentive to engage the public. The changes might speed up projects, but they will also result in many avoidable and unnecessary harms.

In addition to thwarting comments on new projects, the administration is also marginalizing comments on new regulations. The president has directed agencies to issue rules without taking input first, citing the “good cause” exception to the requirement in the Administrative Procedure Act that agencies publish their proposed rules in the Federal Register, triggering a 30- or 60-day comment period. Decades of legal precedent holds that agencies may finalize rules before taking comment only in exceptional cases where they can justify the urgency — for example, where the rule is necessary to foil terrorism, respond to a public health emergency, or prevent investors from gaming the market in anticipation of a regulatory change.

Finalizing rules more quickly has obvious appeal — the current system is slow and cumbersome and blocks both Republican and Democratic administrations from getting things done. But shunting public input to the end of the process will not solve that problem if rules are less sound, prone to error, and more vulnerable to legal challenge as a result.

In another example of limiting public participation, the EPA is proposing to drop a longstanding federal requirement that state and local air agencies allow the public to comment on air pollution permits for new “minor” sources that emit pollution below a certain threshold. This classification could include data centers that divide up their power generation units to make each source appear smaller. These permits are highly controversial because data centers powered by gas or diesel generators emit substantial air pollution. But the rule change could allow these permits to be fast-tracked without any opportunity for the public to weigh in.

Gas turbines at an xAI data center in Memphis, Tennessee. The Trump administration is trying to dismiss a citizen suit against the data center. Brandon Dill for The Washington Post via Getty Images

Besides undercutting notice and comment, the administration has stymied public participation in a broad range of environmental decisions by declaring emergencies and citing national security, among other tactics. For example, in March, the Interior Department invoked a never-before-used national security exemption to relieve the oil and natural gas industry of Endangered Species Act requirements in the Gulf of Mexico on the rationale that court rulings enforcing the law might hamper energy production, which the administration linked to national security. In 2025, President Trump granted two-year exemptions from Clean Air Act hazardous air pollution standards to coal plants, coke ovens, and chemical manufacturers, invoking for the first time a national security-based provision in the Clean Air Act.

A hard-won rule to cut chemical plant pollution is being unraveled. Read more.

In another gambit in June, the EPA found a creative way to revoke three waivers that allowed California to set its own clean car standards: by relabeling them as rules and submitting them to Congress for fast-track “disapproval” under the Congressional Review Act. Both the Government Accountability Office and the Senate Parliamentarian objected to this maneuver but were ignored. Normally, waiver rescissions would require public notice and comment. (A district court has blocked EPA from relabeling a second batch of waivers.)

Separately, the administration is trying to frustrate the citizen suit provisions that Congress included in virtually every major environmental law passed in the 1970s and 1980s. Under these provisions, individuals or groups may file a lawsuit against regulated entities for statutory or regulatory violations (unless the federal government is “diligently prosecuting” the same violations). Citizen suits may also be brought against federal agencies for failing to perform congressionally mandated duties, like setting pollution standards by certain deadlines. This summer, in response to a citizen suit by a coalition of states and nonprofit groups, a federal court held that the EPA had failed to identify areas with unhealthy levels of soot pollution — a first step toward applying stricter control measures — by the legal deadline and ordered it to do so by early next year. (The administration is appealing.)

While it’s true that private enforcement actions can be inconvenient for an administration, there is no doubt that citizen suits have profoundly improved conditions for communities affected by harmful pollution. Both Republican and Democratic administrations have sometimes objected to these suits, but no administration has sought to eliminate them.

Until this summer, that is, when the Trump administration intervened in a citizen suit against xAI for violating the Clean Air Act when generating power for its data centers. The administration claims that the president may demand that a court dismiss a citizen suit for any reason, whether the administration is diligently prosecuting violations or not.

Public participation does take time, but it is valuable to governance. It gives regulated industries, professional associations, public interest groups, experts, other agencies, states, and individuals a chance to be heard. It also helps to build trust in government. As quaint as that claim may sound, it is backed up by evidence. Research shows that when people participate in decisions that affect them, they are more likely to view those decisions as legitimate, even when they don’t go their way. Having a voice matters. There are good reasons to reform our regulatory and permitting system, but not by dispensing with public participation.

Un champagne de prestige ne naît pas du hasard. Il résulte d'un travail patient : un assemblage réfléchi de plusieurs années, puisé dans des terroirs triés sur le volet, puis sublimé par un élevage long sur lies. C'est cette maturation, souvent étalée sur de longues périodes, qui donne naissance à une complexité aromatique si particulière — on y retrouve des notes de fruits secs, des touches pâtissières, parfois une pointe de miel ou de fleurs blanches qui s'affirme avec le temps. Ces cuvées dépassent largement leur statut de boisson festive : elles racontent une histoire, celle d'un savoir-faire transmis et d'une culture viticole exigeante. Mais alors, comment un amateur curieux peut-il apprendre à les apprécier pour ce qu'elles sont vraiment, au-delà de leur image sophistiquée ?

Impossible de parler de champagne haut de gamme sans évoquer les Grands Crus, véritable colonne vertébrale de ces cuvées d'exception. Ils apportent cette minéralité caractéristique, une finesse de bulle incomparable et un potentiel de garde qui fait toute la différence. Dans cet équilibre subtil, le Chardonnay insuffle tension et fraîcheur, quand le Pinot Noir vient structurer le vin et lui offrir ses notes fruitées. En général, une cuvée réussie mise sur une majorité de Chardonnay complétée par du Pinot Noir provenant de villages réputés — le tout orchestré dans un assemblage où terroir et cépages dialoguent avec précision.

Là où un champagne millésimé capture l'essence d'une seule année, l'assemblage de plusieurs millésimes vise un objectif différent : trouver ce point d'équilibre parfait entre fraîcheur, complexité et harmonie. Le chef de cave joue alors les chefs d'orchestre, mariant une année généreuse, une autre plus fraîche, une troisième plus charpentée, pour composer un style qui reste fidèle à lui-même au fil du temps. C'est lui, finalement, qui incarne le garant de la cohérence du style — celui qui veille à ce que la signature de la maison ne se démente jamais.

Vient ensuite le nez, qui se dévoile peu à peu : fleurs blanches, agrumes confits, notes pâtissières se succèdent au fil des minutes. Enfin, la bouche livre son verdict, avec cette tension et cet équilibre si recherchés entre fraîcheur et ampleur. Chaque dégustation devient alors bien plus qu'un simple plaisir gustatif : c'est une occasion d'enrichir sa culture personnelle et de rendre hommage, à sa manière, au travail minutieux qui se cache derrière chaque bouteille.

That is the part of coming home everybody pictures. Nobody pictures the 40 minutes before it, at the baggage belt and the Customs lane, which is where most homecomings actually go wrong.

This balikbayan travel checklist is about those 40 minutes, and the four weeks before them. It is for OFWs flying home from the Gulf, Hong Kong, Singapore and Japan, and for Fil-Am and Fil-Can families bringing the kids home for Christmas 2026. We cover what to sort a month out, what your airline actually lets you carry, what Customs lets through duty-free, the one app every returning OFW needs before the trip back, and which NAIA terminal you land in now that the airlines have been shuffled around.

The cheapest Christmas seats are gone by October, and that is not news to anyone who has done this before. What is still in your control is how the trip is built.

Price the December premium honestly. On Trip.com’s Philippine site, checked 4 October 2026, Los Angeles to Manila averages PHP 99,210 return in December against PHP 57,908 in November and PHP 59,793 in January. Dubai to Manila averages PHP 47,691 return in December against PHP 38,969 in January. Those are whole-month averages, and the real squeeze sits between roughly 18 December and 4 January, so treat them as a floor, not a quote. If your leave allows a 10 December arrival or a mid-January return, that is where the savings are.

Compare flights home to Manila, Cebu and Clark on Trip.com

Put the domestic leg on the same ticket. If you are flying Riyadh to Manila and then Manila to Iloilo, book it as one itinerary. A delayed long-haul flight on one booking means the airline rebooks your connection. On two separate bookings, the second airline owes you nothing and your seat to Iloilo flies without you.

OFWs: get the OFW Pass before you fly home, not after. The paper Overseas Employment Certificate is gone for returning workers. The Department of Migrant Workers folded it into the OFW Pass, a free QR code in the eGovPH app, under DMW Advisory 38-2025 as reported by SunStar in December 2025. Open eGovPH, go to the DMW section and choose Balik-Manggagawa. If your contract is already in the DMW database the pass is issued automatically; if you changed employer or jobsite, update those first. The pass is valid for 90 days from issue, so time it so that it still covers your flight back to work.

It also matters on the way out of Manila. The OFW Pass is your proof for the travel tax and terminal fee exemption, which we explain in our guide to travel tax and terminal fees in the Philippines. Do it in your first week home, not on the morning of your flight, because processing can take a few working days when it is not automatic.

Every Christmas someone repacks a bag on the floor of Terminal 3 because they assumed 30 kg. The allowance is set by your fare, not your airline, and the spread is huge.

Two tips from the check-in line. Weigh every bag at home with a hand scale, because the 32 kg per-piece limit applies even when your total allowance is bigger. And split the heavy items: one bag of canned goods and one bag of clothes is how you end up paying excess on one and wasting space on the other.

If you are sending more than a suitcase can hold, we ran the per-kilo numbers on boxes, air cargo and extra bags in balikbayan box vs excess baggage vs cargo. Most sea cargo cutoffs for Christmas have already passed, so for 2026 the realistic options are a prepaid extra bag or air cargo.

The Bureau of Customs guidelines for arriving travelers come down to four numbers. Know them and the Customs lane is a walk-through.

Returning residents and foreigners have a similar PHP 150,000 cap on new personal items, limited to one of each kind. “One of each kind” is the line that catches people. One new phone for yourself is personal effects. Five new phones of the same model, still boxed, look like stock for a shop, and commercial quantities lose the exemption entirely.

Cash is a separate rule. You can carry up to PHP 50,000 in pesos; more needs written authorization from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas. Foreign currency above USD 10,000 (about PHP 628,000 at the BSP reference rate of PHP 62.80 on 2 October 2026) must be declared. Carrying it is not illegal; failing to declare it is the problem.

Pasalubong deserves its own post, and it is getting one. For now, the safe rule is personal quantities, original packaging and receipts in your hand-carry.

Register on eTravel within 72 hours of arrival. Every arriving passenger fills it in at etravel.gov.ph, and it carries your Customs baggage declaration and currency declaration, which replaced the old paper forms. It is free, so any site that asks for a card payment is not the government site. The QR code you get is for this trip only; do not show a saved one from last year.

Fil-Ams, Fil-Cans and Fil-Aussies: the balikbayan privilege is your best perk. Under Republic Act 9174, a former Filipino citizen gets visa-free entry for one year. So do a foreign spouse and children traveling with them. The Philippine Consulate in Frankfurt’s guidance says to tell the immigration officer you are availing of the privilege and to show your old Philippine passport or a copy of your Philippine birth certificate. Bring the marriage certificate for a spouse and birth certificates for the kids. We covered the spouse and children rules in detail in our post on the one-year visa-free balikbayan stay for foreign spouses and children.

The same law gives balikbayans a tax-exempt shopping allowance of USD 1,500 (about PHP 94,200) at government-operated duty-free shops. The usual window is 15 days from arrival, and the law stretches it to 30 days during the Christmas season, 15 November to 15 January. Duty Free Philippines has applied its own allowances over the years, so ask at the counter what it is honoring before you fill a cart.

Why do fizzy drinks make you burp?

📅 Oct 07, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Have you ever wondered why fizzy drinks make you burp? Read on to find out and try a science activity to demonstrate!

Fizzy drinks are fizzy because they have carbon dioxide gas pumped into them under high pressure. If you have a SodaStream at home, you’ve probably seen how adding the gas makes water fizzy. Fizzy drink bottles are tightly sealed so no air can escape, but when you open the cap, the pressure drops and the carbon dioxide escapes. This is why fizzy drinks go flat over time.

When you drink a fizzy drink, it travels into your stomach. The inside of your body is around 37°C. When gas warms up, it expands and spreads out to fill all the available space. As the gas builds up, it pushes against the top of your stomach. To stop the stomach from inflating too much, you burp to let the trapped gas escape.

We can model burping with fizzy water, a balloon and a bottle! The bottle represents the stomach. As the fizzy water warms up, the carbon dioxide bubbles expand and escape from the water, inflating the balloon!

Carefully open the bottle of fizzy water and place the balloon over the bottle’s mouth.

Gently swirl the water around the bottle. You should see gas bubbles rising and starting to inflate the balloon.

Carefully place the bottle in the bowl of warm water (this represents a warm tummy). The increased temperature should help the gas bubbles escape the water more quickly, making the balloon inflate more.

Gently pull the balloon off the bottle and let the gas out with a burping noise.

WARNING – use warm, not hot water, and an adult should supervise this activity.

Fizzy water contains dissolved carbon dioxide gas.

When the fizzy water enters your tummy, it warms up. Warm liquids can’t hold as much gas as cold liquids, so the gas breaks free and builds up inside the balloon.

When you burp, the sphincter muscle at the top of your stomach opens to let out gas. In this activity, releasing the balloon mimics the sphincter opening.

Keep one bottle in the fridge and one in warm water, investigate to find out which inflates a balloon the fastest.

Jonathan, a 192-Year-Old Giant Tortoise, Holds Insights for Aging Well

📅 Oct 07, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Jonathan, the oldest known living land animal. Saint Helena Government

At 192 years old, Jonathan the giant tortoise is the oldest known living land animal. A new study offers clues as to his incredible longevity.

Jonathan was born in the Seychelles in 1832, but since 1882, he has lived on the South Atlantic island of Saint Helena, where he resides at the home of its governor. Today, he is roughly a century older than most other Seychelles giant tortoises.

So why has Jonathan lived so long? An analysis of cheek scrapings has found that he is unusually blessed with good genes — not just genes that encode for good health, but genes that resist wear and tear.

Jonathan bears dozens of genetic variants that help him combat aging by curbing inflammation, regulating insulin, and warding off cancer. But also, his genes are uncommonly good at repairing themselves, according to the research, which was published in Science Advances.

“Nature has already solved the puzzle of aging in remarkable ways, and Jonathan’s genome provides a blueprint for cellular resilience,” said lead author Stephen Clark, chief scientist at Kallel, a nonprofit that researches longevity.

Increasingly, scientists are finding that older animals play a critical role in nature by helping their younger kin to survive. While conservationists have long prioritized the survival of younger animals, a growing body of research shows that older animals hold knowledge that guides survival, play outsized roles in reproduction, and possess stronger defenses against disease.

“Not all individuals contribute equally,” Keller Kopf, an ecologist at Charles Darwin University, told Yale Environment 360. “Older animals play roles that are often invisible in simple population counts.”

Pluralistic: Disloyalty (07 Oct 2026)

📅 Oct 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Pluralistic: Daily links from Cory Doctorow

Up until very recently indeed, airlines were the only businesses that I dealt with regularly that practiced "price discrimination," charging different customers different prices for the same seats.

Economists broadly like this "second-order" price discrimination, where a company charges customers different prices based on the circumstances of the purchase, such as a bakery discounting bread at the end of the day. When an airline cranks up the price of an itinerary that doesn't include a Saturday stay, they're using that fact as a proxy for "price-insensitive" business travelers whose bosses can afford to pay more for a ticket, rather than paying a worker overtime to spend a weekend away from home after a meeting.

Despite the arguments about "efficiency," it's safe to say that *fliers* hate this. The airlines' pricing algorithms are so opaque and capricious that anyone who buys a ticket at *any* price inevitably feels like they got a bad deal. This situation is only exacerbated by the airlines' extreme and aggressive secrecy over their pricing strategies, which includes lawsuits against websites that use historical pricing data to predict when a plane ticket will be the cheapest to buy:

https://www.eff.org/document/preliminary-injunction-american-airlines-v-farechase-inc

When a company wants the government to intervene to prohibit third parties from publishing factual information about its prices, that's a bad look. Hard to believe that they're so sure that you, the customer, will be delighted by these pricing tactics that they can't bear to have these third parties ruin the surprise.

But this second-order discrimination is *so* 20th century. Here in the 21st century, we have *first-order* price discrimination, in which AI-powered pricing algorithms use commercial surveillance data about *you*, personally, to predict the highest price you're willing to pay. In this world of "surveillance pricing," every traveler (or customer) sees a different price, in a kind of cod-Marxist dystopia whose motto is "from each according to their ability (to pay), to each according to their (desperate) needs":

https://pluralistic.net/2025/01/11/socialism-for-the-wealthy/#rugged-individualism-for-the-poor

Airlines *love* this kind of pricing, and they have means, motive and opportunity to practice it. All over the world, airlines have acquired or entered into joint ventures with surveillance pricing companies. These deals put the airlines on the horns of a dilemma: they love to boast to their shareholders about the way that surveillance pricing will let them gouge fliers, but inevitably the public finds out about these investor calls and breaks out the pitchforks and torches.

This triggers embarrassing climbdowns, like Delta's fiasco of summer 2025, when CEO Ed Bastian bragged to shareholders that his deal with the Israeli surveillance pricing company Fetcherr would boost profits by 50% (!) by figuring out which customers could be safely gouged. When fliers, civil society groups and members of Congress got wind of this and raised a ruckus, Fetcherr publicly disavowed Bastian's remarks, insisting that it wasn't even capable of the kind of price-fixing he'd promised. Then Delta launched a smear campaign against its critics, claiming that Bastian never said the thing he *absolutely said* and accusing surveillance pricing activists of peddling "misinformation":

https://groundworkcollaborative.org/news/amid-deltas-ai-pricing-scheme-groundwork-applauds-congressional-action-to-crack-down-on-surveillance-pricing-schemes/

Meanwhile, airlines all over the world have quietly switched their frequent flier reward programs from "mileage-based" rewards to "revenue-based" rewards. That means that you get points and status based on how much you spend on your ticket, not how far you fly. What may not be obvious from this move is that it forces frequent fliers to buy their tickets directly from the airlines, who claim that they can't tell how much you've spent on your ticket otherwise.

So tickets bought on sites like Expedia or through travel agents often qualify for insultingly small points awards, or no points at all. For example, British Airways once gave me *12 points* on a $2,000 Alaska Air ticket because I didn't buy it through British Airways' site (attaining BA's gold status requires *50,000* points).

Forcing frequent fliers to buy their tickets directly from the airline's website sets things up beautifully for surveillance-driven, first-order price discrimination. Airlines you do business with regularly can augment the personal information you provide to them and the data they collect from you with sensitive information purchased from the unregulated data-broker industry, which will cheerfully disclose your salary, credit card debts, or even if you've recently lost a parent and might be flying out for a funeral.

The airlines strenuously deny that they're doing this, of course, but they continue to make investor-facing announcements about their exciting deals with surveillance pricing companies…and things are getting *weird* for frequent travelers.

Thanks to a lot of book-related travel over the past couple of years, I have "Elite" status with Air Canada. Yesterday, I needed to book a last-minute, one-way fare on a route that is only serviced by Air Canada (AC enjoys a monopoly over many Canadian routes, even between major Canadian cities). I checked it out on Google Flights and found a fare for the eye-watering sum of USD829.73.

Now, my normal daily-use browser is Firefox, but I know from long experience that I can't log in to my Air Canada frequent flier account on any browser except Chromium, which I keep installed on my system for just this kind of hostile garbage website. So I tabbed over to Chromium and pasted the Google Flights outbound link that would take me to a pre-populated reservation form on aircanada.com. To my amazement, that same flight was showing as a USD1200 fare!

These were identical trips, loaded via identical referrer URLs. The difference was that in the browser where Air Canada knew that I was an extremely loyal and prolific customer, they had whacked me with a *44.8% premium* to punish me for my loyalty!

I couldn't believe it. I actually called Air Canada's customer service line for "Elite" fliers, and the customer service rep was at a loss for an explanation and could only apologise and suggest that I write a letter to the company.

I'm publishing this instead, because a) this will do more to punish Air Canada for trying to screw me over; and b) this can help you book your tickets in the future.

Yesterday, I finished buying the ticket in my logged-out Firefox window (Air Canada's website will work with Firefox to buy tickets provided you don't try to log in to your Aeroplan account first), paying $829. Then I flipped back to Chromium, where I was still logged into my Air Canada Aeroplan account, located the reservation, and added my frequent flier number to it.

I guess this is how I'm buying my plane tickets from now on.

ShinyHunters Extorted Boeing Spin-off Prior to Arrests

📅 Oct 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A teenager from Amman, Jordan suspected of leading the prolific data theft and extortion group ShinyHunters has been detained and is reportedly cooperating with the FBI to identify other members of the hacking gang. KrebsOnSecurity has learned that the suspect, who uses the hacker handle “Rey,” was detained as ShinyHunters was in the process of extorting a business unit recently divested by the global aerospace company Boeing, which manufactures the fleet of planes used by the employer of Rey’s father — Royal Jordanian Airlines.

The logo for Jeppesen ForeFlight, a business unit divested last year by the aerospace firm Boeing.

On October 3, Reuters cited three unnamed sources saying a suspected ShinyHunters member in Amman named Saif Al-din Khader was detained by Jordanian authorities and was cooperating with the FBI. KrebsOnSecurity identified Rey as Khader in a November 2025 profile, in which the young man admitted working with multiple ransomware groups.

Rey was featured again in a September 28 exclusive about the Dutch police arresting 24-year-old convicted cybercriminal Pepijn van der Stap on suspicion of aiding in data thefts and extortions by ShinyHunters. The story noted that immediately following the Dutchman’s arrest on the evening of September 15, Rey assumed control over the ShinyHunters brand and boasted publicly about stealing highly sensitive data from the FBI and extorting the ransomware group Cl0p.

Rey taunted both the FBI and Cl0p with memes posted to his longtime account on Twitter/X, while simultaneously including images of the avatar used by Van Der Stap’s former hacker alias “Umbreon” in an apparent attempt to frame the Dutchman for both hacks.

A taunting meme uploaded to Twitter/X by Rey on Sept. 22. A giant sized version of the Pokemon character Umbreon can be seen in the bottom left.

As noted in our September 28 report, ShinyHunters gained access to the FBI site and other victims by exploiting a vulnerability (CVE-2026-35273) in PeopleSoft, a software-as-a-service platform from the tech giant Oracle that is broadly used by companies to manage hiring and human resources, benefits and payroll. Oracle quickly issued a fix for CVE-2026-35273, which ShinyHunters first began exploiting as a zero-day in June, and at the time Mandiant released web application firewall rules intended for organizations that couldn’t apply the security update quickly enough.

ShinyHunters told BleepingComputer in June that the original goal behind exploiting the PeopleSoft vulnerability was to breach the FBI’s own PeopleSoft database, but the hackers said those attacks were unsuccessful for some reason. In recent weeks, however, ShinyHunters turned to a well-known URL-encoding trick to bypass Mandiant’s suggested web application firewall rules.

In a report released Sept. 25, security experts at Mandiant and the Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) confirmed that ShinyHunters had mass-exploited the PeopleSoft vulnerability to steal data from dozens of systems across a range of industries, including higher education, technology, healthcare, agriculture, transportation and government.

Reuters reported October 5 that the FBI has removed a contractor at Accenture over their failure to patch the FBI recruitment website hacked by ShinyHunters, which exposed sensitive data on more than 5,000 FBI personnel, including each’s person’s unit and specialization, as well as medical and psychiatric records.

According to two sources familiar with the ShinyHunters investigation, a navigation and digital aviation unit recently divested by the global aerospace company Boeing was among the victims that ShinyHunters was in the process of extorting when Rey was apprehended by Jordanian authorities.

Those sources said the FBI’s investigation into ShinyHunters gained renewed urgency with the group’s attempted extortion of the former Boeing unit, which allegedly included the theft of sensitive information that sources said could pose operational safety and security risks.

In a brief statement shared with KrebsOnSecurity, Boeing acknowledged the extortion attempts by ShinyHunters, and said the incident concerned data stolen from Jeppesen ForeFlight, a subsidiary that Boeing sold in November 2025 to the private equity firm Thoma Bravo for $10.55 billion.

“We are aware of claims by a threat actor regarding data allegedly associated with Boeing and our former subsidiary Jeppesen ForeFlight,” a Boeing spokesperson shared. “We are actively reviewing the matter with the Jeppesen ForeFlight team.”

A spokesperson for Jeppesen ForeFlight shared a written statement in response to questions, saying the company has seen no impact on their end. “Based on our investigation to date into this claim and proactive security posture, there was no impact to our operations or products.”

Rey’s alleged involvement in attempting to extort the former Boeing unit is noteworthy because there is strong evidence that his father works for Royal Jordanian Airlines, which is mostly controlled by the Jordanian government and operates its long-haul fleet on passenger planes built by Boeing. Rey claimed on Telegram in early 2025 that his father was an airline pilot, although that could not be independently confirmed.

However, as noted in our November 2025 profile of Rey, his family’s shared computer was at one point compromised by password-stealing malware, and the data collected by that malware clearly shows Rey’s father used the same credentials to log in at multiple online portals for Royal Jordanian Airlines employees.

Royal Jordanian Airlines has not yet responded to a request for comment. In advance of our September 28 story, KrebsOnSecurity once again emailed Rey’s father to seek comment and update him on his son’s alleged activities. Neither of the Khaders have responded. But just hours after that request was sent, Rey began deleting his various social media accounts, including the Twitter/X account he previously used to taunt the FBI, Cl0p, and other ShinyHunters victims.

Rey may have nixed many of his social media profiles, but his cybersecurity blog on GitHub somehow escaped the purge, and it shows that Rey was fixated on the leaders of the Cl0p ransomware group. In March 2026, Rey’s blog featured a lengthy post that identified two Russian men as the core developers and hackers behind Cl0p.

Rey’s blog on GitHub. This post doxes two Russian men as the core operators behind Cl0p, one of the oldest and most established ransomware groups still in operation today.

Meanwhile, news outlets in the Netherlands reported explosive new allegations leveled at Van der Stap, whose supposed personal transformation from convicted to reformed hacker has been widely covered in the tech news media. The Dutch daily RTL reported on Sept. 29 that investigators suspect Van der Stap tried to orchestrate at least two murders. According to RTL, the murders were allegedly to be committed abroad, and there are indications Van der Stap gave the order for these attacks.

Van der Stap was released from prison after serving the better part of a four year sentence for data theft and extortion activity that prosecutors said netted between €1.5 million and €2.7 million. In an interview with KrebsOnSecurity on September 9, Van der Stap described his new role as “offensive security lead” at the Dutch cybersecurity company Neo Security, saying the job involved probing client networks for security vulnerabilities.

Neo Security’s owner Benjamin Korper told Reuters he has hired an outside firm to investigate whether Van der Stap had hacked Neo Security or its customers, but that so far investigators have found no evidence he acted against his employer or clients. Korper said Dutch forensic investigators visited his office on September 15, the night Van der ⁠Stap was arrested in a dramatic police raid that reportedly involved flash bang grenades.

A screenshot of a Sept 16 story by the Dutch news outlet at5.nl, describing a police raid on Van Der Stap’s residence that reportedly used flash-bang grenades.

Prior to his first arrest in 2023, Van der Stap was working as a software engineer at the Amsterdam-based cybersecurity startup Hadrian, while volunteering at the Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure (DIVD) — even as he was hacking into and extorting a number of large organizations.

When asked in a recent interview why anyone should believe the word of a self-described “reformed” cybercriminal who had so casually deceived countless friends, co-workers and journalists for years, Van der Stap replied that his work spoke for itself and there was nothing he could say that would convince his worst critics.

“You can throw a bunch of nice words at someone, but you can’t convince them if they don’t want to be convinced,” Van der Stap told KrebsOnSecurity on Sept. 9. “I’m doing what I can to repay victims, and that’s all I can do. If someone doesn’t want to believe me, then that’s on them.”

Cybercriminals aligned with ShinyHunters have been responsible for dozens of data breaches involving billions of stolen records, and breaches claimed by the group stretch back to at least 2019. But experts say the people recently operating behind the ShinyHunters name are not the same core members that populated the group in its early days, most of whom are French citizens who have been arrested (if not also imprisoned) on at least one prior occasion for alleged cybercrime activity.

More to the point, ShinyHunters has become something of a franchise. Think the Dread Pirate Roberts character in the 1980s cult movie classic “The Princess Bride,” only succession by death is replaced with succession by arrest, and there can be multiple simultaneous Dread Pirate Robertses. Sources close to the investigation say the FBI is focusing on a remaining handful of cybercriminal freelancers or affiliates who have been feeding the group stolen credentials to various software-as-a-service (SaaS) platforms used by major companies in exchange for a cut of any data ransoms later paid by victims.

In the days after the news broke of Van der Stap’s arrest, a cybercrime-focused chat server on Telegram that was allegedly operated by Rey erupted with hot takes, with most participants heaping ridicule on the teenage hacker after he publicly backed down from threats against the FBI and Cl0p, and again when the ShinyHunters’s darknet website suddenly went offline. Several commentators accused Rey of resurrecting the ShinyHunters brand after its core members were rounded up in France, and making a mockery of the group’s name and reputation ever since.

“He bought the old forum PGP key and used it to make new Breachforum websites and Telegram channels larping as ShinyHunters to ransom companies and then sell the used data or resell his forum when he goes broke,” one member recounted.

A relatively new Telegram channel called “The Battle” has been doxing and needling Rey and other alleged ShinyHunters members for several weeks, and it has gained a considerable readership among the cybercrime communities operating on Telegram. One of the coordinators of that harassment campaign repeatedly portrayed Rey as clueless greenhorn who sought to ride the coattails of a cybercriminal brand that has long enjoyed a reputation for ruthlessly selling or publishing data stolen from victim companies who refuse to give in to extortion demands.

“Rey (Saif Al-Din Khader) made a serious mistake when he started pretending to be a member of ShinyHunters,” wrote the administrators of The Battle server on Telegram. “That group had already been dismantled, with many of its members either arrested or imprisoned, yet Rey still chose to use its name while carrying out his crimes. We’re aware of claims that [Rey] caused over $200 million in damages and helped around 5–6 friend groups in the community make money by using Shiny Hunters group aliases to negotiate deals for a 25–30% cut over the past few months.”

In an interview with The Register, ShinyHunters claimed they hacked the FBI to counter the agency’s narrative in a May 2026 alert that advised victims against paying a ransom to the group, which came off looking unprofessional and capricious in the FBI’s advisory.

A flash notice on ShinyHunters released by the FBI on May 15, 2026.

The public notice warned the group has been known to pursue a number of different victim harassment strategies, from sending threatening text messages and phone calls to victims and their family members to in some cases swatting victims. The FBI warned ShinyHunters members “may also falsely claim to have sensitive or compromising information, including embarrassing photographs or videos of victims, which frequently do not exist.”

The hackers told The Register their attack on the FBI “demonstrated our technical capabilities and directly refuted the misinformation disseminated by the FBI, journalists, and industry researchers.” At the same time, the group’s leaders seemed to acknowledge that the FBI’s warning materially harmed their prospects for convincing victims to pay, saying “this was fundamentally a public relations and marketing initiative for our business.”
