The religious left makes a play for red-state conservatives

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Democrats in a pair of deep-red states are testing the religious right's dominance by putting their Christian faith at the center of their campaigns — potentially creating a template for the party to compete in similarly forbidding terrain.

Why it matters: Upsets by Texas' James Talarico and Kansas' Adam Hamilton would all but ensure that Democrats take the Senate.

  • A recent poll from Pew shows both an opening and an obstacle: White evangelicals still strongly favor Republicans, while many other Christian groups lean Democratic or are more narrowly split.

The big picture: Talarico, a Presbyterian seminary student, and Hamilton, a United Methodist pastor, are challenging conservatives' claim to speak for faith. Their bids come after Democrat Raphael Warnock, senior pastor of Ebenezer Baptist Church, was elected to the Senate in 2021.

  • President Trump won both Texas and Kansas by double digits in 2024. Neither state has sent a Democrat to the Senate in the 21st century — the last time Kansas did was in 1932. Both races this year are considered toss-ups.

Zoom in: Talarico's "religiosity is a way to soften his liberalism" and potentially win over Christians turned off by his scandal-plagued opponent, says Brandon Rottinghaus, who co-hosts Houston Public Media's "Party Politics."

  • Talarico has also made several visits to Black Baptist churches. Trump made inroads with minority communities in Texas, and Talarico looks to do the same after besting Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-Texas), who is Black, in a brutal primary.
  • He's harnessed scripture in his pitch against Christian nationalism and for protecting the vulnerable, invoking the biblical campaign slogan, "It's time to start flipping tables."
  • "We're in a populist political moment, and many of the teachings of the social gospel give him an opportunity to link the politics and his faith together," Rottinghaus adds.

By the numbers: A September Marist survey of 1,139 Texas registered voters showed Paxton with a 75% to 22% lead over Talarico among white evangelical Christians.

In Kansas, the race will hinge less on whether Hamilton can win large numbers of religious Republicans and more on whether he can replicate Democratic Gov. Laura Kelly's coalition, says Alexandra Middlewood, the political science department chair at Wichita State University.

  • Hamilton has presented himself as a bridge-builder, drawing on his experience leading the largest United Methodist church in the nation.
  • His well-known religious background could appeal to less engaged, moderate Christians and center-right voters, Middlewood says.

Yes, but: While Kansas has elected blue governors and voted to keep abortion legal in the state, Republicans have a massive registration advantage that's made a Democratic Senate win "near impossible," notes Bob Beatty, Washburn University's political science chair.

  • Establishment Democrats have long been viewed with suspicion, especially in western Kansas, he said.
  • But Hamilton being a pastor "gives him an open doorway to approach those voters in western Kansas that I would argue previous Democrats have not had."

Zoom out: Black clergy — including Warnock — have a history of political action. But white clergy are stepping up to run as Democrats in substantial numbers.

  • In Iowa, Sarah Trone Garriott, a Lutheran minister, and Lindsay James, a Presbyterian pastor, are running in tight House races.

Pastor Doug Pagitt, executive director of progressive Christian group Vote Common Good, says the religious left's surge is driven by candidates' personal convictions and years-long efforts from groups like his that have trained politicians on how to connect with faith voters.

  • But Trump, who Pagitt says has repeatedly offended Christians, is also a motivator: "There is no single person who could be more helpful to Democrats reaching out to religious people right now than Donald Trump. He is the greatest recruiting tool."

Yes, but: Trump has repeatedly defied predictions that the religious right wouldn't vote for him.

The other side: Ralph Reed, chair of the conservative Faith and Freedom Coalition, tells Axios he's doubtful the Democrats' effort to lean into religion will attract voters of faith.

  • "Voters will not vote for a candidate who professes to hold to their faith, but doesn't share their values," he says.
  • One GOP opponent labeled clergy running for office as Democrats "PINOs" — Pastors in Name Only.

But while Reed says he's confident Republicans will turn out after Trump's battleground blitz, he acknowledges their economic struggles.

  • "Evangelicals and other conservative voters of faith, they live in the real world too," he says.

Whether evangelicals are motivated to show up will be a major story of the midterms, says Ryan Burge of the John C. Danforth Center on Religion and Politics.

  • The other will be how Catholics shift: "If the Adam Hamiltons, the James Talaricos of the world can win over people, it's going to be ... marginally attached Catholics," Burge predicts.

The bottom line: There's a clear movement on the religious left — but the question remains whether voters will join it.

Qu'est-ce que le PHP et pourquoi a-t-il déclenché une vague mondiale de mesures de cybersécurité ?

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Pendant plus d'une décennie, de graves failles sont passées inaperçues dans la technologie qui sous-tend une grande partie d'Internet.

Il a fallu recourir à l'intelligence artificielle (IA) pour les mettre au jour, ce qui a déclenché une alerte mondiale en matière de cybersécurité.

Ces vulnérabilités se trouvent dans un langage de programmation appelé PHP, utilisé à l'échelle internationale depuis les années 2000 pour créer des sites web.

Les gouvernements et les agences de cybersécurité du monde entier s'efforcent de limiter les répercussions de cette découverte faite mercredi, et ont exhorté les opérateurs de sites web à installer un nouveau correctif d'urgence ou à envisager de mettre leurs plateformes hors ligne.

La société de cybersécurité Wiz – l'une des entreprises dont les chercheurs ont mis au jour ces failles – a qualifié ces failles de « plus graves » jamais détectées par l'IA.

OpenAI, dont la technologie a permis de détecter ces failles, a qualifié cette découverte de « l'un des problèmes de sécurité les plus graves de ces dernières années ».

La plupart des gens n'ont peut-être jamais entendu parler de PHP – abréviation de « PHP : Hypertext Preprocessor » –, mais ils y sont très certainement confrontés au quotidien.

PHP est à la base d'un grand nombre de plateformes, notamment les boutiques en ligne, Wikipédia et WordPress – le système de gestion de contenu qui sous-tend de nombreux sites web tels que les blogs.

Lorsque vous consultez un site web comme Wikipédia, c'est le code PHP qui rassemble les informations dont vous avez besoin et les présente sous une forme lisible sur le web, a expliqué Paul Spain, directeur général de Gorilla Cyber Security, à la BBC.

Mais en raison d'une faille susceptible de permettre à des pirates de prendre le contrôle des serveurs web concernés sans authentification et de voler des données sensibles, toutes les applications développées en PHP se sont retrouvées exposées.

L'IA pourrait faciliter et accélérer ce type d'attaques, a déclaré M. Spain, estimant que cette faille pourrait toucher « des millions et des millions » de sites web.

Ce serait « catastrophique » si des pirates informatiques ou des États utilisaient cette faille à des fins malveillantes et s'emparaient de données privées sur des sites web, a déclaré Jill Slay, chercheuse en cybersécurité à l'université d'Adélaïde.

Cette alerte a perturbé les services à l'échelle mondiale, des plateformes de jeux aux portails gouvernementaux en passant par les forums en ligne.

C'est notamment le cas de Wikimedia — l'organisation à l'origine de l'encyclopédie en ligne Wikipédia — qui a mis hors ligne plusieurs de ses systèmes à titre de mesure de précaution.

Des sites et des pages ont été mis hors ligne par le gouvernement finlandais, le conseil municipal d'Édimbourg et la BBC, tandis que l'agence néerlandaise de cybersécurité a également retiré son site web.

Un certain nombre d'organismes publics écossais, notamment des conseils municipaux et des conseils de santé, ont également mis leurs sites Internet hors ligne.

Certains experts avertissent que cette vulnérabilité pourrait exposer à un risque accru les sites Internet mal entretenus, en particulier ceux appartenant à de petites structures disposant de ressources limitées en matière de sécurité.

D'autres tentatives d'exploitation de ces failles sont à prévoir dans les semaines à venir, puisque les détails techniques sont désormais rendus publics, a déclaré Salil Kanhere, chercheur en cybersécurité à l'UNSW de Sydney.

Des informations vérifiées à portée de main

Les développeurs de la plateforme PHP ont publié mercredi des correctifs visant à corriger les failles de sécurité qui se cachaient dans leur logiciel depuis des années.

« Tous les utilisateurs de PHP sont vivement encouragés à effectuer la mise à jour sans délai », a déclaré la société.

Les autorités et les organismes de sécurité du monde entier se sont fait l'écho de ces avertissements, exhortant les exploitants de sites web à sécuriser leurs plateformes.

L'Agence nationale de sécurité américaine (NSA) a demandé à toutes les organisations utilisant PHP de mettre leurs systèmes hors ligne si elles n'étaient pas en mesure d'appliquer immédiatement les correctifs, ajoutant que cette faille pouvait offrir un « accès direct » permettant d'exploiter la base de données d'un système.

« Ces risques sont suffisamment graves pour nécessiter l'application immédiate de correctifs dans l'ensemble des ministères et agences du gouvernement américain », a déclaré l'agence.

Le Centre national de cybersécurité du Royaume-Uni a émis des recommandations similaires et a conseillé aux organisations de vérifier la présence éventuelle de « portes dérobées installées par un pirate ».

Ses homologues de l'Union européenne, de Singapour et d'Australie ont également publié des alertes similaires.

La Première ministre japonaise, Sanae Takaichi, a exhorté la population à vérifier si leurs sites web accessibles au public fonctionnaient sous PHP.

« Nous demandons instamment à tout le monde de prendre des mesures dès ce soir », a écrit Mme Takaichi sur X.

« S'il est difficile d'appliquer rapidement le correctif, la suspension temporaire des sites web et d'autres mesures similaires peuvent être considérées comme un moyen efficace de réduire les risques », a-t-elle déclaré.

Les entreprises technologiques s'empressent également de sécuriser leurs systèmes.

Le géant du commerce électronique Amazon a déclaré avoir été la cible de multiples tentatives quelques jours avant la divulgation publique de la faille PHP. On ignore comment les attaquants potentiels ont eu connaissance de cette vulnérabilité.

Ces failles ont été découvertes par des chercheurs des sociétés de sécurité Calif et Wiz.

Ces chercheurs ont utilisé des outils d'intelligence artificielle avancés, capables d'analyser le code et de détecter les failles bien plus rapidement que ne le peuvent les humains ou les anciens systèmes.

« Les logiciels modernes sont incroyablement complexes, et certaines failles peuvent passer inaperçues pendant des années », a déclaré M. Kanhere.

« Les bogues sous-jacents n'étaient pas nouveaux ; ce qui a changé, c'est notre capacité à les détecter », a ajouté M. Kanhere.

Des détails concernant cette découverte ont fuité sur Internet cette semaine, contraignant les développeurs de la plateforme PHP à annoncer en urgence l'existence de cette faille de sécurité et son ampleur considérable.

Cet incident « met en évidence la capacité de l'IA à détecter des failles de sécurité », mais aussi la manière dont elle peut « aider les pirates à développer rapidement des outils offensifs et des exploits », a déclaré OpenAI dans un article de blog.

Le créateur de ChatGPT a indiqué avoir publié un programme permettant de vérifier si la plateforme PHP est sécurisée.

« Bon nombre de ces failles proviennent de la manière dont les logiciels ont été conçus par le passé, c'est-à-dire en privilégiant la fonctionnalité au détriment de la sécurité », a déclaré Ryan Ko, du Cyber Research Centre de l'université du Queensland.

Les propriétaires de sites web sont invités à sécuriser leurs sites à l'aide de la dernière version de PHP.

Ce correctif PHP constitue une petite mise à jour de routine pour la plupart des équipes techniques, a déclaré M. Spain.

L'Agence nationale de sécurité américaine (NSA) a appelé à une « vigilance accrue » au cours des prochaines semaines et à suivre de près les mises à jour proposées par les éditeurs de logiciels.

« Il est primordial de faire preuve de vigilance lors de l'application des correctifs liés à ces systèmes », a-t-elle déclaré.

La responsabilité incombe principalement aux exploitants de sites web, mais les utilisateurs doivent rester attentifs à tout conseil de sécurité émis par les services concernés, comme les demandes de changement de mot de passe, a déclaré M. Kanhere.

Il a également averti le public de se méfier de tout message de hameçonnage exploitant la médiatisation de cet incident.

Hundreds of flights cancelled as Typhoon Champi shuts Taiwan

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Taiwan is bracing for Champi's worst effects on Saturday

Hundreds of flights in the East Asia region were cancelled on Friday as powerful Typhoon Champi roared close to Taiwan's north-east coast.

Schools and offices in Taiwan were shut as Champi neared the island. While it is uncertain if it will make landfall, the storm was forecast to bring 90cm (35in) of rain and strong winds to the north-east until Saturday.

One person died after being swept away by waves, authorities said. The storm also injured 171 people and forced 1,000 others to evacuate.

Champi is the latest in a string of intense typhoons as a Super El NiÃ±o heats up the Pacific Ocean and fuels storms. At its peak earlier this week, it was the planet's strongest storm of the year so far.

On Friday afternoon, Champi was packing wind gusts of 191km/h (119mph) .

After passing Taiwan's north-eastern tip and the Okinawa islands in southern Japan, Champi is expected to hover over the waters off China's eastern coast, before curving farther to the north-east to Japan's main island.

Weather agencies in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Japan forecast that the eye of the storm is expected to pass without making landfall in Taiwan.

However, a forecaster in Taiwan's Central Weather Administration told Reuters that they could not rule this out completely - especially if the storm "stalls, loops or wobbles from side to side" as it approaches.

Rainfall is expected to peak on Saturday morning and continue to batter Taiwan until Saturday evening, the Central Weather Administration said.

Taiwanese authorities have urged people to stay away from coasts and mountains.

Work and classes were suspended in nearly all of Taiwan's 22 counties and cities on Friday - what's colloquially known as a "typhoon holiday" - as Champi approached the island.

Taiwan's $5tn (Â£3.7tn) stock exchange was also closed on Friday because of the severe weather.

Only Kinmen county, an archipelago located to the west of Taiwan's main island, continued work and classes as usual.

In Japan, power cuts were reported in nearly 8,400 households in the southern Okinawa prefecture, according to the local power company.

More than 160 flights have been cancelled across four Japanese airlines.

Champi has also stirred up heavy rains and winds in Batanes, a Philippine island chain below southern Taiwan.

Photos posted on the local government's Facebook page showed waves crashing into seaside villages and roads closed to traffic. There were no immediate reports of casualties.

Typhoon Champi became the strongest storm of the year earlier this week, when it attained a maximum sustained wind speed of 185mph (298km/h) as it moved over the Philippine Sea - the equivalent of a Category 5 hurricane.

While initially categorised as a "super typhoon", Champi weakened on Wednesday as it continued barrelling through the sea.

This is shaping up to be one of the most active typhoon seasons the region has seen in years. Champi is Japan's 30th typhoon of the year - making this the first time since 2013 that the country has seen so many storms.

The recent frequency of storms is due to an unusually strong El NiÃ±o - a naturally occurring phenomenon that hits the Pacific Ocean every few years and can trigger extreme weather events, from droughts to typhoons.

96 Prozent der Korallenriffe drohen bis 2075 abzusterben

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Den Prognosen der Weltnaturschutzunion (IUCN) zufolge, sind künftig fast alle Korallenriffe vom Aussterben bedroht.

Wegen des fortschreitenden Klimawandels sind laut einer Prognose der Weltnaturschutzunion (IUCN) innerhalb der kommenden 50 Jahre fast alle Korallenriffe der Welt vom Absterben bedroht. Es bestehe für 96 Prozent der Korallenriffe in den Weltmeeren „ein extrem erhöhtes Risiko“, dass sie bis 2075 absterben, hieß es in einer am Donnerstag veröffentlichten IUCN-Analyse. Es war die erste weltweite Untersuchung dieser Art.

Die IUCN schätzte für ihre Prognose den Zustand und die Entwicklung von Korallenriffen vor den Küsten von mehr als 80 Ländern und Gebieten ein. Dabei stufte sie 96 Prozent in die Kategorie „vom Aussterben bedroht“ ein - dies ist die höchste von der Organisation definierte Gefährdungsstufe.

Korallen bilden natürliche Barrieren gegen Erosion, sind Reservoirs der Artenvielfalt und Kohlenstoffspeicher. Sie bleichen unter Hitzeeinwirkung aus und sind besonders anfällig für die Auswirkungen der globalen Erwärmung: In wärmeren Ozeanen sterben die Mikroorganismen, die ihnen ihre leuchtenden Farben verleihen und sie mit Nahrung versorgen - die Korallen werden bleich und „verhungern“ schließlich. (APA/AFP)

Rubio turns to small Indian town as US faces high oil prices

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio was in India this week

The western Indian port town of Jamnagar is not a usual stop for foreign leaders, yet it featured prominently in US Secretary of State Marco Rubio's recent visit.

So, why did Rubio visit the town? And, more importantly, why did he skip the Indian capital, Delhi?

The answer to both questions may have less to do with India and more to do with Donald Trump administration's domestic compulsions â or, more precisely, its domestic oil needs.

Rubio arrived in the financial capital Mumbai on Tuesday, where he met Indian Foreign Minister S Jaishankar and some of the country's top business leaders. He officially ended his trip in Jamnagar - in the western state of Gujarat - home to the giant oil refinery owned by one of Asia's richest men, Mukesh Ambani.

It was his second trip to the country this year and experts say what is significant this time is the timing of the visit as well as his choice of destinations.

The Trump administration is under pressure to keep fuel prices in check ahead of November's US midterm elections, while rising tensions in the Middle East threaten global energy supplies.

With little new refining capacity expected in the immediate future in the US, India's ability to process large volumes of crude oil offers Washington a potential way to ease pressure on the fuel markets.

And that puts Reliance's Jamnagar refinery at the centre of the equation.

The visit, however, comes at a tricky moment in India-US relations. Trump has imposed tariffs on Indian goods, there are sanctions against Delhi for buying Russian energy (though currently paused) and there is a trade deal between the two countries that has remained elusive for around 20 months now.

Rubio (left) held talks with foreign minister S Jaishankar (right) in Mumbai

Announcing the visit, the US State Department had earlier said that it would focus on energy cooperation, commercial investment and economic ties.

The official itinerary also did not include Delhi, which raised some questions, especially from Indian opposition politicians. But analysts say the choice was strategic.

"Delhi is where the trust gap is largest. Officials there are unhappy with a number of decisions and policies from Trump's administration," Michael Kugelman, a senior fellow at the Atlantic Council, told the BBC.

But former Indian ambassador to the US, Meera Shankar, cautions against reading too much into him skipping the capital.

"This was primarily an energy-focused visit," she says. "And after all, Rubio did meet the foreign minister in Mumbai."

The meeting between the two foreign ministers at the Taj Hotel covered trade, investment, energy security and defence cooperation, according to official statements. India's foreign ministry also said both sides welcomed progress in trade negotiations and reaffirmed their commitment to concluding a deal, which has long been in the works.

But once the diplomatic meetings were over, Rubio headed to Jamnagar, where the crown jewel of the Ambani business empire is located. This came to define the trip.

Shankar says the refinery visit "showed how much importance the US is attaching to getting more refined fuel into the market before the November midterms".

Reliance's Jamnagar complex can process about 1.4 million barrels of crude oil a day, roughly 1.5% of global refining capacity, according to the company website.

It is also a major exporter of diesel and other petroleum products, giving it a crucial role in global fuel markets.

During a tightly controlled visit on Wednesday, with limited media access, Rubio toured the facility and met members of the Ambani family, including Mukesh Ambani.

The company later released a statement, saying, "the visit included wide-ranging discussions on the global energy landscape and opportunities for commercial partnerships across conventional and new energy". It, however, did not specifically address any commitments to buy crude oil or supply refined products.

Just a day earlier, while speaking to the press, Rubio had called India a "refining superpower" and said the US wanted Reliance to process more Venezuelan oil.

"They have the capability to refine heavy crude. They've done so in the past, and I think as more and more Venezuelan oil comes online, that's something they could do," he told reporters.

Rubio added that oil from Venezuela "can certainly provide relief" in the short term and can possibly help ease supply pressures amid tensions in the Middle East.

Reliance was once among the largest buyers of Venezuelan crude before US sanctions disrupted trade. After Venezuelan President NicolÃ¡s Maduro was captured in a US intervention earlier this year and Washington eased sanctions on the country's energy sector, Reliance re-emerged as a major importer.

Rubio's visit to the Jamnagar refinery was closed to the media, with images released afterwards by the Reliance Group

Experts also say the visit might ease some tensions in the Delhi-Washington relationship over India's purchase of Russian oil.

India became one of the biggest buyers of Russian oil since Moscow invaded Ukraine in 2022.

Between December 2022 and July 2026, it accounted for 37% of Russia's crude oil exports, according to the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA).

And Reliance was among the Indian companies that processed Russian crude.

In August 2025, US President Donald Trump imposed an additional 25% tariff on Indian goods over its Russian oil purchases, taking total tariffs on many Indian goods to 50%. The US, however, withdrew the additional penalty in February 2026 but India's import of Russian oil has continued to be an irritant in Washington-Delhi ties.

India has always said that securing affordable and reliable energy for its 1.4 billion people was its priority.

Then earlier this month, the US itself temporarily lifted sanctions on Russian diesel.

In that context, experts say Rubio's Jamnagar visit is aligned to three goals that the US appears to be pursuing: reducing India's dependence on Russian crude, encouraging greater imports of Venezuelan and American oil, and advancing a trade deal in which energy cooperation could become a key area of agreement.

There is also another possible business angle behind this push.

Kugelman says Rubio had two reasons to meet the Ambani family. First, to secure greater refining of Venezuelan oil and second to seek assurances that Reliance remained interested in backing a proposed Texas refinery.

In March, Trump announced a proposed refinery at the Port of Brownsville in Texas on his Truth Social platform. He said that it would be the first major new refinery in the US in 50 years.

Reliance has not publicly commented on it and there are no details on the project or when the work is expected to begin.

"The Texas facility may not be as immediately important to US interests as the arrangement tied to Venezuelan oil, but I still think it's part of the story," he says.

Trump hopes to bring down gas prices as the US heads to midterms next month

However, analysts point out that closer cooperation with Reliance and progress on energy security do not necessarily mean a trade deal is imminent.

"I don't think anyone really expected a trade deal to be announced at this point, but this trip seems to have helped get the relationship back on track, to the extent that it can, given the significant points of tension that remain," Kugelman said.

Rajani Sinha, the chief economist at credit rating agency CareEdge, told the BBC that greater cooperation could allay energy security concerns in both countries, help push the stalled trade negotiations forward and "boost trade and sentiment" in India.

Meera Shankar says that India is possibly "waiting for the US to formalise its tariff positions so that we know whether Delhi has a preferential trade deal".

She adds that, although ties between the two countries have been strained in recent times, their bilateral trade continues to grow.

India's merchandise exports to the US was $50.81bn between April and September, up 11.1% from the previous year, according to India's ministry of commerce. Its imports rose to $32.33bn from $25.66bn in the same period. India's merchandise trade surplus with the US narrowed to $18.48bn from $20.07bn a year earlier.

"The economic relationship remains strong, but a long period of uncertainty could eventually affect it," Shankar said.

So, for now, the visit may have possibly helped get the relationship back on track, but whether that translates into a trade agreement or a lasting shift in energy ties remains to be seen.

Scientists discover a hidden messaging system deep inside the brain

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Deep within the brainstem is a tiny group of nerve cells that plays an outsized role in how the brain functions. This region, called the locus coeruleus (LC), gets its name from the Latin words for "blue place." Although it contains only a small fraction of the brain's neurons, it produces norepinephrine (NE), a chemical messenger that helps regulate attention, learning, heart rate, and the body's reactions to stress and excitement.

Scientists have long assumed that the LC operates like a loudspeaker, sending essentially the same norepinephrine signal across the brain. However, a new study published in Nature challenges that understanding. Using brain imaging, recordings of neural activity, genetic techniques, and behavioral experiments in mice, researchers at the Allen Institute discovered that the LC communicates through several distinct pathways. Rather than broadcasting one general message, it delivers signals to specific destinations, with different groups of neurons responding to different experiences.

A Hidden Brain Network That Sends Precise Messages

By tracing the connections of LC neurons, researchers found that different groups of cells communicate with specific regions of the nervous system. These pathways determine where signals trigger the release of norepinephrine, creating a much more organized communication network than previously recognized.

Neurons in the upper (dorsal) portion of the LC primarily connect to the cerebral cortex and forebrain. Meanwhile, cells in the lower (ventral) portion send their signals mainly to the brainstem and spinal cord. This arrangement allows different parts of the LC to influence separate brain functions.

"The findings suggest the brain's norepinephrine system is far more like a targeted postal network than a foghorn," said Karel Svoboda, director of Neural Dynamics at the Allen Institute and study co-author.

Why the Discovery Could Matter for Brain Disorders

Understanding how this signaling network is organized could eventually change how certain neurological and mental health conditions are treated.

Norepinephrine plays a central role in regulating attention and mood, making it an important target for medications used to treat depression, anxiety, and ADHD. Neurons in the LC are also among the earliest brain cells to deteriorate in Alzheimer's disease, suggesting that the new findings could have implications for understanding and treating these conditions.

Current treatments that influence norepinephrine can affect multiple brain circuits at once, sometimes producing beneficial effects in one region while causing unwanted effects elsewhere. A clearer map of the LC's specialized pathways could help scientists develop therapies that act more selectively on the circuits involved in particular symptoms.

The research received support from the National Institutes of Health's Brain Research Through Advancing Innovative Neurotechnologies® Initiative, also known as The BRAIN Initiative®.

Scientists Identify Brain Cells Involved in Learning

The researchers also uncovered an important distinction between the functions of different LC neurons.

Cells in the dorsal LC that connect to the cortex were particularly active during learning. When mice performed decision-making tasks and adjusted their choices after an unfavorable result, these neurons increased their activity.

Neurons in the ventral LC behaved differently. Their activity rose shortly before mice ignored signals indicating that a reward might be available. This suggests that these cells help regulate whether animals respond to opportunities in their surroundings.

"What emerged was a clear map: neurons in the dorsal LC that send signals upward to the cortex are involved in learning, whereas neurons in the ventral region projecting downward to the brainstem and spinal cord govern whether animals engage with their environment at all," said Svoboda. "We also showed that these anatomical differences are mirrored by distinct gene expression patterns."

These findings indicate that the differences between the two groups extend beyond their locations and connections. They also have distinct patterns of gene activity that reflect their specialized roles.

Researchers Discover a Record-Breaking Neuron

The study involved an extensive examination of the mouse brain. Scientists used whole-brain imaging to study nearly 35,000 neurons, analyzed the genetic profiles of almost 400,000 cells, and reconstructed the complete structures of several exceptionally complex nerve cells.

During this work, they encountered another remarkable finding: LC neurons possess extraordinarily long axons. These projections carry signals from nerve cells to other cells, allowing information to travel across the nervous system.

The researchers found that LC axons measured approximately 35 centimeters long on average. One particularly extraordinary axon had a total reconstructed length of 70.32 centimeters, making it part of the longest single neuron ever measured in a mouse.

"This neuron, like many others that we studied, supplies NE to a very large volume of the cerebral cortex. For the brain, this is highly unusual. Most neurons are more specific in their targets," said Jeremiah Cohen, scientist at the Allen Institute and study co-author. "But this neuron doesn't release NE everywhere. It ignores the cerebellum, brainstem, and spinal cord."

Despite its remarkable length and extensive reach, the neuron does not distribute norepinephrine throughout the entire brain. Instead, its connections remain selective, reinforcing the discovery that the LC operates through carefully organized signaling pathways.

A New Understanding of How the Brain Learns

The researchers also identified intriguing similarities between the norepinephrine system and the brain's dopamine signaling system.

Dopamine is another major chemical messenger that sends signals to the basal ganglia, a brain region involved in forming habits. Scientists suspect that dopamine and norepinephrine work together to support learning, potentially allowing the brain to acquire several complex and abstract concepts at the same time.

By revealing the intricate connections, specialized functions, and unusual physical structure of neurons in the locus coeruleus, the research provides a more detailed picture of how the animal brain communicates and learns.

Although the findings come from experiments in mice, they could eventually help scientists better understand human brain function and design treatments that target specific neural circuits rather than affecting entire signaling systems.

The Indian workers filming their work to train AI

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Mohammed Ashim is paid to film his hands at work

Mohammed Ashim has been cutting hair and trimming beards for four years in Bengaluru.

But recently the 22-year old has been strapping a camera to his head to record his work.

The camera only records his hands and fingers - his client's faces are blurred out.

"The routine is simple, but you have to be disciplined about how you move. The operations team taught me that my hands always have to stay cleanly inside the frame, the lighting has to be sharp, and I can't tilt my head too abruptly while working."

He wears the camera for five or six hours a day.

"It's good money, but honestly, the feeling of doing something this futuristic is what keeps me excited. I never imagined my razor techniques would be useful to a global tech company," says Ashim.

He is one of hundreds of thousands of workers across the world paid to record their activities.

Ashim's footage is collected by a local company, which might have hundreds of people filming their work.

That video is then sold on to technology companies and research organisations who use it to train artificial intelligence - in particular so-called physical AI, a type of artificial intelligence that can control robots in the real world.

Rashdan Technologies collects and processes such human generated data.

"Every robotics company, every frontier AI lab, has a specific kind of data they want: industrial tasks, agriculture, household chores, small business work like pottery, garment-making, embroidery, candle-making. We go wherever the task is," says co-founder and CEO Shahil Baig.

His company pays $5 to $10 (Â£3.74; Â£7.47) for eight to 10 hours of recorded work.

"For a lot of these people, that's meaningful," Baig says.

Making sure workers give their consent and are comfortable with the process is important, he says.

"We record hands, not faces, and where multi-camera rigs do capture more of the body or environment, we blur faces out. No names, no contact details, nothing that identifies them personally goes into what we hand off."

But his customers are getting more demanding.

"Now clients are asking for four to six cameras rigged around a person's head to cover the entire scene, not just the hands.

"Getting someone to agree to one small camera is one conversation. Getting them to agree to wearing what's effectively a five-camera headset is a completely different, and harder, one. We pay more for that tier of data collection because of what we're asking of people."

And the industry is becoming more crowded, Agarwal expects dozens more competitors to emerge in India this year.

Shahil Baig expects dozens more firms to start collecting video of workers

If you want robots to navigate the real world, they can't just be trained inside computer simulations, argues Manish Agarwal, the founder of Humyn Labs, which collects and processes human-generated data.

Most of that data comes from video, but sometimes collectors wear hardware on their hands and fingers to capture physical contact.

"Physical tasks require high-quality signals involving limb movements, fingers, wrists, elbows, shoulders and dynamic force application. For example: applying the correct grip force so a plastic bottle doesn't crumble versus a steel glass," he says.

And until recently, that data was not available.

"There is no existing internet archive of embodied human activity - no dataset of a kitchen being cleaned, or a car being assembled by hands that know exactly how much force to apply. That data has to be captured from scratch, in the real world, across diverse environments," says Agarwal, whose company has bases in Bangalore and San Francisco.

At the moment his firm has 96,000 data collectors across countries in Latin America and Southeast Asia.

Humyn Labs filters the thousands of hours of video supplied by those collectors, selecting the most useful content. It's then labelled so the AI knows what it is looking at and packaged in a format that can be used by clients.

"You need to source across a variety of factories, a variety of tasks, in different geographies because if you want to build deployment for US factories or US houses, you'd want environments that are a proxy for that. Residential houses in India may not be the right context for building a robotics use case for the US."

The factory workers teaching robots how to work like humans

But are workers collecting data for machines that will one day do their jobs?

At the moment it's too early to tell says Bino Paul, senior professor at the School of Management and Labour Studies at the Tata Institute of Social Sciences (TISS) in Mumbai.

For some workers AI might be a useful tool, but robots could automate other jobs, he says.

The problem is that, if machines do start to take over, there will be little workers can do about.

"By the time workers realise what they have participated in, things may have gone too far," he says.

Like many others, he wants governments to regulate AI.

"AI cannot be viewed merely as a technical system. We need to develop a socio-technical system in which ethics is upheld through human agency, ensuring that AI delivers beneficence rather than maleficence."

Despite concerns over where AI is heading, money is still being poured into its development.

Some of that trickles down to workers in India, where there is no shortage of workers prepared to wear a camera for a few dollars a day.

Mohammed Arman has been working in the textiles industry in Bangalore for 10 years.

"Garments stitching and cutting. I know this work so well I could probably do it with my eyes closed. Cloth, thread, needle, machine after 10 years, your hands just know what to do before your mind even tells them," he says.

He has recently started wearing a head-mounted camera that records his work.

"For me, it's simple. This is survival. I have a family back home in Bihar, and every rupee I send back matters. If someone offers me extra money to wear a camera while I do the exact same work I've been doing for 10 years, I'm not going to say no.

"It doesn't cost me anything. I'm already doing the stitching, already doing the cutting. Now I just get paid a little more for it," he says.

Mohammed Arman has been filming his tailoring work

While there is demand for such footage it will be collected somewhere.

"India's current role in this industry is largely as a supplier of raw material - people strap on cameras, record themselves doing ordinary tasks, and that footage gets shipped off to labs abroad," Agarwal says.

But he argues the value of that kind of work will quickly become eroded, as more companies compete for collection contracts.

"India can't afford to stay a footage supplier. It needs to move up the value chain: from recording raw video to owning the pipeline that turns that video into something usable - cleaning it, structuring it, validating it, and ultimately setting the standards for what counts as high-quality training data," he says.

Jolly Arifur has been filming domestic work for a few hours a day

For some people collecting data is attractive part time work.

Science teacher Jolly Arifur was looking for work she could do from home that would fit around her young son.

"For the past seven months, I strapped a rig-mounted camera on my head while doing my routine domestic work scrubbing, cooking, sweeping tile floors, and folding laundry."

Arifur, who lives in Guntakal, Anantapur, fits the recording work around her teaching.

"I get paid two to three dollars per hour. If I record three to five hours a day, that's a good amount in hand it's become an extra source of income for me, on top of teaching. I'm managing both honestly, fantastically."

A handful of neat capabilities help Apple's foldable phone feel even more intuitive.

If you’ve managed to snag a shiny new iPhone Duo, congratulations on surviving the scramble to get one. Now the fun begins.

I’ve spent a little over a week with Apple’s first foldable phone, and it has several features that make it feel like a genuinely practical and intuitive device — and with a $1,999 starting price, I’d sure hope so.

What stands out about the Duo is its versatility. Similar to other passport-size folding phones like the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 and the Xiaomi 18 Fold, you can hop between a more compact, 5.4-inch cover screen when sending a text or checking a notification and a wider, 7.6-inch inner display when watching videos, multitasking or scrolling through social media. It’s like a phone-tablet hybrid that fits neatly into a pocket or the palm of your hand.

Details like the matte inner display, which minimizes glare and makes the crease less noticeable, along with seamless software integration across screens give the Duo a distinct character of its own.

Here are four features worth checking out to get the most of your new iPhone Duo. And if you’re still in the market for one, it’s worth checking out carrier and trade-in deals to help bring down the cost (and maybe get the phone earlier than January).

The iPhone Duo’s foldable design and dual screens open up more ways to take pictures. A few handy features let you easily use the 48-megapixel rear cameras for better shots than you’d get with the 12-megapixel cover-screen selfie camera.

If someone is taking a picture of you or you want to snap a selfie with the rear camera, use Duo Preview to get a glimpse of what the shot will look like. With the phone open, you’ll see an image preview on the cover screen as you or your photographer snaps away, so you can ensure the framing and poses look exactly how you want.

To turn on Duo Preview, unfold the Duo and open the Camera app. Then tap the control in the top left corner (when the phone is in landscape) that looks like a mini Duo. Tap Duo Preview and snap away.

If you’re trying to take a group photo and there’s no one around to snap it for you, you no longer have to awkwardly prop your phone against a surface and then race against a timer — or make do with a lackluster selfie. Place your Duo on a flat surface when it’s half-folded and point the camera at your subjects. A feature called Smart Take will use AI to detect when everyone is in-frame and ready, and then automatically take a photo. As people change their poses, Smart Take will register that there’s a fresh scene to snap. You can also use Duo Preview to check framing via the cover screen.

To enable Smart Take, open the camera app while the Duo is either opened or closed. Then tap the photo options in the corner (six little dots), and tap Smart Take. A button with a yellow circle will then appear to the right of the shutter; tap that to start the process.

Smart Take will keep snapping pictures for around two minutes or until it captures about 20 images as people readjust their poses. You’ll then be able to sift through the options in Photos.

Lastly, Kid Cue can make it easier to hold a child’s attention when snapping photos. With the Duo open, a Snoopy animation will play on the cover screen to draw attention toward the camera. To turn on Kid Cue, unfold the Duo, open the Camera app and tap the Duo icon in the upper left corner. There, you’ll see the option to turn on Kid Cue.

Apple launched StandBy in 2023, which turns the iPhone into a smart display of sorts, with access to widgets like the clock, calendar and weather. But unlike other iPhones, the Duo doesn’t have to be charging to launch into StandBy.

Just set the Duo down while it’s locked, either slightly unfolded and on its side in tent mode, or while standing upright. Then you’ll see your favorite widgets, as well as Live Activities. That way, you can track the status of a food delivery, sports score or approaching rideshare at a glance.

To ensure StandBy is enabled, go to your iPhone’s Settings, then tap StandBy and check that the toggle is on.

Streaming video really shines with the iPhone Duo’s dynamic design. Open the phone to watch on the tablet-like, 7.6-inch display. Minimal black bars at the top and bottom of the screen allow for more immersive viewing.

You can also fold the Duo halfway and set it down, essentially making it look like a mini laptop. Digging Into the Money Behind the US Healthcare System

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The independent source for health policy research, polling, and news.

The United States spends a lot of money on healthcare. To better understand how much, who spends it, and what it’s spent on, we dug into the federal government’s data.

In 2024, the most recent year for which data was available, the U.S. spent $5.3 trillion on healthcare. That means an average of more than $14.4 billion was spent each day, and more than $10 million each minute.

Note: Data is an average based on 2024 spending. Source: Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services’ national health expenditure data Credit: Lydia Zuraw/KFF Health News

Health spending per person increased to an average of $15,474 in 2024, not necessarily because we’re sicker, but because of increased prices. According to the Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker, the U.S. has half as many in-person doctor consultations per person as peer countries. In other words, we pay more for less care.

Note: Dollar amounts are adjusted for inflation and shown in 2024 dollars. Source: Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services’ national health expenditure data; Bureau of Economic Analysis’ personal consumption expenditure data Credit: Lydia Zuraw/KFF Health News

For more than two decades, hospital care has been the largest category of healthcare spending. It’s expected to remain the largest share over the next decade, though the federal government projects that retail prescription drug spending will rise 5.7% a year on average — slightly faster than hospital care.

Spending on goods and services over time, in trillions of dollars:

Note: Dollar amounts are adjusted for inflation and shown in 2024 dollars. Source: Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services’ national health expenditure data; Bureau of Economic Analysis’ personal consumption expenditure data Credit: Lydia Zuraw/KFF Health News

Patients in the U.S. are shouldering higher insurance premium payments and other out-of-pocket costs. People have told KFF Health News that they are forgoing health insurance coverage, rationing their medications, cutting back on doctor visits, and putting off health procedures.

Federal data delineates the main sources of healthcare spending as private health insurance, Medicare, Medicaid, out-of-pocket costs, and other programs. Households, governments, businesses, and other private entities are responsible for financing those sources.

Many current federal healthcare proposals focus on shifting costs from one funder to another rather than lowering them. For example, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, enacted in 2025, shifted more government spending onto households, increasing the burden on low-income patients. If Congress were to restore covid-era subsidies for Affordable Care Act plans, costs would shift back toward government, which is funded disproportionately by wealthier taxpayers.

Meanwhile, healthcare costs are eating up a growing share of our economy. The $5.3 trillion spent on healthcare in 2024 accounted for 18% of the nation’s gross domestic product that year.

And those costs are expected to continue growing faster than the overall economy, hitting 20% of the nation’s gross domestic product by 2032.
