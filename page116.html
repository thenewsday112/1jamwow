Neuer Roman von Dörte Hansen: Henning hat den Hof, Broder das Nachsehen

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Zwei Brüder und ein Hof - das bietet Konfliktpotenzial. In "Broder" erzählt Dörte Hansen mit Härte, Wärme und Witz von brüderlicher Rivalität. Und von einer Landwirtschaft, der die Gewissheiten abhandenkommen.

"Im Februar steht alles auf der Kippe", heißt es auf der ersten Seite von Dörte Hansens neuem Roman. "Die Haselsträucher blühen, und die Amseln singen schon, aber die Böden sind noch nass und kalt und auf den Höfen alle Hoffnungsspeicher leer. (...) Wenn einer aufgibt, tut er es im Februar." Es sind Sätze, die unter die Haut gehen und nach denen gleich klar ist: "Broder" lässt sich nicht mehr aus der Hand legen.

Auch in ihrem vierten Buch greift die Autorin auf ihr erprobtes Erfolgsrezept zurück: Sie schreibt in diesem klaren, unsentimentalen, manchmal auch harten Dörte-Hansen-Sound und nimmt ihre Leserinnen und Leser mit in die norddeutsche Provinz. Dieses Mal geht es auf den Hof der Familie Bahnsen.

Den wollte eigentlich der titelgebende Broder übernehmen, bekommen hat ihn Henning. Die beiden sind Zwillinge, sehen sich zum Verwechseln ähnlich, könnten aber verschiedener nicht sein. Einer "mit Kuhverstand und einer mit Verstand für Technik. Man hätte sie zu einem Mann zusammenbacken sollen, dann wäre der perfekte Bauer dabei herausgekommen."

Broder ging nach Südfrankreich, lebte in einer Kommune, wurde Großtierarzt. Zusammen mit seiner Frau - einer Künstlerin, die Brandruinen malt - kehrte er zurück und kümmert sich nun um die Hochleistungskühe seines Bruders, der auf "digitales Herdenmanagement" umgestellt hat. Broder impft und besamt die Tiere, behandelt ihre entzündeten Euter, ist bei Schwergeburten zur Stelle und reanimiert Kälber mit Mund-zu-Nase-Beatmung - ohne dafür in irgendeiner Weise entlohnt zu werden, ist ja Familie.

Er erträgt es, denn "Ertrag kommt von Ertragen. Von allen Glaubenssätzen, die Paul Bahnsen seinen Söhnen mitgegeben hat, war das der schlimmste und ehrlichste." Aber manchmal muss Broder seine Wut hinausschreien, vor seinem hüftkranken Jagdhund, weitab vom Hof. Er kann den Gedanken einfach nicht verscheuchen, dass eigentlich er es sein sollte, der in der Küche unter den Fliegenfängern sitzt und die Geschicke auf dem Hof lenkt. Und er bemerkt, dass der Vorzeigebetrieb seines Bruders immer stärker in Schieflage gerät - und schweigt.

Mit Broder und der brüderlichen Rivalität im Mittelpunkt entwirft Hansen das schonungslose Bild einer kriselnden Landwirtschaft. Das ist nicht immer leichte Kost. Dass sich der Roman trotzdem nicht erdrückend schwer liest, liegt an Hansens Art, die Zwillinge und die sie umgebenden Menschen zu gestalten: Sie entwirft ihre Figuren als vielschichtige Persönlichkeiten, begegnet ihnen mit großer Zugewandtheit und immer wieder auch einer Prise Humor.

Und mit jeder Person fügt Hansen ihrer Erzählung um Veränderung, Verlust und Verantwortung weitere Facetten hinzu. Da ist zum Beispiel Ellen, Hennings Frau, die die "Dorfbegabung" hat. Sie kennt den Wettkampf der Landfrauen um die schönsten Dahlien, den geglücktesten Hefeteig, die am akkuratesten gestickte Hardanger-Decke. Und ganz hinten im Keller bleibt ihr Blick plötzlich an etwas hängen, das sie hatte vergessen wollen: ein Dutzend Bohnengläser und in ihnen "die ganze eingeweckte Schuld".

Es sind solche Formulierungen, die lange im Gedächtnis haften bleiben. Auch mit Paul verbinden sich einprägende Sprachbilder. Der Vater der Zwillinge lebt auf dem Altenteil, ignoriert konsequent "Astronaut", den Melkroboter, und hält an seiner jahrzehntelagen Tradition fest: Jeden Morgen schiebt er sich mit einem Stock "wie ein Stocherkahn über den Hof" und hangelt sich durch den Stall, wo er die Kühe auf Krankheiten "abfühlt".

Und dann ist da noch die junge Generation: Maibritt reißt sich mit ihrem Vater Henning darum, per Tablet die Kühe zu überwachen und dreht für ihren Blog kleine Videos über den modernen Hofalltag. Ihre Cousine Claire ist angehende Tierärztin. Aber nach einem Praktikum bei ihrem Vater und tiefen Einblicken in tiermedizinische Kompromisse, die sie in Ställen und auf Weiden beobachtet, hat sie mehr Fragen als Antworten.

Von Zweifeln an seinem Berufsstand ist auch Broder nicht frei. Einiges aber bleibt unzweifelhaft immer gleich: Am Sonntag trifft er sich mit den "Rasenrockern" zum Fußballspielen. Und wenn sie feiern, er Bereitschaft hat und nüchtern bleiben muss, fährt er seine Freunde nach Hause. "Alle drei sternhagelvoll, wie immer, einer singend, einer schlafend, einer weinend."

Hansens Kunst ist es, dem (Land-)Leben auf die Finger zu schauen und klug, sensibel und nahbar über die kleinen und die eigentlich viel zu großen Katastrophen menschlichen Miteinanders zu schreiben. Aber Dörte Hansen wäre nicht Dörte Hansen, wenn sie nicht auch etwas Hoffnung durchschimmern lassen würde.

Denn irgendwann geht auch der Februar zu Ende. Und dann kommt der Morgen, "an dem das ganze Fuder Elend kippt. Die Wolkendecke dünn wird. Reißt. Die Sonne plötzlich strahlt wie auf den Fotos der erbaulichen Kalender, die die Pastoren alten Menschen schenken, wenn sie krank sind oder achtzig werden, neunzig, hundert." Und da ist auch er dann wieder: dieser so einnehmende Witz der Autorin.

My video of the moment Thomas Markle received letter from Meghan accusing him of abusing young girls. I was there and know exactly what happened next. And I can reveal secret of why he's only speaking out now, by CAROLINE GRAHAM

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

By CAROLINE GRAHAM, US EDITOR THE MAIL ON SUNDAY

Published: 11:13 GMT, 25 October 2026 | Updated: 19:05 GMT, 25 October 2026

Thomas Markle had only just managed to sit upright in his hospital bed for the first time since having his leg amputated when there was a knock at the door.

Two women walked in. One was clutching a white envelope bearing Mr Markle's name.

It was December last year and this was the moment he discovered that his estranged daughter, Meghan, had sent him a letter.

What neither of us could have imagined was that the envelope would become the focus of a fresh and deeply troubling chapter in the extraordinary saga of the fractured Markle family.

It has now emerged that, according to The Sun, Meghan's letter contained the sensational allegation that she 'knew her father had abused young girls' and that she wrote he would never meet his grandchildren, Archie, seven, and Lilibet, five.

I had absolutely no inkling that the innocuous-looking white envelope contained such a bombshell.

Out of respect for Meghan's privacy, I never asked Mr Markle what the letter said.

But I vividly remember the bizarre circumstances in which it was delivered because it was a scene so surreal it has stayed with me ever since.

The moment in December last year that Thomas Markle discovered that his estranged daughter, Meghan, had sent him a letter

It has now emerged that, according to The Sun, Meghan's letter contained the sensational allegation that she 'knew her father had abused young girls'

I flew to the Philippines last December at Mr Markle's urging. We became friends in 2018 and he asked me to come after doctors discovered a massive blood clot that meant his left leg would have to be amputated below the knee.

By the time I arrived in Cebu – the city to which he and his son Tom Jr had moved in January 2025 in search of a 'fresh start' – Mr Markle was in intensive care.

It was in Cebu that Tom Jr allegedly discovered a stash of child sex images on his father's computer, phone and hard drives, claims Mr Markle has vehemently denied.

A few days after my arrival, Mr Markle was transferred to a private room at the University of Cebu Medical Centre.

For a then 81-year-old man who had already suffered two heart attacks and a stroke, his recovery was remarkable.

I remember a physiotherapist entering the room and announcing: 'OK, Mr Tom, it's time for you to get moving.'

He manoeuvred him from a prone position into a sitting position on the edge of the bed. We were smiling at this small but significant milestone when there was a knock at the door.

In walked two women who introduced themselves as a lawyer and the hospital's president.

There had been no warning of their arrival.

Mr Markle was sitting on the bed with the stump of his leg exposed, still wrapped in a bloodied bandage. Instinctively I reached for a sheet to cover it and preserve his dignity.

The lawyer was carrying a clutch of legal documents. The women explained that they had come to deliver a letter from his daughter Meghan and that Mr Markle would have to sign paperwork authorising them to film the encounter so that footage could be sent to Meghan's office as proof that the sealed letter had been handed to him.

I immediately offered to leave the room but Mr Markle asked me to stay.

Thomas Markle and Filipino nurse Rio Canedo, 46, at the rehab centre in Cebu, Philippines

'If you are going to video this, then I would like Caroline to video it too,' he told the lawyer.

Here was an elderly man, seriously ill and heavily medicated with powerful painkillers, being asked to sign legal documents before receiving a letter from his own daughter.

The hospital's president performatively handed him the envelope bowing slightly as she said: 'Mr Markle, I have been asked to hand you this personally by your daughter Meghan. It is sealed. I need to make sure you know it is sealed.'

There was a nod towards the iPhone camera being held by the lawyer.

Once they knew the encounter had been successfully captured on tape the two women left as quickly as they had arrived, leaving Mr Markle and I alone with the glaringly white envelope lying on his hospital bed.

He told me he was not ready to read Meghan's letter and asked me to move it out of his sight. I put it on a plastic table next to his uneaten lunch.

For the next four hours he fretted about opening it. He told me he was 'scared' it would 'contain bad news'.

Eventually he decided he could put it off no longer.

He asked me to hand him the envelope. Once again, I offered to leave the room while he read it.

He read the letter in silence, saying nothing. He shook his head several times. Then he folded the letter up, slipped it back into its envelope and asked me to put it in the wardrobe.

And that was that. He told me nothing about its contents. I asked no questions.

Tom Jr, Meghan's brother, claimed to have found the child-abuse material packed up in boxes at his father's Philippines home

The letter went into his bag in the wardrobe containing the dirty clothes he'd arrived at the hospital in and we never discussed it again. Until now.

Following The Sun's revelations about the letter on Sunday, I telephoned Mr Markle, who is now living in a rehabilitation centre in Florida after returning to the US in May to continue his medical treatment.

He told me he has 'a vague memory' of receiving the letter but cannot recall what it said.

Mr Markle has since been diagnosed with dementia, a condition he has authorised the Daily Mail to report.

He also gave permission for us to share the video of the extraordinary hospital encounter.

'I remember getting the letter but I don't remember anything else about it,' he said.  'I'm happy for people to see the video. I have nothing to hide.'

I asked him about The Sun's report that Meghan said in the letter that she knew of allegations he was a child sex abuser: 'I'm not a paedophile. I've never done anything bad to a child. Never.

'If Meghan says that's what she wrote, then she's probably right. I was very sick. I have memory issues. I know there was a letter, but I don't remember what it said.'

Mr Markle left the Philippines in May, leaving most of his possessions behind. He intended to return later this year once he had learned to walk using a prosthetic limb.

He has strenuously denied his son's allegations about the child sex abuse images, describing the claims as 'terrible lies'.

He maintains the computers said to contain the material were out of his control for nearly two years following their move to Cebu.

'The stuff was boxed up in Mexico and put on a container ship. I kept asking Tom for my computers, but he said they were in storage.'

Meanwhile, Tom Jr, a man I also have known for years, insists his father is in 'denial' and claims he acted because the material he found was so horrific he had to report it.

Authorities in the Philippines have launched an enquiry and the FBI in the US is likely to follow suit this week.

Mr Markle has said he will fully co-operate with authorities to clear his name and has even offered to take a lie detector test.

For now the letter that arrived under such weird circumstances in that Filipino hospital room remains missing its contents, beyond what has been reported by The Sun, unconfirmed by Mr Markle himself or, as of now, the Duchess of Sussex.

Mr Markle claims he remembers nothing of the letter's contents but I will never forget the dramatic, and frankly weird, way it was delivered.

Pourquoi des personnes qui n'ont jamais fumé deviennent accros à la cigarette électronique

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Crédit photo, TOLGA AKMEN/EPA-EFE/REX/Shutterstock

Lorsque le vapotage a commencé à se répandre dans le lycée de Gracey Mungulu, elle n'a pas hésité à dire haut et fort qu'elle trouvait cela aussi répugnant que de fumer.

« Je disais littéralement aux gens : "Euh, pourquoi tu fais ça ?" », raconte-t-elle.

Mais Gracey explique que cette habitude est devenue si courante parmi ses camarades de classe qu'à l'âge de 16 ans, elle s'y est elle-même mise, même si la première fois, elle s'est mise à tousser au lieu d'inhaler.

« Plus on voit ça, plus on a envie d'essayer un peu plus », explique Gracey, « et puis, au final, je m'y suis habituée et je le faisais tout le temps. »

Aujourd'hui âgée de 21 ans, elle achète chaque jour une cigarette électronique rechargeable, qu'elle utilise dès que ses mains ne sont pas occupées par son travail de coiffeuse et à l'école.

Gracey fait partie du million de personnes au Royaume-Uni qui déclarent n'avoir jamais fumé mais qui vapotent aujourd'hui, selon les données d'une récente enquête de l'Office national des statistiques (ONS) – et ce chiffre augmente d'année en année.

Lorsque la cigarette électronique a fait son apparition sur le marché, elle était considérée comme un moyen d'aider les fumeurs à arrêter la cigarette.

La consommation de nicotine par vapotage, une substance hautement addictive, est moins nocive que la cigarette, mais ses effets à long terme ne sont pas encore clairement établis, selon le NHS.

Des informations vérifiées à portée de main

Alors pourquoi des personnes comme Gracey, qui n'ont jamais fumé de cigarette, s'y mettent-elles ?

Cette Londonienne estime que la cigarette électronique l'aide lorsqu'elle ressent de l'anxiété sociale.

« C'est le geste de la main », explique-t-elle, « le simple fait d'avoir quelque chose dans la main, d'avoir quelque chose dans la bouche. »

Tout comme les cigarettes, la cigarette électronique de Gracey contient de la nicotine, une substance hautement addictive qui provoque des symptômes de sevrage, tels que l'irritabilité et l'anxiété, lorsqu'elle disparaît de l'organisme.

Le vapotage est devenu un « geste machinal », explique-t-elle. Lorsqu'elle a répondu à l'appel de BBC News pour cet article, Gracey a raconté que, sans même y penser, elle était allongée dans son lit, sa cigarette électronique dans une main, tandis que de l'autre, elle faisait défiler Instagram.

Le caractère habituel de la vape régulière est un phénomène dont le Dr Helen Wall, médecin généraliste, entend souvent parler de la part de ses patients.

« C'est ce genre de dépendance psychologique à quelque chose d'autre qui sert presque à réguler leurs émotions », explique-t-elle.

Tout comme Gracey, Malin Andersson, qui vapotait jusqu'à récemment, affirme que son habitude allait de pair avec le « doomscrolling ».

Elle avait également essayé la cigarette, mais ne s'était jamais considérée comme une fumeuse.

À 33 ans, elle fait partie de la tranche d'âge la plus susceptible de vapoter. Le rapport de l'ONS suggère que près d'une personne sur six âgée de 25 à 34 ans au Royaume-Uni pourrait utiliser des cigarettes électroniques quotidiennement ou occasionnellement.

Cette militante contre les violences conjugales et ancienne candidate de « Love Island » a commencé à vapoter alors qu'elle traversait une rupture difficile et cherchait du réconfort. Elle pense que c'est le geste physique qui l'a rendue dépendante.

« C'est une poussée de dopamine », explique Malin. « C'est littéralement la version adulte d'une tétine. »

Elle explique que cela engourdissait ses sens et la rendait démotivée.

Malin était tellement frustrée par la dépendance qu'elle avait développée vis-à-vis de sa cigarette électronique à la saveur de glace à la banane qu'elle a arrêté en mai de cette année.

« Ça m'énervait tellement de l'avoir toujours dans la main que je finissais par la jeter à travers la pièce », raconte-t-elle.

Alex, originaire de Manchester, a elle aussi progressivement pris l'habitude de vapoter, une habitude qui a commencé après qu'elle eut vapoté de temps en temps lors de ses sorties nocturnes, puis qu'elle eut commencé à ramener ses cigarettes électroniques chez elle.

La jeune femme de 26 ans, qui n'a pas souhaité donner son nom de famille, explique que cette habitude était devenue une partie si intégrante de sa vie qu'elle travaillait « allongée dans mon lit, mon ordinateur portable devant moi et ma cigarette électronique posée sur ma poitrine ».

La professeure Sarah Jackson, du groupe de recherche sur l'alcool et le tabac de l'University College London, souligne que si les chiffres récents de l'ONS peuvent « tirer la sonnette d'alarme », il se pourrait que certaines personnes de ce groupe aient commencé à fumer si elles ne s'étaient pas mises à vapoter.

« Dans ce cas, le vapotage les a détournées d'un comportement plus nocif, ce qui est une bonne nouvelle », déclare-t-elle. « D'un autre côté, certaines personnes s'exposent à davantage de risques qu'elles ne l'auraient fait autrement. »

Elle cite une étude réalisée en 2024 par des chercheurs de l'UCL, qui a révélé une augmentation de la consommation de nicotine chez les jeunes adultes en Angleterre, alors que le tabagisme avait diminué.

« On peut en déduire que certaines des personnes qui vapotent n'auraient pas fumé autrement », explique Mme Jackson.

Selon le rapport de l'ONS, environ 5,4 millions de personnes âgées de 16 ans et plus utilisent la cigarette électronique en Grande-Bretagne, tandis que le nombre de fumeurs, tous âges confondus, continue de baisser régulièrement. Quelque 4,9 millions d'adultes au Royaume-Uni fument.

Le Royaume-Uni s'est fixé pour objectif de faire baisser le taux de tabagisme et de réduire l'attrait de la cigarette électronique auprès des enfants et des jeunes.

Le marketing des cigarettes électroniques – qui se caractérise par des couleurs vives et des arômes sucrés – fait actuellement l'objet d'un examen, et une récente consultation gouvernementale sur de nouvelles mesures devrait aboutir à une réponse en temps voulu.

Parmi le 1,1 million de vapoteurs n'ayant jamais fumé, « il existe probablement un groupe qui ne vapoterait pas s'il n'y avait pas le marketing très présent autour de la vape », explique Hazel Cheeseman, de l'association caritative Action on Smoking and Health.

Le NHS met en garde les non-fumeurs et les jeunes de 17 ans et moins contre la pratique de la cigarette électronique, tandis qu'il est illégal pour toute personne de moins de 18 ans d'acheter des cigarettes électroniques. Les cigarettes électroniques jetables – considérées comme un facteur clé de l'essor de la cigarette électronique chez les jeunes – ont été interdites l'année dernière.

Wall affirme que la campagne marketing intensive menée ces dernières années en faveur des cigarettes électroniques a contribué à entretenir une « idée fausse » selon laquelle elles seraient « sans danger et ne créeraient pas de dépendance ».

Gracey estime que les cigarettes électroniques devraient être vendues dans le même emballage neutre et standardisé que les cigarettes traditionnelles, car cela « fait peur ».

« Les cigarettes électroniques proposent toutes sortes d'arômes et de jolies couleurs, ce qui ne fait que nous attirer davantage vers elles ».

Même si elle reconnaît que le vapotage est considéré comme bien plus acceptable socialement que le tabagisme au sein de sa génération, elle trouve cette habitude problématique et a envisagé d'arrêter pour économiser les 35 £ par semaine que cela lui coûte.

Mais tout comme pour le tabagisme, arrêter de vapoter peut s'avérer difficile. Et pour Alex, le vapotage l'a conduite dans la direction opposée.

Elle explique avoir réussi à arrêter de vapoter pendant un voyage de plusieurs mois, mais, n'ayant pas accès à des cigarettes électroniques, elle s'est mise à fumer des cigarettes pour la première fois.

Les experts ne considèrent pas la cigarette comme un moyen sain ou efficace d'arrêter de vapoter. Le NHS recommande vivement aux gens d'arrêter de fumer, car le tabagisme reste la principale cause de maladies évitables et de décès prématurés au Royaume-Uni.

Pour arrêter de vapoter, le NHS suggère de réduire progressivement la concentration en nicotine de la cigarette électronique, d'espacer les séances de vapotage ou de se tourner vers un produit de substitution nicotinique adapté.

Mais l'accompagnement professionnel pour arrêter de vapoter est inégal : une enquête récente a révélé que moins de la moitié (45 %) des professionnels de santé en Angleterre proposaient un accompagnement pour arrêter de vapoter. La situation est contrastée dans le reste du Royaume-Uni.

Même s'il est positif de constater que moins de personnes fument, M. Wall estime que pour les personnes comme Gracey qui se tournent directement vers la cigarette électronique, une partie du message n'a pas été bien comprise. « La cigarette électronique n'était censée [selon le NHS] être qu'un substitut aux cigarettes. »

DeepSeek and MiniMax cleared to take AI models abroad, state media says

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A CCTV-linked account says the China AI export controls target data, sensitive uses and computing power. APIs, apps and SaaS stay outside, and most AI remains free to export.

“Since the policy was announced, two leading companies, DeepSeek and MiniMax, have successfully completed the registration process and obtained open-source and overseas licensing rights.”

That is what Yuyuan Tantian, a WeChat account affiliated with state broadcaster CCTV, said in a post late on Thursday. The post sets out how the China AI export controls proposed on 16 October will work. Most AI technology will stay free to export, it said.

DeepSeek and MiniMax did not immediately reply to requests for comment, Reuters reported. DeepSeek reportedly raised $15bn from Tencent and CATL this week. TNW has not independently verified the post.

The commerce and science ministries want to add 23 AI technologies to China’s list of technologies banned or restricted from export. They include large models, agents and computing clusters, according to the ministry’s announcement. Companies must register before they export a listed technology. Public comments close on 15 November.

The post said the rules target three areas. The first is data. The rules would ban exports of large models trained on China’s “core data”. They would restrict models trained on “important data”, whatever their size.

The second is sensitive use. Agents above a set performance level face restrictions. Agents built mainly for critical information infrastructure, defence or core government affairs face a ban.

The third is computing power. AI chip design and high-speed links for large computing clusters are on the list.

The catalogue also sets numbers for large models, the post said. They are training compute above 1026 floating-point operations, more than 2 trillion parameters, or capability in the global top ten.

Offering a restricted model through API calls, apps, web pages, SaaS or lightweight local deployment is not a technology export, the post said.

“Simply put, AI companies can continue with their commercialization efforts as usual,” it said.

Open-source technology gets automatic licensing, which the post described as “immediate reporting and export”. So do Chinese companies’ own computing centres abroad, chip tape-out overseas, joint research and work on international standards.

Technology already public through open source or academic papers falls outside the rules. Exports tied to cooperation among members of the World AI Cooperation Organization would get more support.

Liu Jing and Liu Hongmei of Beijing Dacheng Law Offices took part in the consultations, the post said. The control points for some AI items changed after the first draft, they told the account. Many of the concerns companies raised show up in the published version, they said.

Restricted technologies can still go abroad with a licence, Liu Hongmei said. That would not disrupt companies’ overseas plans.

The post set the rules against the US approach. It cited the Commerce Department’s AI diffusion framework of January 2025. It also cited an “alliance strategy” it attributed to Anthropic’s CEO, Dario Amodei.

“But China did not take this path,” the post said.

In a separate post on Friday, the account said overseas internet users had urged other Chinese open-source models to follow suit, Reuters reported.

Serbia election: Main players, voter concerns and what is at stake

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Sign up now: Get ST's newsletters delivered to your inbox

Oct 25 - Serbia is holding parliamentary elections on Sunday, in which a popular student movement is seeking to end the rule of longtime leader Aleksandar Vucic.

Here are the main players, issues and stakes in the vote.

WHO ARE THE MAJOR PLAYERS AND WHAT DO THEY STAND FOR?

Aleksandar Vucic – United Serbia: The Serbian Progressive Party (SNS)-led electoral list is campaigning on economic growth, higher wages and pensions, infrastructure investment and political stability. Earth’s Ice Ages May Have Started With a Planet-Wide Ocean Domino Effect

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

New research suggests that cooling in the Northern Hemisphere may have triggered far-reaching ocean changes that helped drive Earth’s past ice ages.

Growing ice sheets in the Northern Hemisphere may have helped push Earth into past ice ages by triggering changes in ocean circulation thousands of miles away, near Antarctica. New research from the University of St Andrews identifies a possible mechanism connecting these distant regions, helping explain how the planet naturally reduced atmospheric carbon dioxide (CO2) as ice ages developed.

Scientists have long known that the Southern Ocean plays a central role in regulating atmospheric CO2. Changes in its circulation and sea ice can increase the amount of carbon stored in the deep ocean, contributing to the cooling associated with ice ages. But researchers have struggled to explain how expanding northern ice sheets could influence waters so far south.

The growth of massive Northern Hemisphere ice sheets begins with changes in Earth’s orbit that alter how solar energy is distributed across the planet. According to the new study, published in the Journal of Climate, these expanding ice sheets may set off a much larger chain of events by cooling nearby North Atlantic waters.

As those colder waters move through the global ocean circulation system, they can eventually reach the Southern Ocean. If the northern cooling is strong enough, it could alter circulation around Antarctica and promote conditions that allow more CO2 to remain stored in the ocean rather than the atmosphere.

This proposed connection provides a physical explanation for how ice sheet growth in one hemisphere could help amplify cooling across the planet.

To test the hypothesis, researchers used computer simulations that cooled only the ocean’s northernmost latitudes (mimicking the effect of a large ice sheet). They then examined whether the temperature changes could travel south and alter Southern Ocean conditions in ways that reduce atmospheric CO2.

The results showed that cooling northern waters alone produced a substantial response. The simulated reduction in atmospheric CO2 reached 74% of the reduction produced when the researchers cooled the entire planet.

The experiments support the proposed connection between Northern Hemisphere ice sheets and Southern Ocean carbon storage. However, they demonstrate that the mechanism is physically plausible in a model, rather than establishing exactly how much it contributed to individual ice ages in Earth’s past.

Lead author Dr. Madison Shankle, from the University of St Andrews’ School of Earth and Environmental Science, explained:

“Essentially, we’ve produced evidence of a plausible mechanism connecting these two far-flung puzzles pieces – Northern Hemisphere ice sheets and Southern Hemisphere ocean circulation changes leading to CO2 drawdown – which has been a tricky thing to explain in our theory of Earth’s past ice ages. Our study takes the step of actually demonstrating them working in model simulations, which is a big step forward for this field.”

Ice age research has traditionally emphasized processes occurring around Antarctica, where ocean circulation strongly influences the exchange of carbon between the atmosphere and deep ocean. The new findings suggest that temperature changes in distant northern waters may also be an important part of that system.

Past ice ages provide natural examples of how carbon moves between Earth’s atmosphere and oceans over long periods. Understanding these processes can help scientists improve climate models and investigate how the global carbon cycle responds to changes in atmospheric CO2, including the additional carbon dioxide released by human activities.

MacGilchrist, Andrea Burke, Alice Marzocchi, Malte F. Jansen and James W. B. Rae, 22 October 2026, Journal of Climate. DOI: 10.1175/JCLI-D-25-0355.1

Scientists discover how pancreatic cancer rewrites its DNA to escape treatment

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Scientists have uncovered new details about how pancreatic cancer constantly changes its genetic makeup, allowing it to become more aggressive, spread throughout the body, and resist treatment. By examining nearly half a million individual cancer cells, researchers identified genetic changes that could reveal new ways to fight one of the deadliest forms of cancer.

The study, conducted by scientists at NYU Langone Health and its Perlmutter Cancer Center, focused on pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, a particularly difficult cancer to treat. Nearly 85 percent of patients die within five years of being diagnosed.

Although researchers have already linked more than half a dozen genes to the disease, much remains unknown about how pancreatic cancer develops and adapts. By examining tumor cells individually rather than studying them in large groups, the team gained a clearer understanding of how different populations of cancer cells change their DNA as the disease progresses.

How Pancreatic Cancer Evolves To Escape Treatment

The researchers discovered that pancreatic tumors contain a variety of distinct cell populations known as clones. Each clone consists of cancer cells with similar genetic characteristics, but their behavior can differ dramatically.

Some clones develop changes that help them withstand cancer treatments, while others become better equipped to invade surrounding tissues and spread to other parts of the body. These findings suggest that the cancer's ability to continually reorganize its genetic material plays a central role in both treatment resistance and metastasis.

Previous studies generally divided pancreatic cancer cells into two main categories: classical (less aggressive) and basal (more aggressive). However, because those studies examined large collections of cells together, scientists could not fully explain how cancer cells move from one category to the other.

The latest findings help explain this process. Genetic changes occurring within individual tumors allow cancer cells to shift between these two states, potentially making the disease more dangerous as it develops.

Scientists Identify a Gene That Could Make Pancreatic Cancer Less Aggressive

Published on October 13 in Nature Genetics, the study identified several genetic changes associated with pancreatic cancer progression. One particularly important finding involved MYC, a gene that showed heightened activity in some of the most aggressive cancer cell populations.

In additional laboratory experiments, the researchers blocked MYC activity in aggressive basal pancreatic cancer cells. Remarkably, the cells shifted back toward the less aggressive classical state.

The change also made the cancer cells more exposed to immune cells, suggesting they could become easier for the immune system to recognize and attack. Although further research is needed, the results point to MYC as a promising target for future pancreatic cancer treatments.

"Our study provides the most granular view to date of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, revealing how ongoing genetic reshaping drives tumor evolution over the course of disease," said study lead investigator Despoina Kalfakakou, PhD, a postdoctoral research fellow at NYU Grossman School of Medicine and Perlmutter Cancer Center. "These findings suggest that targeting the mechanisms underlying tumor evolution, including MYC, may help restrain the cancer's rapid progression."

"Our research also suggests that single-cell clonal analysis offers a better approach to understanding complex cancers, like pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, as it develops over time, by mapping how tumors evolve and adapt at each stage of disease progression," said study co-senior investigator Aristotelis Tsirigos, PhD, a professor in the Departments of Medicine and Pathology at NYU Grossman School of Medicine and a member of Perlmutter Cancer Center.

"More broadly, our study results highlight the critical role of clonal diversity, genetic plasticity, and interactions with the tumor's microenvironment in shaping human pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma biology," said Dr. Tsirigos, who is also co-director of the Division of Precision Medicine and director of NYU Langone's Applied Bioinformatics Laboratories.

Why Pancreatic Cancer Can Evade the Immune System

The investigation also provided important clues about pancreatic cancer's ability to hide from the immune system, a characteristic that may help explain why the disease often responds poorly to immunotherapy.

Researchers discovered that certain cancer cell clones showed increased activity of the TIGIT gene, which is associated with proteins that help tumors escape immune detection.

At the same time, these clones exhibited reduced activity of the PD1 gene. This combination may help explain why immunotherapies designed to target PD1 have shown limited effectiveness against pancreatic cancer.

Understanding these differences between cancer cell populations could help scientists develop treatments that interfere with the specific mechanisms tumors use to avoid immune attack.

According to Dr. Tsirigos, clinical trials investigating drugs that inhibit MYC or TIGIT are already underway for pancreatic ductal carcinoma and other cancers. He said the latest findings underscore the need for larger studies examining the different states cancer cells can adopt and how treatments might be tailored to target them.

Nearly Half a Million Cancer Cells Reveal Hidden Tumor Diversity

To investigate how pancreatic cancer changes over time, the scientists examined gene activity (using transcriptomics) in 443,451 individual cells collected from 62 tumors belonging to 56 men and women diagnosed with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.

Six of these patients also provided tumor samples both before and after chemotherapy, allowing researchers to examine changes associated with treatment.

Unlike traditional approaches that measure changes in gene activity across large groups of cells, the new analysis examined individual cells. This provided a much more detailed picture of the different cancer cell populations living within the same tumor.

The results showed that individual tumors contained approximately two to 11 distinct clones, consisting of varying combinations of classical and basal cancer cells.

By tracing the genetic origins of these clones and comparing patterns of gene activity, the researchers connected differences in DNA copy numbers with the types of cells present in each tumor.

This approach allowed them to identify which genes, including MYC and TIGIT, were particularly active at different points during cancer progression.

Together, the findings provide a more detailed understanding of how pancreatic tumors evolve, resist treatment, and spread. They also suggest that interfering with the genetic mechanisms driving these changes could offer new opportunities to combat a disease that remains exceptionally difficult to treat.

Brazil becomes next test for Latin America’s surging conservative movement

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Flavio Bolsonaro joined supporters in Santa Barbara D'Oeste to lead a motorcade. Polls in Brazil show a tight presidential race with incumbent Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva. (Video: Reuters.)

Brazil's estimated 158 million voters could advance Latin America's conservative wave in Sunday's election as the continent's most populous nation could elect conservative Sen. Flavio Bolsonaro as its next president.

The first round election three weeks ago saw a strong performance by Bolsonaro, proving the polls wrong. While incumbent President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva (Lula) had been predicted to take the first round by single digits, Bolsonaro ended up winning 47% to Lula's 45%. As neither candidate won a majority, the election went to a second round.

Brazil's election follows a long string of victories for the Latin American right across Central and South America that started with the election of Libertarian outsider Javier Milei in Argentina in 2023.

Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, left, and Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro, right, face off in a presidential election runoff. (Getty Images)

BOLSONARO WINS FIRST ROUND OF BRAZILIAN PRESIDENTIAL ELECTION, AS TRUMP-BACKED CANDIDATES CONTINUE RED WAVE

2025 saw a right-wing sweep across the region, as Ecuador, Bolivia, Honduras and Chile all voted for conservative heads of state.

In 2026, Keiko Fujimori narrowly defeated a left-wing candidate to win the Peruvian presidency, while most recently, in Colombia, conservative attorney Abelardo de la Espriella bested left-wing Senator Ivan Cepeda.

Bolsonaro's slate of congressional candidates also fared well, ensuring that Brazil will have one of the most conservative congresses in years, and a legislative branch aligned with Bolsonaro's goals, or ready to thwart Lula's agenda.

The two candidates met Friday night for the final debate in Rio de Janeiro, which featured a series of questions organized around Brazil's five geographic regions. While the debate featured some heated personal exchanges, it appears unlikely to have moved the needle in either direction heading into a close race.

Colombian President Abelardo De la Espriella holds a press conference with the mayor of Bogota and newly appointed city manager Clara Lucia Sandoval in Bogota, on Aug. 25, 2026. (Sebastian Barros/NurPhoto via Getty Images)

TRUMP'S WAR ON DRUG CARTELS COULD GAIN POWERFUL NEW ALLY AFTER PIVOTAL ELECTION

President Donald Trump has heralded these electoral results as vindication for his hardline approach to security, crime, and migration policies, as well as a stamp of approval for the new Shield of the Americas initiative.

The Trump administration counts on considerably more close allies now than it did at the beginning of Trump's second term.

Yet, the question remains: why is the region resoundingly rejecting the left and progressivism?

Argentina's President Javier Milei arrives for the inauguration of Colombia's President Abelardo de la Espriella in Cali, Colombia, Friday, Aug. 7, 2026.  (Matias Delacroix/AP Photo)

Many of the elections have been won by narrow margins, with Brazil being a case in point: Lula bested Jair Bolsonaro in 2022 by just 1.8%. While many Latin American nations remain deeply polarized, there is no denying a right-wing resurgence.

Commentators appear to be expressing a rejection of progressive movements amid growing concerns over safety and security, giving approval to proposals to dramatically ramp up police and military presence to combat crime and drug trafficking.

Meanwhile, leftist parties seem unable to capitalize on recurring concerns over inflation and kitchen table economics, which have played into the hands of the Latin American right, as working class and middle class voters have felt the pinch of rising food, housing and energy costs.

In addition to social and economic concerns, the specter of Jair Bolsonaro also looms over the election.  Flavio Bolsonaro has pledged to free his father, who is currently serving a 27-year sentence on house arrest for allegedly plotting a coup in the wake of the 2022 election.

Lula was also sentenced to a 12-year prison sentence on corruption charges stemming from the infamous Operation Car Wash, until he was freed on a technicality by the Brazilian Supreme Court, and rose from the ashes to win the 2022 presidential election.

Brazil's right-wing senator and presidential candidate Flavio Bolsonaro of the Liberal Party (PL) gestures next to federal deputy and candidate for governor of Rio de Janeiro, Douglas Ruas (L) during a campaign rally on the eve of the general election, in Campo Grande neighborhood, in the western zone of Rio de Janeiro, Brazil on October 3, 2026. Brazilians go to the polls on Oct.r 4, 2026 (Douglas SHINEIDR / AFP via Getty Images)

BRAZIL’S RAPID ELECTION COUNT PUTS SPOTLIGHT ON VOTING SECURITY AHEAD OF US MIDTERMS

Bolsonaro has narrowly led in recent polls, and enters the second round with some structural advantages. Most importantly, he enjoys the backing of three of the four other major candidates in the race, who failed to advance to the second round.

Self-help author Augusto Cury, former Goias Governor Ronaldo Caiado, and former Minas Gerais Governor Romeu Zema all officially backed Bolsonaro in the second round.

Entrepreneur Renan Santos, candidate for the Mission Party, declined to endorse either candidate. However, as his political movement is characterized as free-market and libertarian, these voters are also more likely to trend to Bolsonaro.

Thus, Lula needs to win a significant majority of first round voters who backed right-wing or center-right candidates. It's a daunting, but not impossible, proposition.

The entire election may hinge on voters who are voting against a candidate, as much as for a candidate.

Brazil's President and presidential candidate Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva (C) waves next to Vice-President Geraldo Alckmin (L) and to his candidate for Sao Paulo's governor, Fernando Haddad during a campaign rally on the eve of the general election, in Sao Paulo, Brazil on October 3, 2026. Brazilians go to the polls on October 4, 2026, in a tight race led by leftist President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva and right-wing senator Flavio Bolsonaro, son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving house arrest.  (Luis Robayo/AFP Via Getty Images)

Another question looming over the election: has Lula finally worn out his welcome with the Brazilian electorate?

This will be Lula's seventh time in 40 years appearing on a presidential ballot: he lost elections in 1989, 1994, 1998, won in 2002, 2006, and 2022.

Lula famously promised to serve only one term when he ran to oust Jair Bolsonaro in 2022.

President Trump sent a letter to former Brazilian President Jair Bolsonaro calling for an immediate end to the ‘unjust’ trial against him for allegedly starting a coup. (AP Photo/Evan Vucci and Evaristo Sa/AFP via Getty Images)

On October 25, 2022, just five days before the last second round presidential election, he posted on his official X account: "If elected, I will be a single-term president."

Various polls have shown that voters are not happy with the course of Lula's third term, and even a sizable percentage of Lula backers in 2022 responded with pessimism on the prospect of an unprecedented fourth term.

However, a large percentage of the Brazilian electorate also consistently rejects the prospect of voting for another Bolsonaro for president.

Lula is unquestionably the dominant figure in Brazilian politics over the past half-century. But will his magnetism and charisma be enough to overcome the right-wing resurgence sweeping the continent?
