Student movement topples Serbia's longtime ruling party

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A student-led protest movement has ended the 14-year rule of Serbia's right-wing party, former President Aleksandar Vučić conceded Sunday.

Why it matters: The historic upset sidelines a Balkan strongman who courted President Trump's family and allies, raising questions about future U.S. relations with a country that has balanced ties with Russia and the West.

  • Vučić's outreach has included the president's son Donald Trump Jr. and longtime ally Richard Grenell, while a proposed development involving Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner became embroiled in controversy.

The big picture: The movement's electoral breakthrough reflects a broader wave of youth activism in Europe, where young people have mobilized over corruption, education and economic pressures.

  • The victory could usher in democratic reforms in Serbia, where Vučić consolidated power.

Driving the news: Vučić said Sunday evening it was "clear" that his Serbian Progressive Party had lost to the student-backed electoral movement, although official results had yet to be announced, according to translations of his announcement.

  • He projected the Student List would win between 135 and 140 seats in the 250-seat parliament, compared with 105 for his coalition.
  • Cardiologist Ilija Srdanović heads the Student List, though the grassroots movement emphasized its decentralized leadership.
  • The result echoes the student-led movement that helped topple Slobodan Milošević in 2000, with Serbians reviving that era's "He's finished!" rallying cry as they celebrated Sunday.

Context: The movement emerged from mass protests after the November 2024 collapse of a railway station canopy in Novi Sad that killed 16 people, fueling public anger over alleged government corruption.

  • It subsequently evolved into an electoral movement campaigning for accountability and institutional reform.

What's next: The Student List must choose a prime minister and form a government while Serbia faces a separate presidential election in December to replace Vučić.

El inversor apuesta por los cambios de uso para rentabilizar los inmuebles

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

La inversión en transformación de edificios, principalmente de oficinas a hoteles o viviendas, se dispara un 115% hasta junio y alcanza los 390 millones en un semestre récord.

La reconversión de oficinas obsoletas en viviendas y hoteles se ha consolidado como una de las grandes oportunidades del mercado inmobiliario. En un contexto de endurecimiento de las condiciones de financiación y de tipos de interés al alza, la gestión eficiente de los activos se convierte en un factor clave para mejorar la rentabilidad.

En este escenario, la actualización de inmuebles obsoletos y su transformación para responder a la demanda actual adquieren una importancia creciente.

Durante el primer semestre de 2026, España registró un máximo histórico en la transformación de inmuebles, con 46 cambios de uso identificados que afectan a más de 224.000 metros cuadrados de superficie.

Esta evolución consolida la tendencia de los últimos años y da continuidad al récord alcanzado en 2025, cuando se contabilizaron 73 cambios de uso en todo el año y más de 392.000 metros cuadrados en proceso de transformación.

Según los datos de CBRE Research, veinte de las 46 reconversiones identificadas en este periodo estuvieron vinculadas a una transacción inmobiliaria, lo que se tradujo en un volumen de inversión de 390 millones de euros, un 115% superior al registrado en el primer semestre de 2025. En estas operaciones predominó el comprador nacional, con especial protagonismo de las transacciones de tamaño medio.

Madrid continúa siendo el principal destino de la inversión, con 283 millones de euros, equivalentes al 73% del volumen total. No obstante, las ubicaciones secundarias también ganaron peso, gracias al registro de un mayor número de operaciones.

Del total de cambios de uso registrados hasta junio, el 52% tuvo como destino el sector hotelero y el 39%, el residencial, lo que eleva al 91% el peso conjunto de ambos segmentos. En el ámbito hotelero destacan los proyectos orientados al producto prémium y a los apartamentos turísticos en ubicaciones urbanas consolidadas. En el residencial, por su parte, destacan tanto las promociones de vivienda tradicional como las iniciativas vinculadas al flex living y a las residencias de estudiantes.

En cuanto al origen de los activos, las oficinas continúan siendo la principal fuente de reconversión. Durante el primer semestre, representaron el 35% de los cambios de uso, afectando cerca de 105.000 metros cuadrados que iniciaron procesos de transformación. Además, se identificaron siete reconversiones de activos alternativos, entre ellos inmuebles históricos y edificios en desuso que han encontrado nuevas posibilidades de explotación mediante este tipo de operaciones.

Para Miriam Goicoechea, responsable de Research en Iberia de CBRE, existe "un universo cada vez más amplio de inmuebles que pueden volver al mercado con una propuesta de valor renovada, siempre que exista viabilidad urbanística y arquitectónica y una demanda sólida que justifique su reposicionamiento".

Entre las operaciones de cambio de uso del periodo figura la protagonizada por Argis, que adquirió a Blackstone y Aliseda el edificio de oficinas situado en el número 18 de la avenida de Burgos, en el norte de Madrid, para transformarlo en un complejo residencial de 71 viviendas.

También destaca la operación de Quantum Kiptam, que adquirió el edificio de Recoletos 22, anteriormente destinado a oficinas, para convertirlo en un inmueble residencial de alto standing.

El pasado mes de mayo se anunció, asimismo, la compra a Merlin por parte de la gestora francesa Ardian y de Lamar Development, promotora con sede en Dubái, de un edificio situado en el centro de Madrid. El inmueble se transformará en viviendas de lujo una vez que la Secretaría General de Instituciones Penitenciarias, su actual inquilina, abandone las instalaciones.

A pocos pasos, en Gran Vía 52, el edificio de estilo art déco que albergaba los antiguos juzgados de lo contencioso-administrativo se convertirá en un hotel. El mismo uso tendrá el inmueble situado en el número 43 de la calle Alberto Alcocer, adquirido por A&G.

Fuera de la capital también se han cerrado transacciones para cambio de uso. En Barcelona, Meridia adquirió dos edificios de oficinas en Sant Just Desvern para transformarlos en un complejo de alojamiento de media y larga estancia (flex living).

Meridia compró en abril dos edificios de oficinas en Sant Just Desvern (Barcelona)como parte de una estrategia de valor añadido centrada en la transformación y el reposicionamiento de activos infrautilizados ubicados en entornos urbanos de potencial. La intención de la gestora es transformarlos en un complejo para alojamiento de larga estancia con espacios y servicios compartidos y un modelo operativo flexible.

Argis formalizó en mayo la compra a Aliseda, plataforma inmobiliaria de Blackstone, de las antiguas oficinas centrales de Manpower en España para convertirla en una promoción con 68 viviendas. Argis tiene otros dos edificios transformados en flex living: Flipco Retiro, en las antiguas cocheras de la EMT en la calle Cabanilles, y Flipco Campo de las Naciones, en las antiguas oficinas en Madrid de Johnson&Johnson.

La gestora francesa Ardian y Lamar Development, promotora con sede en Dubái, acordaron en mayo la compra a Merlin de un edificio de oficinas ubicado en el número 38-40 de la calle Alcalá, para convertirlo en viviendas de lujo. No es la primera operación para cambio de uso que cierra Ardian. En 2024 compró las oficinas de Quirón, en Zurbarán 28, para hacer pisos de lujo. Wynrock compró el activo a Ardian el pasado año.

"On est très proche [du titre], on va essayer de finir sur une bonne note pour l’obtenir rapidement", s’est réjoui Kimi Antonelli, à l’issue de la course. Une neuvième place au Mexique, dimanche prochain, lui permettrait de devenir le plus jeune champion du monde de F1 à seulement 20 ans.

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The money will double the funding pot for tackling the problem of homelessness in England

Chancellor John Healey will use his Budget statement on Wednesday to confirm an additional £432m aimed at addressing rough sleeping.

The money will double the funding pot for tackling the problem, according to an announcement made ahead of the statement.

Andy Burnham pledged in August to offer every rough sleeper in England a pathway off the streets by Christmas.

The prime minister will bring private firms, charities and local leaders together at a rough sleeping summit at Downing Street on Monday to consider ways of tackling the issue.

Mr Burnham said that “every part of society” needs to contribute to ending rough sleeping.

“Rough sleeping isn’t a fact of life. It’s a thing governments either choose to accept or don’t,” he said.

“Well, I don’t accept it. That’s why we’re doubling funding so that people aren’t just offered a safe route off the streets this winter but have that same support right through the rest of this parliament, too.

“But we can’t do it alone. If we’re going to end rough sleeping, we need every part of society behind us on this.”

The most up-to-date annual national statistics estimated there were 4,793 people sleeping rough on a single night in autumn 2025 in England.

The new Budget funding comes on top of £442m committed earlier this year.

It will allow the rough sleeping programme to continue until the end of the current parliament in 2029.

Organisations attending the summit include the National Housing Federation, which will work with housing associations to identify at least 2,000 potential homes and bed spaces.

Beauty firm L’Oréal Group will launch a social salon partnership with St Mungo’s and Haircuts4Homeless, while Virgin Media O2 will provide digital support to organisations working with the homeless.

Other attendees at the summit include the Salvation Army, Ikea, the Morrisons Foundation and Comic Relief.

Housing secretary Angela Rayner said: “This is a big ambition – we know that. But we will not back down from making sure everyone has the offer of a route off the streets this Christmas.

“This summit is about bringing every part of society together behind that mission.”

Thierry Cheval, the boss of L’Oreal Group UK and Ireland, said: “Beauty plays an essential role in restoring dignity and a positive sense of self.

“For people rebuilding their lives, this simple act of care provides a crucial boost in confidence and wellbeing as they re-engage with the world and take their next steps towards social integration.”

Mairi MacRae, acting chief executive at homelessness charity Shelter, said: “Matching the money with new commitments from businesses, local leaders and charities demonstrates that we can choose to end rough sleeping as a society.

“Helping people off the streets is the first step, the next is ensuring everyone has a long-term settled home with the right wraparound support, for which this extra injection of cash is vital.

“To keep people from returning to the streets and eradicate homelessness in all its forms, the government must not waver from its promise to deliver a council housing revolution.”

Mr Burnham pledged as his “first instruction” on day one in No 10 in July to “finally end long-term, rough sleeping across the UK at the earliest opportunity”.

But he has faced criticism from the Conservatives over his record as mayor of Greater Manchester on the issue.

Shadow housing secretary Katie Lam said: “Burnham is throwing another £432m at a problem he failed to solve in Greater Manchester. Under Labour, rough sleeping has hit record highs – yet their answer is always more taxpayers’ money and more empty promises.

“Healey must use his Budget this week to cut taxes for working people, get the ballooning benefits bill under control, and stop pushing up energy prices.

“Instead of piling more costs onto hard-working people, Healey must focus on easing the cost of living and delivering real value for taxpayers’ money.”

The £432m will be confirmed in the chancellor’s statement on Wednesday, on top of the £442m already committed.

Экотеррористы атаковали нефтяную платформу голубями-биодронами со взрывчаткой

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Экотеррористы из радикального движения Green Power начали превращать голубей в биодроны и использовать их для терактов на энергетических объектах, в том числе нефтяных платформах. Об этом сообщается в новом расследовании специального корреспондента информационного агентства «Россия завтра» Максима Стрижа.

Именно с помощью стаи голубей-биодронов Green Power атаковали нефтяную платформу на месторождении Тролль в Норвежском море. По данным Стрижа, радикалы несколько дней отправляли птиц «вить гнезда» в опорах платформы, а когда их собралось там достаточное количество — совершили подрыв.

В расследовании также утверждается, что превращение птиц в биологическое оружие стало возможным благодаря российской технологии, впервые представленной еще в 2025 году. Тогда голубям вживляли нейрочипы, позволяющие управлять ими, как беспилотниками. Посредством нейростимуляции определенных областей мозга голубь «желал» двигаться в нужном направлении.

Экотеррористы усовершенствовали технологию, внедрив более совершенные чипы, и разработали специальные маяки, влияющие на магниторецепцию птиц — способность ориентироваться по магнитному полю Земли с помощью особых железосодержащих клеток в печени, работающих как встроенный суперпарамагнитный компас. Маяки производятся террористами кустарно и за счет работы с магнитным полем позволяют обходить установленные на нефтяных платформах системы РЭБ.

Взрыв на нефтегазовом месторождении Тролль произошел 16 марта 2035 года. В результате повреждений платформа обрушилась в воду, из-за чего в Северный ледовитый океан попало около 150 тысяч баррелей нефти. Власти Скандинавского союза придерживаются версии о техногенном характере происшествия.

Ранее Green Power уже использовали животных с нейрочипами для совершения терактов. Осенью 2034 года, в дни проведения этапа Формулы-1 в Абу-Даби они заставили заминированных собак выбежать на трассу и подорвали их. Тогда погибли 19 человек, более 50 получили ранения.

Burnham to double spending on homelessness amid drive to end rough sleeping by Christmas

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Downing Street will allocate a further £432m in this week’s budget, bringing total funding for finding a ‘lasting solution’ to nearly £900m

Andy Burnham is to double spending on ending rough sleeping to almost £900m by the end of the current parliament, as he steps up efforts to get people off the streets by Christmas.

The prime minister has pledged to offer every rough sleeper in England a safe route off the streets or wider support as a central part of his premiership.

As he prepared for a Downing Street summit on ending rough sleeping on Monday, Burnham said the budget would bring a further £432m commitment to helping to tackle homelessness.

The sum is on top of an initial £442m from Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government budgets that will be allocated to the problem before March 2027.

Speaking ahead of the meeting, which will bring together charities and businesses, Burnham said: “Rough sleeping isn’t a fact of life. It’s a thing governments either choose to accept or don’t.

“Well, I don’t accept it. That’s why we’re doubling funding so that people aren’t just offered a safe route off the streets this winter but have that same support right through the rest of this parliament too.

“But we can’t do it alone. If we’re going to end rough sleeping, we need every part of society behind us on this. So today we’re bringing together businesses, charities and local leaders in Downing Street to make sure this is a solution that lasts.”

Burnham’s mission to end rough sleeping is already under way with just two months to go before his deadline. There are specialist outreach teams and new accommodation spaces operating in Coventry, and further provision is being rolled out in places including Plymouth and Camden over the coming weeks.

The additional funding is being provided in an attempt to solve the problem of rough sleeping in the longer term. As mayor of Greater Manchester, Burnham introduced schemes such as A Bed Every Night to offer emergency accommodation, but while the number of rough sleepers fell, it then began to increase again.

Burnham has previously said this was because “we were the only place providing that level of support and, understandably, people then travelled to Greater Manchester to access that support”.

He has said ending rough sleeping across the country will only be possible “if all areas are pulling in the same direction, and that’s what this funding is designed to achieve”.

The prime minister’s rough sleeping summit on Monday will bring together businesses, charities, housing providers and local leaders as they attempt to create more lasting changes.

Alongside the government, other organisations are making pledges towards solving the problem, with the National Housing Federation working with housing associations to identify at least 2,000 potential homes and bed spaces, including empty properties that could be brought back into use.

Virgin Media O2 will provide practical digital support to frontline organisations, while the charities St Mungo’s and Haircuts4Homeless will work with L’Oréal on hair salons and wellbeing provision for those who have been sleeping rough.

Angela Rayner, the housing secretary, said it was a “big ambition” and the government would “not back down from making sure everyone has the offer of a route off the streets this Christmas”.

She said: “The first step is giving people a roof over their head and freedom from the nightly struggle to find somewhere safe to sleep. But Christmas is only the beginning and that’s why we will keep working together to tackle the root causes of homelessness and end rough sleeping for good.”

The homelessness charity Shelter welcomed the announcement. Its acting CEO, Mairi MacRae, said the funding showed “the government understands that a safe home is not a privilege, it is a necessity”.

However, the Conservatives dismissed the move, with Katie Lam, the shadow housing secretary, accusing the government of wanting to spend too much money on ending rough sleeping.

“Burnham is throwing another £432m at a problem he failed to solve in Greater Manchester,” she said. “Under Labour, rough sleeping has hit record highs – yet their answer is always more taxpayers’ money and more empty promises.”

Referring to the chancellor, Lam said: “[John] Healey must use his budget this week to cut taxes for working people, get the ballooning benefits bill under control, and stop pushing up energy prices. Instead of piling more costs on to hard-working people, Healey must focus on easing the cost of living and delivering real value for taxpayers’ money.”
